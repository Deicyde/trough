A **fractional ideal** of an integral domain $R$ is an $R$-submodule of the
[field of fractions](trough:IsFractionRing) $K$ of $R$ whose denominators can be
cleared by a single nonzero element of $R$. Fractional ideals are to
[ideals](trough:Ideal) what fractions are to integers: they allow ideals to be
divided as well as multiplied. In the rings of algebraic number theory, the
Dedekind domains, the nonzero fractional ideals form a group under multiplication,
and the failure of unique factorization of elements is measured by the quotient of
that group by its principal elements, the ideal class group.

## Definition

Let $R$ be an integral domain with field of fractions $K$. An $R$-submodule $I$ of
$K$ is a [fractional ideal](lean:FractionalIdeal) if there is a nonzero $d \in R$
with

$$dI = \{\, dx : x \in I \,\} \subseteq R.$$

Since $R \subseteq K$, every ideal of $R$ is a fractional ideal (take $d = 1$), and
[distinct ideals remain distinct as fractional ideals](lean:FractionalIdeal.coeIdeal_injective).
Conversely,
[a fractional ideal contained in $R$ is an ideal of $R$](lean:FractionalIdeal.le_one_iff_exists_coeIdeal).
Clearing denominators gives a description in terms of ideals alone:
[every fractional ideal has the form $a^{-1}J$ for a nonzero $a \in R$ and an ideal $J$ of $R$](lean:FractionalIdeal.exists_eq_spanSingleton_mul).
Some authors require a fractional ideal to be nonzero; here, as in Mathlib, the
zero submodule counts.

For $x \in K$, [the submodule $Rx$ is a fractional ideal](lean:FractionalIdeal.isFractional_span_singleton),
the [principal fractional ideal generated by $x$](lean:FractionalIdeal.spanSingleton).

## Examples

- In $\mathbb{Q}$, the fraction field of the [integers](trough:Int), the set
  $\tfrac12\mathbb{Z}$ is a fractional ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$ that is not an ideal.
  Since $\mathbb{Z}$ is a [principal ideal domain](trough:Submodule.IsPrincipal) and
  [over a principal ideal domain every fractional ideal is principal](lean:FractionalIdeal.isPrincipal),
  the fractional ideals of $\mathbb{Z}$ are $0$ and the sets $r\mathbb{Z}$ for nonzero
  [rational numbers](trough:Rat) $r$.
- The ring $\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12]$ is a $\mathbb{Z}$-submodule of $\mathbb{Q}$ but not a
  fractional ideal: a nonzero integer $d$ with $d \cdot 2^{-n} \in \mathbb{Z}$ for
  every $n$ would be divisible by every power of $2$.
- If $R$ is itself a field, then
  [its only fractional ideals are $0$ and $R$](lean:FractionalIdeal.eq_zero_or_one_of_isField).

## Operations

Sums, products and intersections of submodules preserve the defining condition:
[the sum of two fractional ideals is a fractional ideal](lean:IsFractional.sup),
[so is their product](lean:IsFractional.mul), the submodule generated by all
products $xy$ with $x \in I$ and $y \in J$, and
[so is the intersection of a fractional ideal with any submodule](lean:IsFractional.inf_right).
Under sum and product the fractional ideals
[form a commutative semiring](lean:FractionalIdeal.commSemiring) whose unit element is $R$.

Fractional ideals can also be divided. For fractional ideals $I$ and $J$ with
$J \neq 0$, the set $(I : J) = \{\, x \in K : xJ \subseteq I \,\}$ is
[again a fractional ideal](lean:IsFractional.div_of_ne_zero). Taking $R$ in place
of $I$ defines the inverse: for $J \neq 0$, the inverse $J^{-1} = (R : J)$
[consists of the $x \in K$ with $xJ \subseteq R$](lean:FractionalIdeal.mem_inv_iff).

## Invertible fractional ideals

A fractional ideal $I$ is invertible if $IJ = R$ for some fractional ideal $J$. Then
[$J$ is necessarily $I^{-1}$](lean:FractionalIdeal.right_inverse_eq), so
[$I$ is invertible exactly when $II^{-1} = R$](lean:FractionalIdeal.mul_inv_cancel_iff_isUnit).

- [A nonzero principal fractional ideal $Rx$ is invertible](lean:FractionalIdeal.spanSingleton_mul_inv),
  and [its inverse is $Rx^{-1}$](lean:FractionalIdeal.spanSingleton_inv).
- [Every invertible fractional ideal is finitely generated](lean:FractionalIdeal.fg_unit).
  Separately, finite generation is enough for fractionality:
  [every finitely generated submodule of $K$ is a fractional ideal](lean:FractionalIdeal.isFractional_of_fg),
  and if $R$ is a [Noetherian ring](trough:IsNoetherianRing),
  [every fractional ideal is finitely generated](lean:FractionalIdeal.fg_of_isNoetherianRing).
- Not every nonzero fractional ideal is invertible. In $R = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-3}]$,
  let $I$ be the ideal generated by $2$ and $1 + \sqrt{-3}$. Since
  $(1 + \sqrt{-3})^2 = 2(-1 + \sqrt{-3})$, one checks $I^2 = 2I$. If $I$ were
  invertible, multiplying by $I^{-1}$ would give $I = 2R$, which is false because
  $(1 + \sqrt{-3})/2$ does not lie in $R$.

The rings in which the last phenomenon cannot occur are exactly the Dedekind
domains, the Noetherian integrally closed domains in which every nonzero prime
ideal is maximal:
[an integral domain is a Dedekind domain if and only if every nonzero fractional ideal $I$ satisfies $II^{-1} = R$](lean:isDedekindDomain_iff_mul_inv_cancel).
The element $(1 + \sqrt{-3})/2$ above is a root of $x^2 - x + 1$, so
$\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-3}]$ is not integrally closed, consistent with this criterion.
For a Dedekind domain the nonzero fractional ideals therefore form an abelian group
under multiplication; Mathlib records this by making
[the fractional ideals of a Dedekind domain a semifield](lean:FractionalIdeal.semifield).

## The ideal class group

The principal fractional ideals $Rx$ with $x \neq 0$ form a subgroup of the group of
invertible fractional ideals, and the quotient is the
[ideal class group](lean:ClassGroup) of $R$. It is trivial when $R$ is a principal
ideal domain, and for a Dedekind domain whose class group is finite,
[the class group has exactly one element if and only if $R$ is a principal ideal domain](lean:card_classGroup_eq_one_iff).
For the ring of integers of a number field the class group is
[always finite](trough:NumberField.RingOfIntegers.instFintypeClassGroup).

## In Mathlib

Mathlib defines fractional ideals in greater generality. It starts from a
commutative ring $R$, a multiplicatively closed subset $S$ of $R$ and an
$R$-algebra $P$, typically a [localization](trough:Localization) of $R$ at $S$, and
calls an $R$-submodule $I$ of $P$ [fractional](lean:IsFractional) if some $a \in S$
multiplies every element of $I$ into the image of $R$ in $P$. A fractional ideal is
such a submodule bundled with the proof that it is fractional. The classical notion
is the case where $S$ consists of the nonzero elements of a domain and $P$ is a field
of fractions; the results on inverses, Dedekind domains and class groups assume this
case. The choice of localization does not matter:
[two localizations of $R$ at $S$ have isomorphic semirings of fractional ideals](lean:FractionalIdeal.canonicalEquiv).
If $S$ contains $0$, taking $a = 0$ satisfies the condition, so every submodule is
fractional. The
inverse is defined for every fractional ideal, with the convention that
[the inverse of $0$ is $0$](lean:FractionalIdeal.inv_zero'), and the class group is
defined using one fixed field of fractions of $R$.
