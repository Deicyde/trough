# Equations of order greater than $4$

We note some selected laws of order more than $5$, which are used in some later chapters of the blueprint.

- [Equation 5093](eq5093.md)

This law of order $5$ was mentioned in ([Kisielewicz 1997](https://doi.org/10.1007/s000120050057)). See [Equation 5093 has no non-trivial finite models](../../../finite-implications-and-infinite-models/austin-laws/5093-nontrivial.md).

A law that characterizes natural central groupoids; see [Natural central groupoid axiom](https://github.com/teorth/equational_theories/blob/8b87204d5b201e1cf6c69b8dcabb0558109bee00/blueprint/src/chapter/implications.tex#L188).

- [Equation 28770](eq28770.md)

This law of order $5$ was introduced by Kisielewicz (Kisielewicz 1990). See [Kisielewicz's second Austin law](../../../finite-implications-and-infinite-models/austin-laws/kis-thm2.md).

This law of order $6$ was shown in ([McCune et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1020542009983)) to characterize the Sheffer stroke in a boolean algebra; see [Sheffer stroke axiom](../../../implications-between-selected-laws/sheffer.md).

- [Equation 374794](eq374794.md)

This law of order $6$ was introduced by Kisielewicz (Kisielewicz 1990); see [Kisielewicz's first Austin law](../../../finite-implications-and-infinite-models/austin-laws/kis-thm.md).
