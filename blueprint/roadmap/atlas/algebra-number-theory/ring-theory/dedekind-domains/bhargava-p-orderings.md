## Prefix-product valuation

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $\mathfrak p$ a nonzero prime
ideal of $R$ and $(a_k)_{k \ge 0}$ a sequence in $R$. For $n \in \mathbb N$ and $x \in R$, the
stage-$n$ prefix-product valuation of $x$ is the multiplicity of $\mathfrak p$ in the principal
ideal generated by $\prod_{k < n} (x - a_k)$: the largest $m \in \mathbb N$ such that
$\mathfrak p^m$ divides this ideal, or $\infty$ if every power of $\mathfrak p$ divides it.

## Bhargava p-ordering

Let $R$ be a Dedekind domain, $\mathfrak p$ a nonzero prime ideal
of $R$, $S \subseteq R$ and $(a_n)_{n \ge 0}$ a sequence in $R$. Write $v_n(x)$ for the stage-$n$
prefix-product valuation of $x \in R$ with respect to $\mathfrak p$ and $(a_n)$, the multiplicity
of $\mathfrak p$ in the ideal generated by $\prod_{k < n} (x - a_k)$. Then $(a_n)$ is a
$\mathfrak p$-ordering of $S$ when $S$ is infinite, $a_n \in S$ for every $n$, and
$v_n(a_n) \le v_n(x)$ for every $n$ and every $x \in S$.

## Unfolding the definition of a p-ordering

Let $R$ be a Dedekind domain, $\mathfrak p$
a nonzero prime ideal of $R$, $S \subseteq R$ and $(a_n)_{n \ge 0}$ a sequence in $R$, and

## A set with a p-ordering is infinite

Let $R$ be a Dedekind domain, $\mathfrak p$ a
nonzero prime ideal of $R$ and $S \subseteq R$. If some sequence in $R$ is a
$\mathfrak p$-ordering of $S$, then $S$ is infinite.

## Each term of a p-ordering attains the least valuation

Let $R$ be a Dedekind domain,
$\mathfrak p$ a nonzero prime ideal of $R$, $S \subseteq R$ and $(a_n)_{n \ge 0}$ a

## The stage-zero valuation vanishes

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $\mathfrak p$ a
nonzero prime ideal of $R$ and $(a_n)_{n \ge 0}$ a sequence in $R$. For every $x \in R$, the
stage-$0$ prefix-product valuation of $x$ with respect to $\mathfrak p$ and $(a_n)$ is $0$: the
product is empty, so the ideal is all of $R$.

## The stage-zero condition of a p-ordering is automatic

Let $R$ be a Dedekind domain,
$\mathfrak p$ a nonzero prime ideal of $R$, $S \subseteq R$ and $(a_n)_{n \ge 0}$ a sequence in
$R$, and write $v_n$ for the stage-$n$ prefix-product valuation with respect to $\mathfrak p$
and $(a_n)$. Then $(a_n)$ is a $\mathfrak p$-ordering of $S$ if and only if $S$ is infinite,
$a_n \in S$ for every $n$, and $v_n(a_n) \le v_n(x)$ for every $n \ge 1$ and every $x \in S$.

Since every stage-zero valuation is $0$, the term $a_0$ may be any element of $S$.
