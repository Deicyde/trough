## PCF variables

PCF uses the typed de Bruijn variables of the simply typed
$\lambda$-calculus over a single base type: a variable of type $A$ in a context $\Gamma$ is a
position in the list $\Gamma$ at which $A$ occurs.

## PCF terms

Write $\mathsf{nat}$ for the ground type of natural numbers and $A \Rightarrow B$
for the type of functions from $A$ to $B$. The intrinsically typed PCF terms of type $A$ in a
context $\Gamma$ (a list of PCF types) are generated by the following rules: every variable of
type $A$ in $\Gamma$ is a term of type $A$; if $t$ has type $B$ in the context $\Gamma$ extended
by a newest variable of type $A$, then $\lambda t$ has type $A \Rightarrow B$; if $f$ has type
$A \Rightarrow B$ and $a$ has type $A$, then $f\,a$ has type $B$; $\mathsf{zero}$ has type
$\mathsf{nat}$; if $t$ has type $\mathsf{nat}$, so do $\mathsf{succ}\,t$ and $\mathsf{pred}\,t$;
if $t$, $s_1$ and $s_2$ have type $\mathsf{nat}$, so does the zero test
$\mathsf{ifz}\,t\,s_1\,s_2$; and if $t$ has type $A \Rightarrow A$, then the fixed point
$\mathsf{fix}\,t$ has type $A$.

Formalized by `Cslib.Languages.LambdaCalculus.PCF.Tm`, an inductive type declared in [CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/PCF/Syntax.lean, line 48](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/035e1808ca0c8983682b74153a02628d616d77ce/CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/PCF/Syntax.lean#L48). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/ComputerScience/programming-language-semantics/pcf-and-computational-adequacy/index.html#tm).

## PCF renamings

For contexts $\Gamma$ and $\Delta$ (lists of PCF types), a renaming from
$\Gamma$ to $\Delta$ is a map sending each variable of $\Gamma$ of type $A$, for every type $A$,
to a variable of $\Delta$ of the same type $A$; it is the STLC notion of renaming at PCF types.

## PCF numerals

For every context $\Gamma$ and natural number $n$, the numeral of $n$ is the
term $\mathsf{succ}^n(\mathsf{zero})$ of type $\mathsf{nat}$ in $\Gamma$: the numeral of $0$ is
$\mathsf{zero}$, and the numeral of $n + 1$ is $\mathsf{succ}$ applied to the numeral of $n$.

## Renaming of PCF terms

Given a renaming $\rho$ from a context $\Gamma$ to a context
$\Delta$, the renaming along $\rho$ of a PCF term of type $A$ in context $\Gamma$ is the term of
type $A$ in context $\Delta$ obtained by replacing each variable $x$ by $\rho(x)$. Under a
$\lambda$-binder, $\rho$ is replaced by its lift, which sends the bound variable to itself and
the successor of each variable $x$ of $\Gamma$ to the successor of $\rho(x)$. Renaming leaves
zero unchanged and commutes with application, successor, predecessor, the zero test and the
fixed-point combinator.

## Weakening of PCF terms

Let $\Gamma$ be a context and $A$, $B$ types. Weakening sends a
PCF term $t$ of type $A$ in context $\Gamma$ to the term of type $A$ in the context obtained by
prepending a new variable of type $B$ to $\Gamma$, by renaming every free variable of $t$ to its
successor, that is, by shifting each de Bruijn index up by one.

## Lifting a PCF substitution under a binder

Given a substitution $\sigma$ from a context
$\Gamma$ to a context $\Delta$ and a type $A$, the lift of $\sigma$ is the substitution from
$\Gamma$ extended by a newest variable of type $A$ to $\Delta$ extended by a newest variable of
type $A$ that sends the newest variable to the newest variable and every older variable $x$ to
the weakening of $\sigma(x)$ by one variable.

## Substitution in PCF terms

Given a substitution $\sigma$ from a context $\Gamma$ to a
context $\Delta$, applying $\sigma$ to a PCF term of type $A$ in context $\Gamma$ gives the term of
type $A$ in context $\Delta$ obtained by replacing each variable $x$ by $\sigma(x)$. Under a
$\lambda$-binder, $\sigma$ is replaced by its lift, which sends the bound variable to itself and
the successor of each variable $x$ of $\Gamma$ to the weakening of $\sigma(x)$. Substitution
leaves zero unchanged and commutes with application, successor, predecessor, the zero test and
the fixed-point combinator.

All variables are replaced simultaneously, and since variables are de Bruijn indices the
substitution is capture-avoiding.

Formalized by `Cslib.Languages.LambdaCalculus.PCF.Tm.subst`, a definition declared in [CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/PCF/Syntax.lean, line 213](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/035e1808ca0c8983682b74153a02628d616d77ce/CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/PCF/Syntax.lean#L213). ## Big-step call-by-name evaluation of PCF

Write $\underline{n}$ for the numeral
$\mathsf{succ}^n\,\mathsf{zero}$. For closed PCF terms $t$ and $v$ of the same type,
$t \Downarrow v$ ($t$ evaluates to $v$) is the least relation closed under the following rules,
where all terms are closed and $n$ ranges over natural numbers.
Every $\lambda$-abstraction evaluates to itself, and $\mathsf{zero} \Downarrow \mathsf{zero}$.
If $t \Downarrow \underline{n}$, then $\mathsf{succ}\,t \Downarrow \underline{n+1}$.
If $t \Downarrow \mathsf{zero}$, then $\mathsf{pred}\,t \Downarrow \mathsf{zero}$; if
$t \Downarrow \underline{n+1}$, then $\mathsf{pred}\,t \Downarrow \underline{n}$.
For the zero test, if $t \Downarrow \mathsf{zero}$ and $s_1 \Downarrow v$, then
$\mathsf{ifz}\,t\,s_1\,s_2 \Downarrow v$; if $t \Downarrow \underline{n+1}$ and $s_2 \Downarrow v$,
then $\mathsf{ifz}\,t\,s_1\,s_2 \Downarrow v$. If $f \Downarrow \lambda x.\,b$ and
$b[a/x] \Downarrow v$, then $f\,a \Downarrow v$, where $b[a/x]$ substitutes the unevaluated
argument $a$ for $x$ in $b$. If $t\,(\mathsf{fix}\,t) \Downarrow v$, then
$\mathsf{fix}\,t \Downarrow v$.

Formalized by `Cslib.Languages.LambdaCalculus.PCF.Tm.Eval`, an inductive type declared in [CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/PCF/BigStep.lean, line 49](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/035e1808ca0c8983682b74153a02628d616d77ce/CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/PCF/BigStep.lean#L49). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/ComputerScience/programming-language-semantics/pcf-and-computational-adequacy/index.html#eval).

## Determinism of PCF big-step evaluation

For closed PCF terms $t$, $v$, $w$ of the same

## Least elements of PCF domains

For every PCF type $A$, the domain denoted by $A$ has a

## Semantic environments for PCF

## Variable lookup in a PCF environment

For a variable $x$ of type $A$ in a context $\Gamma$,

defined by recursion on $x$: if $x$ is the newest variable, it sends an environment $(\eta, a)$ to

## Denotation of PCF terms

The denotation of a PCF term $t$ of type $A$ in a context $\Gamma$
is the Scott-continuous map $[\![t]\!]$ from environments for $\Gamma$ to the domain of $A$,
defined by recursion on $t$: a variable denotes its lookup map; $[\![\lambda t]\!]$ is the
currying of $[\![t]\!]$; $[\![f\,a]\!]\eta = [\![f]\!]\eta\,([\![a]\!]\eta)$;
$[\![\mathsf{zero}]\!]$ is constantly the defined value $0$; $[\![\mathsf{succ}\,t]\!]\eta$ and
$[\![\mathsf{pred}\,t]\!]\eta$ apply $n \mapsto n + 1$ and the predecessor map (with $0 \mapsto 0$)
to the partial natural number $[\![t]\!]\eta$, and are undefined where it is undefined;
$[\![\mathsf{ifz}\,t\,s_1\,s_2]\!]$ is the strict zero test of $[\![t]\!]$, $[\![s_1]\!]$,
$[\![s_2]\!]$; and $[\![\mathsf{fix}\,t]\!]\eta$ is the least fixed point of the continuous map
$[\![t]\!]\eta$.

## Call-by-name reduction step of PCF

Write $\underline{n}$ for the numeral
$\mathrm{succ}^n\,\mathrm{zero}$, and $b[a]$ for the result of substituting the closed term $a$
for the bound variable of an abstraction body $b$. One-step call-by-name reduction $t \to t'$
between closed PCF terms of the same type is the least relation closed under these rules:
$f\,a \to f'\,a$ whenever $f \to f'$; $(\lambda b)\,a \to b[a]$, without evaluating $a$;
$\mathrm{succ}\,t \to \mathrm{succ}\,t'$, $\mathrm{pred}\,t \to \mathrm{pred}\,t'$ and
$\mathrm{ifz}\,t\,s_1\,s_2 \to \mathrm{ifz}\,t'\,s_1\,s_2$ whenever $t \to t'$;
$\mathrm{pred}\,\mathrm{zero} \to \mathrm{zero}$ and
$\mathrm{pred}\,\underline{n+1} \to \underline{n}$ for every natural number $n$;
$\mathrm{ifz}\,\mathrm{zero}\,s_1\,s_2 \to s_1$ and
$\mathrm{ifz}\,\underline{n+1}\,s_1\,s_2 \to s_2$ for every natural number $n$; and
$\mathrm{fix}\,t \to t\,(\mathrm{fix}\,t)$.

Formalized by `Cslib.Languages.LambdaCalculus.PCF.Tm.Step`, an inductive type declared in [CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/PCF/SmallStep.lean, line 41](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/035e1808ca0c8983682b74153a02628d616d77ce/CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/PCF/SmallStep.lean#L41). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/ComputerScience/programming-language-semantics/pcf-and-computational-adequacy/index.html#step).

## Multi-step call-by-name reduction of PCF

For closed PCF terms $t$ and $t'$ of the same
type, $t \to^* t'$ holds when $t$ reduces to $t'$ in finitely many, possibly zero, one-step

reduction.

## Formal approximation relation

For each PCF type $A$, the relation "$d$ formally
approximates $t$" between an element $d$ of the domain of $A$ and a closed term $t$ of type $A$
is defined by recursion on $A$. At the ground type, whose domain is the flat domain of partial
natural numbers, $d$ formally approximates $t$ when, for every natural number $n$ such that $d$
is defined with value $n$, the term $t$ evaluates to the numeral $n$. At a function type
$A \to B$, $f$ formally approximates $t$ when, for every element $d$ of the domain of $A$ and
every closed term $s$ of type $A$ such that $d$ formally approximates $s$, the element $f(d)$
formally approximates the application $t\,s$ at type $B$.

## Formal approximation is closed under head expansion

Let $A$ be a PCF type, let $t$ and
$t'$ be closed terms of type $A$, and let $d$ be an element of the domain of $A$. If $t$
evaluates to every closed term $v$ to which $t'$ evaluates, and $d$ formally approximates $t'$,
then $d$ formally approximates $t$.

## Fundamental lemma of formal approximation

Let $t$ be a PCF term of type $A$ in a context
$\Gamma$, let $\eta$ be a semantic environment for $\Gamma$, and let $\sigma$ assign to each
variable of $\Gamma$ a closed term of the same type. Suppose that for every variable $x$ of
$\Gamma$, of type $B$ say, the value of $x$ in $\eta$ formally approximates the closed term
$\sigma(x)$ at type $B$. Then the denotation of $t$ in $\eta$ formally approximates, at type $A$,
the closed term $\sigma(t)$ obtained by simultaneously substituting $\sigma(x)$ for each
variable $x$ in $t$.

Formalized by `Cslib.Languages.LambdaCalculus.PCF.Tm.approx_denote`, a theorem declared in [CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/PCF/Adequacy.lean, line 83](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/035e1808ca0c8983682b74153a02628d616d77ce/CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/PCF/Adequacy.lean#L83). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/ComputerScience/programming-language-semantics/pcf-and-computational-adequacy/index.html#approx-denote).

## Computational adequacy for PCF

Let $t$ be a closed PCF term of ground type
$\mathsf{nat}$ and let $n$ be a natural number. If the denotation of $t$ in the empty environment
is defined with value $n$, then $t$ evaluates to the numeral $\mathsf{succ}^n(\mathsf{zero})$.

Formalized by `Cslib.Languages.LambdaCalculus.PCF.Tm.adequacy`, a theorem declared in [CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/PCF/Adequacy.lean, line 134](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/035e1808ca0c8983682b74153a02628d616d77ce/CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/PCF/Adequacy.lean#L134). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/ComputerScience/programming-language-semantics/pcf-and-computational-adequacy/index.html#adequacy).

## Evaluation to a numeral agrees with the denotation

For every natural number $n$, a
closed PCF term $t$ of ground type evaluates to the numeral $n$ if and only if the denotation
of $t$ is the defined value $n$.

## Termination agrees with definedness of the denotation

A closed PCF term $t$ of ground
type evaluates to some term if and only if its denotation, an element of the flat domain of
partial natural numbers, is defined.

## One-hole contexts of PCF

Fix a typing context $\Gamma$ and a type $A$. A one-hole context
to $(\Delta, B)$ has a hole that takes a term of type $A$ in typing context $\Gamma$ and turns it
into a term of type $B$ in typing context $\Delta$. These contexts are generated by the following
rules, for typing contexts $\Delta$, $\Delta'$ and types $B$, $C$, with $\mathbb{N}$ the ground
type of natural numbers. The hole itself is a context to $(\Gamma, A)$. If $\Delta \cdot B$
extends $\Delta$ by a new innermost variable of type $B$, a context to $(\Delta \cdot B, C)$
gives by $\lambda$-abstraction a context to $(\Delta, B \to C)$. A context to
$(\Delta, B \to C)$ applied to a term of type $B$ in $\Delta$, and a term of type $B \to C$ in
$\Delta$ applied to a context to $(\Delta, B)$, are contexts to $(\Delta, C)$. Successor and
predecessor of a context to $(\Delta, \mathbb{N})$ are contexts to $(\Delta, \mathbb{N})$, and so
is the zero test with a context to $(\Delta, \mathbb{N})$ in any one of its three argument
positions and terms of type $\mathbb{N}$ in $\Delta$ in the other two. The fixed-point operator
applied to a context to $(\Delta, B \to B)$ is a context to $(\Delta, B)$. Finally, a
type-preserving renaming of variables from $\Delta$ to $\Delta'$ applied to a context to
$(\Delta, B)$ is a context to $(\Delta', B)$, which renames the term the inner context builds.

Formalized by `Cslib.Languages.LambdaCalculus.PCF.Ctx`, an inductive type declared in [CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/PCF/ContextualEquivalence.lean, line 51](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/035e1808ca0c8983682b74153a02628d616d77ce/CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/PCF/ContextualEquivalence.lean#L51). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/ComputerScience/programming-language-semantics/pcf-and-computational-adequacy/index.html#ctx).

## Plugging a term into a PCF context

Let $C$ be a one-hole context that turns terms of type
$A$ in context $\Gamma$ into terms of type $B$ in context $\Delta$, and $t$ a term of type $A$ in
context $\Gamma$. The term $C[t]$ of type $B$ in context $\Delta$ is defined by recursion on $C$:
if $C$ is the hole, $C[t] = t$; if $C$ applies a term former (abstraction, application on either
side, successor, predecessor, the zero test at any of its three arguments, or the fixed point) to
a subcontext $C'$ and fixed terms, $C[t]$ applies the same former to $C'[t]$ and the same terms;
and if $C$ is a subcontext $C'$ carried along a renaming $\rho$, then $C[t]$ is $C'[t]$ renamed
by $\rho$.

Formalized by `Cslib.Languages.LambdaCalculus.PCF.Ctx.plug`, a definition declared in [CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/PCF/ContextualEquivalence.lean, line 69](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/035e1808ca0c8983682b74153a02628d616d77ce/CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/PCF/ContextualEquivalence.lean#L69). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/ComputerScience/programming-language-semantics/pcf-and-computational-adequacy/index.html#plug).

## Composition of PCF contexts

Let $C$ be a one-hole context whose hole takes terms of type
$B$ in typing context $\Delta$ and which produces terms of type $B'$ in typing context $\Theta$,
and let $D$ be a one-hole context whose hole takes terms of type $A$ in typing context $\Gamma$
and which produces terms of type $B$ in typing context $\Delta$. The composite $C \circ D$ is the
context obtained from $C$ by replacing its hole by $D$; its hole takes terms of type $A$ in
typing context $\Gamma$, and it produces terms of type $B'$ in typing context $\Theta$.

## Plugging into a composite PCF context

Let $D$ be a one-hole context that turns terms of
type $B$ in context $\Delta$ into terms of type $C$ in context $\Theta$, and let $E$ be one that
turns terms of type $A$ in context $\Gamma$ into terms of type $B$ in context $\Delta$. Write
$K[t]$ for the term obtained by plugging a term $t$ into the hole of a context $K$, and
$D \circ E$ for the context obtained by replacing the hole of $D$ by $E$. Then
$(D \circ E)[t] = D[E[t]]$ for every term $t$ of type $A$ in context $\Gamma$.

## Associativity of PCF context composition

For one-hole contexts $C$ and $D$ such that the
hole of $C$ takes terms of the type and typing context that $D$ produces, write $C \circ D$ for
the context obtained from $C$ by replacing its hole by $D$. For PCF one-hole contexts $C_1$, $C_2$
and $C_3$ such that the hole of $C_1$ takes terms of the type and typing context that $C_2$
produces, and the hole of $C_2$ takes terms of the type and typing context that $C_3$ produces,
composition is associative: $(C_1 \circ C_2) \circ C_3 = C_1 \circ (C_2 \circ C_3)$.

## Contextual equivalence in PCF

Write $C[s]$ for the term obtained by plugging $s$ into
the hole of a one-hole context $C$. PCF terms $t$ and $u$ of type $A$ in typing context $\Gamma$
are contextually equivalent when, for every one-hole context $C$ whose hole takes terms of type
$A$ in typing context $\Gamma$ and which produces closed terms of the ground type of natural
numbers, and for every natural number $n$, $C[t]$ evaluates to the numeral $n$ if and only if
$C[u]$ evaluates to the numeral $n$. Evaluation is big-step call-by-name evaluation.

## Equal denotations are preserved by plugging into PCF contexts

Let $t$ and $u$ be PCF
terms of type $A$ in typing context $\Gamma$ with the same denotation, and let $C$ be a one-hole
context whose hole takes terms of type $A$ in typing context $\Gamma$. Then the terms $C[t]$ and
$C[u]$ obtained by plugging $t$ and $u$ into the hole of $C$ have the same denotation.

## Equal denotations imply contextual equivalence in PCF

If two PCF terms $t$ and $u$ of
the same type in the same typing context have the same denotation, as continuous maps from
environments to the domain of their type, then $t$ and $u$ are contextually equivalent.

## Contextual approximation in PCF

Write $C[s]$ for the term obtained by plugging $s$ into
the hole of a one-hole context $C$. For PCF terms $t$ and $u$ of type $A$ in typing context
$\Gamma$, $t$ contextually approximates $u$ when, for every one-hole context $C$ whose hole takes
terms of type $A$ in typing context $\Gamma$ and which produces closed terms of the ground type
of natural numbers, and for every natural number $n$: if $C[t]$ evaluates to the numeral $n$,

## Plugging into a PCF context is monotone in the denotation

Let $C$ be a one-hole context
that turns terms of type $A$ in context $\Gamma$ into terms of type $B$ in context $\Delta$, and
let $t$ and $u$ be terms of type $A$ in context $\Gamma$. Write $C[s]$ for the term obtained by
plugging a term $s$ into the hole of $C$, and $[\![ s ]\!]\,\eta$ for the denotation of a term $s$
in an environment $\eta$, ordered in the domain of its type. If
$[\![ t ]\!]\,\eta \le [\![ u ]\!]\,\eta$ for every environment $\eta$ for $\Gamma$, then
$[\![ C[t] ]\!]\,\eta \le [\![ C[u] ]\!]\,\eta$ for every environment $\eta$ for $\Delta$.

## Denotational approximation implies contextual approximation in PCF
