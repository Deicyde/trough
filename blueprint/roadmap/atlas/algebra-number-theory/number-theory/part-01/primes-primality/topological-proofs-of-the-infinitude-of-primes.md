## Macías's coprime basis

For a positive integer $n$ let $\Omega_n$ be the set of positive
integers coprime to $n$. Macías's coprime basis is the family $\beta = \{\Omega_n : n \ge 1\}$ of

## Macías's coprime topology

For a positive integer $n$ let $\Omega_n$ be the set of positive
integers coprime to $n$. Macías's coprime topology $\tau$ is the topology on the positive integers
generated by the sets $\Omega_n$, $n \ge 1$, that is, the coarsest topology in which every
$\Omega_n$ is open.

## Macías's coprime sets form a basis

For a positive integer $n$ let $\Omega_n$ be the set
of positive integers coprime to $n$. The family $\beta = \{\Omega_n : n \ge 1\}$ is a basis of
the topology on the positive integers that it generates (Macías's coprime topology).

## Closure of a prime in Macías's coprime topology

If $p$ is a prime number, then the
closure of $\{p\}$ in the positive integers with Macías's coprime topology is the set of
positive integers divisible by $p$.

## Infinitude of the primes as density

There are infinitely many prime numbers if and only
if the set of prime numbers is dense in the positive integers with Macías's coprime topology.

## Density of the primes passes from the subspace without 1

Let $X$ be the positive
integers with Macías's coprime topology and $X_1$ the subspace of positive integers different
from $1$. If the set of prime numbers is dense in $X_1$, then it is dense in $X$.

## A nonempty set of primes is dense in the comaximal space iff it is infinite

Equip the
positive integers with the comaximal topology, the topology generated by the sets
$\{m : \gcd(n, m) = 1\}$ for positive integers $n$. A nonempty set $A$ of primes is infinite if
and only if it is dense in this topology.

## Coprime arithmetic progressions are dense in the comaximal space

Equip the positive
integers with the comaximal topology, the topology generated by the sets
$\{m : \gcd(n, m) = 1\}$ for positive integers $n$. If $a$ and $b$ are coprime positive integers,
then the arithmetic progression $\{ak + b : k \in \mathbb{N}\} = \{b, a + b, 2a + b, \dots\}$,
with $k$ ranging over the natural numbers including $0$, is dense in this topology.
