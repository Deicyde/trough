## Vertices of the spherically homogeneous rooted tree (Kourovka 15.15)

For a branching
sequence $m \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$, the vertices of the spherically homogeneous rooted
tree are the finite words $(x_0, \dots, x_{k-1})$ of natural numbers with $x_i < m(i)$ for every
$i < k$. The length $k$ of a word is its level and its prefixes are its ancestors.

## Rigid vertex stabilizer (Kourovka 15.15)

Let $\rho$ be a homomorphism from a group $G$ to
the permutations of the vertices of the tree with branching sequence $m$, and let $v$ be a vertex.
The rigid vertex stabilizer of $v$ is the set of $g \in G$ such that $\rho(g)$ fixes every vertex
$w$ of which $v$ is not a prefix, that is, every vertex outside the subtree rooted at $v$.

## Rigid level stabilizer (Kourovka 15.15)

Let $\rho$ be a homomorphism from a group $G$ to
the permutations of the vertices of the tree with branching sequence $m$, and let $n$ be a natural
number. The $n$-th rigid level stabilizer is the subgroup of $G$ generated by the rigid vertex
stabilizers of all vertices at level $n$.

## Branch group (Kourovka 15.15)

A group $G$ is a branch group if there are a sequence
$m \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ with $m(i) \ge 2$ for all $i$ and an injective homomorphism
$\rho$ from $G$ to the permutations of the vertices of the spherically homogeneous rooted tree with
branching sequence $m$ such that each $\rho(g)$ is a tree automorphism (for all vertices $v$ and
$w$, $v$ is a prefix of $w$ if and only if $\rho(g)(v)$ is a prefix of $\rho(g)(w)$), the action is
level-transitive (for any two vertices $v$ and $w$ at the same level some $g \in G$ has
$\rho(g)(v) = w$), and for every $n$ the $n$-th rigid level stabilizer has finite index in $G$.

## A finitely generated branch group with a maximal subgroup of infinite index

The
Kourovka 15.15 statement that every maximal subgroup of a finitely generated branch group has
finite index is false: some finitely generated branch group has a maximal subgroup of infinite
index.

## Rooted tree given by levels

A locally finite rooted tree is given by finite sets $V_n$ for
$n \ge 0$, the vertices at level $n$, where $V_0$ consists of the root alone, together with parent
maps $V_{n+1} \to V_n$, such that every vertex has at least two children: for every $v \in V_n$
there are distinct $x, y \in V_{n+1}$ whose parent is $v$.

## Descendants in a rooted tree

For vertices $v$ at level $n$ and $w$ at level $m$ of a
rooted tree $T$, $w$ is a descendant of $v$ when $w$ is reached from $v$ by moving from a vertex
to one of its children zero or more times: $v$ is a descendant of itself, and a vertex at level
$k + 1$ is a descendant of $v$ whenever its parent is.

## Faithful level-transitive action on a rooted tree

Let $T$ be a rooted tree with vertex
sets $V_n$ at the levels $n \ge 0$ and parent maps $V_{n+1} \to V_n$, and let $G$ be a group. A
faithful level-transitive action of $G$ on $T$ consists of an action of $G$ on each level $V_n$ by
permutations, that is, a homomorphism $G \to \mathrm{Sym}(V_n)$ for every $n$, such that the
actions commute with the parent maps (so $G$ acts by tree automorphisms), only the identity of $G$
fixes every vertex of $T$, and $G$ acts transitively on every level $V_n$.

## Rigid vertex stabilizer

Given a faithful level-transitive action of a group $G$ on a
rooted tree $T$ and a vertex $v$ of $T$, an element $g \in G$ lies in the rigid stabilizer of $v$
when $g$ fixes every vertex of $T$ that is not a descendant of $v$.

## Rigid stabilizer of a level

Given a faithful level-transitive action of a group $G$ on a
rooted tree $T$ and a level $n$, the rigid stabilizer of level $n$ is the subgroup of $G$ generated
by the rigid stabilizers of all vertices of $T$ at level $n$.

## Branch action

A faithful level-transitive action of a group $G$ on a rooted tree $T$ is a
branch action when, for every level $n$, the rigid stabilizer of level $n$ has finite index in
$G$.

## Branch group

A group $G$ is a branch group when there exist a rooted tree $T$ (locally
finite, with every vertex having at least two children) and a faithful level-transitive action of
$G$ on $T$ by tree automorphisms that is a branch action.

## A finitely generated branch group with a non-normal maximal subgroup of finite index

The
Kourovka 20.101 statement that every maximal subgroup of finite index of a finitely generated
branch group is normal is false: some finitely generated branch group has a maximal subgroup of
finite index that is not normal.

Garciarena and Petschick (2024, Theorem 4.1) construct a finitely generated branch group all of
whose maximal subgroups have finite index, one of which is not normal.
