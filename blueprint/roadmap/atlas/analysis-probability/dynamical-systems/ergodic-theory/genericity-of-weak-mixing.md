## Measure-preserving automorphism

An automorphism of a measure space $(X, m)$ is a
bijection $e \colon X \to X$ such that $e$ and its inverse are measurable and $e$ preserves $m$,
that is, $m(e^{-1}(A)) = m(A)$ for every measurable set $A \subseteq X$.

## Weakly mixing automorphism

Let $(X, m)$ be a measure space. An automorphism $T$ of
$(X, m)$ is weakly mixing if for all measurable sets $A, B \subseteq X$,

$$\frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \left| m(T^{-k}(A) \cap B) - m(A) m(B) \right| \to 0$$

as $n \to \infty$, where $T^{-k}(A)$ is the preimage of $A$ under the $k$-th iterate of $T$.

Measures are converted to real numbers here, with an infinite measure counted as $0$.

## Halmos's theorem: weak mixing is generic

Let $X$ be a standard Borel space and $m$ a
probability measure on $X$ for which every singleton has measure $0$. Give the set of
automorphisms of $(X, m)$ the weak topology, the topology generated by the sets of
automorphisms $T$ with $m(T(A) \mathbin{\triangle} T_0(A)) < \varepsilon$, for each automorphism
$T_0$, measurable set $A \subseteq X$ and real number $\varepsilon > 0$. Then there is a dense
$G_\delta$ set of automorphisms all of which are weakly mixing, and every weakly mixing
automorphism of $(X, m)$ is ergodic for $m$.
