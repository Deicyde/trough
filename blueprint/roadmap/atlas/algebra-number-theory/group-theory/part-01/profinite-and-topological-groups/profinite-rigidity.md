## Profinite completion

The profinite completion of a group $G$ is the inverse limit of the
finite quotient groups $G/N$, where $N$ ranges over the normal subgroups of finite index in $G$
and the maps are the quotient maps $G/N \to G/M$ for $N \le M$. It is a profinite group.

## Profinitely rigid group

A group $\Gamma$ is *profinitely rigid* if it is finitely
generated and residually finite, and $\Gamma$ is isomorphic to every finitely generated residually
finite group $\Lambda$ whose profinite completion is isomorphic, as a group, to the profinite
completion of $\Gamma$.

Note that any such isomorphism of profinite completions is automatically a homeomorphism. This is
a consequence of the Nikolov-Segal theorem which states that for a finitely generated group
$\Gamma$, every finite index subgroup of the profinite completion of $\Gamma$ is automatically
open. Conversely, every open subgroup has finite index by compactness, so in this case the open
subgroups (which generate the topology) are exactly the subgroups of finite index. Thus, the
topology on the profinite completion of $\Gamma$ can be characterized algebraically as the
topology generated by the finite index subgroups. In particular, any isomorphism between
profinite completions of finitely generated groups automatically preserves the topology and is
thus a homeomorphism.

## Ring of integers of a field obtained by adjoining a square root to $\mathbb{Q}$

For a
natural number $d$, the ring of integers of the number field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$, realized as
the splitting field of $X^2 + d$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

## Bianchi group

For a natural number $d$, let $\mathcal{O}_d$ be the ring of integers of
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$. The Bianchi group is $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathcal{O}_d)$, the projective
special linear group of $2 \times 2$ matrices over $\mathcal{O}_d$.

## Profinite rigidity of the Bianchi group over the Eisenstein integers

Let $\omega$ be a
primitive cube root of unity, so that the ring $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ of Eisenstein integers is the
ring of integers of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$. The Bianchi group $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{Z}[\omega])$
is profinitely rigid: it is finitely generated and residually finite, and it is isomorphic to
every finitely generated residually finite group whose profinite completion is isomorphic, as a
group, to its own.

This is Theorem 7.1 of the source.
