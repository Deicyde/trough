## Pyber's question on generators of abelian subgroups (Kourovka 20.75)

The proposition that
for every prime $p$, every natural number $k$ and every finite $p$-group $G$, if every abelian
normal subgroup of $G$ can be generated by at most $k$ elements, then every abelian subgroup of $G$
can be generated by at most $2k$ elements.

## Kourovka Problem 20.75 has a negative answer

The statement of Kourovka Problem 20.75 is
false: there are a prime $p$, a natural number $k$, a finite $p$-group $G$ all of whose abelian
normal subgroups can be generated by at most $k$ elements, and an abelian subgroup of $G$ that
cannot be generated by at most $2k$ elements.

Muliarchyk's counterexample (Theorem 1.1) is a finite $2$-group of order $2^{110N+1}$, with
$N = 2124210350$, whose abelian normal subgroups can be generated by $32N + 1$ elements while an
elementary abelian subgroup needs $65N > 2(32N + 1)$ generators; the 21st issue of the Kourovka
Notebook marks 20.75 solved ("No, it is not").

## Pyber's question on orders of abelian subgroups (Kourovka 20.76)

The proposition that for
every prime $p$, every natural number $k$ and every finite $p$-group $G$, if every abelian normal
subgroup of $G$ has order at most $p^k$, then every abelian subgroup of $G$ has order at most
$p^{2k}$.

## Kourovka Problem 20.76 has a negative answer

The statement of Kourovka Problem 20.76 is
false: there are a prime $p$, a natural number $k$, a finite $p$-group $G$ all of whose abelian
normal subgroups have order at most $p^k$, and an abelian subgroup of $G$ of order greater than
$p^{2k}$.

Muliarchyk's counterexample (Theorem 1.1) is a finite $2$-group of order $2^{144e+1}$, with
$e = 256363762$, whose abelian normal subgroups have order at most $2^{42e}$ while it has an
elementary abelian subgroup of order $2^{85e} > 2^{84e}$; the 21st issue of the Kourovka Notebook
marks 20.76 solved ("No, it is not").
