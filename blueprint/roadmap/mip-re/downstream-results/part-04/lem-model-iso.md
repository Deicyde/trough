# What an isomorphism carries over

Let $W, \Phi$ be an isomorphism from $M$ to $M'$. The Born probability of $(\Phi_A(a), \Phi_B(b))$ in $M'$ is that of $(a, b)$ in $M$, and so are both players' squared state norms and the cross-party deviations; the two states have the same norm. Each player's isomorphism carries a POVM in that player's algebra to a POVM, a projective one to a projective one, and the inverse carries it back. Two pairs of families of measurements whose operators correspond under $\Phi$ have the same value in every game and the same inconsistency on every index, so a projective strategy carried across has its value, and a value model that dominates $M'$ dominates $M$. With the players exchanged, a projective strategy for $\mathfrak{G}$ in a model is one, in the exchanged model, for the game with the players exchanged, and it has the same value; the inconsistency of two families is that of the same families with the players exchanged.

## Proof

An isomorphism is a local isometry carrying the state to the state, so the first statements are Lemma [Local isometries carry the Born rule](../part-03/lem-local-isometry.md); a $*$-isomorphism preserves positivity, sums, products and adjoints, hence POVMs and projective measurements. The value and the inconsistency are sums of Born probabilities. Exchanging the players exchanges the two factors of each Born probability, which commute.
