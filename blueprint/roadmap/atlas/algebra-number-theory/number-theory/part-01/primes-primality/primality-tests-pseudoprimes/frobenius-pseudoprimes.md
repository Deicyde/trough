## Greatest common monic divisor

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and let
$h, g_1, g_2 \in R[x]$. Then $h$ is a greatest common monic divisor of $g_1$ and $g_2$ when
$h$, $g_1$ and $g_2$ are monic and the ideal generated by $g_1$ and $g_2$ equals the principal
ideal generated by $h$.

Such an $h$ need not exist.

## Unfolding the greatest common monic divisor

For polynomials $h$, $g_1$ and $g_2$
over a commutative ring $R$, $h$ is a greatest common monic divisor of $g_1$ and $g_2$ if and
only if $h$, $g_1$ and $g_2$ are monic and the ideal of $R[X]$ generated by $g_1$ and $g_2$
equals the principal ideal generated by $h$.

## Frobenius probable prime

Let $f \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ be a polynomial of degree $d$ with
discriminant $\Delta$, and let $n$ be a natural number. Then $n$ is a Frobenius probable prime
with respect to $f$ when $f$ is monic, $n$ is odd, $n > 1$, $n$ is coprime to $|f(0)\Delta|$,
and there exist polynomials $F_i, f_i \in (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})[x]$ and natural numbers
$q_i$, indexed by $i \in \mathbb{N}$, such that:
- $f_0$ is the reduction of $f$ modulo $n$, and $f_d = 1$;
- for each $i$ with $1 \le i \le d$, $F_i$ is a greatest common monic divisor of $x^{n^i} - x$
  and $f_{i-1}$, and $f_{i-1} = F_i f_i$;
- (Frobenius step) for each $i$ with $2 \le i \le d$, $F_i(x)$ divides $F_i(x^n)$;
- for each even $i$ with $1 \le i \le d$, $\deg F_i = i q_i$;
- (Jacobi step) $(-1)^S = \left(\frac{\Delta}{n}\right)$, the Jacobi symbol, where $S$ is the
  sum of $q_i$ over the even $i$ with $1 \le i \le d$.

## Frobenius pseudoprime

A natural number $n$ is a Frobenius pseudoprime with respect to a
polynomial $f \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ when $n$ is not prime and $n$ is a Frobenius probable prime with
respect to $f$.

Since a Frobenius probable prime exceeds $1$, such an $n$ is composite.

## Characterization of Frobenius pseudoprimes

For every natural number $n$ and every
polynomial $f \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$, $n$ is a Frobenius pseudoprime with respect to $f$ if and only
if $n$ is not prime and $n$ is a Frobenius probable prime with respect to $f$.
