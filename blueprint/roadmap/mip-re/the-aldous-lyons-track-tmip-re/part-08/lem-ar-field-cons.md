# Field equations as constraints, and field arithmetic, as programs

A field equation $E(z) = c$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$, linear in the answer bits $z \in \mathbb{F}_2^N$, gives $t$ constraints: for each $s < t$, the $s$-th coordinates of $E$ at the $N$ unit vectors, then that of $c$. A program computing the Shoup bits of $E(z)$ at every $z$ of length $N$, and one computing those of $c$, therefore give a program computing the constraints, which runs the first on the unit vectors and transposes; likewise for an indexed list of equations. A field element of the answer bits is a window of $t$ bits. Sums, products, $0$, $1$, $g (1 - g)$, indexed sums and comparisons of programs computing field elements in their Shoup bits compute the corresponding sums, products, constants and comparisons.

## Proof

The constraints of a field equation are its coordinates at the unit vectors, the equation being linear (Lemma [The Pauli basis test as linear constraints](../part-04/lem-intro-pauli-cons.md)); addition in $\mathbb{F}_q$ is the exclusive or of Shoup bits, and multiplication the Shoup multiplication program (Lemma [Effective polynomial-basis field arithmetic](../../background-results/part-05/lem-effective-binary-field-arithmetic.md)).
