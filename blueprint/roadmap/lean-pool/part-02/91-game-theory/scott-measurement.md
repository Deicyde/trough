# Scott's measurement representation theorems

> Formalizes all eight numbered finite representation theorems of Dana Scott's 1964 Measurement Structures and Linear Inequalities. Develops a shared finite separation and rational-cancellation engine, unit-threshold semiorder representations, additive utility on pairs, ordered utility differences, and normalized finitely additive probabilities on finite Boolean algebras. Includes the Kraft-Pratt-Seidenberg five-atom counterexample to de Finetti's weaker axioms and local real embeddings of finite subsets of ordered abelian groups. A separately labelled modern infinite analogue assumes a generalized Kelley condition: strict-comparison vectors have a countable cover by closed convex upper sets avoiding zero above the weak-comparison cone. It is neither a countably additive measure theorem nor an attribution of Scott's unpublished infinite theorem. The infinite construction uses universal probability evaluation vectors, with a proved faithful Stone representation and probability-state correspondence. The upstream proofs were produced primarily by AI agents under Lars Warren Ericson's direction and review.
