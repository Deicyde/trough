# $K[X]$ is Euclidean

For a [field](../field-theory/field.md) $K$, the ring $K[X]$ of [polynomials in one indeterminate](polynomial-in-one-indeterminate.md) over $K$
is a [Euclidean domain](../divisibility-in-integral-domains/euclidean-domain.md): any polynomial can be divided by a nonzero
polynomial, leaving a remainder of smaller degree. This fact, **$K[X]$ is
Euclidean**, makes the arithmetic of polynomials over a field closely parallel to
the arithmetic of the integers. It yields a Euclidean algorithm for greatest common
divisors, shows that every ideal of $K[X]$ is principal and that polynomials factor
uniquely into irreducibles, and underlies the construction of field extensions by
adjoining roots.

## Statement

Let $K$ be a field. [The polynomial ring $K[X]$ is a Euclidean domain](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Polynomial/FieldDivision.html#Polynomial.instEuclideanDomain),
with the degree as its size function. Concretely, for polynomials $f$ and $g$ with
$g \neq 0$ there are polynomials $q$ (the quotient) and $r$ (the remainder) with

$$f = g\,q + r, \qquad \deg r < \deg g,$$

where the zero polynomial is taken to have degree smaller than every natural number.
The pair $(q, r)$ is unique, as shown in the Proof section. In Mathlib's notation the
existence part consists of the identity [$f = g\,(f / g) + f \bmod g$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/EuclideanDomain/Defs.html#EuclideanDomain.div_add_mod), valid in
every Euclidean domain, and the bound
[$\deg (f \bmod g) < \deg g$ for $g \neq 0$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Polynomial/FieldDivision.html#Polynomial.degree_mod_lt).

## Examples

- Over the rationals, dividing $X^3 + 2X + 1$ by $X^2 + 1$ gives
  $X^3 + 2X + 1 = (X^2 + 1)\,X + (X + 1)$, with remainder of degree $1 < 2$.
- Dividing by a linear polynomial $X - a$ leaves a constant remainder, and that
  constant is the value of the polynomial at $a$: [the remainder of $f$ on division by $X - a$ is $f(a)$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Polynomial/FieldDivision.html#Polynomial.mod_X_sub_C_eq_C_eval).
  Hence [$X - a$ divides $f$ if and only if $a$ is a root of $f$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Polynomial/Div.html#Polynomial.dvd_iff_isRoot),
  the content of the [factor and remainder theorems](../../../hundred-theorems/part-04/the-factor-and-remainder-theorems.md).
- The Euclidean algorithm applied to $X^2 - 1$ and $X^2 - 3X + 2$ over the rationals
  runs $X^2 - 1 = (X^2 - 3X + 2) \cdot 1 + (3X - 3)$ and then
  $X^2 - 3X + 2 = (3X - 3)\,\tfrac{1}{3}(X - 2)$, so the last nonzero remainder
  $3X - 3$ is a greatest common divisor; its monic multiple is $X - 1$.
- The hypothesis that $K$ is a field cannot be dropped. In $\mathbb{Z}[X]$,
  dividing $X$ by $2$ would need a remainder of degree less than $\deg 2 = 0$,
  that is, a remainder of zero and $X = 2q$, which is impossible. More decisively,
  the ideal generated by $2$ and $X$ is not principal, so $\mathbb{Z}[X]$ is not
  Euclidean for any choice of size function.

## Consequences

- **Principal ideals.** [Every Euclidean domain is a principal ideal domain](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/PrincipalIdealDomain.html#EuclideanDomain.instIsPrincipalIdealRing),
  so every ideal of $K[X]$ consists of the multiples of a single polynomial; see
  [principal ideal domain](../divisibility-in-integral-domains/principal-ideal-domain.md).
- **Unique factorization.** Being a principal ideal domain, $K[X]$ is a
  [unique factorization domain](../divisibility-in-integral-domains/unique-factorisation-domain.md): every nonzero
  polynomial is a nonzero constant times a product of irreducible polynomials,
  uniquely up to order and constant factors.
- **Greatest common divisors and Bézout's identity.** The
  [Euclidean algorithm](../../../hundred-theorems/part-03/greatest-common-divisor-algorithm.md) produces a polynomial $d$ that
  [divides both $f$ and $g$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/EuclideanDomain/Basic.html#EuclideanDomain.gcd_dvd) and is
  [divisible by every common divisor of $f$ and $g$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/EuclideanDomain/Basic.html#EuclideanDomain.dvd_gcd);
  running it backwards gives
  [polynomials $a$ and $b$ with $d = f a + g b$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/EuclideanDomain/Basic.html#EuclideanDomain.gcd_eq_gcd_ab).
- **Units.** [A polynomial is a unit exactly when it has degree $0$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Polynomial/FieldDivision.html#Polynomial.isUnit_iff_degree_eq_zero),
  that is, when it is a nonzero constant.
- **Maximal ideals and field extensions.** For a nonzero polynomial $f$,
  [$f$ is irreducible if and only if the ideal it generates is maximal](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/PrincipalIdealDomain.html#Ideal.irreducible_iff_isMaximal_span_singleton)
  (see [irreducible element](../divisibility-in-integral-domains/irreducible-element.md) and [maximal ideal](../ideals-and-quotients/maximal-ideal.md)).
  Hence [the quotient $K[X]/(f)$ by an irreducible $f$ is a field](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot.instField),
  the [rupture field](../field-theory/rupture-field.md) of $f$, in which the class of $X$ is a root of $f$.

## Proof

Fix $g \neq 0$ of degree $m$ with leading coefficient $c$. If $\deg f < m$, take
$q = 0$ and $r = f$. Otherwise let $f$ have degree $n \ge m$ and leading coefficient
$a$. Since $K$ is a field, $c$ is invertible, and $f - a c^{-1} X^{n-m} g$ has degree
less than $n$. By induction on the degree it can be written as $g q' + r$ with
$\deg r < m$, so $f = g\,(q' + a c^{-1} X^{n-m}) + r$. For uniqueness, if
$g q + r = g q' + r'$ then $g\,(q - q') = r' - r$; the right side has degree less
than $m$. If $h = q - q'$ were nonzero, then $c$ times the leading coefficient of $h$
would be nonzero, because $c$ is invertible, and
[when the product of the leading coefficients is nonzero, the degree of a product is the sum of the degrees](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Polynomial/Degree/Operations.html#Polynomial.degree_mul'),
so $g h$ would have degree at least $m$. Hence $q = q'$ and $r = r'$. Finally, $K[X]$
is an integral domain, as a Euclidean domain must be: over a field
[the degree of a product is the sum of the degrees](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Polynomial/Degree/Operations.html#Polynomial.degree_mul), so a
product of nonzero polynomials is nonzero.

In the division argument the only use of the field hypothesis is to invert the
leading coefficient of $g$.
Over any commutative ring the same argument divides by a polynomial whose leading
coefficient is $1$, and for such a monic divisor
[the quotient and remainder are again unique](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Polynomial/Div.html#Polynomial.div_modByMonic_unique).

## Related concepts

The integers are the other basic Euclidean domain, with the absolute value as size
function ([$\mathbb{Z}$ is a Euclidean ring](../../../undergraduate/ring-theory/divisibility-in-integral-domains/z-is-a-euclidean-ring.md)). For an
integral domain $R$ the property singles out fields:
[if $R[X]$ is a principal ideal domain, then $R$ is a field](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Polynomial/Quotient.html#Ideal.IsField.of_isPrincipalIdealRing_polynomial),
so $R[X]$ is Euclidean only when $R$ is a field. Rings such as $\mathbb{Z}[X]$ and
the [polynomial rings](polynomial-in-several-indeterminates.md) in two or more indeterminates over a field
are not principal ideal domains, but they keep unique factorization
([$A[{X_i}]$ is a UFD when $A$ is a UFD](a-x-i-is-a-ufd-when-a-is-a-ufd.md)).

## In Mathlib

Mathlib defines a Euclidean domain as a nontrivial commutative ring with quotient
and remainder operations satisfying $b\,(a/b) + a \bmod b = a$, together with a
well-founded relation under which the remainder on division by a nonzero element
lies below the divisor and no product $ab$ with $b \neq 0$ lies below $a$. This relation
takes the place of a size function to the natural numbers. For
polynomials over a field this relation compares degrees, and Mathlib's degree takes
the value $-\infty$ on the zero polynomial, which places $0$ below every
other polynomial. By convention division by the zero polynomial gives quotient $0$
and leaves the dividend as remainder. The greatest common divisor computed by the
Euclidean algorithm is one greatest common divisor, not necessarily monic; Mathlib
also equips $K[X]$ with a normalized greatest common divisor, which is monic unless
both polynomials are zero
(compare [$A[X]$ has gcd and lcm if $A$ does](a-x-has-gcd-and-lcm-if-a-does.md)).
