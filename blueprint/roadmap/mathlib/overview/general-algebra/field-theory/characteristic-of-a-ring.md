# Characteristic of a ring

The **characteristic** of a [ring](../rings/ring.md) is the least positive integer $n$ for which
adding the identity element $1$ to itself $n$ times gives $0$, or $0$ if no such
integer exists. It measures how the integers sit inside the ring, and it separates rings of
[characteristic zero](characteristic-zero.md), such as the rational and real numbers, from
rings of positive characteristic, such as the integers modulo $n$ and the finite
fields, where arithmetic behaves differently (for instance, $(x + y)^p = x^p + y^p$
can hold).

## Definition

Let $R$ be a ring with identity, and for a natural number $n$ write $n \cdot 1$ for
the sum $1 + 1 + \dots + 1$ of $n$ copies of $1$ (with $0 \cdot 1 = 0$). The set of
$n$ with $n \cdot 1 = 0$ contains $0$ and is closed under addition and under
subtraction of a smaller element from a larger one, so it consists of the
multiples of a single natural number $c$: its least positive element, or $0$ if
it has none. This number $c$ is the [characteristic of $R$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/CharP/Defs.html#ringChar),
written $\operatorname{char} R$. Equivalently, the characteristic is the unique
natural number $c$ such that
[for every natural number $n$, $n \cdot 1 = 0$ in $R$ if and only if $c$ divides $n$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/CharP/Defs.html#ringChar.spec).
In particular [$(\operatorname{char} R) \cdot 1 = 0$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/CharP/Defs.html#ringChar.Nat.cast_ringChar).

Two other descriptions are often useful. There is exactly one
[ring homomorphism](../rings/ring-morphism.md) from the [integers](../numbers/integer.md) to $R$, and
[its kernel is the ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$ generated by the characteristic](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/CharP/Quotient.html#CharP.ker_intAlgebraMap_eq_span).
And [the characteristic is the additive order of $1$ when that order is finite, and $0$ when $1$ has infinite additive order](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/OrderOfElement.html#CharP.addOrderOf_one);
Mathlib assigns order $0$ to an element of infinite order, so in its terms the
characteristic is simply the additive order of $1$.
A ring with $\operatorname{char} R = p$ is said to have [characteristic $p$](characteristic-p.md).

## Examples

- The [integers](../numbers/integer.md), the [rational numbers](../numbers/rational-number.md), the
  [real numbers](../numbers/real-number.md) and the [complex numbers](../numbers/complex-number.md) have
  characteristic $0$.
- For every positive integer $n$,
  [the ring $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ of integers modulo $n$ has characteristic $n$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Data/ZMod/Basic.html#ZMod.ringChar_zmod_n).
- [A ring has characteristic $1$ exactly when it is the zero ring](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/CharP/Defs.html#ringChar.ringChar_eq_one),
  the ring in which $1 = 0$.
- [A polynomial ring $R[X]$ has the same characteristic as $R$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Polynomial/Coeff.html#Polynomial.charP)
  (see [polynomial](../polynomials-and-power-series/polynomial-in-one-indeterminate.md)). So for every integer $p \geq 2$,
  $(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})[X]$ is an infinite ring of characteristic $p$: positive characteristic does not force finiteness.
- [The product $R \times S$ of a ring of characteristic $p$ and a ring of characteristic $q$ has characteristic $\operatorname{lcm}(p, q)$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/CharP/Basic.html#Nat.lcm.charP),
  where the least common multiple of $0$ and any number is $0$. Thus
  $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ has characteristic $6$,
  while $\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ has characteristic $0$.

## Properties

- **Domains.** If $R$ is a nonzero ring without zero divisors, for example an
  integral domain or a [field](field.md), then
  [its characteristic is either $0$ or a prime number](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/CharP/Defs.html#CharP.char_is_prime_or_zero).
  Indeed, suppose $c \neq 0$. The zero ring is excluded, so $c \neq 1$; and if $c = ab$ with
  $1 < a, b < c$, then $(a \cdot 1)(b \cdot 1) = c \cdot 1 = 0$, so one factor
  vanishes, against the minimality of $c$. The ring $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$, where
  $2 \cdot 3 = 0$, shows the hypothesis is needed.
- **Finite rings.** In a finite ring the map $n \mapsto n \cdot 1$ cannot be
  injective, so
  [a finite ring has nonzero characteristic](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/CharP/Lemmas.html#CharP.ringChar_ne_zero_of_finite), and
  [a finite nonzero ring without zero divisors has prime characteristic](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/CharP/Lemmas.html#CharP.prime_ringChar).
  A [finite field](../../../undergraduate/ring-theory/field-theory/part-01/finite-fields.md) $K$ of characteristic $p$
  is a vector space over $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, so
  [$K$ has $p^n$ elements for some positive integer $n$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/FieldTheory/Finite/Basic.html#FiniteField.card).
- **Homomorphisms.** If $f \colon R \to S$ is a ring homomorphism, then
  $f(n \cdot 1) = n \cdot 1$, so
  [the characteristic of $S$ divides the characteristic of $R$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/CharP/Algebra.html#CharP.dvd_of_ringHom).
  [If $f$ is injective, $R$ and $S$ have the same characteristic](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/CharP/Algebra.html#charP_of_injective_ringHom);
  in particular a [subring](../rings/subring.md) has the characteristic of the whole ring.
  [A field and any nonzero commutative algebra over it have the same characteristic](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/CharP/Algebra.html#Algebra.ringChar_eq),
  so every field extension preserves the characteristic.
- **Additive structure.** [The characteristic equals the exponent of the additive group of $R$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Exponent.html#ringChar_eq_addMonoidExponent):
  the least positive $n$ with $n x = 0$ for every $x$ in $R$, or $0$ if there is none.
- **Prime subring.** If $\operatorname{char} R$ divides $m$, there is
  [a ring homomorphism from $\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$ to $R$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Data/ZMod/Basic.html#ZMod.castHom); for
  $m = \operatorname{char} R$ it is injective, and its image is the smallest subring of $R$.
  A field of characteristic $0$ contains a copy of $\mathbb{Q}$, and a field of
  characteristic $p$ contains a copy of $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$.
- **Freshman's dream.** In a commutative ring of prime characteristic $p$, every
  binomial coefficient $\binom{p}{k}$ with $0 < k < p$ is divisible by $p$, so the
  [binomial theorem](../../../hundred-theorems/part-03/the-binomial-theorem.md) collapses to
  [$(x + y)^p = x^p + y^p$ for all $x$ and $y$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/CharP/Lemmas.html#add_pow_char).
  Hence $x \mapsto x^p$ is a ring homomorphism, the
  [Frobenius morphism](frobenius-morphism.md).

## In Mathlib

Mathlib defines the characteristic for any semiring, not necessarily commutative
or even associative, and does not require subtraction. The basic notion is the
predicate "$R$ has characteristic $p$", which says that $n \cdot 1 = 0$ exactly
when $p$ divides $n$; every semiring satisfies it for exactly one $p$, and the
characteristic function returns that number, chosen noncomputably.
[The characteristic equals $p$ precisely when this predicate holds for $p$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/CharP/Defs.html#ringChar.eq_iff).
The conventions match the textbook ones: characteristic $0$ when no positive
multiple of $1$ vanishes, and characteristic $1$ for the zero ring. For rings,
[characteristic $0$ is equivalent to the injectivity of the map from the natural numbers](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/CharP/Defs.html#CharP.ringChar_zero_iff_CharZero),
which Mathlib records as a separate characteristic-zero predicate; for semirings
without subtraction the two can differ. Mathlib's integers modulo $n$ are defined
for every natural number $n$, with modulus $0$ giving the integers themselves, so
the statement about their characteristic covers $\mathbb{Z}$ as well.
