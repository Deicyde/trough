# Sufficient statistics and the factorization theorem

## Sufficient sub-σ-algebra

A sub-σ-algebra `m` is *sufficient* for the family of measures `P` on `(Ω, m₀)` if
`m ≤ m₀`, every `P θ` is finite, and for every `m₀`-measurable set `s` one `m`-measurable
function is a version of the conditional probability `P_θ[1_s | m]` under every `P θ`.
Bundling `m ≤ m₀` and finiteness into the predicate means an `IsSufficient` hypothesis
itself represents a sufficient sub-σ-algebra for statistical laws: the conditional
expectations it mentions are genuine, not Mathlib's totalized fallback on unrelated
σ-algebras or infinite measures.

## Common conditional expectation under a sufficient σ-algebra

If `m` is sufficient for a family of probability measures, every `m₀`-measurable real
function `W` that is integrable under all the measures has one `m`-measurable version of the
conditional expectation `P_θ[W | m]` for every `θ`. (Measurability of `W` itself, not only
under each `P θ`, is what lets one approximating sequence serve every parameter.)

## Factorization implies sufficiency

**Factorization implies sufficiency** (Halmos–Savage, the sufficiency half). If every
`P θ` has a density `g θ` with respect to one measure `ν` that is σ-finite on `m`, and each
`g θ` is `m`-measurable, then `m` is sufficient for `P`.

Formalized by `MathlibExt.Probability.Statistics.Sufficiency.isSufficient_of_withDensity`, a theorem declared in [MathlibExt/Probability/Statistics/Sufficiency/Basic.lean, line 265](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/035e1808ca0c8983682b74153a02628d616d77ce/MathlibExt/Probability/Statistics/Sufficiency/Basic.lean#L265). ## Factorization criterion, sufficiency direction, for a statistic, under an extra σ-finiteness hypothesis

**Factorization criterion, sufficiency direction, for a statistic, under an extra
σ-finiteness hypothesis.** If `dP_θ/dμ = g_θ(T) · h` with `g_θ` and `h` measurable and
`h · μ` σ-finite on the σ-algebra generated by `T`, then `T` is sufficient for `P`.

The σ-finiteness hypothesis on `(μ.withDensity h).trim (comap T)` is an extra,
nonstandard assumption, named in the theorem: it is strictly stronger than `μ` being
σ-finite, since restriction to a sub-σ-algebra need not preserve σ-finiteness, and it
can fail even when `μ` is σ-finite and `h` is a density. This theorem is therefore the
factorization criterion under that extra hypothesis, not the classical criterion as
stated. Removing the hypothesis would require a different proof.

## Sufficient statistic for a discrete family

A statistic `T : X → τ` is *sufficient* for the discrete family `P : Θ → PMF X` if the
conditional probability `P θ x / P_θ(T = T x)` of `x` given `T = T x` is the same under any
two parameters `θ, θ'` for which `{T = T x}` has positive probability. Here
`P_θ(T = t) = (P θ).map T t`.

## Factorization, necessity

If `T` is sufficient for `P`, then
`P θ x = g θ (T x) * h x` with `g θ t = P_θ(T = t)` and a parameter-free `h` with values in
`[0, 1]`.

## Factorization, sufficiency

If `P θ x = g θ (T x) * h x` for nonnegative real `g`, `h`
with `h` free of `θ`, then `T` is sufficient for `P`.

## The Fisher–Neyman factorization theorem

**The Fisher–Neyman factorization theorem** (discrete case). A statistic `T` is sufficient
for the family of mass functions `P` if and only if there are nonnegative functions `g` and
`h`, with `h` free of the parameter, such that `P θ x = g θ (T x) * h x` for every `θ`, `x`.

## Halmos–Savage factorization, necessity

If the family of probability measures `P` is
dominated by a σ-finite measure `μ` and the statistic `T` is sufficient for `P`, then the
densities factor as `dP_θ/dμ = g_θ(T) · h` with measurable `g_θ` and `h`, `h` free of `θ`.
