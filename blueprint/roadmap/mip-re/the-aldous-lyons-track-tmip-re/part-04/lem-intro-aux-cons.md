# The auxiliary checks as linear constraints

Read an auxiliary answer of the padded layout as the kernel's parsed answer: its register, its dual and tail, and the input's answer made of the readable window of the input's readable length and the linear window of its linear length. At every ordered pair of auxiliary labels there is a list of constraints, given a readable condition $G$ implying that the input's answer fits the cutoff, the input's split and constraints, and lists of vectors spanning the kernels of the hiding stages' linear maps. It holds of two answers of the labels' lengths exactly when $G$ holds at both registers, the parsed answers are equal when the labels are, and the kernel's directed checks accept the parsed answers in both orders: sampling ($y = L_w(z)$ and equal input answers), reading (equal registers and input answers), the last hiding level against Read, two consecutive hiding levels, and the source game, whose constraints are the input's, re-indexed.

## Proof

A comparison of readable data is a readable condition; one of linear data is a list of linear constraints, bit by bit for an equality of windows, coordinate by coordinate for an equality on a coordinate set, generator by generator for an equality of dual readouts. The input's answer is compared by its split, readable, and its two windows. The other order swaps the two blocks of every constraint.
