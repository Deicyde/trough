## Involution

An element $x$ of a group $G$ is an involution when $x^2 = 1$ and
$x \ne 1$.

## The special unitary group $\mathrm{SU}_3(4)$

Let $\mathbb{F}_{16}$ be the field with
$16$ elements and, for a matrix $A$ over $\mathbb{F}_{16}$, let $\bar{A}$ be the matrix obtained by
applying the involutive field automorphism $x \mapsto x^4$ of $\mathbb{F}_{16}$ to each entry.
Then $\mathrm{SU}_3(4)$ is the subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_{16})$ generated by the
invertible $3 \times 3$ matrices $A$ over $\mathbb{F}_{16}$ with $\det A = 1$ and
$\bar{A}^{\top} A = 1$, that is, by the determinant-one isometries of the Hermitian form
$x_1 \bar{y}_1 + x_2 \bar{y}_2 + x_3 \bar{y}_3$.

These matrices already form a subgroup, so $\mathrm{SU}_3(4)$ consists exactly of them.

## The projective special unitary group $\mathrm{PSU}_3(4)$

It is the quotient
$\mathrm{PSU}_3(4) = \mathrm{SU}_3(4) / Z(\mathrm{SU}_3(4))$ of the special unitary group
$\mathrm{SU}_3(4)$ by its centre $Z(\mathrm{SU}_3(4))$.

## Jabara's conjecture on groups with $\{3, 5\}$-involutions

Kourovka 19.36 (E. Jabara):
let $G$ be a periodic group (every element has finite order) that is generated by its involutions
(elements $x \ne 1$ with $x^2 = 1$). Suppose that the order of $xy$ lies in $\{1, 2, 3, 5\}$ for
all involutions $x, y$ of $G$, and that each of these four values is the order of $xy$ for some
involutions $x, y$ of $G$. Then $G$ is isomorphic to the alternating group $A_5$ or to the
projective special unitary group $\mathrm{PSU}_3(4)$.

## Jabara's conjecture on groups with $\{3, 5\}$-involutions holds

Let $G$ be a periodic group
(every element has finite order) that is generated by its involutions (elements $x \ne 1$ with
$x^2 = 1$). Suppose that the order of $xy$ lies in $\{1, 2, 3, 5\}$ for all involutions $x, y$ of
$G$, and that each of these four values is the order of $xy$ for some involutions $x, y$ of $G$.
Then $G$ is isomorphic to the alternating group $A_5$ or to the projective special unitary group
$\mathrm{PSU}_3(4)$.

Bettio proved this even without the hypothesis that $G$ is periodic; the Kourovka Archive records
the proof.
