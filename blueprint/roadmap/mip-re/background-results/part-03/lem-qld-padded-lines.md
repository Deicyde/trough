# Padded line POVM

For every padded line $\ell$, there is a POVM with polynomial outcomes of degree at most $md+1$, and — given Lemma [Axis degree for expanded line measurements](../part-02/lem-qld-axis-degree.md) — at most $d$ when $\ell$ is axis-parallel. On the padded line–point distribution it is consistent with the opposite padded point measurement, with error $\delta_{\mathrm{combine}}=\mathrm{poly}(m)\cdot\mathrm{poly}(\varepsilon,md/q)$, in both register versions. The answer is the restriction of $\alpha f_X(x)+\beta f_Z(z)$. On an axis line, either an $x$ or $z$ coordinate varies with constant $\alpha,\beta$, or only one coefficient varies, giving degree at most $d$ for $d\geq1$. The general bound $md+1\leq4md$ and the separate axis bound make these legal answers for the seeded test at $(q,4m,d,1)$. The Lean proves it for a strategy in any bipartite model, the measurement being a family in the players' algebras of its register model, with the consistency in agreement form against the padded sandwich combination; the statement above is its tensor-product instance.

## Proof

The measurement is the conditional average, over the sublines the construction of Lemma [The sublines distribution](../part-02/lem-qld-sublines.md) draws for the given padded line, of the pasted line measurement coarse-grained by the combining map; its outcome at a point of $\ell$ is $\alpha f_X(x) + \beta f_Z(z)$ by construction, and the degree bounds are the ones the Comments below record.

For the consistency, the joint law of the two sublines-with-points is, at a fixed padded seed, *exactly* a product of the two marginal laws, each restricted or not according to which block the padded axis index lies in. So Lemma [Pairs of lines](../part-02/lem-qld-pairs-of-lines.md) on a product of two independent line–point laws applies directly, and the padded law is at most $m^2$ times the product of the two unrestricted laws. Coarse-graining the outcome pair $(f_X(x),f_Z(z))$ along $(r_1,r_2)\mapsto \alpha r_1 + \beta r_2$ only increases agreement, and $\alpha$ and $\beta$ at the sampled point are uniform and independent of both sublines, because translating the padded point in those two coordinates alone leaves both sublines fixed. Chaining the three gives $\delta_{\mathrm{combine}} = m^2\,\delta_P$. The other register version is this one applied to the strategy with the players exchanged, in the model with the players exchanged.

The paper instead chains three Cauchy–Schwarz claims, because it takes the joint law to be correlated; that is so given the padded *line*, but the quantity conditions on the two *sublines*, which do not determine the relative offset of the two blocks. See `reports/padded-lines-product-law.md`.
