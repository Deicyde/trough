A **subfield** of a [field](trough:Field) $K$ is a subset of $K$ that contains $0$ and $1$ and is closed under addition, negation, multiplication and taking inverses of nonzero elements, so that it is itself a field under the operations of $K$. Subfields are central to field theory: a field extension $L/K$ can be viewed as a field $L$ together with a subfield isomorphic to $K$, and for a finite Galois extension the [Galois correspondence](trough:IsGalois.intermediateFieldEquivSubgroup) describes the fields lying in between by means of subgroups of the Galois group.

## Definition

Let $K$ be a field. A subset $F \subseteq K$ is a subfield if

- $F$ is a [subring](trough:Subring) of $K$: it contains $0$ and $1$ and is closed under $+$, $-$ and $\times$;
- $F$ is closed under inverses: if $x \in F$ and $x \neq 0$, then $x^{-1} \in F$.

Thus a subring that contains the inverse of each of its nonzero elements determines a subfield with the same elements. With the addition and multiplication of $K$ restricted to it, a subfield is a field in its own right, and the inclusion $F \hookrightarrow K$ is a [ring homomorphism](trough:RingHom). If $F$ is a subfield of $K$, one also says that $K$ is an extension field of $F$.

The same definition makes sense in a division ring (a ring with $1 \neq 0$ in which every nonzero element is invertible, but multiplication need not commute), and there a subfield is again a division ring. Such a subset is usually called a sub-division ring; Mathlib still calls it a subfield.

## Examples

- The [rational numbers](trough:Rat) form a subfield of the [real numbers](trough:Real), which form a subfield of the [complex numbers](trough:Complex).
- The set $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 2) = \{a + b\sqrt 2 : a, b \in \mathbb{Q}\}$ is a subfield of $\mathbb{R}$. It is a subring, since $(a + b\sqrt 2)(c + d\sqrt 2) = (ac + 2bd) + (ad + bc)\sqrt 2$; if $a + b\sqrt 2 \neq 0$ then $a^2 - 2b^2 \neq 0$, because $\sqrt 2$ is irrational, and

$$(a + b\sqrt 2)^{-1} = \frac{a}{a^2 - 2b^2} - \frac{b}{a^2 - 2b^2}\sqrt 2.$$

- The integers form a subring of $\mathbb{Q}$ but not a subfield, since $2^{-1} \notin \mathbb{Z}$.
- If $f, g \colon K \to L$ are ring homomorphisms from a field $K$, then the set of $x \in K$ with $f(x) = g(x)$ is a subfield of $K$. Applied to complex conjugation and the identity of $\mathbb{C}$, this shows that $\mathbb{R}$, the set of complex numbers fixed by conjugation, is a subfield of $\mathbb{C}$.
- For a ring homomorphism $f \colon K \to L$ between fields, the image of $f$ is a subfield of $L$; more generally, the image under $f$ of any subfield of $K$ is a subfield of $L$, and the preimage under $f$ of any subfield of $L$ is a subfield of $K$.

## Properties

**Intersections.** The intersection of any family of subfields of $K$ is a subfield: the infimum of a set of subfields has as underlying set the intersection of their underlying sets. A union of two subfields is usually not a subfield, but the supremum of a nonempty directed family of subfields is exactly their union.

**Generated subfields.** For a subset $S \subseteq K$, the subfield generated by $S$ is the intersection of all subfields containing $S$. It is the smallest subfield containing $S$: it is contained in a subfield $F$ exactly when $S \subseteq F$. Concretely, it consists of the quotients $a b^{-1}$ of elements $a, b$ of the subring generated by $S$, with $b \neq 0$. One consequence is that two ring homomorphisms out of $K$ that agree on a set $S$ generating $K$ as a field are equal.

**The prime subfield.** The intersection of all subfields of $K$ is the smallest subfield of $K$, called its prime subfield. Since every subfield contains $1$, it contains every integer multiple of $1$ and every quotient of two such multiples with nonzero denominator; in a field of characteristic zero this says that every subfield contains the image of every rational number. The prime subfield is determined by the [characteristic](trough:ringChar) of $K$:

- if $K$ has [characteristic zero](trough:CharZero), the prime subfield is the image of the canonical map $\mathbb{Q} \to K$, a copy of $\mathbb{Q}$;
- if $K$ has [characteristic $p$](trough:CharP) for a prime $p$, the prime subfield has exactly $p$ elements, and it is a copy of the field $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$.

In particular $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ have no subfields other than themselves.

## Related concepts

A subfield of $K$ is the field analogue of a [subgroup](trough:Subgroup) of a group or a [subring](trough:Subring) of a ring. When a field $L$ is an extension of a field $K$, the subfields of $L$ that contain the image of $K$ are called intermediate fields; they are the objects of the Galois correspondence, and they appear throughout the theory of [algebraic extensions](trough:Algebra.IsAlgebraic) and [splitting fields](trough:Polynomial.SplittingField). The [finite fields](trough:Mathlib.FieldTheory.Finite.Basic) give a clean picture: a field with $p^n$ elements has exactly one subfield with $p^m$ elements for each divisor $m$ of $n$, and no others.

## In Mathlib

Mathlib defines subfields for any division ring, not only for fields, so its notion covers sub-division rings of noncommutative division rings such as the quaternions. A subfield is a bundled structure: a subring together with the condition that it is closed under inversion. Because Mathlib sets the inverse of $0$ to be $0$, this condition is stated for every element, with no exception for zero, and quotients by zero are likewise allowed in the description of generated subfields. The subfields of a division ring are ordered by inclusion and form a complete lattice, whose least element is the prime subfield and whose greatest element is the whole ring. Intermediate fields of an extension are a separate structure, each of which can be viewed as a subfield of the larger field.
