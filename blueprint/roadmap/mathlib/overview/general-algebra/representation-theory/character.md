# Character

The **character** of a finite-dimensional [representation](representation.md) of a group is the
function that assigns to each group element the trace of the linear map by which it acts. It
compresses a representation, a family of matrices, into a single scalar-valued function on the
group, yet for finite groups over the complex numbers it loses nothing: two representations with
the same character are isomorphic. Characters turn many questions about representations into
calculations with sums over the group, and they are the main computational tool of the
representation theory of finite groups.

## Definition

Let $G$ be a [group](../group-theory/part-01/group.md), $k$ a [field](../field-theory/field.md), and $V$ a finite-dimensional
vector space over $k$ on which $G$ acts through a homomorphism $\rho$ from $G$ to the invertible
[linear maps](../../linear-algebra/fundamentals/linear-map.md) of $V$. The character of $V$ is the function
$\chi_V \colon G \to k$ given by
[$\chi_V(g) = \operatorname{tr} \rho(g)$, the trace of the linear map $\rho(g)$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/Character.html#FDRep.character).
After choosing a basis of $V$, $\chi_V(g)$ is the sum of the diagonal entries of the matrix of
$\rho(g)$; since similar matrices have the same trace, the result does not depend on the basis.
The definition uses only the multiplication of $G$, so it applies equally to a monoid acting by
linear maps that need not be invertible. The dimension of $V$ is called the degree of the character.

## Examples

- **One-dimensional representations.** If $V$ is a line, each $\rho(g)$ is multiplication by a
  nonzero scalar, and $\chi_V$ is the resulting homomorphism from $G$ to the multiplicative group
  of $k$. In particular the trivial representation on $k$ has the constant character $1$. For the
  cyclic group of order $n$ generated by $g$ and a complex $n$-th root of unity $\zeta$, the
  rule $g^j \mapsto \zeta^j$ is such a character.
- **Permutation representations.** If $G$ acts on a finite set $X$, it permutes the standard
  basis of the space $k^X$ of functions on $X$. Each $\rho(g)$ is then a permutation matrix, whose
  diagonal entries are $1$ exactly at the points of $X$ fixed by $g$, so $\chi(g)$ is the number
  of fixed points of $g$, read in $k$. For the regular representation, where $G$ acts on itself
  by left multiplication, this gives $\chi(1) = |G|$ and $\chi(g) = 0$ for $g \neq 1$.

**The symmetric group on three letters.** Over the complex numbers, the
[permutation group](../group-theory/part-02/permutation-group-of-a-type.md) $S_3$ has three conjugacy classes (the identity, the
transpositions and the $3$-cycles) and three irreducible representations: the trivial one, the
sign representation, and the two-dimensional representation on the plane of vectors in
$\mathbb{C}^3$ whose coordinates sum to $0$. Their characters, listed class by class, are

$$\begin{array}{c|ccc} & 1 & (1\,2) & (1\,2\,3) \\ \hline \text{trivial} & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ \text{sign} & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ \text{standard} & 2 & 0 & -1 \end{array}$$

The permutation representation on $\{1, 2, 3\}$ has character $(3, 1, 0)$, the sum of the
trivial and standard characters, matching its decomposition into those two pieces.

## Properties

Throughout, $V$ and $W$ are finite-dimensional representations of $G$ over $k$.

- **Value at the identity.** [$\chi_V(1)$ is the dimension of $V$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/Character.html#FDRep.char_one), read as
  an element of $k$.
- **Class function.** [$\chi_V(gh) = \chi_V(hg)$ for all $g, h$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/Character.html#FDRep.char_mul_comm), and
  hence [$\chi_V(hgh^{-1}) = \chi_V(g)$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/Character.html#FDRep.char_conj): the character is a
  [class function](../../../undergraduate/group-theory/representation-theory-of-finite-groups/class-function-over-a-group.md),
  constant on each [conjugacy class](../../../undergraduate/group-theory/basic-definitions/conjugacy-classes.md).
- **Invariance.** [Isomorphic representations have the same character](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/Character.html#FDRep.char_iso). The
  converse holds for finite groups over the complex numbers but fails in positive characteristic:
  over a field of characteristic $p$, the direct sum of $p$ copies of the trivial representation
  has the same character as the zero representation.
- **Operations.** Write $\sigma$ for the action on $W$. The character of a direct sum
  $V \oplus W$ is $\chi_V + \chi_W$, and
  [the character of the tensor product $V \otimes W$, where $g$ acts by $\rho(g) \otimes \sigma(g)$, is the product $\chi_V \chi_W$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/Character.html#FDRep.char_tensor).
  For the dual representation on the [dual space](../../linear-algebra/duality/dual-vector-space.md) $V^*$, where $g$ acts by
  the transpose of $\rho(g^{-1})$,
  [$\chi_{V^*}(g) = \chi_V(g^{-1})$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/Character.html#FDRep.char_dual), and for the representation on the
  space of linear maps from $V$ to $W$, where $g$ sends $f$ to $\sigma(g) \circ f \circ \rho(g^{-1})$,
  [the character at $g$ is $\chi_V(g^{-1})\,\chi_W(g)$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/Character.html#FDRep.char_linHom).
- **Integrality.** If $G$ is finite, each eigenvalue of $\rho(g)$ in an algebraic closure of $k$
  is a root of unity, so
  [each value $\chi_V(g)$ is integral over the integers](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/Character.html#FDRep.isIntegral_character).
  Over the complex numbers these eigenvalues lie on the unit circle, which gives
  $\chi_V(g^{-1}) = \overline{\chi_V(g)}$.

## Orthogonality

Suppose now that $G$ is finite and that its order $|G|$ is invertible in $k$, that is, the
characteristic of $k$ does not divide $|G|$. Averaging a character over the group gives
the dimension of the space of invariant vectors:
[$\frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} \chi_V(g)$ equals the dimension of the subspace of vectors fixed by all of $G$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/Character.html#FDRep.average_char_eq_finrank_invariants).
Applied to the space of linear maps from $V$ to $W$, this shows that
[$\frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} \chi_W(g)\,\chi_V(g^{-1})$ is the dimension of the space of $G$-equivariant linear maps from $V$ to $W$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/Character.html#FDRep.scalar_product_char_eq_finrank_equivariant),
both dimensions read in $k$. If in addition $k$ is
[algebraically closed](../field-theory/algebraically-closed-field.md) and $V$ and $W$ are irreducible, then
[Schur's lemma](../../../undergraduate/linear-algebra/linear-representations/schurs-lemma.md) makes this dimension $1$ or $0$, which
gives the [orthogonality of characters](orthogonality-of-characters.md):
[$\frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} \chi_V(g)\,\chi_W(g^{-1})$ is $1$ if $V$ and $W$ are isomorphic and $0$ otherwise](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/Character.html#FDRep.char_orthonormal).

## Related concepts

Over the complex numbers, the irreducible characters of a finite group form an
[orthonormal basis of the space of class functions](../../../undergraduate/group-theory/representation-theory-of-finite-groups/orthonormal-basis-of-irreducible-characters.md),
so their number equals the number of conjugacy classes. Their orthonormality, together with
[Maschke's theorem](../../../undergraduate/group-theory/representation-theory-of-finite-groups/maschke-theorem.md), is what makes the
character determine the representation over the complex numbers. The [irreducible representations](../../../undergraduate/linear-algebra/linear-representations/irreducible-representation.md)
and their characters are recorded in the character table of the group. The one-dimensional
characters of any group are its homomorphisms to the multiplicative group of the field. For a
finite abelian group, the one-dimensional complex characters take values in the unit circle and
form the [dual group](../../../undergraduate/group-theory/representation-theory-of-finite-groups/dual-groups.md)
of harmonic analysis.

## In Mathlib

Mathlib defines the character for objects of the
[category of finite-dimensional representations](category-of-finite-dimensional-representations.md) over a field, with the group
allowed to be any monoid; the results that use inverses assume a group, and the averaging and
orthogonality results assume a finite group whose order is invertible in the field. The trace is
the basis-free trace of a linear map, and the character takes values in the field itself, so
dimensions appear as their images in the field: in positive characteristic they are known only
modulo the characteristic. Irreducible representations are the simple objects of the category,
and orthogonality is stated with $g^{-1}$ in place of complex conjugation, which makes it valid
over every algebraically closed field whose characteristic does not divide the order of the
group. A parallel version, [the character of a representation given as a homomorphism into the linear maps of a vector space](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/Character.html#Representation.character),
carries the same basic results.
