# Effective Frobenius, trace Gram matrices and table transport

The canonical Shoup-field coordinates form a binary linear equivalence. Uniform polynomial-time ambient programs construct the binary Frobenius matrix, the trace Gram matrix of a supplied family, its inverse when that family is a basis, and coordinates and multiplication tables in any supplied basis. The Frobenius matrix has minimal polynomial $X^k-1$, has $k$th power equal to the identity, and has squarefree minimal polynomial when $k$ is odd. Constructing the Frobenius matrix and printing all $k^3$ multiplication-table bits take polynomial time in $k$.

## Proof

Canonical encoding and decoding identify field vectors with binary coordinates. Evaluate a linear map on the standard coordinate vectors to compute its matrix; apply this to squaring. Transport Mathlib's Frobenius minimal-polynomial theorem through the basis matrix equivalence. Extract the binary trace from its field encoding, then evaluate it on each pairwise product. Nondegeneracy of the finite field trace form makes the Gram matrix of a basis nonsingular. Binary elimination computes its inverse and the coordinates of every basis-vector product. The encoded size of a supplied basis is quadratic in $k$, so the ambient polynomial bounds give the stated source bounds.
