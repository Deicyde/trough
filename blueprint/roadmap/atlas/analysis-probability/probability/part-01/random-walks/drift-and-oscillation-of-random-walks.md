## Tail σ-algebra of a sequence of σ-algebras

Given σ-algebras
$\mathcal{A}_0, \mathcal{A}_1, \dots$ on $\Omega$, the tail σ-algebra is

$$\mathcal{T} = \bigcap_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \sigma(\mathcal{A}_n, \mathcal{A}_{n+1}, \dots),$$

where $\sigma(\mathcal{A}_n, \mathcal{A}_{n+1}, \dots)$ is the smallest σ-algebra containing
every $\mathcal{A}_i$ with $i \ge n$. It contains the information observable by looking only at
events arbitrarily far into the future.

## Tail σ-algebra of a sequence of functions

Let $\beta$ be a measurable space and let
$X_n : \Omega \to \beta$ for $n \in \mathbb{N}$. The tail σ-algebra of $X$ is

$$\mathcal{T} = \bigcap_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \sigma(X_n, X_{n+1}, \dots),$$

the tail σ-algebra of the sequence of σ-algebras $\sigma(X_n)$, where $\sigma(X_n)$ consists of
the preimages under $X_n$ of measurable subsets of $\beta$. This is the tail σ-algebra
appearing in Kolmogorov's 0-1 law.

## Drift of a random walk to +∞ is measurable from every index

Let $X_0, X_1, \dots$ be
real-valued functions on a set $\Omega$, let $S_m = \sum_{i < m} X_i$ be their partial sums, and
for $n \in \mathbb{N}$ let $\sigma(X_n, X_{n+1}, \dots)$ be the smallest σ-algebra on $\Omega$ for
which every $X_i$ with $i \ge n$ is Borel measurable. For every $n$, the set of $\omega \in \Omega$
with $S_m(\omega) \to +\infty$ as $m \to \infty$ is measurable with respect to
$\sigma(X_n, X_{n+1}, \dots)$.

The proof writes this set as a countable Boolean combination of sets defined by the tail
partial sums $\sum_{n \le i < m} X_i$.

## One-step shift identity for the limsup of partial sums

For a real sequence
$\omega = (\omega_0, \omega_1, \dots)$ write $S_n(\omega) = \sum_{i < n} \omega_i$, and let
$\theta\omega = (\omega_1, \omega_2, \dots)$ be the one-step shift. Then for every $\omega$,
