# Homotopy of finite spaces, posets and subgroup complexes

> The homotopy theory of finite spaces and of posets, applied to the posets of p-subgroups of a group. Two maps between finite spaces are homotopic exactly when a fence of pointwise comparable maps joins them; removing a beat point of a finite T₀ space leaves a strong deformation retract; a homotopy equivalence between finite T₀ spaces without beat points is a homeomorphism (Stong's classification); and a finite T₀ space with a group action has an invariant core, so that a contractible one has a fixed point. For order complexes, comparable monotone maps realize to homotopic maps, through an explicit prism homotopy, and a conical preorder has a contractible realization. If a finite group acts monotonically on a finite poset and the alternating count of nonempty chains of every nontrivial subgroup's fixed subposet is 1, that count for the poset is 1 modulo the order of the group. The realization of the poset of nontrivial subgroups satisfying Mathlib's elementwise p-group condition is contractible when one of those subgroups is normal, following the proof of Quillen's Proposition 2.4. For finite groups, the poset with its upper-set topology is contractible exactly when such a normal subgroup exists (Stong). For finite groups and prime p, the alternating nonempty-chain count is 1 modulo the p-part of the group order (Brown's congruence).
