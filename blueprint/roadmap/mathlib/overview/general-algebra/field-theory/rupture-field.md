# Rupture field

A **rupture field** of an [irreducible](../divisibility-in-integral-domains/irreducible-element.md) polynomial $f$ over a [field](field.md) $K$ is an extension of $K$ generated by a single root of $f$. The standard model is the [quotient ring](../ideals-and-quotients/quotient-ring.md) $K[X]/(f)$, in which the class of $X$ is a root of $f$ by construction. Applied to an irreducible factor, this quotient construction is how one proves that every nonconstant polynomial over a field has a root in some extension, and iterating it produces [splitting fields](splitting-field.md). The same quotient makes sense for any polynomial over any commutative ring, and is then called adjoining a root.

## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative [ring](../rings/ring.md) and $f$ a [polynomial](../polynomials-and-power-series/polynomial-in-one-indeterminate.md) in $R[X]$. The ring obtained by adjoining a root of $f$ to $R$ is
[the quotient of $R[X]$ by the principal ideal generated by $f$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot):

$$R[X]/(f).$$

It comes with a ring homomorphism $R \to R[X]/(f)$ sending a constant to its class, and with a distinguished element $\alpha$,
[the class of $X$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot.root). Every element is the class of some polynomial, and
[two polynomials have the same class exactly when $f$ divides their difference](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot.mk_eq_mk). Since $f$ itself has class zero,
[$\alpha$ is a root of $f$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot.isRoot_root), where the coefficients of $f$ are read in the new ring.

When $R = K$ is a field, $K[X]/(f)$ is
[a field if and only if $f$ is irreducible](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot.isField_iff_irreducible): in a [principal ideal domain](../divisibility-in-integral-domains/principal-ideal-domain.md) that is not a field, such as $K[X]$, the ideal $(f)$ is [maximal](../ideals-and-quotients/maximal-ideal.md) exactly when $f$ is irreducible. For irreducible $f$ the field $K[X]/(f)$ is the rupture field of $f$, and $K$ embeds in it, since a ring homomorphism out of a field is injective.

## Examples

- Over the real numbers, $X^2 + 1$ is irreducible, and $\mathbb{R}[X]/(X^2+1)$ is isomorphic to the [complex numbers](../numbers/complex-number.md), with $\alpha$ corresponding to $i$.
- Over the field with two elements, $X^2 + X + 1$ has degree $2$ and no root, so it is irreducible, and its rupture field is a field with four elements.
- Over $\mathbb{Q}$, the polynomial $X^3 - 2$ is irreducible by [Eisenstein's criterion](../polynomials-and-power-series/eisensteins-criterion.md). Its rupture field is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})$, a subfield of $\mathbb{R}$. The other two complex roots of $X^3 - 2$ are not real, so $X^3 - 2$ does not split in this field: a rupture field need not be a splitting field.
- If $f = X^2$ over a field $K$, the quotient $K[X]/(X^2)$ is the ring of dual numbers. Here $\alpha \neq 0$ but $\alpha^2 = 0$, so the quotient is not a field, matching the reducibility of $X^2$.
- For an element $r$ of a commutative ring $R$, adjoining a root of $rX - 1$
  [makes $r$ invertible, with inverse $\alpha$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot.root_isInv).

## Properties

Let $K$ be a field and $f$ a nonzero polynomial of degree $d$ over $K$, and write $\alpha$ for the class of $X$ in $L = K[X]/(f)$.

- **Basis and degree.** [The powers $1, \alpha, \dots, \alpha^{d-1}$ form a basis of $L$ over $K$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot.powerBasis), so [the dimension of $L$ over $K$ is the degree of $f$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#finrank_quotient_span_eq_natDegree). Neither statement needs $f$ to be irreducible.
- **Minimal polynomial.** [The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ is $f$ divided by its leading coefficient](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot.minpoly_root) (see [minimal polynomial](../../linear-algebra/endomorphism-polynomials/minimal-polynomial.md)).
- **Universal property.** For any commutative ring $R$, polynomial $f$ over $R$, ring homomorphism $i \colon R \to S$ to a commutative ring $S$ and element $s$ of $S$ that is a root of $f$ with coefficients mapped by $i$, there is a ring homomorphism from $R[X]/(f)$ to $S$ that [extends $i$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot.lift_comp_of) and [sends $\alpha$ to $s$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot.lift_root). It is unique, because [a ring homomorphism out of $R[X]/(f)$ is determined by its values on $R$ and on $\alpha$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot.ringHom_ext).
- **Embeddings and roots.** If $E$ is an integral domain that is a $K$-[algebra](../algebras-over-a-ring/associative-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring.md), then
  [the $K$-algebra homomorphisms $L \to E$ correspond one to one with the roots of $f$ in $E$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot.equiv), each homomorphism corresponding to the image of $\alpha$. Hence there are at most $d$ of them. For $f = X^3 - 2$ and $E = \mathbb{C}$ there are exactly three, one for each complex [root](../polynomials-and-power-series/roots-of-a-polynomial.md).
- **Uniqueness.** If $E$ is a field extension of $K$ and $\beta \in E$ is algebraic over $K$ (equivalently, integral: a root of a monic polynomial over $K$) with minimal polynomial $p$, then
  [$K[X]/(p)$ is isomorphic as a $K$-algebra to the subfield $K(\beta)$ generated by $\beta$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/FieldTheory/IntermediateField/Adjoin/Basic.html#IntermediateField.adjoinRootEquivAdjoin), by
  [an isomorphism sending $\alpha$ to $\beta$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/FieldTheory/IntermediateField/Adjoin/Basic.html#IntermediateField.adjoinRootEquivAdjoin_apply_root). If $\beta$ is a root of an irreducible $f$, so that $K(\beta)$ is a rupture field of $f$, then $f$ divided by its leading coefficient is monic, irreducible and has $\beta$ as a root, so it is the minimal polynomial $p$ of $\beta$. Since $(f) = (p)$, the field $K(\beta)$ is isomorphic over $K$ to $K[X]/(f)$; hence any two rupture fields of $f$ are isomorphic over $K$. The isomorphism depends on the choice of root and is not unique in general.

Over a general commutative ring the quotient behaves less uniformly. If $f$ is monic of degree $d$, then
[$R[X]/(f)$ is a free $R$-module with basis $1, \alpha, \dots, \alpha^{d-1}$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot.powerBasis'). If $R$ is an integral domain and $f$ is not a nonzero constant,
[the map from $R$ to $R[X]/(f)$ is injective](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot.of.injective_of_degree_ne_zero), and if $f$ is a prime element of $R[X]$,
[the quotient is an integral domain](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot.isDomain_of_prime).

## Related concepts

A splitting field of $f$ is obtained by repeatedly adjoining a root of an irreducible factor of what remains of $f$; the rupture field is the first step of that process. By the [primitive element theorem](../../../thousand-plus/algebra/12-field-theory-and-polynomials/primitive-element-theorem.md), every finite separable extension is generated by one element, so it is a rupture field of that element's minimal polynomial. Adjoining a root of $rX - 1$ is one way to build the [localization](../rings/localization.md) of a ring at the powers of $r$.

## In Mathlib

Mathlib does not define rupture fields separately. Its construction works for an arbitrary polynomial over an arbitrary commutative ring, as the quotient by the [ideal](../ideals-and-quotients/ideal-of-a-commutative-ring.md) generated by $f$, with no irreducibility assumed. When the base is a field and irreducibility of $f$ is supplied as a standing fact, the quotient [carries a field structure](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.html#AdjoinRoot.instField). Results about bases, degree and minimal polynomials do not use this standing fact and need only that $f$ is nonzero; the dimension formula holds even for $f = 0$, because the quotient is then infinite-dimensional, whose dimension Mathlib records as $0$, and the natural-number degree of the zero polynomial is also $0$. Separately, Mathlib has [a structure recording that a given ring is presented as $R[X]/(f)$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/IsAdjoinRoot.html#IsAdjoinRoot), through a surjective algebra map from $R[X]$ whose kernel is the ideal generated by $f$; this lets results about the construction apply to rings that are only isomorphic to it.
