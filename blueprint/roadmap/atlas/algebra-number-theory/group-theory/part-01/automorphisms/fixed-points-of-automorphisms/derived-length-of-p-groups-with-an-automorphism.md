## Groups of sectional rank at most $r$

Let $G$ be a group and $r$ a natural number. The group
$G$ has sectional rank at most $r$ when every subgroup of $G$ is generated by a set of at most $r$
elements.

For a finite $p$-group $G$, the least such $r$ is the largest rank of an elementary abelian section
of $G$.

## Shalev and Khukhro's question on the derived length of $p$-groups with an automorphism

The
statement that there is a function $f : \mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ such that for
every prime $p$, every finite $p$-group $G$, all natural numbers $r$ and $m$ such that $G$ has
sectional rank at most $r$ (every subgroup of $G$ is generated by at most $r$ elements), and every
automorphism $\varphi$ of $G$ with exactly $m$ fixed points, the derived length of $G$ is at most
$f(r, m)$, that is, the term $G^{(f(r, m))}$ of the derived series of $G$ is trivial, where
$G^{(0)} = G$ and $G^{(i+1)} = [G^{(i)}, G^{(i)}]$.

This is Kourovka Notebook Problem 13.56 (A. Shalev; E. I. Khukhro).

## The derived length of a $p$-group is bounded by its sectional rank and the fixed points of an automorphism

**The derived length of a $p$-group is bounded by its sectional rank and the fixed points of an
automorphism.** There is a function $f : \mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ such that for
every prime $p$, every finite $p$-group $G$, all natural numbers $r$ and $m$ such that $G$ has
sectional rank at most $r$ (every subgroup of $G$ is generated by at most $r$ elements), and every
automorphism $\varphi$ of $G$ with exactly $m$ fixed points, the derived length of $G$ is at most
$f(r, m)$, that is, the term $G^{(f(r, m))}$ of the derived series of $G$ is trivial, where
$G^{(0)} = G$ and $G^{(i+1)} = [G^{(i)}, G^{(i)}]$.

Resolved true: the Kourovka Archive answers 13.56 "Yes, it is", citing Jaikin-Zapirain: the derived
length of a finite $p$-group of sectional rank $r$ with an automorphism having exactly $m$ fixed
points is bounded by a function of $r$ and $m$.
