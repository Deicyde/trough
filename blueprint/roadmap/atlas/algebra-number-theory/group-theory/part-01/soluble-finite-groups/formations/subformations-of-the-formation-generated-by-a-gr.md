## Classes of finite groups

A class of finite groups is a predicate on finite groups: it
assigns to every finite group $G$ a proposition, read as "$G$ belongs to the class". The class need
not be closed under isomorphism.

## Formations of finite groups, possibly empty

A class $\mathfrak{F}$ of finite groups is a
formation if it is closed under homomorphic images (whenever $f \colon G \to H$ is a surjective
homomorphism of finite groups and $G \in \mathfrak{F}$, then $H \in \mathfrak{F}$) and under finite
subdirect products (whenever $N_1$ and $N_2$ are normal subgroups of a finite group $G$ with
$G / N_1 \in \mathfrak{F}$ and $G / N_2 \in \mathfrak{F}$, then
$G / (N_1 \cap N_2) \in \mathfrak{F}$). In particular the empty class is a formation.

## Formation generated by a finite group

For a finite group $G$, the formation generated by
$G$ is the class of finite groups $H$ such that $H$ belongs to every formation that contains $G$;
that is, it is the intersection of all formations containing $G$.

## Kourovka Problem 16.31 for finite groups

The statement (V. A. Vedernikov) that for every
finite group $G$ only finitely many formations of finite groups are contained in the formation
generated by $G$. Here formations may be empty, and a formation $\mathfrak{F}$ is contained in the
formation generated by $G$ when every finite group in $\mathfrak{F}$ belongs to the formation
generated by $G$.

## The formation generated by a finite group can have infinitely many subformations

There is
a finite group $G$ such that infinitely many formations are contained in the formation generated
by $G$. That is, the answer to Kourovka Problem 16.31 is negative, even for finite $G$.
