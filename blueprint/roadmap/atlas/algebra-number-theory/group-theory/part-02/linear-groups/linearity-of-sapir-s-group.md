## Sapir's group

Sapir's group is the group with presentation
$\langle a, b, t \mid a^t = ab, b^t = ba \rangle$, where $a^t = t^{-1} a t$: the quotient of the
free group on $a$, $b$, $t$ by the normal subgroup generated by $t^{-1} a t (ab)^{-1}$ and
$t^{-1} b t (ba)^{-1}$.

## Linearity of Sapir's group over the integers (Kourovka 17.108)

The statement that the
group $\langle a, b, t \mid a^t = ab, b^t = ba \rangle$, where $a^t = t^{-1} a t$, embeds in
$\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$ for some natural number $n$, that is, that it is linear over
$\mathbb{Z}$.

This is the form of the Kourovka Archive's resolution of M. Sapir's question whether the group is
linear.

## Sapir's group is linear over the integers

The group
$\langle a, b, t \mid a^t = ab, b^t = ba \rangle$, where $a^t = t^{-1} a t$, embeds in
$\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$ for some natural number $n$. That is, Kourovka Problem 17.108 has a
positive answer.

The Kourovka Archive answers 17.108 "Yes, this group is linear" (M. F. Hagen, letter of 6
August 2018): the group is the mapping torus of an irreducible atoroidal self-monomorphism of a
free group, so it is virtually special and hence $\mathbb{Z}$-linear by Theorem B of Hagen and
Wise (2016).
