## Pro-$p$ group

For a natural number $p$, a group $G$ with a topology is a pro-$p$ group
when $G$ is compact, Hausdorff and totally disconnected, and every open normal subgroup of $G$ has
index $p^k$ for some $k \in \mathbb{N}$.

## Free pro-$p$ group on given generators

Let $p$ and $d$ be natural numbers, $F$ a
topological group and $x_1, \dots, x_d \in F$. Then $F$ is a free pro-$p$ group on
$x_1, \dots, x_d$ when $F$ is a pro-$p$ group, the closure of the subgroup generated by
$x_1, \dots, x_d$ is all of $F$, and for every finite $p$-group $P$ and all $y_1, \dots, y_d \in P$
there is a group homomorphism $\varphi \colon F \to P$ with open kernel such that
$\varphi(x_i) = y_i$ for every $i$.

## Ribes's question on fixed subgroups of free pro-$p$ groups (Kourovka 12.70)

The statement
that for every prime $p$, every free pro-$p$ group $F$ on finitely many generators
$x_1, \dots, x_d$ and every continuous automorphism $\Delta \ne 1$ of $F$ with $\Delta^{p^k} = 1$
for some $k \in \mathbb{N}$, the fixed subgroup
$\mathrm{Fix}_F(\Delta) = \{z \in F \mid \Delta(z) = z\}$ has finite rank: there is a finite set
$S \subseteq \mathrm{Fix}_F(\Delta)$ such that the closure of the subgroup generated by $S$ equals
$\mathrm{Fix}_F(\Delta)$.

## Fixed subgroup of a free pro-p group is finitely generated

Resolved true: the Kourovka Archive answers 12.70 "Yes, it is finite" (C. Scheiderer, 1999);
moreover, the fixed points of a finite `p`-group of automorphisms of a free pro-`p` group form a
free factor (Herfort, Ribes and Zalesskii, 1999), which has finite rank when `F` does. Source: E. I.
Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The Kourovka Notebook, 21st
issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Archive of Solved Problems, Problem 12.70 (L. Ribes); resolution:
C. Scheiderer, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 127 (1999), no. 3, 695-700; W. N. Herfort, L. Ribes and P. A.
Zalesskii, Forum Math. 11 (1999), no. 1, 49-61, Kourovka Archive, Problem 12.70: Yes, it is finite
(Scheiderer); the fixed points of a finite p-group of automorphisms form a free factor
(Herfort-Ribes-Zalesskii).
