# Extracting exactly linear combined codewords

Let $1\leq r\leq K$ and $d\geq1$, split the points of $\mathbb{F}_q^{K+m}$ as $(x,y)$ with the $K$ combining coordinates first, and let $\{A^y_b\}$, $\{B^y_b\}$ be the two players' point measurements for the $r$-codeword test, with outcomes $b\in\mathbb{F}_q^r$. The *combined* point measurement at $(x,y)$ measures $A^y$ and answers $\sum_{j\leq r}x_jb_j$. Let $R^\textsc{A}$, $R^\textsc{B}$ be projective measurements of polynomials in the $K+m$ variables of individual degree at most $d$ which satisfy the three conclusions of the single-codeword contract against the combined point measurements with error $\delta$. Coarse-grain each by keeping the outcomes that are exactly linear in $x$, $g(x,y)=\sum_{i\leq K}x_ig_i(y)$, reading off $(g_1,\ldots,g_r)$, and sending every other outcome to the zero tuple. The results $G^\textsc{A}$, $G^\textsc{B}$ are projective measurements of complete $r$-tuples of polynomials of individual degree at most $d$ in $m$ variables; each evaluated at a uniform $y$ is consistent with the other player's point measurement with error $\delta+(K+m)d/q$, and $G^\textsc{A}$, $G^\textsc{B}$ are consistent with each other with error $\delta$. It does not select independent single-codeword measurements.

## Proof

Fix $y$, the original answer $b$ and the outcome $g$, and average over $x$ alone: the Born probabilities do not depend on $x$, since the original point measurement is asked $y$ and $R$ is asked nothing. If $g$ is exactly linear in $x$ and its tuple at $y$ is not $b$, then $x\mapsto g(x,y)$ and the combined answer are two different linear forms and agree with probability at most $Kd/q$. If $g$ is not exactly linear, some monomial with a forbidden $x$-part has a nonzero coefficient $Q_g$, a polynomial in $y$; where $Q_g(y)\neq0$ the same bound holds, and $Q_g(y)=0$ has probability at most $md/q$, paid once per outcome weighted by its total Born weight, because summing the Born probabilities over $b$ removes $y$. Both bounds are Lemma [Schwartz–Zippel](../part-05/lem-schwartz-zippel.md), applied to coefficient vectors through the polynomials they denote: a nonzero coefficient vector in $n$ variables, of individual degree at most $d$, vanishes at a uniform point with probability at most $nd/q$. The full-tuple conclusion is data processing: the same coarse-graining applied to both players can only increase agreement. The linear-forms case costs $Kd/q$ rather than the source's $1/q$, since one Schwartz–Zippel lemma serves all cases.
