## Extended Cunningham chain

For a natural number $p$, the extended Cunningham chain
generated by $p$ is the sequence $e_0, e_1, e_2, \ldots$ of natural numbers given by $e_0 = p$ and
$e_k = 2 e_{k-1} + 1$ for $k \ge 1$; in binary, $e_k$ is obtained from $p$ by appending $k$ digits
$1$ on the right.

The paper requires the generator $p$ to be prime; here $p$ may be any natural number, and results
that need primality assume it explicitly.

## Recurrence of the extended Cunningham chain

For all natural numbers $p$ and $k$, the
terms of the extended Cunningham chain generated by $p$ satisfy $e_{k+1} = 2 e_k + 1$.

## Closed form of the extended Cunningham chain

For all natural numbers $p$ and $k$, the
$k$-th term $e_k$ of the extended Cunningham chain generated by $p$ satisfies
$e_k + 1 = 2^k (p + 1)$, that is, $e_k = 2^k (p + 1) - 1$.

## Walking to infinity on primes by appending decimal digits

For every prime $p_0$ there is
a sequence of primes $p_0, p_1, p_2, \ldots$ starting at $p_0$ such that for every $i \ge 1$ there
are integers $n_i \ge 1$ and $k_i$ with $0 < k_i < 10^{n_i}$ and $p_i = 10^{n_i} p_{i-1} + k_i$;
that is, each term is obtained from the previous one by appending $n_i$ decimal digits on the
right.

## A power of two minus a much smaller positive number is not a power of two

Let $p$
and $k$ be natural numbers with $k \ge \lceil \log_2 (p + 1) \rceil + 2$. Then $2^k - (p + 1)$
is not a power of $2$: there is no natural number $m$ with $2^k - (p + 1) = 2^m$.

Under this hypothesis $2^k > p + 1$, so the subtraction is exact. The source assumes $p$ is
prime; the statement holds without that assumption.

## Extended Cunningham chains generated by primes contain arbitrarily long composite runs

Let $p$ be a prime and let $e_0 = p, e_1, e_2, \ldots$ be the extended Cunningham chain
generated by $p$. For every $n \ge 1$ there is $i \ge 1$ such that none of
$e_i, e_{i+1}, \ldots, e_{i+n-1}$ is prime.

Since $e_i \ge 2p + 1 \ge 5$ for $i \ge 1$, these terms are composite.

## No binary prime walk to infinity in steps of one or two digits

There is no sequence of
primes $p_0, p_1, p_2, \ldots$ such that for every $i \ge 0$ there are $n \in \{1, 2\}$ and a
natural number $k < 2^n$ with $p_{i+1} = 2^n p_i + k$. That is, starting from any prime, one
cannot walk to infinity on primes by appending one or two binary digits at a time on the right.

The appended blocks are the paper's $0, 1, 00, 01, 10, 11$ (a block may begin with $0$).

## Counting right-truncatable square-free numbers

For natural numbers $b$ and $k$,
$L_{b,k}$ is the number of right-truncatable square-free numbers with exactly $k$ digits in
base $b$: the number of natural numbers $x$ with $b^{k-1} \le x < b^k$ such that
$\lfloor x / b^{\ell} \rfloor$ is square-free for every $\ell \in \{0, \dots, k-1\}$. When
$k = 0$ the range is empty, so $L_{b,0} = 0$.

## Small counts of right-truncatable square-free numbers

Let $L_{b,k}$ denote the number of
right-truncatable square-free numbers with exactly $k$ digits in base $b$. Then $L_{2,1} = 1$,
$L_{2,2} = 2$, $L_{2,3} = 3$, $L_{2,4} = 5$ and $L_{10,1} = 6$.

## Bounds on the binary expected square-free walk length

Let $L_{2,k}$ denote the number of
right-truncatable square-free numbers with exactly $k$ digits in base $2$. The real series
$E_2 = \sum_{k \ge 1} L_{2,k} / 2^k$ satisfies
$636163720502 / 2^{38} \le E_2 \le 636163930777 / 2^{38}$.

This is the series the proof of Theorem 3.8 uses. The paper's Lemma 3.7 prints the denominators
$b^k - b^{k-1}$, which for $b = 2$ would double the sum.
