## Simple types

Given a type $B$ of base types, the simple types over $B$ are generated by
two rules: every base type $b \in B$ is a simple type, and for simple types $A$ and $C$ the
function type $A \Rightarrow C$ is a simple type.

## Typed de Bruijn variables of the simply typed lambda calculus

For a context $\Gamma$ (a
list of simple types) and a simple type $A$, the variables of type $A$ in $\Gamma$ are generated
by two rules: the variable $0$ is a variable of type $A$ in the context $A :: \Gamma$ obtained
by prepending $A$ to $\Gamma$, and if $x$ is a variable of type $A$ in $\Gamma$, then for every
simple type $B$ the successor of $x$ is a variable of type $A$ in $B :: \Gamma$.

Formalized by `Cslib.Languages.LambdaCalculus.Stlc.Var`, an inductive type declared in [CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/DeBruijn/Stlc/Syntax.lean, line 56](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/a3690dd2ad41e1ad4d871b062779ed7b0452800d/CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/DeBruijn/Stlc/Syntax.lean#L56). ## Intrinsically typed terms of the simply typed lambda calculus

Let $A$ and $B$ range over
simple types and $\Gamma$ over contexts (lists of simple types); write $A \Rightarrow B$ for the
function type from $A$ to $B$ and $A :: \Gamma$ for the context obtained by prepending $A$ to
$\Gamma$. The terms of type $A$ in context $\Gamma$ are generated by three rules: every variable
of type $A$ in $\Gamma$ is a term of type $A$ in context $\Gamma$; if $f$ is a term of type
$A \Rightarrow B$ and $a$ is a term of type $A$, both in context $\Gamma$, then the application
$f\,a$ is a term of type $B$ in context $\Gamma$; and if $t$ is a term of type $B$ in the context
$A :: \Gamma$, then the abstraction $\lambda t$ is a term of type $A \Rightarrow B$ in context
$\Gamma$.

[Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/ComputerScience/type-systems-and-lambda-calculi/simply-typed-lambda-calculus/index.html#tm).

## Renamings of simply typed contexts

For contexts $\Gamma$ and $\Delta$ (lists of simple
types), a renaming from $\Gamma$ to $\Delta$ assigns to every simple type $A$ and every variable
of type $A$ in $\Gamma$ a variable of type $A$ in $\Delta$.

## Lifting a renaming under a binder

Let $\rho$ be a renaming from a context $\Gamma$ to a
context $\Delta$ and let $A$ be a simple type, and write $A :: \Gamma$ for the context obtained
by prepending $A$ to $\Gamma$. The lift of $\rho$ is the renaming from $A :: \Gamma$ to
$A :: \Delta$ that sends the variable $0$ to $0$ and sends the successor of a variable $x$ of
$\Gamma$ to the successor of $\rho(x)$.

## Renaming a simply typed term

For a renaming $\rho$ from a context $\Gamma$ to a context
$\Delta$, the renaming $t[\rho]$ of a term $t$ of type $A$ in context $\Gamma$ is the term of
type $A$ in context $\Delta$ defined by recursion on $t$: $x[\rho] = \rho(x)$ for a variable
$x$, $(f\,a)[\rho] = f[\rho]\,a[\rho]$ for an application, and
$(\lambda s)[\rho] = \lambda (s[\rho^{\uparrow}])$ for an abstraction, where $\rho^{\uparrow}$
is the lift of $\rho$ under one binder.

## Weakening a simply typed term

For a term $t$ of type $A$ in a context $\Gamma$ and a
simple type $B$, write $B :: \Gamma$ for the context obtained by prepending $B$ to $\Gamma$. The
weakening of $t$ is the term of type $A$ in the context $B :: \Gamma$ obtained by renaming $t$
along the renaming that sends each variable of $\Gamma$ to its successor in $B :: \Gamma$.

## Lifting a substitution under a binder (simply typed lambda calculus)

Write $A :: \Gamma$
for the context $\Gamma$ extended by a newest variable of type $A$. Given a substitution $\sigma$
from a context $\Gamma$ to a context $\Delta$ and a type $A$, its lift is the substitution from
$A :: \Gamma$ to $A :: \Delta$ that sends the newest variable to the term consisting of the
newest variable alone, and sends the successor of each variable $x$ of $\Gamma$ to the weakening

## Simultaneous substitution (simply typed lambda calculus)

Given a substitution $\sigma$
from a context $\Gamma$ to a context $\Delta$, the result $t[\sigma]$ of applying $\sigma$ to a
term $t$ of type $A$ in context $\Gamma$ is the term of type $A$ in context $\Delta$ defined by
recursion on $t$: $x[\sigma] = \sigma(x)$ for a variable $x$,
$(f\,a)[\sigma] = f[\sigma]\,a[\sigma]$ for an application, and
$(\lambda u)[\sigma] = \lambda\,(u[\sigma'])$ for an abstraction with body $u$, where
$\sigma'$ is the lift of $\sigma$ under the binder. Because variables are de Bruijn indices, this
substitution is capture-avoiding.

Formalized by `Cslib.Languages.LambdaCalculus.Stlc.Tm.subst`, a definition declared in [CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/DeBruijn/Stlc/Substitution.lean, line 74](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/a3690dd2ad41e1ad4d871b062779ed7b0452800d/CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/DeBruijn/Stlc/Substitution.lean#L74). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/ComputerScience/type-systems-and-lambda-calculi/simply-typed-lambda-calculus/index.html#subst).

## Instantiating a binder body (simply typed lambda calculus)

Write $A :: \Gamma$ for the
context $\Gamma$ extended by a newest variable of type $A$. Given a term $t$ of type $B$ in
context $A :: \Gamma$ and a term $a$ of type $A$ in context $\Gamma$, the instantiation of $t$

## Lifting commutes with renaming substitution images

Let $\sigma$ be a substitution from
$\Gamma$ to $\Delta$ and $\rho$ a renaming from $\Delta$ to $\Xi$. Lift $\sigma$ under a binder
of type $A$ and rename each of its images by the lift of $\rho$ under the same binder. At every
variable $x$ of the context $\Gamma$ extended by a newest variable of type $A$, this gives the
same term as the lift under that binder of the substitution that renames each image of $\sigma$
by $\rho$.

## Composing a single substitution with a substitution

Let $a$ be a term of type $A$ in a
context $\Gamma$ and $\sigma$ a substitution from $\Gamma$ to $\Delta$, and write $a[\sigma]$ for
the result of applying $\sigma$ to $a$. The composite of the single substitution of $a$ for the
newest variable followed by $\sigma$ agrees, at every variable $x$ of the context $\Gamma$
extended by a newest variable of type $A$, with the extension of $\sigma$ that sends the newest
variable to $a[\sigma]$.

## Lifting followed by single substitution

Let $\sigma$ be a substitution from $\Gamma$ to
$\Delta$ and $a$ a term of type $A$ in $\Delta$. The composite of the lift of $\sigma$ under a
binder of type $A$ followed by the single substitution of $a$ for the newest variable agrees, at
every variable $x$ of the context $\Gamma$ extended by a newest variable of type $A$, with the
extension of $\sigma$ that sends the newest variable to $a$ and each older variable $y$ to
$\sigma(y)$.

## Single substitution cancels weakening

Let $t$ be a term of type $A$ in the typing context
$\Gamma$ and $a$ a term of type $B$ in context $\Gamma$. Weakening $t$ by a new newest variable of
type $B$ and then applying the substitution that replaces the newest variable by $a$ and leaves
every older variable unchanged gives back $t$.

## Instantiating a weakened term

For terms $b$ of type $B$ and $a$ of type $A$ in a context
$\Gamma$, weakening $b$ to the context $\Gamma$ extended by a newest variable of type $A$ and then
instantiating that newest variable by $a$ gives back $b$.

## Renaming commutes with instantiation

Let $\rho$ be a renaming from $\Gamma$ to $\Delta$,
$b$ a term of type $B$ in the context $\Gamma$ extended by a newest variable of type $A$, and $a$
a term of type $A$ in $\Gamma$. Renaming by $\rho$ the instantiation of the newest variable of $b$
by $a$ gives the instantiation of the newest variable of $b'$ by $a'$, where $b'$ is $b$ renamed
by the lift of $\rho$ under the binder and $a'$ is $a$ renamed by $\rho$.

## Substitution commutes with instantiation for intrinsically typed STLC terms

Let $\sigma$
be a substitution from the typing context $\Gamma$ to the typing context $\Delta$, $s$ a term of
type $B$ in the context $\Gamma$ extended by a newest variable of type $A$, and $a$ a term of type
$A$ in context $\Gamma$. Replacing the newest variable of $s$ by $a$ and then applying $\sigma$
gives the same term as applying the lift of $\sigma$ to $s$ and then replacing the newest variable
of the result by the term obtained by applying $\sigma$ to $a$.

## Substituting by an extended substitution, via lifting

Let $\sigma$ be a substitution from
the typing context $\Gamma$ to the typing context $\Delta$, $s$ a term of type $B$ in the context
$\Gamma$ extended by a newest variable of type $A$, and $a$ a term of type $A$ in context
$\Delta$. Applying to $s$ the extension of $\sigma$ that sends the newest variable to $a$ gives the
same term as applying the lift of $\sigma$ to $s$ and then replacing the newest variable of the
result by $a$.

## Instantiation is substitution by an extended identity

Let $b$ be a term of type $B$ in
the context $\Gamma$ extended by a newest variable of type $A$, and $a$ a term of type $A$ in
$\Gamma$. Instantiating the newest variable of $b$ by $a$ gives the same term as applying to $b$
the substitution that sends the newest variable to $a$ and each older variable to itself, that
is, the extension of the identity substitution on $\Gamma$ by $a$.
