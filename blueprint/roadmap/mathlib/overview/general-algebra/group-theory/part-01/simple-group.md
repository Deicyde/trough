# Simple group

A **simple group** is a nontrivial [group](group.md) with no
[normal subgroups](normal-subgroup.md) other than the trivial subgroup and
the group itself. Normal subgroups are exactly the kernels of homomorphisms, so a
simple group has no quotient other than itself and the trivial group: every
homomorphism out of it is either injective or sends everything to the identity.
Simple groups play the role for finite groups that prime numbers play for
integers. Every finite group is assembled from simple pieces, and the
classification of finite simple groups determines all
the finite ones.

## Definition

A group $G$ is simple if it has more than one element and its only normal
subgroups are $\{e\}$ and $G$, where $e$ is the identity element
([the definition in Mathlib](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Subgroup/Simple.html#IsSimpleGroup)). Equivalently, $G$ has exactly
two normal subgroups. The requirement that $G$ be nontrivial excludes the
one-element group, just as $1$ is not counted as a prime.

Simplicity is a property of the abstract group: a group
[isomorphic to a simple group is simple](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Subgroup/Simple.html#MulEquiv.isSimpleGroup_congr).

## Examples

- **Groups of prime order.** By [Lagrange's theorem](../part-02/lagranges-theorem.md)
  a group of prime order $p$ has no subgroups at all besides $\{e\}$ and itself,
  so [every group of prime order is simple](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Cyclic.html#isSimpleGroup_of_prime_card).
  These groups are [cyclic](cyclic-group.md), isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$.
- **Alternating groups.** The [alternating group](../part-02/alternating-group.md) $A_n$
  of even permutations of $n$ letters
  [is simple when $n \ge 5$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Alternating/Simple.html#alternatingGroup.isSimpleGroup); see
  [the article on that theorem](../part-02/alternating-groups-on-5-letters-or-more-are-simp.md). The
  group $A_5$, of order $60$, is the smallest simple group that is not abelian.
- **Projective special linear groups.** For a field $F$, the projective special
  linear group $\mathrm{PSL}_n(F)$ is simple for every $n \ge 2$, except for
  $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{F}_2)$ and $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{F}_3)$. For $n = 2$
  the condition is that $F$ have a nonzero element whose square is not $1$, which
  every field other than $\mathbb{F}_2$ and $\mathbb{F}_3$ has, and
  [under that condition $\mathrm{PSL}_2(F)$ is simple](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Projectivization/PSL/PSL2.html#Matrix.ProjectiveSpecialLinearGroup.rank_two_simple').
  Over finite fields these groups are among the finite groups of Lie type and
  give infinitely many finite simple groups; over infinite fields they give
  infinite simple groups.

Many familiar groups are not simple. The symmetric group $S_n$ for $n \ge 3$
contains $A_n$ as a normal subgroup of index $2$, the group $A_4$ contains a
normal subgroup of order $4$, and the integers under addition contain the normal
subgroup of even integers.

## Properties

- **Abelian simple groups.** In an abelian group every subgroup is normal, so an
  abelian simple group has no proper nontrivial subgroups at all. Such a group
  is generated by any element other than $e$, so it is cyclic, and a cyclic
  group with no proper nontrivial subgroups has prime order. Hence
  [an abelian group is simple exactly when its order is a prime](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Cyclic.html#CommGroup.is_simple_iff_prime_card);
  in particular [an abelian simple group is finite](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Cyclic.html#IsSimpleGroup.finite),
  since [its order is prime](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Cyclic.html#IsSimpleGroup.prime_card). The
  [classification of abelian simple groups](../part-02/classification-of-abelian-simple-groups.md)
  is therefore the list of cyclic groups of prime order.
- **Homomorphic images.** If $f \colon G \to H$ is a surjective homomorphism from a
  simple group onto a nontrivial group, then the kernel of $f$ is a proper normal
  subgroup, hence trivial, so $f$ is an isomorphism and
  [$H$ is simple](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Subgroup/Simple.html#IsSimpleGroup.isSimpleGroup_of_surjective).
- **Solvability.** The commutator subgroup of a simple group is normal, so it is
  either trivial or the whole group. Hence
  [a simple group is solvable if and only if it is abelian](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Solvable.html#IsSimpleGroup.comm_iff_isSolvable).
  A simple [nilpotent group](nilpotent-group.md) is likewise abelian, and so
  cyclic of prime order.
- **Prime power order.** A finite group of order $p^n$ with $n \ge 1$, for a prime $p$, has a
  nontrivial center, which is a normal subgroup. Such a group is therefore
  [simple only when its order is $p$ itself](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/PGroup.html#IsPGroup.isSimpleGroup_iff_card_eq).
- **Simple quotients.** By the correspondence between subgroups of a
  [quotient group](quotient-group.md) $G/N$ and subgroups of
  $G$ containing $N$, the quotient $G/N$ is simple exactly when $N$ is a proper
  normal subgroup that is maximal among the proper normal subgroups of $G$.
  Mathlib records the case in which $N$ is maximal among all proper subgroups:
  [the quotient by such a normal subgroup is simple](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/QuotientGroup/Simple.html#Group.isSimpleGroup_of_isCoatom).

## Composition series

A composition series of a group $G$ is a chain of subgroups
$$\{e\} = G_0 \le G_1 \le \cdots \le G_k = G$$
in which each $G_{i-1}$ is normal in $G_i$ and each quotient $G_i / G_{i-1}$ is
simple. Every finite group has one, obtained by repeatedly choosing maximal
normal subgroups. The [Jordan–Hölder theorem](../../../../thousand-plus/algebra/20-group-theory-and-generalizations/jordan-holder-theorem.md)
says that the list of quotients, called the composition factors, does not
depend on the series up to order and isomorphism. In this sense the finite
simple groups are the building blocks of all finite groups, although groups
with the same composition factors need not be isomorphic: the cyclic group of
order $4$ and the Klein four-group both have two factors of order $2$.

A finite group is solvable precisely when all of its composition factors are
cyclic of prime order. The Feit–Thompson theorem, that
every finite group of odd order is solvable, is equivalent to the statement that
every nonabelian finite simple group has even order.

## In Mathlib

Mathlib defines simplicity as a property that a group may or may not have,
rather than as a new kind of structure. Nontriviality is part of the definition,
so the one-element group is not simple, and the defining property is available
for any simple group as the statement that
[each of its normal subgroups is trivial or the whole group](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Subgroup/Simple.html#Subgroup.Normal.eq_bot_or_eq_top). An additive version covers groups
written additively, such as $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$. Statements about orders
use a cardinality that is $0$ for an infinite group; since $0$ is not prime, the
characterization of abelian simple groups by prime order also shows that no
infinite abelian group is simple. The simplicity of alternating groups is stated
for the alternating group on any finite set with at least five elements.
Mathlib proves the simplicity of $\mathrm{PSL}_2(F)$ through
[Iwasawa's criterion](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/GroupAction/Iwasawa.html#MulAction.IwasawaStructure.isSimpleGroup), in a form
slightly more general than the usual one: a nontrivial group equal to its own
commutator subgroup is simple if it acts faithfully on a set, every normal
subgroup acting nontrivially acts transitively, and each point carries a
commutative subgroup, moved by conjugation along with the point, with these
subgroups together generating the group. Its result on projective special linear
groups covers only $n = 2$.
