## Exponent 4 detected on boundedly generated subgroups (Kourovka 13.10)

The proposition
that there is a function $f : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ with the following property: for every
$k \in \mathbb{N}$, every group $G$ whose $k$-th derived subgroup $G^{(k)}$ is trivial (a soluble
group of derived length at most $k$), and every generating set $A$ of $G$, if $x^4 = 1$ for every
element $x$ of every subgroup generated by a finite subset of $A$ with at most $f(k)$ elements,
then $x^4 = 1$ for every $x \in G$. This is Kourovka Notebook Problem 13.10 (V. V. Bludov).

## Exponent 4 is not detected on boundedly generated subgroups

There is no function
$f : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ with the following property: for every $k \in \mathbb{N}$, every
group $G$ whose $k$-th derived subgroup $G^{(k)}$ is trivial, and every generating set $A$ of $G$,
if $x^4 = 1$ for every element $x$ of every subgroup generated by a finite subset of $A$ with at
most $f(k)$ elements, then $x^4 = 1$ for every $x \in G$. This is the negative answer to Kourovka
Notebook Problem 13.10 (V. V. Bludov), given by G. S. Deryabina and A. N. Krasil'nikov (2003).
