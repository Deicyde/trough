## Hall subgroups (order coprime to index)

A subgroup $H$ of a group $G$ is a Hall subgroup
when its order $|H|$ is coprime to its index $|G : H|$.

## Pronormality in a subgroup

Let $H$ and $K$ be subgroups of a group $G$. Then $H$ is
pronormal in $K$ when for every $g \in K$ there is an element $x$ of the subgroup
$\langle H, gHg^{-1} \rangle$ generated by $H$ and $gHg^{-1}$ such that $xHx^{-1} = gHg^{-1}$,
that is, $H$ and its conjugate $gHg^{-1}$ are conjugate by an element of
$\langle H, gHg^{-1} \rangle$.

## Vdovin–Revin question on Hall subgroups and their normal closures

This is the statement
that for every finite group $G$ and every Hall subgroup $H$ of $G$ (a subgroup whose order $|H|$
is coprime to its index $|G : H|$), $H$ is pronormal in its normal closure $\langle H^G \rangle$,
the smallest normal subgroup of $G$ containing $H$: for every $g \in \langle H^G \rangle$ there is
an element $x$ of the subgroup $\langle H, gHg^{-1} \rangle$ generated by $H$ and $gHg^{-1}$ with
$xHx^{-1} = gHg^{-1}$.

This is Kourovka Notebook Problem 18.32 (E. P. Vdovin, D. O. Revin).

## Hall subgroups need not be pronormal in their normal closure

There exist a finite group
$G$ and a Hall subgroup $H$ of $G$ (that is, the order $|H|$ is coprime to the index $|G : H|$)
such that $H$ is not pronormal in its normal closure $\langle H^G \rangle$, the smallest normal
subgroup of $G$ containing $H$: for some $g \in \langle H^G \rangle$, no element $x$ of the subgroup
$\langle H, gHg^{-1} \rangle$ generated by $H$ and $gHg^{-1}$ satisfies $xHx^{-1} = gHg^{-1}$.

This answers Kourovka Notebook Problem 18.32 (E. P. Vdovin, D. O. Revin) in the negative; the
Kourovka Archive records the answer "No, not always" (M. N. Nesterov, 2017).
