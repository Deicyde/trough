# The sectors of a gauge field theory

> The covariant field algebra is spanned by sectors labelled by the kinds of field they contain.

## i. Overview

> The covariant field algebra `T.LocalCovFieldAlgebra` of a field datum `T` is generated by three
> kinds of covariant expressions, `GaugeFieldData.FieldKind`.
>
> - Gauge. The field strength `F_μν` and its covariant derivatives `∇_l F_μν`.
> - Scalar. The bosonic matter fields `φ`, their conjugates `φ†`, and their covariant
>   derivatives `∇_l φ` and `∇_l φ†`.
> - Fermion. The fermionic matter fields `ψ`, their conjugates `ψ̄`, and their covariant
>   derivatives `∇_l ψ` and `∇_l ψ̄`.
>
> A product of generators contains a set of kinds. For a set `A` of kinds, the sector `T.sector A`
> is the span of the products whose set of kinds is exactly `A`. Two such products multiply to a
> third, so each sector is a non-unital subalgebra. The empty set gives the constants `ℂ • 1`.
> The seven nonempty sets give the seven named sectors, from the pure gauge sector, which
> contains `tr F_μν F^μν`, to the mixed sector, which contains the dipole operators
> `ψ̄ σ^μν φ F_μν ψ`. The gauge field enters minimal couplings only through covariant derivatives,
> so the kinetic terms `ψ̄ γ^μ ∇_μ ψ` and `(∇_μ φ)† ∇^μ φ` lie in the fermion and scalar sectors.
>
> The gauge and Lorentz groups preserve every sector. A jet carries each generator to a generator
> of the same kind, and a Lorentz transformation carries it to a combination of generators of the
> same kind. Both act by algebra maps, so a product of generators goes to a combination of
> products with the same set of kinds.
>
> Refining by mass weight, `T.sectorMassWeight A w` is the span of the products of kinds `A` and
> total weight `w`. For a mass-weight polynomial `P` it is the intersection of the sector with the
> weight-`w` piece, and the weight-`w` component of an element of a sector lies in this
> intersection. Each weight piece is the join of the weight pieces of the sectors. A lower bound on
> the weights of the generators of each kind gives a lower bound on the weights at which a sector
> can be nonzero, and a sector of two kinds splits into products of the pieces of the two kinds.
>
> Every element of the covariant field algebra is a constant plus a sum of one element from each
> of the seven nonempty sectors. Under the conditions of the descent theorem, an element invariant
> under the gauge and Lorentz groups has such a decomposition with every component invariant. The
> proof applies a Reynolds element for the joint action to a decomposition. The Reynolds element
> fixes the invariant element, and it is a combination of group elements, so it keeps every
> component in its sector. Projecting the components onto weights keeps them in their sectors and
> invariant, so an invariant of weight `w`, or of weight at most `w`, decomposes into invariant
> components of the same weights.
>
> The decomposition is not unique. The covariant field algebra is not free on its generators. The
> Ricci identity `[∇_μ, ∇_ν] ψ = F_μν ψ` and the Bianchi identity relate products of different
> kinds, so the sectors span the covariant field algebra but their sum is not direct.

> component keeps an element in its sector and commutes with both groups, and an element of the
> filtration at `w` is the sum of its components of weight at most `w`. The argument uses only
> these projections, never an expansion of `x` in products of generators, which would not be
> unique.

> Under `IsMassWeightPoly P`, an invariant element of mass weight at most `w` is a sum of one

> invariant under the gauge and Lorentz groups.
