## Relatively free group

A group $G$ is relatively free if it has a family $(x_i)_{i \in I}$
of elements that generates $G$ such that for every family $(y_i)_{i \in I}$ of elements of $G$
there is an endomorphism $\varphi$ of $G$ with $\varphi(x_i) = y_i$ for all $i \in I$.

Equivalently, $G$ is free on $(x_i)$ in the variety of groups it generates.

## Values of a word

For a word $v$ in the free group on $n$ generators and a group $G$, the
set of values of $v$ on $G$ is the set of elements $v(g_1, \dots, g_n)$ obtained by substituting
elements $g_1, \dots, g_n \in G$ for the generators, that is, the set of images of $v$ under all
homomorphisms from that free group to $G$.

## Hall's verbal finiteness conjecture for relatively free groups (Kourovka 12.31)

The
statement that for every relatively free group $G$, every $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and every word $v$ in
the free group on $n$ generators, if $v$ takes only finitely many values on $G$, then the verbal
subgroup $v(G)$ of $G$ generated by these values is finite.

[Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/GroupTheory/varieties-and-equations/hall-s-verbal-conjectures/index.html#relatively-free-finite-word-values-finit).

## Hall's conjecture fails for relatively free groups

Hall's verbal finiteness conjecture for
relatively free groups is false: there are a relatively free group $G$, a natural number $n$ and a
word $v$ in the free group on $n$ generators such that $v$ takes only finitely many values on $G$
but the verbal subgroup $v(G)$ of $G$ generated by these values is infinite.
