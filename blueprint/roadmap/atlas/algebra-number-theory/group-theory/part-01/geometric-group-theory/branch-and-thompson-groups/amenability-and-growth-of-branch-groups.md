## Vertices of a spherically homogeneous rooted tree (Kourovka 15.14 version)

For a sequence
$m$ of natural numbers, the vertices of the spherically homogeneous rooted tree with branching
sequence $m$ are the finite words $l_0 l_1 \cdots l_{k-1}$ of natural numbers with $l_i < m(i)$ for
every $i < k$. The length of a word is its level and its prefixes are its ancestors.

## Rigid vertex stabilizers (Kourovka 15.14 version)

Let $G$ be a group acting by permutations
$\rho$ on the vertices of the rooted tree with branching sequence $m$, and let $v$ be a vertex. The
rigid vertex stabilizer of $v$ is the set of elements $g \in G$ whose support lies in the subtree
rooted at $v$: $\rho(g)$ fixes every vertex $w$ of which $v$ is not a prefix.

## Rigid level stabilizers (Kourovka 15.14 version)

Let $G$ be a group acting by permutations
$\rho$ on the vertices of the rooted tree with branching sequence $m$, and let $n$ be a natural
number. The $n$-th rigid level stabilizer of $\rho$ is the subgroup of $G$ generated by the rigid
vertex stabilizers of all vertices at level $n$, the words of length $n$.

## Branch groups (Kourovka 15.14 version)

A group $G$ is a branch group (Kourovka 15.12) if
there are a sequence $m$ of natural numbers with $m(i) \ge 2$ for every $i$ and an action $\rho$ of
$G$ by permutations of the vertices of the spherically homogeneous rooted tree with branching
sequence $m$ such that: $\rho$ is faithful; each $\rho(g)$ is a tree automorphism, that is, for all
vertices $v$ and $w$, $v$ is a prefix of $w$ if and only if $\rho(g)(v)$ is a prefix of
$\rho(g)(w)$; the action is level-transitive, that is, for any two vertices $v$ and $w$ of the same
level some $g \in G$ has $\rho(g)(v) = w$; and for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$ the $n$-th rigid level
stabilizer of $\rho$ has finite index in $G$.

## Growth rate of a finitely generated group

The growth rate `ω(G, P) = limsup β_P(n)^(1/n)` of a finite generating family `P`, where
`β_P(n)` is the number of elements that are products of at most `n` generators and their
inverses.

## Groups of exponential growth

A group $G$ has exponential growth when it is finitely
generated and every finite generating family $P$ of $G$ has growth rate $\omega(G, P) > 1$.

## Groups containing a free subgroup of rank two

A group $G$ contains a free subgroup of
rank $2$ when there is an injective group homomorphism from the free group $F_2$ on two
generators into $G$.

## Non-amenable branch groups exist (Kourovka 15.14(a))

There is a finitely generated branch
group that is not amenable.

The Kourovka Archive answers 15.14(a) "Such groups do exist" (Sidki and Wilson): their finitely
generated branch groups contain free subgroups of rank $2$ and so are non-amenable.
