## Types of the simply typed lambda calculus with references

Fix a type $B$ of base types.
The types over $B$ are generated by the grammar
$T ::= b \mid \mathsf{Unit} \mid T_1 \to T_2 \mid \mathsf{Ref}\,T$ with $b \in B$: base types,
the unit type, function types, and types $\mathsf{Ref}\,T$ of reference cells holding values of
type $T$.

## Terms of the simply typed lambda calculus with references

Fix a type $B$ of base types.
The terms are generated by: variables, given as de Bruijn indices $x \in \mathbb{N}$;
abstractions $\lambda{:}T.\,t$, where $T$ is a type over $B$ and $t$ is a term in which index $0$
refers to the abstraction's parameter; applications $t_1\,t_2$; the unit value $\mathsf{unit}$;
store locations $l \in \mathbb{N}$; allocations $\mathsf{ref}\,t$; dereferences $!t$; and
assignments $t_1 := t_2$.

Store locations arise during evaluation, not in source programs.

## Lifting a renaming under a binder in the lambda calculus with references

Given a
renaming $\rho : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ of de Bruijn indices, its lift is the renaming that
sends $0$ to $0$ and each $x + 1$ to $\rho(x) + 1$.

## Renaming the variables of a term

Given a renaming $\rho : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ of
de Bruijn indices, renaming a term by $\rho$ replaces each variable $x$ by $\rho(x)$; renames
the body of an abstraction $\lambda{:}T.\,t$ by the lift of $\rho$ (which sends $0$ to $0$ and
each $x + 1$ to $\rho(x) + 1$), keeping the type annotation $T$; leaves the unit value and store
locations unchanged; and acts componentwise on applications, allocations, dereferences and
assignments.

## Lifting a substitution under a binder

Given a substitution $\sigma$ assigning a term to
each de Bruijn index, its lift is the substitution that sends $0$ to the variable $0$ and each
$x + 1$ to the term $\sigma(x)$ renamed by $n \mapsto n + 1$, that is, with every free variable

## Parallel substitution in a term

## Substitution of a term for variable zero

For terms $t$ and $s$, the instantiation of $t$
by $s$ applies to $t$ the parallel substitution that sends the de Bruijn index $0$ to $s$ and
each index $x + 1$ to the variable $x$. Thus the free occurrences of variable $0$ in $t$ are
replaced by $s$, with the free variables of $s$ raised by the number of binders crossed, and
every other free variable of $t$ is lowered by one.

This is the substitution of β-reduction, written $\uparrow^{-1}([0 \mapsto \uparrow^1 s]\,t)$ in
TAPL Section 6.3; TAPL's $[0 \mapsto s]\,t$ alone does not lower the other free variables.

Formalized by `Cslib.Languages.LambdaCalculus.References.Term.instantiate`, a definition declared in [CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/References/Syntax.lean, line 120](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/035e1808ca0c8983682b74153a02628d616d77ce/CSLibExt/Languages/LambdaCalculus/References/Syntax.lean#L120). ## Store

A store $\mu$ is a finite list of terms; each location $l$ less than the length of
$\mu$ holds the term $\mu[l]$, the entry of $\mu$ at index $l$.

## One-step evaluation with references

One step of call-by-value evaluation
$t \mid \mu \to t' \mid \mu'$, from a term $t$ with a store $\mu$ to a term $t'$ with a store
$\mu'$, is the least relation closed under the following rules, in which $v$, $v_1$ and $v_2$ are
values. If $t \mid \mu \to t' \mid \mu'$, then $t\,t_2 \mid \mu \to t'\,t_2 \mid \mu'$,
$v_1\,t \mid \mu \to v_1\,t' \mid \mu'$,
$\mathsf{ref}\,t \mid \mu \to \mathsf{ref}\,t' \mid \mu'$, $!t \mid \mu \to {!t'} \mid \mu'$,
$t := t_2 \mid \mu \to t' := t_2 \mid \mu'$ and $v_1 := t \mid \mu \to v_1 := t' \mid \mu'$. The
application $(\lambda{:}T.\,s)\,v_2$ with store $\mu$ steps, with $\mu$ unchanged, to the result of
substituting $v_2$ for variable $0$ in $s$. The allocation $\mathsf{ref}\,v$ with store $\mu$ steps
to the location $n$, where $n$ is the length of $\mu$, and the store becomes $\mu$ with $v$
appended at the end. If $\mu$ has an entry $u$ at location $l$, then $!l \mid \mu \to u \mid \mu$.
If $l$ is less than the length of $\mu$, then $l := v \mid \mu \to \mathsf{unit} \mid \mu'$, where
$\mu'$ is $\mu$ with its entry at $l$ replaced by $v$.

## Multi-step evaluation with references

The relation $t \mid \mu \to^* t' \mid \mu'$ holds
when the configuration $(t, \mu)$ reaches $(t', \mu')$ in zero or more evaluation steps; that is,
it is the reflexive-transitive closure of one-step evaluation on pairs of a term and a store.

## Values do not step

If $v$ is a value, then there are no stores $\mu$, $\mu'$ and term $t'$
such that the configuration $(v, \mu)$ steps to $(t', \mu')$: values are normal forms.

## Determinism of evaluation with references

If a configuration $(t, \mu)$ steps to
$(t_1, \mu_1)$ and also to $(t_2, \mu_2)$, then $t_1 = t_2$ and $\mu_1 = \mu_2$.

This relies on allocating each fresh location at the end of the store, at the index equal to the
length of $\mu$; with TAPL's arbitrary choice of fresh location only the result up to renaming of
locations is unique.

## Renaming respects pointwise equality

## Instantiating a shifted term

For all terms $t$ and $s$ of the simply typed lambda calculus
with references, shift every free de Bruijn variable of $t$ up by one (rename by
$x \mapsto x + 1$), then instantiate the result with $s$, replacing variable $0$ by $s$ and
lowering every other variable $x + 1$ to $x$. The outcome is $t$.

## Typing judgment for the lambda calculus with references

The judgment
$\Gamma \mid S \vdash t : T$ (TAPL Figure 13-1), where the context $\Gamma$ lists the types of
the free de Bruijn variables (index $0$ first) and the store typing $S$ lists the types of the
locations $0, 1, \ldots$, is the least relation closed under the following rules, in which
$t : T$ abbreviates $\Gamma \mid S \vdash t : T$. A variable $x$ has type $T$ if $\Gamma$
assigns $T$ to index $x$. If $t$ has type $T_2$ in the context $\Gamma$ extended by $T_1$ at
index $0$ (with the same $S$), then $\lambda{:}T_1.\, t : T_1 \to T_2$. If $t_1 : T_1 \to T_2$
and $t_2 : T_1$, then $t_1\, t_2 : T_2$. The term $\mathtt{unit}$ has type $\mathrm{Unit}$. A
location $l$ has type $\mathrm{Ref}\, T$ if $S$ assigns $T$ to $l$. If $t : T$, then
$\mathrm{ref}\, t : \mathrm{Ref}\, T$. If $t : \mathrm{Ref}\, T$, then $!t : T$. If
$t_1 : \mathrm{Ref}\, T$ and $t_2 : T$, then $t_1 := t_2$ has type $\mathrm{Unit}$.

## Well-typed stores

Let $\Gamma$ be a variable context and $S$ a store typing. A store
$\mu$, a list of terms whose entry at index $l$ is the contents of location $l$, is well typed
under $\Gamma$ and $S$, written $\Gamma \mid S \vdash \mu$, when $\mu$ and $S$ have the same
length and, for every location $l$ less than this length, the entry of $\mu$ at $l$ is a value
that has, under $\Gamma$ and $S$, the type that $S$ assigns to $l$.

Stores here are lists of terms rather than of values, so each entry is required to be a value.

## Well-typed stores hold a typed value at every location

Let $\Gamma$ be a typing
context, $S$ a store typing, and $\mu$ a store (a list of terms, whose entry at index $l$ is
the contents of location $l$) that is well typed, $\Gamma \mid S \vdash \mu$. If $S$ assigns
type $T$ to location $l$, then $\mu$ has an entry $v$ at location $l$, $v$ is a value, and
$\Gamma \mid S \vdash v : T$.

## Landin's knot

The term

$$
(\lambda r{:}\mathrm{Ref}\,(\mathrm{Unit}\to\mathrm{Unit}).\ (\lambda y{:}\mathrm{Unit}.\ (!r)\,
\mathrm{unit})\ (r := \lambda x{:}\mathrm{Unit}.\ (!r)\,x))
\ (\mathrm{ref}\,(\lambda x{:}\mathrm{Unit}.\ x))
$$

of the simply typed lambda calculus with references.

It allocates a cell $r$ holding the identity on $\mathrm{Unit}$, overwrites it with a function that
calls whatever $r$ holds, and then calls the contents of $r$ on $\mathrm{unit}$: a recursive knot
tied through the store.

## The first four steps of Landin's knot

Write $\ell_0$ for store location $0$ and $[t]$

$\mathrm{id} = \lambda x{:}\mathrm{Unit}.\ x$ and $b_0 = \lambda x{:}\mathrm{Unit}.\ (!\ell_0)\,x$.

$[\mathrm{id}]$; that term with store $[\mathrm{id}]$ steps to
$(\lambda y{:}\mathrm{Unit}.\ (!\ell_0)\,\mathrm{unit})\ (\ell_0 := b_0)$ with store
$[\mathrm{id}]$; that term with store $[\mathrm{id}]$ steps to
$(\lambda y{:}\mathrm{Unit}.\ (!\ell_0)\,\mathrm{unit})\ \mathrm{unit}$ with store $[b_0]$; and
that term with store $[b_0]$ steps to $(!\ell_0)\,\mathrm{unit}$ with store $[b_0]$.

## Configurations on the evaluation path of Landin's knot

## Embedding of raw STLC terms into the calculus with references

Fix a type $B$ of base
types. The embedding $\iota$ sends a raw (extrinsically typed, binder-annotated) term of the
simply typed lambda calculus over $B$ to a term of the calculus with references over $B$, by

assignments.

## Substitution lemma for the lambda calculus with references

Let $\Gamma$ be a variable
context and $S$ a store typing. If a term $t$ has type $T$ under $S$ and the context that extends

## Retyping a well-typed store under an extended store typing

Let $\Gamma$ be a variable
context, let $S$ and $S'$ be store typings such that $S$ is a prefix of $S'$, and let $\mu$ be a
store that is well typed under $\Gamma$ and $S$. Then $\mu$ and $S$ have the same length, and for
every location $l$ less than this length, the entry of $\mu$ at $l$ is a value that has, under
$\Gamma$ and $S'$, the type that $S$ assigns to $l$.

## Allocation preserves store typing

Let $\Gamma$ be a variable context, $S$ a store
typing and $\mu$ a store that is well typed under $\Gamma$ and $S$. If $v$ is a value of type
$T$ under $\Gamma$ and $S$, then the store obtained by appending $v$ at the end of $\mu$ is well
typed under $\Gamma$ and the store typing obtained by appending $T$ at the end of $S$.

## Assignment preserves store typing

Let the store $\mu$ be well typed under the variable
context $\Gamma$ and the store typing $S$, and let $l$ be a location to which $S$ assigns the
type $T$. If $v$ is a value of type $T$ under $\Gamma$ and $S$, then the store obtained from
$\mu$ by overwriting the entry at $l$ with $v$ is well typed under $\Gamma$ and $S$.

This is TAPL Lemma 13.5.5.

## Preservation for the lambda calculus with references

Let $\Gamma$ be a variable context
and $S$ a store typing. If a term $t$ has type $T$ under $\Gamma$ and $S$, the store $\mu$ is
well typed under $\Gamma$ and $S$, and $t$ with store $\mu$ takes one evaluation step to $t'$
with store $\mu'$, then there is a store typing $S'$ having $S$ as a prefix such that $t'$ has
type $T$ under $\Gamma$ and $S'$ and $\mu'$ is well typed under $\Gamma$ and $S'$.

This is TAPL Theorem 13.5.3, with the extension of store typings taken as the prefix order on
lists.

## Type safety

Let $S$ be a store typing, $T$ a type, $t$ a term of type $T$ in the
empty variable context under $S$, and $\mu$ a store that is well typed under $S$ in the empty
context. If the configuration $(t, \mu)$ evaluates in zero or more steps to $(t', \mu')$, then
$t'$ is a value or $(t', \mu')$ takes a step to some configuration $(t'', \mu'')$. So a closed
term that is well typed in a well-typed store never reaches a stuck configuration.

## Type safety from the empty store

A closed term typed under the empty store typing, run
from the empty store, never gets stuck: every term it reaches in zero or more steps, together
with the store reached alongside it, is a value or can take a further step.
