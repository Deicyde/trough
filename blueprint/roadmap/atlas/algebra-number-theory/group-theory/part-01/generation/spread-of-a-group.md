## Spread at least $k$

A group $G$ has spread at least $k$, for a natural number $k$, when for
every family $x_1, \dots, x_k$ of nontrivial elements of $G$, not necessarily distinct, there is an
element $y \in G$ such that the subgroup $\langle x_i, y \rangle$ generated by $x_i$ and $y$ is
all of $G$ for every $i$.

## Spread exactly $k$

A group $G$ has spread exactly $k$, for a natural number $k$, when $G$
has spread at least $k$ and, for every natural number $m > k$, $G$ does not have spread at
least $m$.

## There is a group of spread exactly one

There exists a group $G$ of spread exactly $1$:
for every nontrivial $x \in G$ there is $y \in G$ with $\langle x, y \rangle = G$, but for every
natural number $m > 1$ there are nontrivial elements $x_1, \dots, x_m \in G$ such that no single
$y \in G$ satisfies $\langle x_i, y \rangle = G$ for all $i$. This answers Kourovka Problem 21.38
affirmatively.

The Nilradical note A group of spread one (17 September 2026, Kourovka Notebook site), Theorem 1,
shows that the subgroup $\{f \in F : f'(0^+) = f'(1^-)\}$ of Thompson's group $F$ is infinite and
has spread exactly one. The note is not refereed; the Kourovka Notebook (arXiv:1401.0300v49) marks
Problem 21.38 solved (AI solution confirmed by S. Corson).

## Spread of a group

Let $G$ be a group. The spread $s(G)$ of $G$ is the supremum in
$\mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$ of the natural numbers $k$ such that for any non-trivial elements
$x_1, \dots, x_k$ of $G$ (not necessarily distinct) there exists $y \in G$ such that the
subgroup $\langle x_i, y \rangle$ generated by $x_i$ and $y$ is all of $G$ for every $i$. In
particular $s(G) = \infty$ when there is no largest such $k$.

## Burness–Guralnick–Harper spread theorem

Let $G$ be a finite group. Then the spread $s(G)$
is at least $2$ if and only if every proper quotient of $G$ is cyclic, that is, $G/N$ is cyclic
for every non-trivial normal subgroup $N$ of $G$.
