## Right-invariant linear order

A linear order $\le$ on a group $G$ is right-invariant (a
right order) when $a \le b$ implies $ac \le bc$ for all $a, b, c \in G$.

## Right-relatively convex subgroup

A subgroup $H$ of a group $G$ is right-relatively convex
when there is a right-invariant linear order $\le$ on $G$ for which $H$ is convex: whenever
$h_1 \le g \le h_2$ with $h_1, h_2 \in H$ and $g \in G$, also $g \in H$.

## Sublattice question for right-relatively convex subgroups (Kourovka Problem 21.147, non-abelian case)

**Sublattice question for right-relatively convex subgroups (Kourovka Problem 21.147,
non-abelian case).** The statement that for every non-abelian right-orderable group $G$ and all
right-relatively convex subgroups $H$ and $K$ of $G$, both the intersection $H \cap K$ and the
join $H \vee K$ (the subgroup generated by $H$ and $K$) are right-relatively convex; that is, the
right-relatively convex subgroups of $G$ form a sublattice of the lattice of subgroups of $G$.

The question is restricted to non-abelian groups, as the proposers meant.

Formalized by `MathlibExt.GroupTheory.RightRelativelyConvexSublatticeKourovka21Problem147Wanted.rightRelativelyConvexSublattice`, a definition declared in [WantedExt/GroupTheory/RightRelativelyConvexSublatticeKourovka21Problem147Wanted.lean, line 61](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/035e1808ca0c8983682b74153a02628d616d77ce/WantedExt/GroupTheory/RightRelativelyConvexSublatticeKourovka21Problem147Wanted.lean#L61). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/GroupTheory/orderable-groups/relatively-convex-subgroups/index.html#right-relatively-convex-sublattice).

## Negative answer to Kourovka Problem 21.147 for non-abelian groups

It is not the case that for
every non-abelian right-orderable group $G$ and all right-relatively convex subgroups $H$ and $K$ of
$G$, both the intersection $H \cap K$ and the join $H \vee K$ (the subgroup generated by $H$ and
$K$) are right-relatively convex.

In the integer Heisenberg group, which is non-abelian, the cyclic subgroups
$\langle (1, 0, 1) \rangle$ and $\langle (1, 0, -1) \rangle$ are convex for suitable right orders,
but the subgroup $J$ they generate contains $2e$ and not $e = (1, 0, 0)$, and every right order
puts $e$ between $0$ and $2e$, so $J$ is convex for no right order (van Doorn, Judin, Monticone and
Morrison, Theorem 8.1; Monticone's note). The 21st issue of the Kourovka Notebook marks 21.147
solved ("No, not always"; AI solution confirmed by A. V. Zenkov).
