## Conjugate subgroup

For a subgroup $A$ of a group $G$ and an element $x \in G$, the
conjugate subgroup is $A^x = x^{-1} A x = \{x^{-1} a x : a \in A\}$.

## Paranormal subgroup

A subgroup $A$ of a group $G$ is paranormal (Z. I. Borevich) when,
for every $x \in G$, the conjugate $A^x = x^{-1} A x$ is contained in the subgroup generated by
the conjugates $A^u$ with $u$ in the subgroup $\langle A, A^x \rangle$ generated by $A$ and
$A^x$.

## Polynormal subgroup

A subgroup $A$ of a group $G$ is polynormal (Z. I. Borevich) when,
for every $x \in G$, the conjugate $A^x = x^{-1} A x$ is contained in the subgroup generated by
the conjugates $A^u$ with $u \in A^{\langle x \rangle}$, where $A^{\langle x \rangle}$ is the
subgroup generated by the conjugates $A^{x^n}$ with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

## Polynormal subgroups of finite groups are paranormal

Kourovka 14.50 (Z. I. Borevich; A. S. Kondratiev): every polynormal subgroup of a finite group
is paranormal.

## A polynormal subgroup that is not paranormal (Kourovka 14.50 refuted)

There is a finite
group $G$ with a subgroup $A$ that is polynormal but not paranormal. So the conjecture of
Kourovka Problem 14.50 is false.
