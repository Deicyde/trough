# Group rings

> Reusable Lean algebra equivalences from group algebras equipped with explicit free-abelian coordinates to multivariable Laurent polynomial algebras, with canonical FreeAbelianGroup specialization and forward/inverse basis and free-generator laws. Laurent monomials are finitely supported integer exponent vectors; no finite-variable or decidable-equality premise is added. Separately, field-valued finite-abelian characters give evaluation bases when enough roots exist; right convolution has positive-character eigenvalues, diagonalization and a Nat.card rank formula. Monoid-algebra augmentation sums coefficients over any semiring and monoid. For a right-cancellative monoid M, any nonzero R[M]-linear map from R with its left augmentation action into the left regular R[M] forces M finite. Such maps vanish for infinite M, including infinite groups, without coefficient commutativity, nontriviality or a domain condition. Over any ring, the augmentation kernel is the left ideal generated by single m 1 - 1. A monoid generating set, or a subgroup generating set for groups, suffices for these left-ideal generators. Generic difference-membership lemmas propagate through monoid and subgroup closure in any ring's left ideals. As a left coefficient-ring module it also has a basis indexed by nonidentity monoid elements, with those vectors and the original coefficients as coordinates. A generic pointed Finsupp sum-kernel equivalence underlies this basis. Single-term units over nontrivial semirings are exactly the terms whose coefficient and monoid index are both units. Coefficient-unit reflection holds over every semiring, including the trivial one. For an arbitrary subgroup of a group, its left ideal of basis differences consists of exactly the elements with zero coefficient sums on left cosets. Over a nontrivial ring these ideals reflect subgroup membership and inclusion, and two-sidedness is equivalent to subgroup normality. No group or coefficient commutativity or finiteness is needed. The coset map itself is semiring-general. Subgroup finite generation implies finite generation of its left group-ring ideal over any ring; over a nontrivial ring the converse holds. For the whole group this characterizes finite generation of the augmentation kernel. Left-Noetherian group algebras over nontrivial rings have every subgroup finitely generated. Surjective augmentation combines with the generic surjective-homomorphism Noetherianity theorem for coefficient descent over arbitrary semirings and monoids. For a finitely generated commutative monoid, its monoid algebra over any left-Noetherian ring is left-Noetherian, in independent universes and without coefficient commutativity. Augmentation gives the reverse implication; the exponent monoid need not be finite. For any monoid homomorphism over any ring, its monoid-algebra kernel is the left ideal spanned by all equal-image unit-basis differences. A finitely generated commutative source admits one finite pair family that works over every coefficient ring, without coefficient Noetherianity or target commutativity, finite generation or surjectivity. More generally, finite generation of the integer kernel is an explicit sufficient premise. For any injective homomorphism from a group to a monoid, left-Noetherianity of the monoid algebra descends to the group algebra over every semiring, with three independent universes. Every subgroup algebra is therefore left-Noetherian, without finite index or normality. The generic criterion uses an additive right retraction and ideal extension-contraction; no target cancellation or freeness is assumed. For any semiring and subgroup, right cosets index a basis of the group algebra as a left subgroup-algebra module, with explicit coefficient, reconstruction and scalar compatibility laws. Finite index makes this module finite over any semiring. For ring coefficients, finite-index subgroup algebras and group algebras are left-Noetherian simultaneously. Over every ring, the infinite cyclic group algebra is left-Noetherian iff its coefficient ring is. Group equivalences and finite-index infinite cyclic subgroups transport this criterion, including finite-group products. Every left ideal of an arbitrary semiring extends along monoid-algebra constants to exactly the elements whose coefficients lie in that ideal. Coefficient-change kernels are therefore extended coefficient kernels. Their powers vanish at exactly the same exponents as the coefficient kernel, including degree zero, for arbitrary semiring homomorphisms and monoids. With ring source and semiring target coefficients, surjectivity identifies the quotient with the target monoid algebra; otherwise it is the range subsemiring. Polynomial transport preserves constants, every coefficient, ideal extension and coefficient-change kernels. For a group-to-monoid map, simultaneous coefficient/index change over a ring source has kernel the sum of the subgroup-relation ideal and the extended coefficient kernel. Surjective coefficients give the algebra of the index range; if the index map is also surjective the full target is available. No nontriviality or commutativity is imposed, and the target coefficients need only be a semiring. No sufficient-condition converse for arbitrary groups is asserted. Over a nontrivial possibly noncommutative ring with prime p equal to zero, nilpotence of the augmentation kernel implies one common p-power exponent for an arbitrary group, hence IsPGroup p G, without finiteness. Uniform vanishing of mixed ordered products gives the same conclusion. A generic left-ideal power API over any semiring identifies zero powers with vanishing of every ordered product of that length, including degree zero. For finite p-groups over any ring with p=0, including the zero ring, the augmentation kernel has zero Nat.card G-th power. Every ordered mixed product of that length therefore vanishes. Over nontrivial rings, finite-group augmentation nilpotence is equivalent to IsPGroup p G. The proof uses central subgroups of order p, canonical cyclic quotient kernels, ideal extension bounds and multiplication of power degrees. For locally finite p-groups over any ring with prime p=0, each augmentation-zero element is nilpotent, with an element-dependent exponent. Under these same hypotheses, the Jacobson radical is the augmentation preimage of the coefficient radical. Radical membership is detected by augmentation, and a basis term is radical exactly when its coefficient is. If the coefficient radical is zero, the group-algebra radical equals the augmentation kernel. No uniform kernel exponent or vanishing kernel power is asserted; noncommutative and zero coefficient rings are allowed. Uniform nilpotence of an ordinary left-ideal action over any semiring yields a nonzero common annihilated vector in every nontrivial semimodule. Two-sided ideals have a canonical common-annihilator submodule, agreeing with commutative set torsion. For any ring and monoid, augmentation-kernel annihilation is equivalent to being fixed by every canonical monoid symbol. Every nontrivial unital module of a finite p-group algebra over a ring with prime p=0 therefore has a nonzero canonical fixed vector, without finite generation, finite dimension or finiteness of the module. Simple modules of these finite p-group algebras have trivial group action on every vector, hence augmentation action. Simplicity over the group algebra is equivalent to simplicity under canonical restriction along singleOneRingHom, including noncommutative and zero coefficient rings. The augmentation-action/trivial-monoid-action characterization is valid for semirings and semimodules. For any map of modules over a finite p-group algebra with prime p=0 in any ring, canonical fixed vectors detect injectivity and canonical augmentation coinvariants detect surjectivity, without finite generation or coupled module universes. The generic injection theorem concerns nilpotent two-sided ideals over semirings acting on additive groups; quotient surjectivity detection allows nilpotent left ideals over rings. Nilpotent Nakayama itself holds for arbitrary semimodules. For arbitrary rings S and A, any ring homomorphism phi, a given A-module V, and a two-sided ideal I with one power contained in the annihilator of V, surjectivity of the canonical S-to-A/I map implies separate Noetherian and Artinian equivalences and equality of composition lengths in ENat under exact restriction along phi. This includes nilpotent ideals, infinite length, noncentral scalar images and zero rings, without finite generation. For any ring R with prime p=0 and finite p-group G, these three invariants of every given R[G]-module agree with canonical coefficient restriction. For a finite group whose order is a unit in any semiring, projectivity under canonical coefficient restriction implies projectivity of the original group-algebra module. Noncommutative and zero semirings, additive commutative monoids and independent coefficient, group and module universes are allowed. Monoid algebras commute canonically with finite square matrix semirings, preserving every coefficient entry. The equivalence requires a semiring, a monoid and a finite decidable matrix index type in independent universes; empty indices and zero or noncommutative coefficients are allowed. Group-algebra centrality over any semiring is equivalent to central coefficients and conjugation invariance. A nonzero coefficient of a central element has a finite conjugation orbit, even when the group is infinite. At any one fixed group index, coefficients of all right products determine a group-algebra element; left-product tests independently do so. Either family detects zero, over any possibly zero or noncommutative semiring and any group, without finiteness. Identity evaluation is the index-one case, not augmentation. No group-algebra/convolution bridge is asserted.

**Single terms and augmentation.** Over a nontrivial semiring and any monoid,
`IsUnit (single m r) ↔ IsUnit r ∧ IsUnit m`; coefficient-unit reflection
also allows the zero semiring. Over any semiring and an index type with
only multiplication, a single term is left-regular iff its coefficient is
left-regular and either the coefficients are subsingleton or its index is
left-regular; the [right-hand criterion](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/Regular.lean)
is analogous. Augmentation is the coefficient-sum ring
homomorphism over any semiring. For a right-cancellative monoid `M`, a nonzero
`R[M]`-linear map from `R` with its left augmentation action into the left
regular `R[M]` forces `M` finite; all such maps vanish when `M` is infinite,
including infinite groups. No coefficient nontriviality or domain condition
is needed. See the [augmentation Hom theorems](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/AugmentationHom.lean).
Over a ring its kernel is the **left** span of `single m 1 - 1`, with monoid
or subgroup generating-set refinements.
`MonoidAlgebra.augmentationKerBasis` is a basis over the coefficient ring
`R`, **not** over `R[M]`, indexed by nonidentity monoid elements.
[Statements and modules](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#single-terms-and-augmentation).
Over a nontrivial semiring and any group `G`, projectivity of `R` over
`R[G]` for the canonical **left augmentation action** forces `G` to be
finite and `(Nat.card G : R)` to be a unit; coefficients need not commute.
See the [augmentation-projectivity theorem](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/AugmentationProjective.lean).

**Units with two unique products.** Over a no-zero-divisor semiring `R`
and a monoid `M` with `TwoUniqueProds M`, an arbitrary `f : R[M]` is a unit
iff `f = single m.val r.val` for some `m : Units M` and `r : Units R`.
Both kinds of units are multiplicative; `R` may be the zero semiring.
For an additive monoid `A` with `TwoUniqueSums A`, the companion theorem
uses `a : AddUnits A`; coefficient and algebra units remain multiplicative.
See the [unit theorem](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/UnitsUniqueProds.lean) and
[precise scope and zero-semiring boundary](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#units-with-two-unique-products).

**Coefficient tests, centers and finite matrices.** Over any possibly zero
or noncommutative semiring, a group-algebra element is determined by one
chosen coefficient after all right multiplications, and independently after
all left multiplications. Centrality means central coefficients invariant
under conjugation; coefficients vanish on infinite conjugacy orbits.
For any monoid, finite decidable matrix indices give the ring equivalence
`MonoidAlgebra (Matrix n n R) M ≃+* Matrix n n (R[M])`, preserving both
multiplication orders, including empty indices.
[Precise statements](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#coefficient-tests-and-finite-matrices).

**Crossed maps and averaging.** Over any ring and monoid, linear maps from
the full augmentation ideal to a given left `R[M]`-module correspond
additively to normalized crossed maps
`z(mn) = z(m) + single m 1 • z(n)` for its canonical action.
For a finite group of unit order in the coefficient ring, every crossed map
is principal, with negative inverse-order sum witness, natural for additive
maps. See the [universal property](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/AugmentationCrossedUniversal.lean)
and [averaging](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/AugmentationAveraging.lean).

**Augmentation cotangents.** For any unital ring and monoid, coefficient-linear
maps from `I/I²` to a left `R`-module `V` correspond **as a set** to
`M →* Multiplicative V`. For a group,
`I/I² ≃ₗ[R] R ⊗[ℤ] Additive (Abelianization G)`, where `I` is the augmentation
ideal and `I²` its ordinary ideal square. Coefficient covariance is semilinear; monoid products split by projections
and sums of inclusions. No commutativity or freeness is required.
[Statements](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#augmentation-cotangents) ·
[cotangent guide](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/AugmentationCotangent.md).

**Finite `p`-groups and fixed vectors.** For prime `p`, any ring with
`(p : R) = 0` and finite `p`-group `G`, the augmentation ideal has zero
`Nat.card G`-th power. Every nontrivial unital `R[G]`-module has a nonzero
canonical fixed vector; simplicity is equivalent to simplicity under
canonical coefficient restriction, and simple modules have trivial group
action. For any group over any ring, augmentation nilpotence forces
`Subsingleton R ∨ Finite G`, without a characteristic assumption. If
`(p : R) = 0`, nilpotence is equivalent to
`Subsingleton R ∨ (Finite G ∧ IsPGroup p G)`; over nontrivial `R` it also
forces one common `p`-power exponent. For locally finite `p`-groups over
any ring with `(p : R) = 0`, every augmentation-zero element is nilpotent,
with an element-dependent exponent; no uniform kernel power is asserted.
Under these same locally finite hypotheses, the Jacobson radical is the
augmentation preimage of the coefficient radical. In particular, a single
term is radical exactly when its coefficient is, and a zero coefficient
radical gives the augmentation kernel. See the
[radical guide](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/Jacobson.md) and
[statements and guides](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#finite-p-groups-and-fixed-vectors).

**Given modules and scalar restriction.** Over any semiring and monoid,
canonical representations and an additive Hom/intertwiner correspondence
retain the given carriers and coefficient actions. For finite groups of
unit order in the coefficient semiring, coefficient projectivity implies
group-algebra projectivity. The normalized norm is natural for `R[G]`-linear
maps and is the unique `R[G]`-linear retraction onto the fixed vectors' span.
For rings `S`, `A`, a map `φ : S →+* A` surjective modulo a
two-sided ideal `I`, and an `A`-module `V` with
`∃ n, I^n ≤ Module.annihilator A V`, the exact restriction
`Module.compHom V φ` preserves and reflects both chain conditions and
preserves length in `ℕ∞`, including infinity. Semisimplicity is equivalent
to **annihilation by `I` and semisimplicity of that restriction**.
For finite `p`-groups over rings with prime `p = 0`, this gives the canonical
coefficient comparisons: semisimplicity requires coefficient semisimplicity
**and** trivial group action. Fixed vectors detect injectivity of module
maps; coinvariants detect surjectivity, without finite generation.
[Statements and modules](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#given-modules-and-scalar-restriction).

**Finiteness of modules.** For rings `S`, `A`, compatible scalar towers,
and a two-sided ideal `I`, a Noetherian `A`-module with uniformly nilpotent
`I`-action is finite over `S` whenever `A ⧸ I` is finite over `S`; no
surjective scalar map or nilpotence of `I` itself is needed. Separately,
a simple algebra over a commutative ring admitting a nontrivial compatible
module Noetherian over that base is itself Noetherian, hence finite, over
the base; the base ring need not be Noetherian.
A Noetherian `A`-semimodule also has a greatest `A`-submodule
finite over compatible scalars `S`, preserved by every `A`-linear
endomorphism. For the regular module this is a two-sided ideal. Both
scalar semirings may be noncommutative, and `S` need not be Noetherian.
[Greatest finite submodules](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/GroupRings/RingTheory/Module/ScalarRestriction.lean) ·
[other statements and hypotheses](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#given-modules-and-scalar-restriction).

**Subgroup ideals and coset bases.** Over any ring, a subgroup's left
difference ideal consists of elements whose coefficient sums vanish on
**left cosets** `gH`. Over nontrivial rings these ideals reflect subgroup
membership, inclusion, finite generation and normality via two-sidedness.
Separately, over any semiring, `R[G]` has a **left** `R[H]`-basis indexed by
**right cosets** `H * g`; finite index gives module finiteness.
No normality is required for these coset constructions.
[Statements](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#subgroup-ideals-and-coset-bases) ·
[subgroup guide](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/SubgroupIdeals.md).

**Coefficient and index quotients.** Over semirings, extension of any left
coefficient ideal is characterized coefficientwise. For any semiring
homomorphism and monoid, coefficient change preserves and reflects zero
kernel powers at each fixed exponent, including zero, without surjectivity.
For `Ring R`,
`Semiring S`, `Group G`, `Monoid M`, and maps `f : R →+* S`, `φ : G →* M`,
the mixed kernel is the sum of the extended coefficient kernel and the
kernel-subgroup difference ideal. For arbitrary maps the quotient is the
composite's range subsemiring; surjective `f` gives `S[MonoidHom.mrange φ]`, and
surjectivity of both gives `S[M]`. Normal-subgroup quotients and unchanged
coefficient kernel/range quotients have full basis and factorization laws.
[Statements and guides](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#coefficient-and-index-quotients).

**Commuting scalar actions and central idempotents.** Commuting ring
actions on a module remain commuting after quotienting both rings by
annihilating ideals; two-sidedness is needed for quotient rings, not just
their scalar actions. For any ring `A` and two-sided ideal `I`, the
[first ideal-square quotient](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/GroupRings/RingTheory/Ideal/SquareQuotient.lean)
`I/I²` has commuting left and right `A ⧸ I`-actions. In any ring, an idempotent
central modulo a nilpotent two-sided ideal `I` is central if **both**
off-diagonal corner maps send `I` into `I²`; quotient centrality alone is
insufficient.
[Actions](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#given-modules-and-scalar-restriction) ·
[centrality criterion](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#chain-conditions-and-regularity).

**Central summands detected by characters.** For a commutative ring `R`
and an `R`-algebra `A` Artinian on both sides, every `χ : A →ₐ[R] R`
is one on a central idempotent whose principal left ideal is finite over
`R` with its inherited action. Zero and nonreduced base rings are allowed;
the whole algebra need not be finite over `R`.
[Statement and proof strategy](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#central-summands-finite-over-the-base-ring).

**Uniform binomial kernels.** For any monoid homomorphism and any ring,
its monoid-algebra kernel is the left span of equal-image unit-basis
differences. If the source is finitely generated and commutative, one finite
family of index pairs works over **every** coefficient ring. Neither the
target monoid nor the coefficients need commute; no coefficient-Noetherian
hypothesis is needed.
[Statements and modules](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#uniform-binomial-kernels).

**Relative module finiteness.** For any semiring `R` and homomorphism
`f : M →* N` of arbitrary monoids, `R[N]` is finite as a left `R[M]`-module
for its regular action restricted along `mapDomainRingHom R f` iff there is
a finite set `T` with every `n` expressible as `f m * t`, `t ∈ T`, or `R`
is subsingleton.
The source image is on the **left**; coefficients may be noncommutative,
and neither cancellation nor finite generation is assumed. Monomial
subalgebra inclusions over nontrivial commutative coefficients satisfy the
corresponding finite-cover criterion with their inclusion-induced action.
[Statements and boundaries](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#relative-module-finiteness) ·

**Finite type.** For any commutative semiring `R` and any monoid `M`,
`Algebra.FiniteType R (MonoidAlgebra R M)` is equivalent to
`Monoid.FG M ∨ Subsingleton R`. Nontrivial coefficients give simply finite
generation of `M`; neither commutativity nor cancellation of `M` is needed.
The additive-monoid counterpart uses the same criterion.

**Finite presentation.** For any commutative ring `R` and finitely generated
commutative monoid `M`, the canonical `R`-algebra `R[M]` is finitely presented.
The additive-monoid version is also an instance. Coefficients need not be
Noetherian, and exponents need not be cancellative or finite.

**Chain conditions and regularity.** For any ring `R` and finitely generated
**commutative** monoid `M`, `R[M]` is left-Noetherian iff `R` is.
For a commutative group `G`, the exact criterion is left-Noetherianity of `R`
and `Subsingleton R ∨ Group.FG G`. A finite-index finitely generated
commutative subgroup gives the coefficient-ring criterion even when the
ambient group is noncommutative and the subgroup is not normal. For a
cancellative monoid over any semiring, a **nonzero** left ideal finite over
the coefficients forces the monoid to be finite. Additive right-compatible
coefficient retractions give left-Artinian descent without multiplicativity.
Such a retraction exists for every homomorphism from a division ring to a
nontrivial semiring, without centrality or finite-dimensionality assumptions.
Left-Noetherianity descends to embedded group algebras over any semiring;
finite-index subgroup algebras over rings share it with the whole algebra.
Over nontrivial rings it forces every subgroup to be finitely generated,
without a general converse. The infinite cyclic and finite-index cyclic
criteria reduce to the coefficient ring; import
`GroupRings.MonoidAlgebra.CyclicNoetherian` directly for these.
Over any ring, infinite-order elements of cancellative monoids give regular
monic basis differences, forcing torsion in left-Artinian monoid algebras
with nontrivial coefficients. For any ring `R` and either a left-cancellative
or a right-cancellative monoid `G`,
[`MonoidAlgebra.isArtinianRing_iff_of_isLeftCancelMul` and
`MonoidAlgebra.isArtinianRing_iff`](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/GroupRings/MonoidAlgebra/Artinian.lean)
give that `R[G]` is left-Artinian iff `R` is left-Artinian and
`Subsingleton R ∨ Finite G`. The corresponding `finite_of_isArtinianRing`
theorems give monoid finiteness directly over any nontrivial ring,
specializing to the classical group criterion.
Locally, over a nontrivial **semiring** and any monoid, left Artinianity
makes every right-regular index a unit; over a nontrivial **ring**, it also
makes every left-regular index a unit, without global cancellation.
The global criteria require **ring** coefficients. All these conditions
use the original left regular action.
The zero ring is allowed in the criterion, the
universes are independent, and no commutativity or right-Artinian hypothesis
is imposed.
Separately, `LaurentPolynomial R` is left-Artinian iff `R` is subsingleton,
even for noncommutative or zero rings.
[Statements and modules](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#chain-conditions-and-regularity).

**Laurent coordinates.** Supplied coordinates `G ≃+ (σ →₀ ℤ)` identify an
additive commutative group's algebra over a commutative semiring with
`MvLaurentPolynomial σ A`. Free abelian groups have canonical coordinates,
with arbitrary, empty or infinite variable types.
[Statements and module](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#laurent-coordinates).

**Characters and right convolution.** Character evaluations on an additive
commutative group `G` are linearly independent over any field `K`, even for
infinite groups without enough roots. For finite `G`, enough roots of unity
of exponent `Monoid.exponent (Multiplicative G)` give a character basis;
with `[Fintype G]` they give diagonalization and rank for right convolution,
whose entry is `f (-sigma + tau)` and eigenvalue is `∑ x, f x * psi x` with
**positive** sign. The eigenvector law needs no enough-roots assumption.
[Statements](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/HeadlineResults.md#characters-and-right-convolution) ·
[Fourier guide](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/group-rings/blob/8e90e6563300656410a098a0fea83332930218b5/docs/FiniteAbelianRightConvolution.md).
