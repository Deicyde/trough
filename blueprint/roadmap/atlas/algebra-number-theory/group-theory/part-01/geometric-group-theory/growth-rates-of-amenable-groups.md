## Growth rate of a $k$-element generating family (Kourovka 14.7)

Let $\Gamma$ be a group
and $S = (s_1, \dots, s_k)$ a family of $k$ elements generating $\Gamma$. For $n \in \mathbb{N}$ let
$\beta_S(n)$ be the number of elements of $\Gamma$ that are products of at most $n$ elements of
$S \cup S^{-1}$. The growth rate of $\Gamma$ with respect to $S$ is the real number
$\omega(\Gamma, S) = \limsup_{n \to \infty} \beta_S(n)^{1/n}$.

## Kourovka Problem 15.4 (large growth rate implies non-amenability)

The proposition, asked
by P. de la Harpe, that there are $k \ge 2$ and a real $\varepsilon > 0$ such that every group
$\Gamma$ generated by a family $S$ of $k$ distinct elements whose growth rate satisfies
$\omega(\Gamma, S) \ge 2k - 1 - \varepsilon$ is non-amenable (amenability being the Følner
condition).

## Amenable groups with growth rate close to $2k - 1$ (Kourovka 15.4 refuted)

For a group
$\Gamma$ generated by a family $S$ of $k$ elements and $n \in \mathbb{N}$, let $\beta_S(n)$ be the
number of elements of $\Gamma$ that are products of at most $n$ elements of $S \cup S^{-1}$, and
let $\omega(\Gamma, S) = \limsup_{n \to \infty} \beta_S(n)^{1/n}$ be the growth rate. For every
$k \ge 2$ and every real $\varepsilon > 0$ there are an amenable group $\Gamma$ (amenability being
the Følner condition) and a family $S$ of $k$ distinct elements generating $\Gamma$ such that
$\omega(\Gamma, S) \ge 2k - 1 - \varepsilon$. Equivalently, there are no $k \ge 2$ and
$\varepsilon > 0$ such that every group generated by a family of $k$ distinct elements with growth
rate at least $2k - 1 - \varepsilon$ is non-amenable.

Arzhantseva, Guba and Guyot showed that for every $m \ge 2$, with $F_m$ the free group of rank
$m$, $R$ a normal subgroup of $F_m$ and $R'$ its commutator subgroup, the growth rate of $F_m/R'$
tends to $2m - 1$ as the shortest relation in $R$ gets longer, so $m$-generated amenable groups
(abelian-by-nilpotent ones, for instance) have growth rates arbitrarily close to $2m - 1$; the
Kourovka Archive answers 15.4 "No, it does not".
