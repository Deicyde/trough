# The sublines distribution

For the seeded padded line distribution on $\mathbb{F}_q^{4m}$ there is a distribution of $(\ell,\ell_X,\ell_Z)$ with the following properties. Its $\ell$-marginal is the padded line marginal. Every $u=(u_X,u_Z,\alpha,\beta,w)\in\ell$ satisfies $u_X\in\ell_X$ and $u_Z\in\ell_Z$. For uniform $u\in\ell$, the law of $(\ell_X,\ell_Z)$ is a mixture of products of restricted unpadded line marginals; conditional on this pair, each of $u_X,u_Z$ is uniform on its respective line. Joint independence of these two points is not asserted. Here a restricted axis distribution fixes direction $e_i$; a restricted diagonal distribution fixes the seed index $i$ and has direction with its first $i-1$ coordinates zero. These are restrictions of the seeded laws, including degenerate lines, not new uniform laws on distinct geometric lines. Finally, if $\ell$ is axis-parallel, both $\ell_X$ and $\ell_Z$ are axis-parallel. This last clause is needed for the legal degree-$d$ answers.

## Proof

The distribution is a sampling procedure, and the procedure is forced by the first property it has to satisfy.

**What the containment forces.** Moving along the padded line moves the $X$ block of a point along the $X$ block of the padded direction. So where that block is nonzero the $X$ subline has no choice: its direction must be exactly that block, and the only freedom left is *which seed* presents it — a seed of the prescribed axis index, drawn uniformly from that index's block of $q/m$ elements. Where the block vanishes the $X$ block of a point is constant along the padded line, the containment is free, and the subline is sampled fresh. Same for $Z$.

**The four cases, and why the two sides differ.** A coordinate position of $\mathbb{F}_q^{4m}$ lies in the $X$ block, in the $Z$ block, at $\alpha$ or $\beta$, or among the dummy coordinates, and which blocks of the padded direction vanish depends on the case *and* on the line type. For an axis-parallel padded line the direction is a single coordinate, so at most one of the two blocks is nonzero. For a diagonal one it is $v$ truncated below the axis index, so an index in the $X$ block leaves the whole $Z$ block untouched: both blocks are nonzero and the $Z$ subline is forced as well, at axis index $0$ since nothing of its direction is truncated. That is the only place the construction looks at the line type.

**The law.** The $X$ and $Z$ blocks of a uniform padded point are uniform and independent, because $\mathbb{F}_q^{4m}$ is the disjoint union of the two blocks and the $2m$ positions above them, and precomposition with an injection of index types carries the uniform measure to the uniform measure. The two sides' data are therefore functions of disjoint parts of the randomness: the point and the raw direction contribute one block each, and each side's fresh seed is its own. Averaging over the sampling space is then exactly averaging over a product of two line-point laws, restricted on a side precisely when that side is forced, with no constant left over — the multiplicities are the assignments the construction does not read, and they divide out. A side that is not forced gets the unrestricted law, which is the uniform mixture of the $m$ restricted ones; that is the mixture in the second property.

**The third property is definitional here**, the line type being a parameter handed to both sides rather than something the construction chooses. What the paper draws from its Property 3 — that the directions agree — is the four-case statement above.
