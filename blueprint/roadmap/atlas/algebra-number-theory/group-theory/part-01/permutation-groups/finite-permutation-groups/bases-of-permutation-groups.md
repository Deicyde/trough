## Base of a group action

For a group $G$ acting on a set $\Omega$, a finite set
$B \subseteq \Omega$ is a base when its pointwise stabilizer is trivial: the only $g \in G$ with
$g \cdot \omega = \omega$ for all $\omega \in B$ is $g = 1$.

## Minimal base

For a group $G$ acting on a set $\Omega$, a finite set $B \subseteq \Omega$
is a minimal base when it is a base, that is, its pointwise stabilizer in $G$ is trivial, and for
every $\omega \in B$ the set $B \setminus \{\omega\}$ is not a base.

## Maximal size of a minimal base

For a group $G$, the number $b(G)$ is the supremum in
$\mathbb{N}$ of the sizes of the minimal bases of $G$ over all actions of $G$ on a set $\Omega$,
where a minimal base is a finite set $B \subseteq \Omega$ whose pointwise stabilizer in $G$ is
trivial while that of $B \setminus \{\omega\}$ is not, for every $\omega \in B$. It is the largest
such size when these sizes are bounded, as they are for finite $G$, and $0$ when they are
unbounded.

## Independent subset of a group

A finite subset $S$ of a group $G$ is independent when no
element $s \in S$ lies in the subgroup of $G$ generated by $S \setminus \{s\}$.

## Maximal size of an independent set

For a group $G$, a finite subset $S \subseteq G$ is
independent when no $s \in S$ lies in the subgroup generated by $S \setminus \{s\}$. The number
$\mu'(G)$ is the supremum in $\mathbb{N}$ of the sizes of the finite independent subsets of $G$:
their largest size when these sizes are bounded, as they are for finite $G$, and $0$ when they are
unbounded.

## Cameron's question on minimal bases and independent sets

The statement that
$b(G) = \mu'(G)$ for every finite group $G$, where $b(G)$ is the largest size of a minimal base of
$G$ over all actions of $G$ on a set, and $\mu'(G)$ is the largest size of an independent subset
of $G$ (a subset none of whose elements lies in the subgroup generated by the others).

This is Kourovka Notebook Problem 16.45, posed by P. J. Cameron.

## Kourovka Problem 16.45 has a negative answer

It is not true that $b(G) = \mu'(G)$ for every
finite group $G$, where $b(G)$ is the largest size of a minimal base of $G$ over all actions of $G$
on a set, and $\mu'(G)$ is the largest size of an independent subset of $G$.

The counterexample in the source is Kriebel's affine group $G = \mathbb{F}_{29}^2 \rtimes
\mathrm{SL}_2(5)$ of order $100920$, for which $b(G) = 3 < 4 = \mu'(G)$.
