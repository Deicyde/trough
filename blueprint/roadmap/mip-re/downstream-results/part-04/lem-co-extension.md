# An extension of a commuting-operator model is a commuting-operator model

Let $\mathscr{S} = (\mathcal{H}, \psi, E, F)$ be a commuting-operator strategy and $e$ a unit vector of $\mathbb{C}^{\alpha \times \beta}$. There is a commuting-operator strategy on $\mathcal{H} \otimes \mathbb{C}^{\alpha \times \beta}$, in the state $\psi \otimes e$, whose commuting-operator model (Definition [Bipartite model](../part-02/def-bipartite-model.md)) is isomorphic, by the identity of the space, to the extension in $e$ of the commuting-operator model of $\mathscr{S}$ (Definition [Ancilla extension of a bipartite model](../part-02/def-ancilla-extension.md)). Its first player asks one question and always gives the same answer, with the operator ${I}$; its second player answers a question $y$ of $\mathscr{S}$ with the operators $F^y_b \otimes {I}_{\alpha \times \beta}$, and a question about the register $\beta$ with the two-outcome measurement of the projection ${I}_\alpha \otimes |b\rangle\langleb|$ onto a basis vector or ${I}_\alpha \otimes |u\rangle\langleu|$ onto the uniform superposition. Its first player's algebra, the commutant of the second player's operators, is $\{X \otimes {I}_\beta : X \in M_\alpha({\mathscr{A}}_A)\}$ for the first player's algebra ${\mathscr{A}}_A$ of $\mathscr{S}$, and its second player's algebra is $\{{I}_\alpha \otimes Y : Y \in M_\beta({\mathscr{A}}_B)\}$.

## Proof

An operator commuting with the second player's operators commutes with $F^y_b \otimes {I}$ and with the register's diagonal and uniform projections, hence with its matrix units, so it is $X \otimes {I}_\beta$ with the entries of $X$ in the commutant ${\mathscr{A}}_A$ of the $F^y_b$ (Lemma [The commutant of a matrix amplification](lem-ampl-commutant.md)); conversely every such operator commutes with them. That algebra contains $M_\alpha(\mathbb{C}) \otimes {I}$ and ${\mathscr{A}}_A \otimes {I}$, so the same lemma, for the register $\alpha$, makes its commutant $\{{I}_\alpha \otimes Y : Y \in M_\beta({\mathscr{A}}_A')\}$, and ${\mathscr{A}}_A'$ is ${\mathscr{A}}_B$ by definition. Both identifications are the representations of the extension, so the identity of the space intertwines them, and it carries the state to the state.
