# The Convex-Octagon Case of Erdős Problem 97

> Proves that among eight labelled points in the Euclidean plane in convex position, at least one point has no four other points at a common distance. The development reduces a geometric counterexample to a finite incidence model, classifies normalized row patterns up to symmetry, proves geometric and algebraic obstructions, and excludes seven canonical first-row masks with a kernel-audited exhaustive search over the remaining legal row masks and checked geometric obstruction witnesses. This is the modern four-equidistant formulation; Erdős's 1946 paper posed the stronger three-equidistant version. This is the convex-octagon case only; the general Erdős Problem 97 remains open.
