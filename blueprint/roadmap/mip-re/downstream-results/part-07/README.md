## Provenance of this chapter

**Remark (Stage 1.8 of the ledger, and how much of it has been challenged).** The statements of this chapter were roadmap sketches until September 2026, when the companion campaign opened a stage for them: stage $1.8$, twenty-seven nodes, which the statements above now account for one for one. Three things about that stage are worth carrying here rather than leaving in the other repository.

First, it is where two of the campaign's three remaining admissions live — Lemma [Decidability of the first-order theory of real closed fields](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/08_downstream.tex#L125) (node $1.8.3$) and the forward Kirchberg implication inside Lemma [CEP forces equality of the correlation sets](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/08_downstream.tex#L678) (node $1.8.6.1$). Neither is reached by the main theorem: they are assumptions of the *corollaries*, which is the right place for them, and Remark [What is still admitted, after the discharges of September 2026](../../background-results/README.md) keeps the main theorem's own surface to a single classical algorithmic result. The Lean proof of Corollary [Tsirelson's problem, negative answer](../part-01/cor-tsirelson.md) does not rest on node $1.8.3$: it proves Lemma [Upper semidecision of the commuting-operator value](../part-01/lem-valco-upper-re.md) through Theorem [Positivstellensatz for the commuting-operator value](../part-01/thm-nc-positivstellensatz.md) instead. The ledger's own dependencies are unchanged, so `scripts/ledger-sync.py` keeps reporting $1.8.3$ as reached from these statements.

Second, the ledger's separation route needs more than the main theorem. The construction needs effective bounded self-reference at a fixed $C_0 \geq 2$ through one canonical specialization-and-timeout compiler (node $1.8.8$); the commuting value is $1$ because halting of the upper semidecider would supply one-dimensional perfect PCC strategies at sufficiently large levels, and ordinary completeness would propagate perfect finite-dimensional strategies to the initial level (node $1.8.9$); and $\mathrm{val}^*\leq\frac12$ uses divergence of the *bipartite Schmidt-rank* requirement along $n_{j+1}=2^{n_j}$ (node $1.8.10$). The Lean proof of Theorem [Separation](../part-01/thm-separation.md) needs none of this: it applies the recursion theorem to the halting reduction and the upper semidecider, and its correspondence with these nodes is at the level of the conclusion. The three obligations remain those of the paper's own game, recorded in Remark The paper's construction and its premises.

Third, and this is the part to be honest about: these nodes have had far less adversarial scrutiny than the rest of the ledger. Two of the twenty-seven carry a challenge, against $85\%$ of the nodes in the original campaign state, and the adversarial round of 2026-09-14 — which re-examined seventy-five nodes and found one real defect (Remark [The printed transfer corollary is false without the marginal condition](../../background-results/README.md)) — worked entirely within stage $1.2$ and did not touch stage $1.8$ at all. Node $1.8$'s own statement says its derivations “remain pending adversarial review”. So the annotations in this chapter record that a decomposition exists and that it has been checked once, not that it has been attacked; treat the statements here as better-specified sketches rather than as verified mathematics, and expect the numbered details above to move.
