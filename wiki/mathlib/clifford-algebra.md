The **Clifford algebra** of a [module](trough:Module) $M$ over a commutative [ring](trough:Ring) $R$, equipped with a
[quadratic form](trough:QuadraticForm) $Q$, is the associative $R$-algebra generated by $M$ subject only to the rule
that every vector $v$ squares to the scalar $Q(v)$: it is the universal associative algebra in which
each vector squares to the value of the quadratic form. The complex numbers, the quaternions, the dual numbers
and the [exterior algebra](trough:ExteriorAlgebra) all arise as Clifford algebras, and the construction
underlies spin groups and spinors in geometry, topology and physics.

## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $M$ an $R$-module and $Q \colon M \to R$ a quadratic form. The Clifford
algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(M, Q)$ is the quotient of the [tensor algebra](trough:TensorAlgebra) $T(M)$ by the two-sided ideal
generated by the elements $v \otimes v - Q(v)\cdot 1$ for $v \in M$ ([the Clifford algebra of $Q$](lean:CliffordAlgebra)).
Composing the inclusion of $M$ into $T(M)$ with the quotient map gives
[a linear map $\iota \colon M \to \mathrm{Cl}(M, Q)$](lean:CliffordAlgebra.ι), and by construction
[$\iota(v)^2 = Q(v)\cdot 1$ for every $v \in M$](lean:CliffordAlgebra.ι_sq_scalar).

The algebra is characterized by a universal property. Let $A$ be an
[associative $R$-algebra](trough:Algebra) and $f \colon M \to A$ a [linear map](trough:LinearMap) with $f(v)^2 = Q(v)\cdot 1$ for all $v$.
Then there is exactly one algebra homomorphism $\varphi \colon \mathrm{Cl}(M, Q) \to A$ with
$\varphi \circ \iota = f$; in other words,
[restriction along $\iota$ is a bijection between algebra homomorphisms out of $\mathrm{Cl}(M, Q)$ and such linear maps](lean:CliffordAlgebra.lift).
This determines $\mathrm{Cl}(M, Q)$ up to unique isomorphism, and
[the algebra is generated by the image of $\iota$](lean:CliffordAlgebra.adjoin_range_ι).

Replacing $v$ by $u + v$ in the defining relation gives

$$\iota(u)\,\iota(v) + \iota(v)\,\iota(u) = \bigl(Q(u+v) - Q(u) - Q(v)\bigr)\cdot 1,$$

so [the symmetrized product of two vectors is the scalar given by the polar form of $Q$](lean:CliffordAlgebra.ι_mul_ι_add_swap)
(see [polar form](trough:QuadraticMap.polar)). In particular, if $Q(u+v) = Q(u) + Q(v)$, that is, if $u$ and $v$ are
orthogonal, then [$\iota(u)\,\iota(v) = -\iota(v)\,\iota(u)$](lean:CliffordAlgebra.ι_mul_ι_comm_of_isOrtho).

When $M$ is free with basis $e_1, \dots, e_n$, the ordered products $e_{i_1} e_{i_2} \cdots e_{i_k}$ with
$i_1 < i_2 < \dots < i_k$, the empty product $1$ included, form a basis of $\mathrm{Cl}(M, Q)$ over $R$, so it is free of
rank $2^n$. If moreover $Q(x_1 e_1 + \dots + x_n e_n) = a_1 x_1^2 + \dots + a_n x_n^2$, the multiplication is
determined by $e_i^2 = a_i$ and $e_i e_j = -e_j e_i$ for $i \neq j$.

## Examples

- **Zero form.** For $Q = 0$ the relations say that every vector squares to zero, and the result is
  the exterior algebra; [Mathlib defines the exterior algebra of $M$ as the Clifford algebra of the zero form](lean:ExteriorAlgebra).
- **Zero module.** When $M = 0$ only the scalars remain:
  [the Clifford algebra of the zero module is $R$ itself](lean:CliffordAlgebraRing.equiv).
- **Complex numbers.** Take $M = \mathbb{R}$ and $Q(x) = -x^2$. The element $i = \iota(1)$ satisfies $i^2 = -1$, and
  [the Clifford algebra is isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}$ as a real algebra](lean:CliffordAlgebraComplex.equiv).
- **Quaternions.** Take $M = R^2$ and $Q(x, y) = c_1 x^2 + c_2 y^2$. Then $i = \iota(1, 0)$ and $j = \iota(0, 1)$ satisfy
  $i^2 = c_1$, $j^2 = c_2$ and $ij = -ji$, and
  [the Clifford algebra is isomorphic to the quaternion algebra with these parameters](lean:CliffordAlgebraQuaternion.equiv).
  Over the [real numbers](trough:Real) with $c_1 = c_2 = -1$ this gives Hamilton's quaternions.
- **Dual numbers.** Take $M = R$ and $Q = 0$, the zero form on a module of rank one, so this is the
  exterior algebra of $R$. Then
  [the Clifford algebra is the algebra of dual numbers $R[\varepsilon]$ with $\varepsilon^2 = 0$](lean:CliffordAlgebraDualNumber.equiv).

## Properties

- **Functoriality.** A linear map $g \colon M_1 \to M_2$ with $Q_2 \circ g = Q_1$ is an isometry, and
  [every isometry induces an algebra homomorphism of Clifford algebras](lean:CliffordAlgebra.map) sending $\iota(v)$
  to $\iota(g(v))$; [an isometric isomorphism induces an algebra isomorphism](lean:CliffordAlgebra.equivOfIsometry).
- **Even and odd parts.** Products of an even number of vectors span the even part, and products of
  an odd number span the odd part. [The algebra is the direct sum of these two submodules](lean:CliffordAlgebra.evenOdd_isCompl),
  and [multiplication adds parities, making $\mathrm{Cl}(M, Q)$ a $\mathbb{Z}/2$-graded algebra](lean:CliffordAlgebra.gradedAlgebra).
  The even part is therefore [a subalgebra](lean:CliffordAlgebra.even).
- **One dimension up.** Let $Q'$ be the quadratic form $Q'(v, r) = Q(v) - r^2$ on $M \oplus R$. Then
  [$\mathrm{Cl}(M, Q)$ is isomorphic to the even subalgebra of $\mathrm{Cl}(M \oplus R, Q')$](lean:CliffordAlgebra.equivEven).
  Also, [the even subalgebras for $Q$ and for $-Q$ are isomorphic](lean:CliffordAlgebra.evenEquivEvenNeg).
- **Involutions.** The grade involution is
  [the algebra homomorphism from $\mathrm{Cl}(M, Q)$ to itself that sends each vector $v$ to $-v$](lean:CliffordAlgebra.involute);
  it fixes the even part and negates the odd part. Reversion is the linear map that fixes vectors and
  [reverses the order of products, sending $ab$ to the reverse of $b$ times the reverse of $a$](lean:CliffordAlgebra.reverse.map_mul).
- **Relation to the exterior algebra.** If $2$ is invertible in $R$,
  [$\mathrm{Cl}(M, Q)$ is isomorphic as an $R$-module to the exterior algebra of $M$](lean:CliffordAlgebra.equivExterior).
  In general this is not an isomorphism of algebras: for $M = \mathbb{R}$ and $Q(x) = -x^2$ it matches $\mathbb{C}$ with the dual numbers, which are not
  isomorphic as rings.
- **Orthogonal sums.** For quadratic forms $Q_1$ on $M_1$ and $Q_2$ on $M_2$, let $Q_1 \oplus Q_2$ be the form
  $(m_1, m_2) \mapsto Q_1(m_1) + Q_2(m_2)$. Then
  [$\mathrm{Cl}(M_1 \oplus M_2, Q_1 \oplus Q_2)$ is isomorphic to the graded tensor product of $\mathrm{Cl}(M_1, Q_1)$ and $\mathrm{Cl}(M_2, Q_2)$](lean:CliffordAlgebra.prodEquiv),
  in which odd elements of the two factors anticommute.

## Related concepts

The invertible products of vectors in a Clifford algebra act on $M$ by twisted conjugation through
isometries, which leads to the pin and spin groups. For a definite form on $\mathbb{R}^n$ the pin group,
generated by the unit vectors, and the spin group, its even part, are double
covers of the orthogonal group $\mathrm{O}(n)$ and the special orthogonal group $\mathrm{SO}(n)$, and their
representations give the spinors. In Mathlib
[the pin group](lean:pinGroup) consists of the elements of the group generated by invertible vectors that
are unitary for Clifford conjugation (the composite of reversion and the grade involution), and
[the spin group](lean:spinGroup) is its intersection with the even subalgebra. A vector $v$ is unitary
in this sense only when $Q(v) = -1$, so this definition matches the textbook pin group for a negative
definite form; for a positive definite form it keeps only products of an even number of vectors, and the
pin group coincides with the spin group. The Clifford algebra is a quotient of the
[tensor algebra](trough:TensorAlgebra), which imposes no relations, and specializes to the
exterior algebra when $Q = 0$.

## In Mathlib

Mathlib defines the Clifford algebra for any commutative ring $R$ and any module $M$, with no
assumption that $2$ is invertible. A quadratic form there is a map $Q$ with $Q(av) = a^2 Q(v)$ whose polar
form is bilinear; it need not come from a symmetric bilinear form, which matters in characteristic
$2$. The algebra is built as the quotient of the tensor algebra by the smallest congruence that
identifies the square of each vector with $Q(v)$, which is the same as the quotient by the two-sided
ideal above. The universal property is packaged as a single bijection rather than as separate
existence and uniqueness statements, and its target may be any $R$-algebra. Mathlib asks only that the target be a semiring
with an $R$-algebra structure; negation then comes for free, as multiplication by the image of $-1$. The exterior algebra is not built separately: it is the Clifford
algebra of the zero form, so results about Clifford algebras apply to it directly. Results that
use a hypothesis on $2$ state it: the module isomorphism with the exterior algebra, and Mathlib's
proof that the algebra is nontrivial over a nontrivial ring, both assume that $2$ is invertible.
