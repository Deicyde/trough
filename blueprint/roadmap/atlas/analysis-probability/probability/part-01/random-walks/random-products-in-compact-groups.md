## Fair coin measure

The fair coin measure is the measure
$\tfrac12 \delta_{\mathrm{false}} + \tfrac12 \delta_{\mathrm{true}}$ on the Booleans
$\{\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}\}$: it gives mass $1/2$ to each of the two values.

## Fair coin product measure

The fair coin product measure is the measure on the space of
Boolean sequences $c : \mathbb{N} \to \{\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}\}$ given by the infinite
product, over $n \in \mathbb{N}$, of the fair coin measure, which gives mass $1/2$ to each of
$\mathrm{false}$ and $\mathrm{true}$.

## Generator chosen by a coin sequence

Let $\varphi$ and $\psi$ be elements of a type $G$
and let $c : \mathbb{N} \to \{\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}\}$ be a sequence. For
$n \in \mathbb{N}$, the $n$-th chosen generator is $\varphi$ if $c(n) = \mathrm{false}$ and
$\psi$ if $c(n) = \mathrm{true}$.

Index $0$ corresponds to $f_1$ in the Scottish Book typescript.

## Successive products along a coin sequence

Let $G$ be a group, let $\varphi, \psi \in G$,
and let $c : \mathbb{N} \to \{\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}\}$. For $i \ge 1$ let $f_i$ be the
generator chosen at index $i - 1$: $f_i = \varphi$ if $c(i - 1) = \mathrm{false}$ and
$f_i = \psi$ if $c(i - 1) = \mathrm{true}$. The successive products are defined by $P_0 = 1$
and $P_{n+1} = P_n f_{n+1}$, so $P_n = f_1 f_2 \cdots f_n$ for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, with
$P_0$ the empty product.

The typescript writes the terms as $f_1, f_1(f_2), f_1(f_2 f_3), \ldots$, read as successive
products.

## Scottish Book Problem 116(b), measurable-set form

The claim is the following. Let $G$ be
any compact Hausdorff topological group with its Borel $\sigma$-algebra, and let $\mu$ be a Haar
measure on $G$ that is a probability measure. For $\varphi, \psi \in G$ and a Boolean sequence
$c : \mathbb{N} \to \{\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}\}$, let $f_{n+1} = \varphi$ if
$c(n) = \mathrm{false}$ and $f_{n+1} = \psi$ if $c(n) = \mathrm{true}$, and define the successive
products by $P_0 = 1$ and $P_{n+1} = P_n f_{n+1}$. Then for $(\mu \otimes \mu)$-almost every pair
$(\varphi, \psi) \in G \times G$, if the subgroup generated by $\varphi$ and $\psi$ is dense in
$G$, then for almost every $c$ with respect to the fair coin product measure, for every
measurable set $V \subseteq G$,

$$\lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{\#\{n < N : P_n \in V\}}{N} = \mu(V).$$

The typescript's "region" is read as an arbitrary measurable set, since the typescript does not
specify a class of regions; the set $V$ is quantified after $c$ and may depend on it.

Formalized by `MathlibExt.Dynamics.ScottishBook116bWanted.ScottishBookProblem116bClaim`, a definition declared in [WantedExt/Dynamics/ScottishBook116bWanted.lean, line 71](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/a3690dd2ad41e1ad4d871b062779ed7b0452800d/WantedExt/Dynamics/ScottishBook116bWanted.lean#L71). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/Probability/random-walks/random-products-in-compact-groups/index.html#scottish-book-problem116b-claim).

## Scottish Book Problem 116(b) fails for arbitrary measurable sets

The measurable-set form
of Scottish Book Problem 116(b) is false. For $\varphi, \psi$ in a group $G$ and a Boolean
sequence $c : \mathbb{N} \to \{\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}\}$, let $f_{n+1} = \varphi$ if
$c(n) = \mathrm{false}$ and $f_{n+1} = \psi$ if $c(n) = \mathrm{true}$, and define the successive
products by $P_0 = 1$ and $P_{n+1} = P_n f_{n+1}$. It is not the case that for every compact
Hausdorff topological group $G$ with its Borel $\sigma$-algebra and Haar probability measure
$\mu$, for $(\mu \otimes \mu)$-almost every pair $(\varphi, \psi) \in G \times G$ generating a
dense subgroup of $G$, for almost every $c$ under the fair coin product measure and every
measurable set $V \subseteq G$, the proportion of $P_0, \ldots, P_{N-1}$ that lie in $V$ tends
to $\mu(V)$ as $N \to \infty$.

Counterexample: take $G$ to be the circle $S^1$ with normalized Lebesgue measure. Almost every
pair $(\varphi, \psi)$ generates a dense subgroup. For any such pair and any $c$, the set $V_c$
of all successive products is countable, hence measurable with $\mu(V_c) = 0$, yet every
successive product lies in $V_c$, so the proportion equals $1$ for every $N \ge 1$. The set $V$
may depend on $c$ because it is quantified after $c$, and the only condition on $V$ is
measurability. The version restricted to sets $V$ whose frontier has measure $0$ is a different
statement, filed separately in OpenConjectures.
