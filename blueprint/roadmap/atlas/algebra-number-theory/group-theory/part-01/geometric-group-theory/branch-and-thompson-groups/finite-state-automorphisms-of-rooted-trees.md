## Automorphism of the regular rooted tree

For $d \in \mathbb{N}$, the vertices of the
$d$-regular rooted tree are the finite words over the alphabet $\{0, \dots, d-1\}$. A
permutation $f$ of this set of words is a tree automorphism when, for all words $u$ and $v$,
writing $|u|$ for the length of $u$ and $uv$ for the concatenation of $u$ and $v$, the word
$f(u)$ has length $|u|$ and the first $|u|$ letters of $f(uv)$ form $f(u)$.

## Section of a map of the regular rooted tree at a vertex

For $d \in \mathbb{N}$, let
$f$ be a permutation of the finite words over $\{0, \dots, d-1\}$ (the vertices of the
$d$-regular rooted tree) and let $u$ be a word, of length $|u|$. The section (state) of $f$ at
$u$ is the map sending each word $w$ to the word obtained from $f(uw)$ by deleting its first
$|u|$ letters, where $uw$ is the concatenation of $u$ and $w$.

## Finite-state automorphism of the regular rooted tree

For $d \in \mathbb{N}$, a
permutation $f$ of the finite words over $\{0, \dots, d-1\}$ is a finite-state automorphism of
the $d$-regular rooted tree when it is an automorphism of the tree and the sections of $f$ at

## Value of a word in the generators

For $d, k \in \mathbb{N}$, let $g_0, \dots, g_{k-1}$
be permutations of the finite words over $\{0, \dots, d-1\}$, and let $w$ be a word whose
letters are pairs $(i, b)$ with $i < k$ and $b$ a boolean. The value of $w$ is the element of
the group generated by $g_0, \dots, g_{k-1}$ obtained by multiplying, from left to right, $g_i$
for each letter $(i, \mathrm{true})$ and $g_i^{-1}$ for each letter $(i, \mathrm{false})$.

## Kourovka Problem 16.34: conjugacy in finite-state automorphism groups

The proposition
that every finitely generated group of finite-state automorphisms of a regular rooted tree has
decidable conjugacy problem: for all $d, k \in \mathbb{N}$ and all finite-state automorphisms
$g_0, \dots, g_{k-1}$ of the $d$-regular rooted tree, some algorithm decides, given two words
in the $g_i$ and their inverses, whether the elements they spell are conjugate in the group
generated by $g_0, \dots, g_{k-1}$.

The problem was posed by R. I. Grigorchuk, V. V. Nekrashevich and V. I. Sushchanskiĭ.

## Finite-state tree automorphism groups with undecidable conjugacy problem

The answer
to Kourovka Problem 16.34 is negative: there exist $d, k \in \mathbb{N}$ and finite-state
automorphisms $g_0, \dots, g_{k-1}$ of the $d$-regular rooted tree such that no algorithm
decides, given two words in the $g_i$ and their inverses, whether the elements they spell are
conjugate in the group generated by $g_0, \dots, g_{k-1}$.

Šunić and Ventura (Theorem 1.1) construct automaton groups, finitely generated groups
generated by finite self-similar sets of finite-state automorphisms of a regular rooted tree,
with unsolvable conjugacy problem; the Kourovka Notebook (21st issue) answers 16.34 "No, not
always".

## Faithful actions by finite-state tree automorphisms

A group $G$ acts faithfully on a
regular rooted tree by finite-state automorphisms when there are a natural number $d \ge 2$ and an
injective group homomorphism $\varphi$ from $G$ to the group of permutations of the set of finite
words over $\{0, \dots, d-1\}$ such that $\varphi(g)$ is a finite-state automorphism of the
$d$-regular rooted tree for every $g \in G$.

## Fedorova–Oliynyk theorem (Kourovka Problem 16.85)

For all groups $G$ and $H$, if $G$ and $H$
each act faithfully on a regular rooted tree by finite-state automorphisms, then their free product
$G \ast H$ acts faithfully on some regular rooted tree by finite-state automorphisms.

## Automorphisms of the regular rooted tree

For a natural number $d$, the $d$-regular rooted
tree has as vertices the finite words over the alphabet $\{0, \dots, d-1\}$; write $vw$ for the
concatenation of words $v$ and $w$. The root is the empty word and the children of a word $v$ are
the words $vi$ for letters $i$. A permutation $g$ of the words is a tree automorphism when for all
words $v$ and $w$ there is a word $u$ of the same length as $w$ with $g(vw) = g(v)u$.

For a permutation of the words this says that $g$ preserves lengths and the prefix order, so it is
an automorphism of the tree as a graph.

## Section of a word permutation at a vertex

For a natural number $d$, let $g$ be a
permutation of the finite words over the alphabet $\{0, \dots, d-1\}$ and let $v$ be a word. The
section (state) of $g$ at $v$ is the map sending a word $w$ to the word obtained from $g(vw)$ by
deleting its first $|g(v)|$ letters, where $vw$ is the concatenation of $v$ and $w$ and $|g(v)|$ is
the length of $g(v)$. When $g$ is a tree automorphism, the image of $w$ is the word $u$ with
$g(vw) = g(v)u$.

## Finite-state automorphisms of a regular rooted tree

For a natural number $d$, consider the
$d$-regular rooted tree whose vertices are the finite words over the alphabet $\{0, \dots, d-1\}$.
The group of finite-state automorphisms of this tree is the subgroup of the group of all
permutations of the words generated by the tree automorphisms that have only finitely many
distinct sections.

These automorphisms are closed under products and inverses, so the subgroup consists of exactly
them.
