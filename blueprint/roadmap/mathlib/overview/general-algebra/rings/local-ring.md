# Local ring

A **local ring** is a commutative [ring](ring.md) with exactly one [maximal ideal](../ideals-and-quotients/maximal-ideal.md). Equivalently, it is a nonzero ring in which the elements that are not units are closed under addition. Local rings are the algebraic counterpart of looking at a geometric object near a single point: the ring of germs of functions at a point (functions defined near it, identified when they agree near it), with the germs vanishing at the point as the maximal ideal, is the model case. Through [localization](localization.md) many questions about arbitrary commutative rings reduce to questions about local rings, which is why they are central in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry.

## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring with $1 \neq 0$. The following conditions are equivalent, and $R$ is called local when they hold.

1. $R$ has a unique maximal ideal.
2. The nonunits of $R$ form an [ideal](../ideals-and-quotients/ideal-of-a-commutative-ring.md).
3. Whenever $a + b = 1$ in $R$, at least one of $a$ and $b$ is a unit.

When $R$ is local, its maximal ideal is usually written $\mathfrak m$ (or $\mathfrak m_R$), and the [quotient ring](../ideals-and-quotients/quotient-ring.md) $k = R/\mathfrak m$ is a [field](../field-theory/field.md), called the residue field. One often speaks of the local ring $(R, \mathfrak m)$ or $(R, \mathfrak m, k)$.

The equivalence is short. If the nonunits form an ideal, that ideal is proper and contains every proper ideal, so it is the only maximal ideal. If $\mathfrak m$ is the only maximal ideal, every nonunit generates a proper ideal, which, like [every proper ideal, lies in some maximal ideal](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Maximal.html#Ideal.exists_le_maximal), and hence lies in $\mathfrak m$; so $\mathfrak m$ is exactly the set of nonunits. Condition 2 implies condition 3, because an ideal containing two elements with sum $1$ is the whole ring. Conversely, under condition 3 a multiple of a nonunit is a nonunit, and if nonunits $a$ and $b$ had a unit sum $u$, then $u^{-1}a + u^{-1}b = 1$ would be a sum of two nonunits; so the nonunits form an ideal.

## Examples

- **Fields.** A field is local, with maximal ideal $0$. Conversely, [a commutative local ring is a field exactly when its maximal ideal is zero](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/MaximalIdeal/Basic.html#IsLocalRing.isField_iff_maximalIdeal_eq).
- **Residues modulo a prime power.** For a prime $p$ and $n \ge 1$, the ring $\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z$ is local: a residue is a unit precisely when it is prime to $p$, so the nonunits are the multiples of $p$, which form an ideal.
- **Localization at a prime.** For a [prime ideal](../ideals-and-quotients/prime-ideal.md) $\mathfrak p$ of a commutative ring $R$, [the localization $R_{\mathfrak p}$ is a local ring](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/AtPrime/Basic.html#Localization.AtPrime.isLocalRing), and [its maximal ideal is $\mathfrak p R_{\mathfrak p}$, the ideal generated by the image of $\mathfrak p$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/AtPrime/Basic.html#Localization.AtPrime.map_eq_maximalIdeal). For instance, for a prime number $p$, the localization $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ consists of the rationals $a/b$ with $p \nmid b$; it is local, and its maximal ideal consists of those with $p \mid a$.
- **Power series.** Over a field $k$, a [power series](../polynomials-and-power-series/power-series.md) is invertible exactly when its constant term is nonzero, so $k[[X]]$ is local and [its maximal ideal is generated by $X$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/PowerSeries/Inverse.html#PowerSeries.maximalIdeal_eq_span_X).
- **$p$-adic integers.** For a prime $p$ the ring $\mathbb Z_p$ of [$p$-adic integers](../numbers/p-adic-integer.md) is local, and [its maximal ideal is generated by $p$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/PadicIntegers.html#PadicInt.maximalIdeal_eq_span_p).

Many familiar rings are not local. The [integers](../../../undergraduate/ring-theory/fundamentals/ring-structure-z.md) have the distinct maximal ideals $(2)$ and $(3)$, and for a field $k$, in the [polynomial](../polynomials-and-power-series/polynomial-in-one-indeterminate.md) ring $k[X]$ the nonunits $X$ and $1 - X$ add up to $1$. More generally, [a product of two nonzero rings is never local](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/NonLocalRing.html#IsLocalRing.not_isLocalRing_of_prod_of_nontrivial), since $(1, 0) + (0, 1) = (1, 1)$ is a sum of two nonunits.

## Properties

Let $(R, \mathfrak m)$ be a commutative local ring.

- **Characterizations.** A nonzero ring [is local as soon as its nonunits are closed under addition](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/Basic.html#IsLocalRing.of_nonunits_add), and conversely [in a local ring a sum of two nonunits is a nonunit](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/Basic.html#IsLocalRing.nonunits_add). In a local ring, [for every $a$ either $a$ or $1 - a$ is a unit](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/Basic.html#IsLocalRing.isUnit_or_isUnit_one_sub_self). A local ring [has exactly one maximal ideal](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/MaximalIdeal/Basic.html#IsLocalRing.maximal_ideal_unique), and [a commutative ring with exactly one maximal ideal is local](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/Basic.html#IsLocalRing.of_unique_max_ideal).
- **The maximal ideal.** [An element lies outside $\mathfrak m$ if and only if it is a unit](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/MaximalIdeal/Basic.html#IsLocalRing.notMem_maximalIdeal), and [every proper ideal is contained in $\mathfrak m$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/MaximalIdeal/Basic.html#IsLocalRing.le_maximalIdeal). In particular [the Jacobson radical of $R$, the intersection of all its maximal ideals, equals $\mathfrak m$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/MaximalIdeal/Basic.html#IsLocalRing.ringJacobson_eq_maximalIdeal).
- **Residue field.** [The residue field is the quotient $R/\mathfrak m$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/ResidueField/Defs.html#IsLocalRing.ResidueField), and [it is a field](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/ResidueField/Defs.html#IsLocalRing.ResidueField.field) because $\mathfrak m$ is maximal.
- **Quotients.** [The image of a local ring under a surjective ring homomorphism onto a nonzero ring is local](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/Basic.html#IsLocalRing.of_surjective'); in particular $R/I$ is local for every proper ideal $I$.
- **Local homomorphisms.** A ring homomorphism $f \colon R \to S$ is called [local](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Group/Units/Hom.html#IsLocalHom) if every element whose image is a unit is itself a unit. For local rings $(R, \mathfrak m)$ and $(S, \mathfrak n)$, [$f$ is local if and only if $f(\mathfrak m) \subseteq \mathfrak n$, and if and only if $f^{-1}(\mathfrak n) = \mathfrak m$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/RingHom/Basic.html#IsLocalRing.local_hom_tfae). A [locally ringed space](../../geometry/algebraic-geometry/locally-ringed-space.md) is a ringed space whose stalks are local rings, and its morphisms are required to induce local homomorphisms on stalks.
- **Nakayama's lemma.** For a finitely generated [module](../../linear-algebra/fundamentals/module.md) $M$ over $R$, [a submodule $N$ equals $M$ if and only if its image in $M/\mathfrak m M$ is all of $M/\mathfrak m M$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/Module.html#IsLocalRing.map_mkQ_eq_top). Consequently, elements of $M$ generate $M$ exactly when their images span the vector space $M/\mathfrak m M$ over the residue field.
- **Flat modules.** A module is free when it has a basis, and flat when tensoring with it preserves injective maps. [Every finitely generated flat module over a local ring is free](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/Module.html#Module.free_of_flat_of_isLocalRing). Projective modules (direct summands of free modules) are flat, so the same holds for finitely generated projective modules.

## Related concepts

Important special classes of local rings include discrete valuation rings (local principal ideal domains that are not fields), such as $k[[X]]$ and $\mathbb Z_p$, and Noetherian local rings, which are local rings that are also [Noetherian](noetherian-ring.md) and carry a well-behaved dimension theory. A semilocal ring is one with finitely many maximal ideals. In algebraic geometry, the stalk of the structure sheaf of a [scheme](../../geometry/algebraic-geometry/scheme.md) at a point is a local ring, and the residue field at the point is its residue field.

## In Mathlib

Mathlib defines being local as a property of a semiring that need not be commutative: [a semiring is local](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/Defs.html#IsLocalRing) when it is nontrivial (that is, $0 \neq 1$) and, whenever $a + b = 1$, [$a$ or $b$ is a unit](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/Defs.html#IsLocalRing.isUnit_or_isUnit_of_add_one). This is condition 3 above, a form that does not mention ideals and so needs neither commutativity nor a choice between left, right and two-sided ideals. For noncommutative rings it is stronger than having a unique maximal two-sided ideal: the ring of $2 \times 2$ matrices over a field has $0$ as its only maximal two-sided ideal, but it is not local. The maximal ideal is defined, for commutative semirings, as the set of nonunits, and the equivalence with having a unique maximal ideal is proved as a theorem in that setting. The residue field is defined for commutative rings. Local homomorphisms are defined more generally, for maps between arbitrary monoids, by the same condition that only units map to units.
