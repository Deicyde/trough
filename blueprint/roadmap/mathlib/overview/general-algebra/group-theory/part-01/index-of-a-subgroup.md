# Index of a subgroup

The **index of a subgroup** $H$ of a [group](group.md) $G$ is the number of cosets of $H$ in
$G$, written $[G : H]$. It measures how large $G$ is relative to $H$, and it stays meaningful for
infinite groups, where orders cannot be compared directly: the integers and the even integers are
both infinite, yet the even integers have index $2$ in the integers, since they and the odd
integers are the only two cosets. The index is the quantity behind
[Lagrange's theorem](../part-02/lagranges-theorem.md), and small indices force strong
structure, such as normality in index $2$.

## Definition

Let $H$ be a [subgroup](subgroup.md) of $G$. The left cosets $gH$, for $g \in G$, partition
$G$. The [index of $H$ in $G$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#Subgroup.index) is the number of left cosets, which may be
finite or infinite. Inversion $g \mapsto g^{-1}$ carries the left coset $gH$ to the right coset
$Hg^{-1}$, so [left and right cosets are equal in number](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Coset/Defs.html#QuotientGroup.quotientRightRelEquivQuotientLeftRel)
and either may be used. A subgroup is said to have finite index when $[G : H]$ is finite.

For any subgroup, [the index is the cardinality of the coset space $G/H$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#Subgroup.index_eq_card).
When $H$ is a [normal subgroup](normal-subgroup.md), $G/H$ is the
[quotient group](quotient-group.md) and the index is its order.

## Examples

- For every group, [$[G : G] = 1$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#Subgroup.index_top) and
  [$[G : \{e\}]$ is the order of $G$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#Subgroup.index_bot).
- In the [integers](../../numbers/integer.md), the cosets of $n\mathbb{Z}$ are the residue classes modulo $n$, so
  [the index of $n\mathbb{Z}$ is $|n|$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Data/ZMod/QuotientGroup.html#Int.index_zmultiples) for $n \neq 0$; the subgroup
  $\{0\}$ has infinite index.
- The [alternating group](../part-02/alternating-group.md) $A_n$, made of the even permutations, has
  [index $2$ in the symmetric group $S_n$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Alternating.html#alternatingGroup.index_eq_two) for $n \ge 2$.
- In $S_3$ the subgroup generated by one transposition has order $2$ and index $3$; it is not
  normal, so the normality that comes with index $2$ does not extend to index $3$.
- The integers have infinite index in the additive group of real numbers.

## Properties

- **Lagrange's formula.** For a subgroup $H$ of a group $G$,
  [$|G| = |H| \, [G : H]$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#Subgroup.card_mul_index). For finite $G$ this shows that both
  $|H|$ and $[G : H]$ divide $|G|$, and that $[G : H] = |G| / |H|$.
- **Tower law.** If $H \le K \le G$, then
  $$[G : H] = [G : K]\,[K : H],$$
  where [$[K : H]$ is the index of $H$ in $K$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#Subgroup.relIndex_mul_index). In particular
  [$[G : K]$ divides $[G : H]$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#Subgroup.index_dvd_of_le).
- **Index one.** [$[G : H] = 1$ exactly when $H = G$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#Subgroup.index_eq_one).
- **Index two.** [A subgroup of index $2$ is normal](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/IndexNormal.html#Subgroup.normal_of_index_eq_two): its only
  left cosets are $H$ and $G \setminus H$, and the same holds for right cosets, so $gH = Hg$ for
  every $g$. No finiteness of $G$ is needed.
- **Prime index.** If $[G : H]$ is a prime $p$, the tower law leaves no room for a subgroup strictly
  between $H$ and $G$, so [$H$ is a maximal subgroup](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/IndexNormal.html#Subgroup.isCoatom_of_index_prime).
- **Intersections.** For subgroups $H$ and $K$,
  [$[G : H \cap K] \le [G : H]\,[G : K]$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#Subgroup.index_inf_le), and
  [the intersection of two subgroups of finite index again has finite index](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#Subgroup.index_inf_ne_zero).
- **Homomorphisms.** For a [group homomorphism](group-morphism.md) $f$ defined on $G$,
  [the index of the kernel equals the order of the image](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#Subgroup.index_ker), a counting form
  of the [first isomorphism theorem](first-isomorphism-theorem.md).
- **Group actions.** When $G$ acts on a set $X$ (see [group action](group-action.md)), the
  [index of the stabilizer of a point is the size of its orbit](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#MulAction.index_stabilizer).
  This orbit-stabilizer relation underlies the [class formula](class-formula.md)
  and the counting in the Sylow theorems, beginning with the
  [first Sylow theorem](../part-02/first-sylow-theorem.md).
- **Normal core.** Every subgroup $H$ of finite index $n$ contains a normal subgroup of finite
  index, the intersection of all conjugates of $H$; this normal core comes from the action of $G$
  on the $n$ cosets of $H$, and [its index divides $n!$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/IndexNormal.html#Subgroup.index_normalCore_dvd_factorial_index).

## Generalizations

The relative index $[K : H \cap K]$ compares two subgroups $H$ and $K$ when neither contains the
other. When $H \le K$ it is the index of $H$ in the group $K$, the quantity that appears in the
tower law.

## In Mathlib

Mathlib defines the index of a subgroup as a natural number, the cardinality of the set of left
cosets, with the convention that an infinite set has cardinality $0$; so
[the index is $0$ exactly when there are infinitely many cosets](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#Subgroup.index_eq_zero_iff_infinite).
Under this convention Lagrange's formula, the tower law and the divisibility statements hold for
all groups without finiteness hypotheses: when some quantity is infinite, both sides of each
equation are $0$, and any number divides $0$. Finite index is recorded
by [a separate property of the subgroup](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#Subgroup.FiniteIndex), which results such as the
normal core bound assume. The [relative index](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#Subgroup.relIndex) of $H$ in $K$ is defined for
any two subgroups as the index of $H \cap K$ in $K$. The general results about the index of a
subgroup come with additive versions for additive groups, stated for additive subgroups.
