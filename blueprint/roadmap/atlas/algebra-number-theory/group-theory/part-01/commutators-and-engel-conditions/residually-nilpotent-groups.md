## Residually nilpotent group

A group $G$ is residually nilpotent if for every element
$g \ne 1$ of $G$ there is a normal subgroup $N$ of $G$ with $g \notin N$ such that the quotient
$G/N$ is nilpotent.

## Residually torsion-free nilpotent group

A group $G$ is residually torsion-free nilpotent
if for every element $g \ne 1$ of $G$ there is a normal subgroup $N$ of $G$ with $g \notin N$ such
that the quotient $G/N$ is nilpotent and torsion-free, that is, every nontrivial element of $G/N$
has infinite order.

## Mikhailov–Passi question on free central extensions (Kourovka 16.65)

The assertion that
there exist a free group $F$ and a normal subgroup $R$ of $F$ such that $F/R$ is finitely
presented and residually torsion-free nilpotent, while $F/[F, R]$ is not residually nilpotent,
where $[F, R]$ is the subgroup of $F$ generated by all commutators $[f, r]$ with $f \in F$ and
$r \in R$.

## Free central extensions need not be residually nilpotent

There exist a free group $F$ and a
normal subgroup $R$ of $F$ such that $F/R$ is finitely presented and residually torsion-free
nilpotent, while $F/[F, R]$ is not residually nilpotent; here $[F, R]$ is the subgroup of $F$
generated by all commutators $[f, r]$ with $f \in F$ and $r \in R$.
