# $p$-adic integer

For a [prime number](../../../undergraduate/ring-theory/divisibility-in-integral-domains/prime-numbers.md) $p$, the **$p$-adic integers** form the ring $\mathbb{Z}_p$ of
[$p$-adic numbers](p-adic-number.md) whose $p$-adic absolute value is at most $1$.
They play for the field $\mathbb{Q}_p$ the role that the
[integers](integer.md) play for the rational numbers, and they encode, in a single
ring, all the congruences modulo $p, p^2, p^3, \dots$ at once. This makes
$\mathbb{Z}_p$ a basic tool of number theory: a problem about solving a congruence
modulo every power of $p$ becomes a problem about solving an equation in
$\mathbb{Z}_p$, where analytic methods such as Newton's iteration are available.

## Definition

Fix a prime $p$. Every nonzero $p$-adic number $x$ has a valuation $v(x) \in \mathbb{Z}$,
and its absolute value is $|x|_p = p^{-v(x)}$, with $|0|_p = 0$. The ring of
$p$-adic integers is

$$\mathbb{Z}_p = \{\, x \in \mathbb{Q}_p : |x|_p \le 1 \,\},$$

the closed unit ball of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ ([the definition](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/PadicIntegers.html#PadicInt)). The
ultrametric inequality $|x + y|_p \le \max(|x|_p, |y|_p)$ shows that this set is
closed under addition, and multiplicativity of the absolute value shows it is closed
under multiplication; it contains $0$ and $1$ and, since $|-x|_p = |x|_p$, is closed
under negation, so $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is a [subring](../rings/subring.md) of $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

Two other descriptions give the same ring. Up to isomorphism, $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is the
[completion](../../topology/uniform-notions/completion.md) of $\mathbb{Z}$ for the metric
$d(a, b) = |a - b|_p$, in which integers are close when their difference is divisible
by a high power of $p$. It is also the inverse limit of the rings
$\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$: a $p$-adic integer amounts to a sequence of residues
$x_n \in \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$, each reducing to the previous one modulo $p^{n-1}$.
Concretely, every $p$-adic integer has a unique expansion

$$x = a_0 + a_1 p + a_2 p^2 + \cdots, \qquad 0 \le a_i \le p - 1,$$

which converges because $|p^n|_p = p^{-n}$ tends to $0$.

## Examples

- Every integer is a $p$-adic integer, and
  [an integer $k$ has absolute value less than $1$ exactly when $p$ divides $k$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/PadicIntegers.html#PadicInt.norm_int_lt_one_iff_dvd).
- More generally,
  [a rational number whose denominator in lowest terms is not divisible by $p$ has $p$-adic absolute value at most $1$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/PadicNumbers.html#Padic.norm_rat_le_one),
  so it lies in $\mathbb{Z}_p$; conversely, a rational number whose reduced denominator
  is divisible by $p$ does not. For instance $1/2 \in \mathbb{Z}_3$ but $1/3 \notin \mathbb{Z}_3$.
- The geometric series gives $1 + p + p^2 + \cdots = 1/(1 - p)$, and the expansion of
  $-1$ is $(p - 1) + (p - 1)p + (p - 1)p^2 + \cdots$, since its partial sums are
  $p^n - 1$.
- In $\mathbb{Z}_5$ there is a square root of $-1$: the congruence $x^2 \equiv -1$ has the
  solution $x \equiv 2 \pmod 5$, and Hensel's lemma (below) lifts it to an element of
  $\mathbb{Z}_5$ whose expansion begins $2 + 1 \cdot 5 + 2 \cdot 5^2 + \cdots$.

## Properties

**Units and factorization.** For $x \in \mathbb{Z}_p$,
[$x$ is a unit exactly when $|x|_p = 1$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/PadicIntegers.html#PadicInt.isUnit_iff), and
[it is a non-unit exactly when $|x|_p < 1$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/PadicIntegers.html#PadicInt.mem_nonunits). Every
nonzero $x$ has
[$|x|_p = p^{-v(x)}$ for a natural number $v(x)$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/PadicIntegers.html#PadicInt.norm_eq_zpow_neg_valuation), and
[it can be written as $x = u\,p^{v(x)}$ with $u$ a unit](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/PadicIntegers.html#PadicInt.unitCoeff_spec).
The valuation is additive on nonzero elements:
[$v(xy) = v(x) + v(y)$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/PadicIntegers.html#PadicInt.valuation_mul).

**Ideals.** $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is an integral domain of characteristic zero. It is a
[local ring](../rings/local-ring.md):
[its unique maximal ideal is the ideal generated by $p$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/PadicIntegers.html#PadicInt.maximalIdeal_eq_span_p),
and [$p$ is a prime element](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/PadicIntegers.html#PadicInt.prime_p) of $\mathbb{Z}_p$. Every
[ideal](../ideals-and-quotients/ideal-of-a-commutative-ring.md) is principal; in fact
[every nonzero ideal equals $p^n \mathbb{Z}_p$ for some $n \ge 0$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/PadicIntegers.html#PadicInt.ideal_eq_span_pow_p).
Thus $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is a discrete valuation ring, that is, a local
[principal ideal domain](../divisibility-in-integral-domains/principal-ideal-domain.md) that is not a field, and
[its field of fractions is $\mathbb{Q}_p$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/PadicIntegers.html#PadicInt.isFractionRing).

**Reduction modulo powers of $p$.** For each $n$ there is a ring homomorphism
$\mathbb{Z}_p \to \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$ that sends $x$ to the residue of the
truncation $a_0 + a_1 p + \cdots + a_{n-1}p^{n-1}$, and
[its kernel is $p^n \mathbb{Z}_p$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/RingHoms.html#PadicInt.ker_toZModPow). In particular
[the residue field, the quotient of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ by its maximal ideal $p\mathbb{Z}_p$, is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/RingHoms.html#PadicInt.residueField).
[Two $p$-adic integers are equal if their reductions modulo $p^n$ agree for every $n$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/RingHoms.html#PadicInt.ext_of_toZModPow).
The inverse-limit description is the following universal property: given a ring $R$ and
ring homomorphisms $f_n \colon R \to \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$ compatible with reduction,
[there is a ring homomorphism $R \to \mathbb{Z}_p$ whose reduction modulo $p^n$ is $f_n$ for every $n$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/RingHoms.html#PadicInt.lift_spec),
and [it is the only such homomorphism](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/RingHoms.html#PadicInt.lift_unique).

**Topology.** The absolute value makes $\mathbb{Z}_p$ a metric space satisfying
[the ultrametric inequality](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/PadicIntegers.html#PadicInt.nonarchimedean). It is
[complete](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/PadicIntegers.html#PadicInt.completeSpace) (see [completeness](../../topology/uniform-notions/completeness.md)) and
[compact](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/ProperSpace.html#PadicInt.compactSpace), and
[the integers are dense in it](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/RingHoms.html#PadicInt.denseRange_intCast). Each ideal
$p^n\mathbb{Z}_p$ is both open and closed, and the ring is totally disconnected.

**[Hensel's lemma](../number-theory/hensels-lemma-for-z-p.md).** Let $F$ be a polynomial with coefficients in
$\mathbb{Z}_p$ and $a \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ with $|F(a)|_p < |F'(a)|_p^2$. Then
[there is a unique root $z$ of $F$ with $|z - a|_p < |F'(a)|_p$, and it satisfies $|F'(z)|_p = |F'(a)|_p$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/Hensel.html#hensels_lemma).
When $|F'(a)|_p = 1$, this says that a simple root of $F$ modulo $p$ lifts to a root in
$\mathbb{Z}_p$.

## Related concepts

The field $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of [$p$-adic numbers](p-adic-number.md) is obtained from
$\mathbb{Z}_p$ by inverting $p$. Continuous functions on $\mathbb{Z}_p$ are described by
[Mahler's theorem](../../../thousand-plus/number-theory/part-04/mahlers-theorem.md), which expands them in binomial
coefficients. The [ring of Witt vectors](../number-theory/ring-of-witt-vectors.md) of the field with $p$
elements is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p$, and the Witt vector construction generalizes
$\mathbb{Z}_p$ to other perfect fields of characteristic $p$. The ring of integers of a
finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is again a complete discrete valuation ring and shares
most of the properties above.

## In Mathlib

Mathlib defines the $p$-adic integers for a natural number $p$ together with the
hypothesis that $p$ is prime, passed as a typeclass argument so that instance search can
find it once it is in scope; outside a few small explicit primes, the user puts that fact
in scope by hand. The ring is the
subtype of the $p$-adic numbers of norm at most $1$, with the ring operations inherited
from the subring, so membership is a property of a $p$-adic number rather than a separate
construction. The norm is real-valued and normalized so that $p$ has norm $1/p$. The
valuation of a $p$-adic integer is a natural number, and by convention
[the valuation of $0$ is $0$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/PadicIntegers.html#PadicInt.valuation_zero) rather than infinity, which
is why statements about the valuation carry nonzero hypotheses. The inverse-limit
description is not the definition but a theorem, phrased as the universal property
above, and the local, discrete valuation ring, domain and compactness properties are
recorded as typeclass instances.
