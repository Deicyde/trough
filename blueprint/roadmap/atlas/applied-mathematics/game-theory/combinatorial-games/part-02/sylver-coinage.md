## Legal moves in Sylver Coinage

For a set $M$ of natural numbers (a position of Sylver
Coinage), the set $L(M)$ of legal moves consists of the natural numbers that are not finite sums
of elements of $M$, repetitions allowed, i.e. the natural numbers outside the additive submonoid
of $\mathbb{N}$ generated by $M$. Since $0$ is the empty sum, every legal move is positive.

## P-positions of Sylver Coinage

A set $M$ of natural numbers is a P-position of Sylver
Coinage when $1 \notin M$ and, for every legal move $q$ of $M$, the position $M \cup \{q\}$ is
an N-position. P-positions and N-positions are defined together, as a mutual inductive
definition. In particular a position whose only legal move is $1$ is a P-position (the player to
move must name $1$ and loses).

## N-positions of Sylver Coinage

A position $M$ of Sylver Coinage is an N-position (the
player to move wins) when either $1 \in M$, so that the previous player named $1$ and lost, or
some legal move $q$ of $M$ leads to a P-position $M \cup \{q\}$. N-positions and P-positions are
defined together inductively by these rules.

P-position: a first player who opens with $p$ wins.

## Ends of a Sylver Coinage position

Let $M$ be a position of Sylver Coinage with set of
legal moves $L(M)$. A natural number $x$ is an end of $M$ if $x \in L(M)$ and every
$y \in L(M)$ with $y \ne x$ is still a legal move of the position $M \cup \{x\}$; that is,
naming $x$ eliminates no other legal move.

## Sources

## The largest legal move is an end

Let $M$ be a position of Sylver Coinage whose set of
legal moves $L(M)$ has a greatest element $F$. Then $F$ is an end of $M$.

Every other legal move $y$ is smaller than $F$, so a way of writing $y$ as a sum of elements of
$M \cup \{F\}$ cannot use $F$; hence naming $F$ eliminates no legal move other than $F$ itself.

## In an ender, every smaller move eliminates the largest legal move

Let $M$ be a set of
natural numbers whose set of legal moves $L(M)$ has a greatest element $F > 1$, and suppose that
$F$ is the only end of $M$ (so $M$ is an ender). Then for every legal move $x$ of $M$ with
$x < F$, the number $F$ is not a legal move of $M \cup \{x\}$.

Coinage whose set of legal moves $L(M)$ has a greatest element $F$ with $F > 1$. If every end of

## Two-generated numerical semigroups are symmetric

Let $a$, $b$ and $F$ be natural numbers
and let $S = \langle a, b \rangle$ be the additive submonoid of $\mathbb{N}$ generated by $a$ and
$b$. If $F \notin S$ and every natural number $n > F$ lies in $S$ (so $F$ is the Frobenius number
of $S$), then $x \in S \iff F - x \notin S$ for every natural number $x \le F$.

The hypotheses on $F$ force $\gcd(a, b) = 1$ and $a, b \ge 2$, so these are exactly the
numerical semigroups of embedding dimension $2$ other than $\mathbb{N}$.

## Sources

- Morgan Boyers, Natalie Burton, Jeff Rushall, James Warner and Tara Zurick, *On Winning Strategies in Sylver Coinage When 4 Has Been Chosen*, INTEGERS 24 (2024), Article G2, Proposition 3, part 1.

## Pseudo-symmetric semigroups of multiplicity 4 and embedding dimension 3

Write
$\langle A \rangle$ for the additive submonoid of $\mathbb{N}$ generated by a set $A$. Let $a$, $b$
and $F$ be natural numbers with $4 < a < b$, $a \notin \langle 4 \rangle$ and
$b \notin \langle 4, a \rangle$ (so $\{4, a, b\}$ is the minimal generating set of
$S = \langle 4, a, b \rangle$), and suppose that $F \notin S$ and that every natural number $n > F$
lies in $S$. Then $S$ is pseudo-symmetric, meaning that $F$ is even and $x \in S \iff
F - x \notin S$ for every natural number $x \le F$ with $x \ne F / 2$, if and only if $a$ is odd
and $b = a + 2$.

## A canonical position is the minimal generating set of its semigroup

Let $M$ be a finite
set of natural numbers whose set of legal moves $L(M)$ is finite, and suppose that removing any
element changes the legal moves: $L(M \setminus \{a\}) \ne L(M)$ for every $a \in M$. Let $S$ be
the additive submonoid of $\mathbb{N}$ generated by $M$. Then $M$ is the set of nonzero elements
$s \in S$ such that whenever $s = a + b$ with $a, b \in S$, one of $a$ and $b$ is $0$; that is,
$M$ is the minimal system of generators of $S$.

## Sources

- Morgan Boyers, Natalie Burton, Jeff Rushall, James Warner and Tara Zurick, *On Winning Strategies in Sylver Coinage When 4 Has Been Chosen*, INTEGERS 24 (2024), Article G2, Proposition 4, part 1.

## Sicherman's claim on replies to 4 and an odd number

For every odd natural number
$x \ge 5$ there is exactly one legal move $q$ of the position $\{4, x\}$ such that $\{4, x, q\}$
is a P-position, and every legal move $q$ of $\{4, x\}$ with $\{4, x, q\}$ a P-position
satisfies $q \equiv x - 2 \pmod 4$.

## Unique odd partner after 4

Let $q > 3$ be an odd natural number. Then $\{4, q\}$ is an
N-position of Sylver Coinage, and there is a unique odd natural number $x_q$ such that
$\{4, x_q\}$ is an N-position and $\{4, q, x_q\}$ is a P-position.

When $q < x_q$, the pair $(q, x_q)$ is a 4-pair.

## Conway's bounds for 4-pairs

Let $q$ and $x$ be odd natural numbers with $7 < q < x$ such
that $\{4, x\}$ is an N-position and $\{4, x, q\}$ is a P-position of Sylver Coinage. Then
$2q < x < 3q$.

By the characterization of Proposition 7, such pairs $(q, x)$ are the 4-pairs of the source.

## Four-element P-positions containing 4

Let $x > 1$ be a natural number. If
$x \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, then $\{4, x, x + 2, 6\}$ is a P-position; if $x \equiv 7 \pmod 8$, then
$\{4, x, x + 2, 10\}$ is a P-position; if $x \equiv 5 \pmod 8$, then $\{4, x, x + 6, 10\}$ is
a P-position; and if $x \equiv 1 \pmod 8$, then $\{4, x, x + 6, 14\}$ is a P-position.

In each case $x$ is odd. The analogous claim that $\{4, x, x - 2, 14\}$ is a P-position for
$x \equiv 5 \pmod 8$ is false: for $x = 13$, $\{4, 11, 13, 14\}$ is an N-position, since naming
$5$ wins.
