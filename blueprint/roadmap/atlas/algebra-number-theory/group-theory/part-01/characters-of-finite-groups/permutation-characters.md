## Complex permutation character on the cosets of a subgroup

For a subgroup $H$ of a group
$G$, the permutation character $1_H^G : G \to \mathbb{C}$ sends $g \in G$ to the number of left
cosets $xH$ of $H$ with $gxH = xH$, and to $0$ when $g$ fixes infinitely many cosets. For finite
$G$ and the trivial subgroup $H = 1$ this is the regular character $1^G$.

## Strunkov's class function

For elements $a, b$ of a group $G$, Strunkov's function
$\varphi : G \to \mathbb{C}$ is

$$\varphi = 1^G - 1_{\langle a \rangle}^G - 1_{\langle b \rangle}^G - 1_{\langle ab \rangle}^G + 2$$

where $1_H^G$ is the complex permutation character of $G$ on the cosets of a subgroup $H$,
$1^G = 1_1^G$ is the one for the trivial subgroup, $\langle x \rangle$ is the cyclic subgroup
generated by $x$, and $2$ is the constant function with value $2$.

## Characters of a group

A function $\varphi : G \to \mathbb{C}$ on a group $G$ is a
character of $G$ when there are a finite-dimensional complex vector space $V$ and a
representation $\rho$ of $G$ on $V$ whose character $g \mapsto \operatorname{tr} \rho(g)$ equals
$\varphi$.

## Strunkov's two-generation criterion (Kourovka 12.79)

The assertion that for every finite
group $G$ and all $a, b \in G$, if Strunkov's class function
$1^G - 1_{\langle a \rangle}^G - 1_{\langle b \rangle}^G - 1_{\langle ab \rangle}^G + 2$ is a
character of $G$, then $a$ and $b$ generate $G$. Here $1_H^G$ is the permutation character of $G$
on the cosets of a subgroup $H$, $1^G = 1_1^G$, $\langle x \rangle$ is the cyclic subgroup
generated by $x$, and $2$ is the constant function with value $2$.

## Strunkov's two-generation criterion fails (Kourovka 12.79)

It is not true that for every
finite group $G$ and all $a, b \in G$ such that Strunkov's class function
$1^G - 1_{\langle a \rangle}^G - 1_{\langle b \rangle}^G - 1_{\langle ab \rangle}^G + 2$ is a
character of $G$, the elements $a$ and $b$ generate $G$. Here $1_H^G$ is the permutation character
of $G$ on the cosets of a subgroup $H$, $1^G = 1_1^G$, $\langle x \rangle$ is the cyclic subgroup
generated by $x$, and $2$ is the constant function with value $2$.

Counterexample (S. V. Skresanov, 2019): $G = A_4$ and $a = b = (1\,2\,3)$. The function takes the
values $2, 2, -1, -1$ on the classes of $1$, $(1\,2)(3\,4)$, $(1\,2\,3)$, $(1\,3\,2)$, so it is
the sum of the two nontrivial linear characters of $A_4$, while $\langle a, b \rangle$ has
order $3$.

## Finite insoluble groups with independent permutation characters (Kourovka 11.9)

The assertion that there is a finite non-soluble group $G$ such that, choosing a representative
$H$ of each conjugacy class of subgroups of $G$, the rational-valued permutation characters
$1_H^G$ of $G$ on the left cosets of these subgroups $H$ are linearly independent over
$\mathbb{Q}$.

## No insoluble group has independent permutation characters (Kourovka 11.9)

There is no
finite non-soluble group $G$ such that, choosing a representative $H$ of each conjugacy class of
subgroups of $G$, the rational-valued permutation characters $1_H^G$ of $G$ on the left cosets of
these subgroups $H$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$.

Brescia, Ingrosso and Trombetti prove that these permutation characters are linearly independent
if and only if $G$ is cyclic; the Kourovka Notebook (21st issue) marks 11.9 solved, "No, there
are no such groups".
