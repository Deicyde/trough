## Homogeneous forms taking the values of a power on recurrence sequences

Let
$\gamma_0, \dots, \gamma_{k-1} \in \mathbb{C}$ and let $\chi(x) = x^k - \sum_{i\lt k} \gamma_i x^i$
be the characteristic polynomial of the recurrence $G_{n+k} = \sum_{i\lt k} \gamma_i G_{n+i}$. Let
$G^{(1)}, \dots, G^{(k)} \colon \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{C}$ be $\mathbb{C}$-linearly independent
sequences, each satisfying this recurrence for every $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. Then there is a
homogeneous polynomial $F$ of degree $k$ in $k$ variables with complex coefficients such that
$F(G^{(1)}_n, \dots, G^{(k)}_n) = ((-1)^k \chi(0))^n$ for every $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

For $k \ge 1$ we have $\chi(0) = -\gamma_0$, so the right side is $((-1)^{k+1} \gamma_0)^n$.
The source also assumes $\gamma_0 \neq 0$; for $\mathbb{Z}$-indexed solutions, linear
independence already forces it.

## Decomposable forms on consecutive terms of one recurrence sequence

Let
$\gamma_0, \dots, \gamma_{k-1} \in \mathbb{C}$, let $\chi(x) = x^k - \sum_{i\lt k} \gamma_i x^i$,
and let $G \colon \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{C}$ satisfy $G_{n+k} = \sum_{i\lt k} \gamma_i G_{n+i}$ for
every $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. Suppose that the $k$ vectors
$(G_j, G_{j+1}, \dots, G_{j+k-1}) \in \mathbb{C}^k$ for $0 \le j < k$ are linearly independent.
Then there are linear forms $L_1, \dots, L_k$ in $k$ variables with complex coefficients whose
product $F = L_1 \cdots L_k$ satisfies $F(G_n, G_{n+1}, \dots, G_{n+k-1}) = ((-1)^k \chi(0))^n$
for every $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

For $k \ge 1$ we have $\chi(0) = -\gamma_0$, so the right side is $((-1)^{k+1} \gamma_0)^n$.

## Algebraic dependence of recurrence sequences and a power sequence

Let $k$ be a natural
number, let $\gamma_0, \dots, \gamma_{k-1} \in \mathbb{C}$ and let
$\chi(x) = x^k - \sum_{i\lt k} \gamma_i x^i$. Let
$G^{(1)}, \dots, G^{(k)} \colon \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{C}$ be sequences such that
$G^{(j)}_{n+k} = \sum_{i\lt k} \gamma_i G^{(j)}_{n+i}$ for every $j$ and every $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
Then the family of the $k + 1$ sequences $G^{(1)}, \dots, G^{(k)}$ and $n \mapsto (-\chi(0))^n$
(integer powers, with $0^n = 0$ for $n < 0$) is not algebraically independent over $\mathbb{C}$
in the $\mathbb{C}$-algebra of all sequences $\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{C}$.

For $k \ge 1$ we have $\chi(0) = -\gamma_0$, so the last sequence is $n \mapsto \gamma_0^n$.

## Integer decomposable forms on integer recurrence sequences

Let
$\gamma_0, \dots, \gamma_{k-1} \in \mathbb{Z}$, let $\chi(x) = x^k - \sum_{i\lt k} \gamma_i x^i$,
suppose $\chi(0) \neq 0$, and let $G^{(1)}, \dots, G^{(k)} \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Z}$ each
satisfy $G_{n+k} = \sum_{i\lt k} \gamma_i G_{n+i}$ for every $n \ge 0$. Suppose that the
determinant $\Delta$ of the $k \times k$ matrix of initial values $(G^{(j)}_i)$, with rows
$1 \le j \le k$ and columns $0 \le i < k$, is nonzero. Then there is a polynomial $\tilde F$ in
$k$ variables with integer coefficients that factors over $\mathbb{C}$ as a product of $k$
linear forms and satisfies $\tilde F(G^{(1)}_n, \dots, G^{(k)}_n) = \Delta^k ((-1)^k \chi(0))^n$
for every $n \ge 0$.

For $k \ge 1$ we have $\chi(0) = -\gamma_0$, so the hypothesis says $\gamma_0 \neq 0$ and the
right side is $\Delta^k ((-1)^{k+1} \gamma_0)^n$. The theorem in the source says "homogeneous
form"; decomposability is the paper's description of $\tilde F$ in the introduction and follows
from Theorem 2, since $\tilde F$ is $\Delta^k$ times the form given there. The source's standing
assumption $k \ge 2$ is omitted (for $k \le 1$ the claim holds with $\tilde F$ constant or
linear); definitions are read from arXiv:2308.13817v1.

## Quadratic form identity for binary recurrences

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, let
$A, B \in R$, and let $(G_n)_{n \ge 0}$ and $(H_n)_{n \ge 0}$ be sequences in $R$ with
$G_{n+2} = A G_{n+1} + B G_n$ and $H_{n+2} = A H_{n+1} + B H_n$ for all $n \ge 0$. Put
$C_G = G_1^2 - A G_0 G_1 - B G_0^2$, $C_H = H_1^2 - A H_0 H_1 - B H_0^2$,
$E_{10} = G_1 H_1 - A G_1 H_0 - B G_0 H_0$, $E_{01} = G_1 H_1 - A G_0 H_1 - B G_0 H_0$ and
$\Delta = G_0 H_1 - G_1 H_0$. Then for every $n \ge 0$,

$$C_H G_n^2 - (E_{10} + E_{01}) G_n H_n + C_G H_n^2 = (-B)^n \Delta^2.$$

The source assumes $\Delta \neq 0$; the identity holds without it, since it is polynomial in the
initial values. The notation follows Section 6.1 of arXiv:2308.13817v1.
