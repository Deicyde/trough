## Propositional formulas

The propositional formulas over a set of atoms are generated by
the atoms, the constant truth $\top$, negation $\neg\varphi$ and conjunction
$\varphi \wedge \psi$.

## Evaluating a propositional formula at a letter

A letter is a set $s$ of atoms (the
atoms true at it). A propositional formula is evaluated at $s$ by recursion: an atom $a$ is true
iff $a \in s$, truth $\top$ is true, $\neg\varphi$ is true iff $\varphi$ is not, and
$\varphi \wedge \psi$ is true iff both $\varphi$ and $\psi$ are true.

Formalized by `Cslib.LTL.PropFormula.eval`, a definition declared in [CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean, line 96](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/a3690dd2ad41e1ad4d871b062779ed7b0452800d/CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean#L96). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/Logic/temporal-logic/b-chi-automata-for-a-fragment-of-linear-temporal/index.html#eval).

## Propositional formulas as LTL formulas

The translation of propositional formulas into LTL
formulas is defined by recursion: an atom $a$ goes to the LTL atom $a$, truth goes to the LTL
truth constant $\top$, $\neg\varphi$ goes to the LTL negation of the translation of $\varphi$, and
$\varphi \wedge \psi$ goes to the LTL conjunction of the translations of $\varphi$ and $\psi$.

Formalized by `Cslib.LTL.PropFormula.toLTL`, a definition declared in [CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean, line 103](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/a3690dd2ad41e1ad4d871b062779ed7b0452800d/CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean#L103). ## Propositional formulas are evaluated at the first letter

Let $\varphi$ be a
propositional formula and let $w = w_0 w_1 w_2 \dots$ be an ω-word whose letters are sets of
atoms. Under the valuation in which an atom holds at a letter exactly when it belongs to that
letter, $w$ satisfies the LTL translation of $\varphi$ if and only if $\varphi$ evaluates to true
on the first letter $w_0$.

## Propositional formulas are evaluated at the current letter

Let $\varphi$ be a
propositional formula, let $w = w_0 w_1 w_2 \dots$ be an ω-word whose letters are sets of atoms,
and let $k \in \mathbb{N}$. Under the valuation in which an atom holds at a letter exactly when it
belongs to that letter, the LTL translation of $\varphi$ holds at position $k$ of $w$ if and only
if $\varphi$ evaluates to true on the letter $w_k$.

## The always automaton recognizes G φ

For a propositional formula $\varphi$, the
one-state Büchi automaton for $\mathbf{G}\,\varphi$ accepts exactly the infinite words of letters
(sets of atoms) that satisfy the LTL formula $\mathbf{G}\,\varphi$, where an atom $a$ holds at a
letter $s$ iff $a \in s$.

Formalized by `Cslib.LTL.language_alwaysNa`, a theorem declared in [CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean, line 138](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/a3690dd2ad41e1ad4d871b062779ed7b0452800d/CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean#L138). ## The infinitely-often automaton recognizes G F φ

Let $\varphi$ be a propositional
formula and $p$ the set of letters (sets of atoms) at which $\varphi$ evaluates to true. The
two-state Büchi automaton for “infinitely often $p$” accepts exactly the infinite words that
satisfy the LTL formula $\mathbf{G}\,\mathbf{F}\,\varphi$, where an atom $a$ holds at a letter $s$
iff $a \in s$. Equivalently, a word is accepted iff $\varphi$ holds at infinitely many positions.

Formalized by `Cslib.LTL.language_always_eventually`, a theorem declared in [CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean, line 159](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/a3690dd2ad41e1ad4d871b062779ed7b0452800d/CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean#L159). ## The eventually-always automaton recognizes F G φ

## Büchi automaton for a propositional formula

For a propositional formula $\varphi$, this
Büchi automaton over letters that are sets of atoms has a start state $u$ and two verdict states
$t$ and $f$, of which only $t$ is accepting. From $u$, on a letter $s$ it moves to $t$ if
$\varphi$ evaluates to true at $s$ and to $f$ otherwise; from $t$ and from $f$ it stays in the
same state on every letter. So it records the verdict of $\varphi$ on the first letter.

Formalized by `Cslib.LTL.propNa`, a definition declared in [CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean, line 193](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/a3690dd2ad41e1ad4d871b062779ed7b0452800d/CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean#L193). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/Logic/temporal-logic/b-chi-automata-for-a-fragment-of-linear-temporal/index.html#prop-na).

## The shift of a Büchi automaton

Let $A$ be a Büchi automaton over the alphabet of sets of
atoms. Its shift is the Büchi automaton whose states are the states of $A$ together with one new
state $\bot$, whose only start state is $\bot$, and whose accepting states are the accepting states
of $A$. Its transitions are exactly the following: on every letter, from $\bot$ to each start state
of $A$; and on a letter $x$, from a state $q$ of $A$ to a state $q'$ of $A$ whenever $A$ has a
transition from $q$ to $q'$ on $x$.

The shift accepts a word exactly when $A$ accepts the word with its first letter removed.

Formalized by `Cslib.LTL.shiftBuchi`, a definition declared in [CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean, line 260](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/a3690dd2ad41e1ad4d871b062779ed7b0452800d/CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean#L260). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/Logic/temporal-logic/b-chi-automata-for-a-fragment-of-linear-temporal/index.html#shift-buchi).

## The shift automaton reads the tail

For a Büchi automaton $A$ over letters that are sets
of atoms, the shift automaton of $A$ accepts an infinite word $x_0 x_1 x_2 \ldots$ iff $A$
accepts its tail $x_1 x_2 x_3 \ldots$.

## The restricted safety fragment of LTL

The safety formulas over a type of atoms are
generated by the following rules: every propositional formula $\varphi$ is a safety formula; if
$\psi$ and $\rho$ are safety formulas, then so are $\psi \wedge \rho$, $\psi \vee \rho$ and
$X\,\psi$ (next $\psi$); and for every propositional formula $\varphi$, $G\,\varphi$ (always
$\varphi$) is a safety formula.

The operator $G$ is applied only to propositional formulas: for atoms $p$ and $q$, the formula
$G(p \vee X\,q)$ is not in this fragment, since $G$ does not distribute over $\vee$.

Formalized by `Cslib.LTL.Safety`, an inductive type declared in [CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean, line 355](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/a3690dd2ad41e1ad4d871b062779ed7b0452800d/CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean#L355). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/Logic/temporal-logic/b-chi-automata-for-a-fragment-of-linear-temporal/index.html#safety).

## Safety formulas as LTL formulas

The translation $t$ of safety formulas into LTL formulas
is defined by recursion: $t(\varphi)$ is the LTL translation of $\varphi$ for a propositional
formula $\varphi$; $t(\psi \wedge \rho) = t(\psi) \wedge t(\rho)$;
$t(\psi \vee \rho) = \neg(\neg t(\psi) \wedge \neg t(\rho))$, the derived LTL disjunction;
$t(X\,\psi) = X\,t(\psi)$, where $X$ is next; and $t(G\,\varphi) = G\,t(\varphi)$ for a
propositional formula $\varphi$, where $G$ is the derived LTL operator always.

Formalized by `Cslib.LTL.Safety.toLTL`, a definition declared in [CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean, line 370](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/a3690dd2ad41e1ad4d871b062779ed7b0452800d/CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean#L370). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/Logic/temporal-logic/b-chi-automata-for-a-fragment-of-linear-temporal/index.html#to-ltl-2).

## Büchi automata for the restricted safety fragment

Take as letters the sets of atoms,
an atom $a$ holding at a letter $s$ iff $a \in s$. For every formula $\psi$ of the restricted
safety fragment there is a nondeterministic Büchi automaton with finitely many states whose
ω-language is exactly the set of infinite words satisfying $\psi$, read as an LTL formula.

The finite state set is what makes the automaton a certificate of ω-regularity: every
ω-language is accepted by some Büchi automaton with infinitely many states. The fragment applies
the always operator $\mathbf{G}$ only to propositional formulas, so for example
$\mathbf{G}(p \vee \mathbf{X}\,q)$, for atoms $p$ and $q$ and the next operator $\mathbf{X}$, is
not covered.

Formalized by `Cslib.LTL.exists_buchi_of_safety`, a theorem declared in [CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean, line 386](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/a3690dd2ad41e1ad4d871b062779ed7b0452800d/CSLibExt/Logics/LTL/FragmentBuchi.lean#L386). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/Logic/temporal-logic/b-chi-automata-for-a-fragment-of-linear-temporal/index.html#exists-buchi-of-safety).
