## Locally finite fields (Kourovka 18.59)

A field $P$ is locally finite when, for every
finite subset $s$ of $P$, the subfield of $P$ generated by $s$ is finite.

## Kourovka Problem 18.59 on involution centralizers

The proposition (Kourovka 18.59, D. V.
Lytkina) that there are a periodic group $G$ which contains an involution and in which all
involutions are conjugate, and an infinite locally finite field $P$ of characteristic $2$, such
that the centralizer of every involution $i$ of $G$ is isomorphic to
$\langle i \rangle \times \mathrm{PSL}_2(P)$. Here $\langle i \rangle$ is the cyclic subgroup
generated by $i$ and $\mathrm{PSL}_2(P) = L_2(P)$ is the quotient of $\mathrm{SL}_2(P)$ by its
centre.

The field $P$ is chosen once for all involutions; since all involutions are conjugate, this is
equivalent to allowing a field for each involution.

Formalized by `MathlibExt.GroupTheory.InvolutionCentralizerPSLTwoKourovka18Problem59Wanted.periodicGroupWithPSLInvolutionCentralizers`, a definition declared in [WantedExt/GroupTheory/InvolutionCentralizerPSLTwoKourovka18Problem59Wanted.lean, line 45](https://github.com/Deicyde/atlas-lean/blob/a3690dd2ad41e1ad4d871b062779ed7b0452800d/WantedExt/GroupTheory/InvolutionCentralizerPSLTwoKourovka18Problem59Wanted.lean#L45). [Its page on the ATLAS site](https://deicyde.github.io/atlas-lean/roadmap/GroupTheory/finite-simple-groups/involution-centralizers/index.html#periodic-group-with-pslinvolution-centra).

## Negative answer to Kourovka Problem 18.59

There is no periodic group $G$ that contains an
involution, in which all involutions are conjugate, and for which there is an infinite locally
finite field $P$ of characteristic $2$ such that the centralizer of every involution $i$ of $G$ is
isomorphic to $\langle i \rangle \times \mathrm{PSL}_2(P)$. Here $\langle i \rangle$ is the cyclic
subgroup generated by $i$ and $\mathrm{PSL}_2(P)$ is the quotient of $\mathrm{SL}_2(P)$ by its
centre.

Muliarchyk's Theorem 3 proves more: no group, periodic or not, has a single nonempty conjugacy
class of involutions whose centralizers are isomorphic to $C_2 \times \mathrm{PSL}_2(P)$ for an
infinite locally finite field $P$ of characteristic $2$.

## Involutions commuting with an element

For an element $t$ of a group $G$, this is the
set of involutions of $G$ that commute with $t$: the elements $s \in G$ of order $2$ with
$st = ts$.

## Strunkov's finiteness question for simple groups (Kourovka 11.96(a))

This is the statement
that for every natural number $n$ there is a bound $N$ such that every finite simple group $G$
containing an involution $t$ that commutes with at most $n$ involutions of $G$ (counting $t$
itself) has order at most $N$. Bounding the order expresses "only finitely many such groups up to
isomorphism".

## Finitely many finite simple groups with an involution commuting with few involutions

For
every natural number $n$ there is a bound $N$ such that every finite simple group $G$ containing
an involution $t$ that commutes with at most $n$ involutions of $G$ (counting $t$ itself) has
order at most $N$.

This answers Kourovka Problem 11.96(a) in the affirmative; the Kourovka Notebook (21st issue)
marks it solved, "Yes", modulo the classification of finite simple groups.

## An infinite simple group with an involution commuting with few involutions

There exist a
natural number $n$ and an infinite simple group $G$ containing an involution $t$ that commutes
with at most $n$ involutions of $G$ (counting $t$ itself). This answers Kourovka Problem 11.96(b)
in the negative.

For example, $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{R})$ is an infinite simple group that contains an involution
but no Klein four-subgroup, so each of its involutions commutes with no involution other than
itself ($n = 1$); the Kourovka Notebook (21st issue) marks 11.96(b) solved, "No", citing Skresanov.
