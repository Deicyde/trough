# Consequences of 1571

Magmas satisfying [E1571](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?1571) $({x = (y \diamond z) \diamond (y \diamond (x \diamond z))})$ also satisfy [E2662](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?2662), [E40](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?40), [E23](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?23), [E8](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?8), [E16](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?16), [E14](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?14), [E43](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?43), and [E4512](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?4512), and are in fact abelian groups of exponent two. Conversely, all abelian groups of exponent two satisfy [E1571](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?1571).

## Proof

Suppose that a magma $G$ satisfies [E1571](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?1571), thus

$$
x = (y \diamond z) \diamond (y \diamond (x \diamond z)).\qquad\text{(9)}
$$

$$
x = ((x \diamond y) \diamond (x \diamond y)) \diamond ((x \diamond y) \diamond (x \diamond (x \diamond y)))
$$

and

$$
x = (x \diamond y) \diamond (x \diamond (x \diamond y))
$$

whence

$$
x = ((x \diamond y) \diamond (x \diamond y)) \diamond x
$$

which is [E2662](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?2662). This gives

$$
y = ((y \diamond z) \diamond (y \diamond z)) \diamond y
$$

while from (9) one has

$$
(y \diamond z) \diamond (y \diamond z) = (x \diamond y) \diamond (x \diamond ((y \diamond z) \diamond (y \diamond z) \diamond y))
$$

whence

$$
(x \diamond y) \diamond (x \diamond y) = (y \diamond z) \diamond (y \diamond z).
$$

This implies that $(x \diamond y) \diamond (x \diamond y)$ does not depend on $x$, or on $y$, hence is equal to some constant $e$:

$$
(x \diamond y) \diamond (x \diamond y) = e.
$$

From (9) the magma operation is surjective, hence

$$
x \diamond x = e\qquad\text{(10)}
$$

which gives [E40](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?40). Applying (9) with $x=y=z$ we conclude

$$
x = e \diamond (x \diamond e)
$$

while if we instead take $y=z=e$ we have

$$
x = e \diamond (e \diamond (x \diamond e))
$$

hence

$$
x = e \diamond x
$$

and then also

$$
x = x \diamond e
$$

from which we readily conclude [E23](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?23), [E8](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?8); thus $e$ is an identity element. From (9) with $z=e$ we now have

$$
x = y \diamond (y \diamond x)\qquad\text{(11)}
$$

which is [E16](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?16). If instead we take $y=e$ we have

$$
x = z \diamond (x \diamond z)\qquad\text{(12)}
$$

which is [E14](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?14). So if we substitute $z = x \diamond y$ and use (11) we obtain

$$
x = (x \diamond y) \diamond y
$$

and hence

$$
y \diamond x = y \diamond ((x \diamond y) \diamond y) = x \diamond y
$$

thanks to (12). This gives [E43](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?43), thus $G$ is now commutative. From (9) once more one has

$$
x \diamond (y \diamond z) = (y \diamond x) \diamond (z \diamond ((x \diamond (y \diamond z)) \diamond x))
$$

which one can simplify using commutativity and (11) (or (12)) to eventually obtain

$$
x \diamond (y \diamond z) = (x \diamond y) \diamond z
$$

which is [E4512](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/implications/?4512). $G$ is now commutative and associative, and every element is its own inverse and of exponent $2$, hence is an abelian group thanks to (10), so $G$ is an abelian group of exponent $2$ as claimed. The converse is easily verified.
