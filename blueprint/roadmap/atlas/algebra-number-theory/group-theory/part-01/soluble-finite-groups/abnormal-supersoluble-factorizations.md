## Supersoluble groups via cyclic normal series

A group $G$ is supersoluble if there are a
natural number $k$ and subgroups $1 = S_0 \le S_1 \le \cdots \le S_k = G$, each normal in $G$, such
that for every $i < k$ we have $S_i \le S_{i+1}$ and there is an element $x \in S_{i+1}$ such that
$S_{i+1}$ is generated by $x$ and $S_i$; that is, every factor $S_{i+1}/S_i$ is cyclic.

## Abnormal subgroup

A subgroup $H$ of a group $G$ is abnormal in $G$ if every element
$g \in G$ lies in the subgroup generated by $H$ and its conjugate $gHg^{-1}$.

## Abnormal supersoluble trifactorizations (Kourovka 19.100)

The proposition that for every
finite group $G$ and all subgroups $A$, $B$, $C$ of $G$ that are abnormal in $G$ and supersoluble,
if $G = AB = BC = CA$ as sets, then $G$ is supersoluble.

## Kourovka Problem 19.100 has a positive answer

Let $G$ be a finite group and let $A$, $B$,
$C$ be subgroups of $G$, each abnormal in $G$ and supersoluble, such that $G = AB = BC = CA$ as
sets. Then $G$ is supersoluble.

The Kourovka Notebook (21st issue) answers 19.100 "Yes, it is" (L. S. Kazarin and V. N. Tyutyanov,
2023).
