An **ideal** of a commutative [ring](trough:Ring) $R$ is a nonempty subset of $R$ that
is closed under addition and under multiplication by arbitrary elements of $R$.
Ideals play for rings the part that [normal subgroups](trough:Subgroup.Normal)
play for groups: they are exactly the kernels of [ring morphisms](trough:RingHom),
and they are what one divides by to form [quotient rings](trough:Ideal.Quotient.ring).
They also carry the arithmetic of divisibility over from elements to sets, which
is how factorization is studied in rings where elements fail to factor uniquely.

## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring. An [ideal](lean:Ideal) of $R$ is a subset
$I \subseteq R$ such that

- $0 \in I$;
- if $a, b \in I$, then $a + b \in I$;
- if $b \in I$ and $a \in R$, then [$ab \in I$](lean:Ideal.mul_mem_left).

Taking $a = -1$ in the last condition shows that $I$ is closed under negation,
so an ideal is an additive subgroup of $R$ that absorbs multiplication by
elements of $R$. Equivalently, an ideal is a submodule of $R$ regarded as a
[module](trough:Module) over itself. Every ring has the zero ideal $\{0\}$ and
the ideal $R$ itself; an ideal different from $R$ is called proper.

For a subset $S \subseteq R$, the ideal generated by $S$ is the intersection of
all ideals containing $S$; it consists of the finite sums $a_1 s_1 + \dots + a_n s_n$
with $a_i \in R$ and $s_i \in S$, including the empty sum $0$, and it is the smallest ideal containing $S$:
[it lies inside an ideal $I$ exactly when $S \subseteq I$](lean:Ideal.span_le)
(see [generating sets](trough:Submodule.span)). For instance,
[$z$ lies in the ideal generated by $x$ and $y$ exactly when $z = ax + by$ for some $a, b \in R$](lean:Ideal.mem_span_pair).
An ideal generated by a single element $x$ is principal and is written $(x)$ or
$xR$; it consists of the multiples of $x$, since
[$z \in (x)$ exactly when $x$ divides $z$](lean:Ideal.mem_span_singleton).

## Examples

- In the [integers](trough:Int), the multiples of a fixed integer $n$ form the
  ideal $n\mathbb{Z}$. These are all the ideals:
  [every ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$ is generated by a single nonnegative integer](lean:Int.ideal_span_absNorm_eq_self).
  The quotient of $\mathbb{Z}$ by $n\mathbb{Z}$, for a natural number $n$, is
  [the ring of integers modulo $n$](lean:Int.quotientSpanNatEquivZMod).
- In a [field](trough:Field) $K$,
  [the only ideals are $\{0\}$ and $K$](lean:Ideal.eq_bot_or_top), since a nonzero
  element of an ideal can be multiplied by its inverse to give $1$. Conversely,
  [a commutative ring is a field exactly when it has precisely two ideals](lean:Ring.isField_iff_isSimpleOrder_ideal).
- For a commutative ring $R$ and an element $c \in R$, the
  [polynomials](trough:Polynomial) $f \in R[x]$ with $f(c) = 0$ form an ideal,
  the kernel of evaluation at $c$, and
  [this ideal is generated by $x - c$](lean:Polynomial.ker_evalRingHom).
- In $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ the ideal $(2, x)$, made of the polynomials with even
  constant term, is not principal: a generator would have to divide both $2$
  and $x$, so it would be $\pm 1$, but $1 \notin (2, x)$.

## Properties

**Ideals and units.** An ideal is the whole ring as soon as it contains a unit:
[an ideal containing an invertible element is all of $R$](lean:Ideal.eq_top_of_isUnit_mem),
and in particular [$I = R$ exactly when $1 \in I$](lean:Ideal.eq_top_iff_one).
For principal ideals,
[$(x) = R$ exactly when $x$ is a unit](lean:Ideal.span_singleton_eq_top).

**Divisibility.** Inclusion of principal ideals reverses divisibility:
[$(x) \subseteq (y)$ exactly when $y$ divides $x$](lean:Ideal.span_singleton_le_span_singleton).
In an integral domain,
[$(x) = (y)$ exactly when $x$ and $y$ are associates](lean:Ideal.span_singleton_eq_span_singleton),
that is, when $x = uy$ for a unit $u$. Elements $x$ and $y$ are
[coprime](trough:IsCoprime), meaning $ax + by = 1$ for some $a, b \in R$,
[exactly when $(x) + (y) = R$](lean:Ideal.sup_eq_top_iff_isCoprime).

**Operations.** The ideals of $R$ are closed under several operations.

- The intersection $I \cap J$ is an ideal, and so is the intersection of any
  family of ideals: [an element lies in it exactly when it lies in every member](lean:Ideal.mem_sInf).
- The sum $I + J$ is the smallest ideal containing both;
  [its elements are the sums $a + b$ with $a \in I$ and $b \in J$](lean:Submodule.mem_sup).
- The product $IJ$ is the ideal generated by the products $ab$ with $a \in I$
  and $b \in J$, so [it contains each such product](lean:Ideal.mul_mem_mul), and
  [it is contained in $I \cap J$](lean:Ideal.mul_le_inf). For principal ideals,
  [$(x)(y) = (xy)$](lean:Ideal.span_singleton_mul_span_singleton).

**Kernels and quotients.** For a ring morphism $f : R \to S$, the
[kernel](lean:RingHom.ker), the set of $r \in R$ with
[$f(r) = 0$](lean:RingHom.mem_ker), is an ideal of $R$. More generally the
preimage of an ideal of $S$ is an ideal of $R$, though the image of an ideal
need not be an ideal: the image of $\mathbb{Z}$ in the rational numbers is not
one. Conversely, every ideal $I$ is a kernel: in the quotient ring $R/I$,
[two elements $a$ and $b$ have the same class exactly when $a - b \in I$](lean:Ideal.Quotient.eq),
and [the kernel of the quotient map $R \to R/I$ is $I$](lean:Ideal.mk_ker).

**Maximal ideals.** By Zorn's lemma,
[every proper ideal is contained in a maximal ideal](lean:Ideal.exists_le_maximal),
an ideal that is proper and contained in no larger proper ideal.

## Related concepts

Special kinds of ideals are defined through their quotients: for a commutative
ring, $I$ is a [prime ideal](trough:Ideal.IsPrime) exactly when
[$R/I$ is an integral domain](lean:Ideal.Quotient.isDomain_iff_prime), and a
[maximal ideal](trough:Ideal.IsMaximal) exactly when
[$R/I$ is a field](lean:Ideal.Quotient.maximal_ideal_iff_isField_quotient).
A ring whose ideals are all principal is a principal ideal ring; an integral
domain with this property is a [principal ideal domain](trough:Submodule.IsPrincipal),
a class that includes every [Euclidean domain](trough:EuclideanDomain). A ring in
which every ideal is generated by finitely many elements is a
[Noetherian ring](trough:IsNoetherianRing). The
[first isomorphism theorem](trough:RingHom.quotientKerEquivOfSurjective) and the
[Chinese remainder theorem](trough:Ideal.quotientInfRingEquivPiQuotient) are
statements about ideals and their quotients, and
[fractional ideals](trough:FractionalIdeal) extend the notion to certain submodules of
the field of fractions of a domain, those that become ideals after
multiplication by a single nonzero element of the domain.

## In Mathlib

Mathlib defines an ideal for any semiring, not only for commutative rings, as a
submodule of the ring viewed as a module over itself. In a noncommutative ring
this is a left ideal; whether it is also closed under multiplication on the right
is a separate property, which holds automatically for every ideal of a
commutative ring, so results stated for two-sided ideals apply to commutative
rings without extra hypotheses. Because ideals are submodules, the general facts
about submodules apply to them directly. The ideals of a ring are ordered by
inclusion and form a complete lattice, in which the zero ideal is the least
element, the whole ring is the greatest, intersection is the meet and sum is the
join; the ideal generated by a set is the span of that set. Images of ideals
under a morphism are defined as the ideal generated by the image, which may be
larger than the image itself.
