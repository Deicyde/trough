> **Stacks 019K** (part 1).
> Given simplicial objects `U`, `V` of a category `C` for which the product
> `U × Δ[1] = U.copower Δ[1]` of [Stacks, Tag 017C] exists, a morphism `h : U × Δ[1] ⟶ V` is a
> *homotopy from `a` to `b`* for `a, b : U ⟶ V` when `a = e₀ ≫ h` and `b = e₁ ≫ h`, where
> `e₀, e₁ : U ⟶ U × Δ[1]` come from the two vertices `Δ[0] ⟶ Δ[1]` and the identification
> `U × Δ[0] = U`. In degree `m` these two vertex inclusions are the inclusions of the summands of
> `∐_{α ∈ Δ[1]_m} U_m` indexed by the constant `m`-simplices `SSet.stdSimplex.const 1 0 m` and
> `SSet.stdSimplex.const 1 1 m` of `Δ[1]`, which is how the two conditions are spelled here;
> equivalently, `h_{m, m + 1} = a_m` and `h_{m, 0} = b_m` in the notation of [Stacks, Tag 019L].

The attribute's comment: “item (2): a and b are homotopic, in the same homotopy class”.

> **Stacks 019K** (part 2).
> Two morphisms `a, b : U ⟶ V` of simplicial objects are *homotopic*, or *in the same homotopy
> class*, when there is a sequence of morphisms `a = a₀, a₁, …, aₙ = b` from `U` to `V` such that
> for each `i = 1, …, n` there is a homotopy from `aᵢ₋₁` to `aᵢ` or a homotopy from `aᵢ` to
> `aᵢ₋₁` (`IsHomotopy`). Such chains are exactly the equivalence relation generated by "there is a
> homotopy from `a` to `b`", which is how it is spelled here: `Relation.EqvGen.rel` is a single
> step, and `Relation.EqvGen.refl`, `Relation.EqvGen.symm`, `Relation.EqvGen.trans` are the empty
> chain, the reversal of a chain and the concatenation of two chains.

```lean
public def Homotopic (a b : U ⟶ V) : Prop
```

> **Stacks 019M** (the definition of being homotopic).
> Let `C` be any category and let `a, b : U ⟶ V` be morphisms of simplicial objects of `C`. A
> *homotopy from `a` to `b`* is a family of morphisms `h_{n, i} : U_n ⟶ V_n`, for `n ≥ 0` and
> `i = 0, …, n + 1`, satisfying the relations of [Stacks, Tag 019L], that is a family `hc` with
> `HtpyRel a b hc`. The morphisms `a` and `b` are *homotopic* when there is a chain
> `a = a₀, a₁, …, a_n = b` such that for each `i` there is a homotopy from `a_{i - 1}` to `a_i` or
> from `a_i` to `a_{i - 1}`: this is the equivalence relation generated by the existence of a
> homotopy, whose constructors `Relation.EqvGen.rel`, `Relation.EqvGen.refl`,
> `Relation.EqvGen.symm` and `Relation.EqvGen.trans` produce exactly these chains.

```lean
public def HtpyHomotopic {U V : SimplicialObject C} (a b : U ⟶ V) : Prop
```

> **Stacks 08RJ**.
> Let `C` be any category. Being homotopic — the equivalence relation on morphisms of simplicial
> objects of `C` generated by the existence of a simplicial homotopy — is a congruence: if `a` and
> `a'` are homotopic then so are `b ∘ a` and `b ∘ a'` for any `b : V ⟶ W`, and so are `a ∘ c` and
> `a' ∘ c` for any `c : X ⟶ U`. Hence `CategoryTheory.Quotient` of this relation is the source's
> `hSimp(C)`, the category whose objects are the simplicial objects of `C` and whose morphisms are
> the homotopy classes of morphisms of simplicial objects, and `CategoryTheory.Quotient.functor` is
> the canonical functor `Simp(C) ⟶ hSimp(C)`, essentially surjective and surjective on sets of
> morphisms.

```lean
public theorem congruence_homotopic :
    Congruence fun (U V : SimplicialObject C) (a a' : U ⟶ V) ↦
      Relation.EqvGen (fun a a' : U ⟶ V ↦ Nonempty (Homotopy a a')) a a'
```

> inclusion as a homotopy inverse. Let `U` be a simplicial object of a category `C`, and let `X`
> be a cylinder on `U`: a simplicial object together with morphisms `ι m α : U_m ⟶ X_m`, one for

> Products of homotopies and of homotopy equivalences of simplicial objects. Let `X t`, `Y t`
> (`t : T`) be families of simplicial objects of a category `C`, and let `p t : P ⟶ X t` and
> `q t : Q ⟶ Y t` exhibit `P` and `Q` as the products `∏_t X t` and `∏_t Y t`, that is, degreewise
> products in the sense of [Stacks, Tag 016Q]. Let `a t, b t : X t ⟶ Y t` be morphisms and let
> `f, g : P ⟶ Q` be their products, pinned by `f ≫ q t = p t ≫ a t` and `g ≫ q t = p t ≫ b t`. Then:
> * if there is a homotopy from `a t` to `b t` for every `t`, there is a homotopy from `f` to `g`
>   (part (2), no finiteness needed);
> * if `a t` and `b t` are homotopic for every `t` and `a t = b t` for all but finitely many `t`,
>   then `f` and `g` are homotopic (part (3));
> * if `T` is finite and every `a t` is a homotopy equivalence, then `f` is a homotopy
>   equivalence, so `∏_t X t` and `∏_t Y t` are homotopy equivalent (part (1)).
