# Free algebra of a commutative ring

The **free algebra** on a set $X$ over a commutative ring $R$, written $R\langle X\rangle$, is the
[associative algebra](associative-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring.md) with identity generated by the elements of $X$ subject to no
relations other than those every associative $R$-algebra must satisfy. Its elements are the
noncommutative polynomials in variables from $X$: finite $R$-linear combinations of words in those
variables, in which $xy$ and $yx$ are different monomials. It plays the role for associative algebras
that the [free group](../group-theory/part-01/free-group.md) plays for groups: every algebra generated by a family of
elements is a quotient of a free algebra, and many algebras of interest are defined by naming
generators and relations and dividing a free algebra by the relations.

## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $X$ a set. The
[free algebra $R\langle X\rangle$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/FreeAlgebra.html#FreeAlgebra) is an $R$-algebra together with a map
$\iota : X \to R\langle X\rangle$ that has the following universal property: for every associative
$R$-algebra $A$ with identity, commutative or not, and every function $f : X \to A$, there is exactly one
homomorphism of $R$-algebras $\bar f : R\langle X\rangle \to A$ with $\bar f \circ \iota = f$.
In other words, sending $\bar f$ to $\bar f \circ \iota$ gives a
[bijection between algebra homomorphisms $R\langle X\rangle \to A$ and functions $X \to A$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/FreeAlgebra.html#FreeAlgebra.lift).
The two halves of this statement are that
[the extension $\bar f$ restricts to $f$ on the generators](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/FreeAlgebra.html#FreeAlgebra.%CE%B9_comp_lift) and that
[a homomorphism $g$ with $g \circ \iota = f$ is equal to $\bar f$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/FreeAlgebra.html#FreeAlgebra.lift_unique).
In particular,
[two algebra homomorphisms out of $R\langle X\rangle$ that agree on the generators are equal](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/FreeAlgebra.html#FreeAlgebra.hom_ext).
As with any universal property, it determines $R\langle X\rangle$ up to a unique isomorphism
compatible with $\iota$.

Concretely, $R\langle X\rangle$ can be built as the free [module](../../linear-algebra/fundamentals/module.md) on the set of words
$x_1 x_2 \cdots x_n$ in the alphabet $X$, the empty word included, with multiplication given on words by
concatenation and extended bilinearly. The empty word is the identity, and $\iota$ sends $x$ to the word
of length one. This construction is the monoid algebra of the free monoid on $X$ (the free monoid
being the set of words under concatenation, and its monoid algebra the free module on it with
multiplication extended from the monoid). The free algebra is
[isomorphic to this monoid algebra](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/FreeAlgebra.html#FreeAlgebra.equivMonoidAlgebraFreeMonoid), and [the words form a basis of $R\langle X\rangle$ as an $R$-module](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/FreeAlgebra.html#FreeAlgebra.basisFreeMonoid).

## Examples

- If $X$ is empty, the only word is the empty one and $R\langle X\rangle$ is $R$ itself.
- If $X$ has a single element $t$, the words are the powers $t^n$, and $R\langle X\rangle$ is the
  [polynomial ring](../polynomials-and-power-series/polynomial-in-one-indeterminate.md) $R[t]$. Here the free algebra is commutative, because an
  algebra generated by one element over a commutative ring is commutative.
- If $X = \{x, y\}$ and $R$ is not the zero ring, then $xy \neq yx$, since they are distinct words and
  hence distinct basis elements. So, over a nonzero ring, the free algebra on two or more generators
  is not commutative.
  Its quotient by the two-sided ideal generated by $xy - yx$ is the
  [polynomial ring in two variables](../polynomials-and-power-series/polynomial-in-several-indeterminates.md) $R[x, y]$.

## Properties

- **Generation.** The free algebra is
  [generated as an $R$-algebra by the image of $\iota$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/FreeAlgebra.html#FreeAlgebra.adjoin_range_%CE%B9). More generally,
  for any function $f : X \to A$ into an $R$-algebra,
  [the subalgebra generated by the values of $f$ is the image of $\bar f$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/FreeAlgebra.html#Algebra.adjoin_range_eq_range_freeAlgebra_lift).
  Consequently
  [an $R$-algebra is finitely generated exactly when it receives a surjective algebra homomorphism from the free algebra on some finite set of its elements](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/FiniteType.html#Algebra.FiniteType.iff_quotient_freeAlgebra),
  so every finitely generated algebra is a quotient of a free algebra by a two-sided ideal.
- **Scalars embed.** The structure map $R \to R\langle X\rangle$ is injective:
  [$r$ and $s$ have the same image exactly when $r = s$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/FreeAlgebra.html#FreeAlgebra.algebraMap_inj).
  When $R$ is not the zero ring,
  [the map $\iota$ is injective](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/FreeAlgebra.html#FreeAlgebra.%CE%B9_injective).
- **No zero divisors.** Suppose $R$ has no zero divisors, and let $a$ and $b$ be nonzero. If $u$ and
  $v$ are words of maximal length occurring in $a$ and $b$, the word $uv$ arises in the product $ab$ only
  as $u$ times $v$, so its coefficient is the nonzero product of their coefficients. Hence
  [$R\langle X\rangle$ has no zero divisors](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/FreeAlgebra.html#FreeAlgebra.instNoZeroDivisors), and
  [it is a domain, a nonzero ring without zero divisors, when $R$ is an integral domain](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/FreeAlgebra.html#FreeAlgebra.instIsDomain).
- **Rank.** For a nonzero commutative ring $R$,
  [the rank of $R\langle X\rangle$ as an $R$-module is the cardinality of the set of finite words in $X$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/FreeAlgebra.html#FreeAlgebra.rank_eq);
  this is countably infinite when $X$ is finite and nonempty, and equals $|X|$ when $X$ is infinite.
- **Characteristic.** Because $R$ embeds in $R\langle X\rangle$,
  [if $R$ has characteristic $p$, so does the free algebra](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/CharP/Algebra.html#FreeAlgebra.charP) (see
  [characteristic $p$](../field-theory/characteristic-p.md)).

## Related concepts

The free algebra is the noncommutative analogue of the
[polynomial ring in several variables](../polynomials-and-power-series/polynomial-in-several-indeterminates.md), which is the free commutative algebra on $X$
and the quotient of $R\langle X\rangle$ by the ideal generated by all commutators $xy - yx$. The
[tensor algebra](tensor-algebra-of-a-commutative-ring.md) of a module generalizes it: the tensor algebra of a free module
with a basis indexed by $X$ is
[isomorphic to the free algebra on $X$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/TensorAlgebra/Basis.html#TensorAlgebra.equivFreeAlgebra). Algebras presented by
generators and relations, such as the [exterior algebra](exterior-algebra.md), the
[Clifford algebra](clifford-algebra.md) and the universal enveloping algebra of a
[Lie algebra](lie-algebra.md), arise as quotients of a free or tensor algebra by a two-sided ideal.

## In Mathlib

Mathlib defines the free algebra over any commutative semiring, not only a commutative ring, and on any
type of generators. It is constructed as a quotient of formal expressions built from scalars and
generators by sums and products, modulo the relations that make the result an algebra; after the basic
operations are set up, the library works through the universal property and an induction principle
rather than this construction. The universal property is packaged as a bijection between functions on
the generators and algebra homomorphisms, with targets any associative algebra, commutative or not, over
the same semiring. The description by words is a theorem, given by the isomorphism with the monoid
algebra of the free monoid. When the scalars are a commutative ring, the free algebra is automatically
a ring. The rank statement requires a nonzero commutative ring, and the injectivity of the generator map
requires nonzero scalars, matching the hypotheses above.
