# Removing dummy-coordinate dependence

Call an outcome $g$ of a global measurement of Lemma [A global padded polynomial PVM](lem-qld-global-pvm.md) $w$-independent if no monomial of $g$ involves a dummy coordinate: one of the $2m - 2$ coordinates of $\mathbb{F}_q^{4m}$ that the padded point measurements do not read. If the evaluated global measurement is $\delta$-consistent, on average over a uniform point, with a point measurement that does not read the dummy coordinates, then the state weight $W$ of the outcomes that are not $w$-independent satisfies $(1 - 8md/q)\,W \leq 2\delta$; under the standing assumption $16md \leq q$, $W \leq 4\delta$. For the measurements of Lemma [A global padded polynomial PVM](lem-qld-global-pvm.md), on either side, $W \leq 4\delta_{\mathrm{ld}}$. The bad set is empty for $m = 1$. Retaining the other outcomes gives a projective submeasurement. The Lean proves it in any bipartite model in a unit state, for a global measurement in one player's algebra against a point measurement in the other's, and for a `GlobalPair` in any model; the statement above is its tensor-product instance.

## Proof

Resample the dummy coordinates: for independent uniform points $u, u'$ let $\mathrm{mix}(u, u')$ be $u$ with its dummy coordinates replaced by those of $u'$. The point measurement is the same at $u$ and at $\mathrm{mix}(u, u')$, and $(u, u') \mapsto (\mathrm{mix}(u, u'), \mathrm{mix}(u', u))$ is an involution of the pairs, so the consistency estimate holds at both points simultaneously. Adding the two, the operator $P_u(g(u)) + P_u(g(\mathrm{mix}(u, u')))$ is at most $2\,{I}$ when the two values agree and at most ${I}$ when they differ, because two distinct elements of a POVM sum to at most the identity; no projectivity of the point measurement is used. Hence $\sum_g \langle G_g \rangle \Pr[g(u) = g(\mathrm{mix}(u, u'))] \geq 1 - 2\delta$. For a $w$-independent outcome the probability is one. For an outcome that reads a dummy coordinate, $g(u) - g(\mathrm{mix}(u, u'))$ is a nonzero polynomial in the $8m$ coordinates of the pair, of individual degree at most $d$ — its coefficient at a monomial of $g$ involving a dummy coordinate survives — so by Lemma [Schwartz–Zippel](../part-05/lem-schwartz-zippel.md) the probability is at most $8md/q$. The two bounds give $(1 - 8md/q)\,W \leq 2\delta$.
