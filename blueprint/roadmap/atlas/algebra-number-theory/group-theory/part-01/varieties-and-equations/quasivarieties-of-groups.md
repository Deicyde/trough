## Quasi-identities of groups

A quasi-identity in $n$ unknowns $x_0, \dots, x_{n-1}$ is a
formula $\forall x\,(w_1 = 1 \wedge \dots \wedge w_k = 1 \to w = 1)$, given by the number $n$, a
finite list of premises $w_1, \dots, w_k$ and a conclusion $w$, all of them elements of the free
group on $x_0, \dots, x_{n-1}$.

## Quasi-identities holding in a group

A quasi-identity
$\forall x\,(w_1 = 1 \wedge \dots \wedge w_k = 1 \to w = 1)$ in the unknowns $x_0, \dots, x_{n-1}$
holds in a group $G$ when every assignment of elements of $G$ to the unknowns under which each
premise $w_1, \dots, w_k$ evaluates to $1$ in $G$ also makes the conclusion $w$ evaluate to $1$.

## The quasivariety defined by a set of quasi-identities

For a set $Q$ of quasi-identities,
a group $H$ belongs to the quasivariety defined by $Q$ when every quasi-identity in $Q$ holds in
$H$. Every quasivariety of groups has this form.

Only groups in the lowest universe are considered; a quasivariety is determined by its countable
members.

## The quasi-identities of a group

The quasi-theory of a group $G$ is the set of all
quasi-identities that hold in $G$. The quasivariety defined by this set is the quasivariety $qG$
generated by $G$.

## Inclusion of quasivarieties

For sets $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ of quasi-identities, the
quasivariety defined by $Q_1$ is contained in the quasivariety defined by $Q_2$: every group in
which all quasi-identities of $Q_1$ hold also satisfies all quasi-identities of $Q_2$.

Only groups in the lowest universe are considered; a quasivariety is determined by its countable
members.

## Maximal proper subquasivarieties

Let $G$ be a group and let $qG$ be the quasivariety
generated by $G$, that is, the quasivariety defined by the set of all quasi-identities that hold in
$G$. A set $Q$ of quasi-identities defines a maximal proper subquasivariety of $qG$ when the
quasivariety defined by $Q$ is contained in $qG$ but does not contain $qG$, and every quasivariety
$V$ defined by a set of quasi-identities with $V$ containing the quasivariety defined by $Q$ and
contained in $qG$ either is contained in the quasivariety defined by $Q$ or contains $qG$.

## Budkin's problem on maximal subquasivarieties (Kourovka 14.25)

The statement that there
is a finitely generated group $G$ such that the quasivariety $qG$ generated by $G$ has infinitely
many maximal proper subquasivarieties: for every natural number $k$ there are sets of
quasi-identities $Q_0, \dots, Q_{k-1}$, each defining a maximal proper subquasivariety of $qG$,
such that for $i \ne j$ the quasivarieties defined by $Q_i$ and $Q_j$ are different.

## Some finitely generated group has infinitely many maximal subquasivarieties

There is a
finitely generated group $G$ such that the quasivariety $qG$ generated by $G$ has infinitely many
maximal proper subquasivarieties: for every natural number $k$ there are sets of quasi-identities
$Q_0, \dots, Q_{k-1}$, each defining a maximal proper subquasivariety of $qG$, such that for
$i \ne j$ the quasivarieties defined by $Q_i$ and $Q_j$ are different.

This answers Kourovka Problem 14.25 positively; the answer is due to Bludov.
