# Theorem Pauli basis test /… to The sublines distribution

Source. The pinned paper (AuditSync26), `qld-appendix.tex` and its five proof files, states this Pauli theorem and its reduction to the seeded CL test. The reduction follows the architecture of (JNVWY20, Section 7); the canonical-line soundness theorem of (JNVWY21qld) is related background. Lemma [Direct Magic Square anticommutation — what the pipeline actually needs](../part-01/lem-ms-direct-anticomm.md) supplies the base anticommutation directly.

Comments. This is the workhorse of question reduction: introspection certifies the players' sampling of their own questions against Pauli basis measurements. The paper's reduction uses direct Magic Square anticommutation, linearity and orthonormalization, together with the seeded CL theorem; the formalized proof invokes neither linearity (Theorem [Exact linearity from approximate linearity](thm-linearity.md)) nor orthonormalization (Corollary [Orthonormalization from consistency](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/03_background_results.tex#L2095)), its strategies being projective. The dependency graph still draws Theorem [Exact linearity from approximate linearity](thm-linearity.md) below it, through matrix-level declarations that Lemma [Combined $XZ$ point measurements](lem-qld-combined-points.md) lists beside its model form and that share that theorem's Fourier and ancilla-embedding definitions; `soundIn_of_lidt` reaches none of them. The available canonical-line theorem can replace the paper's classical route through Remark The CL-to-canonical adapter and parameter corollary's adapter, which supplies the $\mathrm{ldc}=1$ case this reduction uses (Theorem [Quantum soundness of the seeded CL test, one codeword](../part-01/thm-lidt-cl-soundness-one.md)).

The theorem's bipartite statement matches the pipeline's $\mathrm{val}^*$ soundness interface, so its uses require no additional synchronous-value transport. The verification campaign (Audit26) further reports three corrections in the tensor-code material the proof passes through — $\kappa$-convexity requires $c_1 \leq 1$, the synchronicity defect obeys $\delta_{\mathrm{sync}} \leq 4\varepsilon$ rather than $3\varepsilon$, and one corner block pairs with a transpose — none affecting the statement above. The formal proof is checked end to end with no assumption beyond Lean's standard axioms — in the tensor-product model hypothesis (L) is discharged by Lemma [The low-individual-degree test is sound in the tensor-product model](../../downstream-results/part-03/lem-lidt-sound-in-tensor.md) — so it does not rest on the paper's account of that material.

*The formalization.* `MIPRE.QLD.soundIn_of_lidt` (`MIPRE/Background/QLD/Soundness.lean`) is the theorem: for a bipartite model $\mathcal{M}$ with ordered, proper algebras compatible with the scalars and a value model $\omega$, the two hypotheses give `QLD.SoundIn` $\omega$ $\mathcal{M}$ (Definition [The Pauli basis test is sound in a model](../../downstream-results/part-03/def-qld-sound-in.md)). For every field $\mathbb{F}_q$ of characteristic two, every $m \geq 1$ with $m \mid q$, every $d \geq 1$ and every projective strategy in $\mathcal{M}$ failing with probability at most $\varepsilon$, it provides an extraction (`QLD.Extraction`) at error `qldErr` (Lemma [The error of the Pauli basis test](../part-03/lem-qld-error.md)): an ancilla model, which $\omega$ dominates, a local isometry of $\mathcal{M}$ into its register model, the state error, and both players' summed squared errors for both bases. Its tensor-product instance (`soundIn_tensor`) and its commuting-operator instance (`soundCo_of_lidt`) are recorded in Chapter [Downstream results](../../downstream-results/README.md), as Lemmas [The Pauli basis test is sound in the tensor-product model](../../downstream-results/part-04/lem-qld-sound-in-tensor.md) and [The Pauli basis test is sound in the commuting-operator model, given the seeded test](../../downstream-results/part-04/lem-qld-sound-co.md), where their consumers are. Four points of the statement are worth knowing, the first two of them differences from the paper's.

- **The strategy is projective, in a model.** The paper states the theorem for POVM strategies on a tensor product of finite-dimensional spaces; here it is about the projective strategies of a bipartite model satisfying the two hypotheses. The restriction is deliberate. The matrix theorem this replaces did cover POVM strategies, by dilating the strategy to a projective one, running the chain on the dilation and carrying item 2 back down to the original POVM through its agreement with $\tau^W$ on the other half of the pair, at a cost of $8\sqrt{\eta}$ for item 1's bound $\eta$. That descent was specific to matrices, and its one consumer, introspection, applies the theorem to projective strategies only (Lemma [Pauli extraction for the introspection game](../../structure-of-the-proof/part-05/lem-intro-pauli-extraction.md)); so the POVM form is not restated. This is a choice of the formalization, not a discrepancy with the paper, which was not consulted for this revision.
- **The conclusion is a local isometry of models** into the register model of an ancilla model, in place of a pair of isometries $\phi_\textsc{A} \otimes \phi_\textsc{B}$ into $\mathcal{H}' \otimes (\mathbb{C}^q)^{\otimes M}$ and a state $|\textsc{aux}\rangle$. The players' operators move by its homomorphisms, as $X \mapsto \phi_\textsc{A} X \phi_\textsc{A}^\dagger$ does, and the errors are the model's state norms; in the tensor-product model the two forms coincide (Lemma [The Pauli basis test is sound in the tensor-product model](../../downstream-results/part-04/lem-qld-sound-in-tensor.md)).
- **It asks less than admissibility**: characteristic two, $m \mid q$ and $d \geq 1$. The regime where the chain runs is $48md \leq q$ rather than the paper's $16md \leq q$, $48$ being where Lemma [Separating the two point variables](../part-03/lem-qld-global-separate.md) is formalized; the regime and the divisibility $4m \mid q$ are discharged inside the proof, not assumed (Lemma [Admissible padded setup](../part-03/lem-qld-global-setup.md)), and outside the regime the error absorbs the trivial bound (Lemma [The error of the Pauli basis test](../part-03/lem-qld-error.md)), as the paper's own enlargement of $a$ does.
- **Item 2 is about the strategy's Pauli measurement read as cube data**, `(S.PA (.pauli W)).map rdPauliVec`, which puts every malformed Pauli answer at $h = 0$, as the game's reading does. The paper assumes well-formatted answers; this is a relabelling of outcomes, not a restriction, and it is why legalizing the strategy is free (`rdPauliVec_legalize_pauli`). Read at the genuine answers instead, item 2 is Corollary [Theorem Pauli basis test / quantum low individual degree test on the valid answers](cor-qld-valid.md).

Inside the regime the proof is `exists_extraction_of_regime`: the extraction is the swap isometry `MirrorSimul.swapPhi` at the `MirrorSimul` that `exists_mirrorSimul` builds for the legalized strategy (`legalStrat`), with the padded extension in the swap lemma's auxiliary state as its ancilla model. Outside it the proof is `Extraction.trivial`. Nothing follows the swap isometry, so the matrix route's explicit embedding of the strategy's space into the physical state, its descent from the dilation, and its reading of the isometries with the register first are all gone.

*The error* the proof produces, `qldErr`, and its closed form are Lemma [The error of the Pauli basis test](../part-03/lem-qld-error.md), which rests on the chain's constants alone; `QLD.SoundIn` is stated at that function.

The ledger node $1.2.2$ also records completeness of the game. That is Lemma [Perfect PCC completeness of the full Pauli game](../../structure-of-the-proof/part-05/lem-intro-honest-full-pauli.md), formalized separately; this theorem is the soundness half, node $1.2.2.17$'s assembly.

*A property of the shape itself.* Because of the $2^{-bmd}$ term the right-hand side never falls below $a \cdot 2^{-bmd}$, so for any bound on $md$ the theorem is satisfied trivially by a large enough $a$, the conclusions being about unit vectors and projective measurements. The statement has content only as $md$ grows, with $\varepsilon$ small and $q$ large. That is inherent in the stated form, not a defect of the formalization, but it is worth knowing when reading the constants the proof produces.

- [Theorem Pauli basis test / quantum low individual degree test on the valid answers](cor-qld-valid.md)

Comments. The paper's game has no other answers to a $(\textsc{Pauli}, W)$ question; the formal game has one answer type for all questions, and Theorem [Pauli basis test / quantum low individual degree test](../part-01/thm-qld.md) reads every answer that is not a Pauli answer as the outcome $0$, as the game does. The introspection consumer (Lemma [Finite soundness from the valid-answer extraction interface](../../structure-of-the-proof/part-05/lem-intro-valid-isometric-soundness.md)) sums over the valid answers only, and this corollary is the bridge, in `MIPRE/Background/Introspection/QLDExtractionAdapter.lean`. `FieldExtraction` (`MIPRE/Background/Introspection/BinaryExtraction.lean`) is the conclusion at the genuine answers: the ancilla model, the local isometry, the state error, and the two estimates introspection uses, the first player's $X$ and the second player's $Z$. `FieldExtraction.ofQLD` reads an extraction of Theorem [Pauli basis test / quantum low individual degree test](../part-01/thm-qld.md) there under the support hypothesis (`PauliSupported`, through `rdPauliVec_op_of_supported`), `fieldExtraction_exists` is the corollary, and `degreeOne_fieldExtraction_exists` is its case $d = 1$, at the error the canonical parameter estimates use.

**It costs nothing.** The matrix corollary this replaces assumed only that the two Pauli measurements were projective, and paid at $h = 0$: the answer $0$ is one element of everything the game reads as $0$, so the triangle inequality cost twice the weight of the rank-one projector $\tau^W_0$ on $|\textsc{EPR}_q\rangle^{\otimes M} \otimes |\textsc{aux}\rangle$, at most $1/q$, and the error became $2\delta_{qld} + 8/q$. The support hypothesis removes the malformed answers instead, so the fibre at $0$ is the answer $0$ alone. It is free where the corollary is used: the decoder of the introspection game leaves only genuine full-register answers at the Pauli questions (`ParsedPauliSupported`), and the restriction to the Pauli basis test keeps that (`completePauliPOVM_invalid_of_supported`). The universal constants are fixed once (`qldCoefficient`, `qldExponent`), from the closed form `exists_qldErr_le` of the error (Lemma [The error of the Pauli basis test](../part-03/lem-qld-error.md)), a fact about that function alone, so they are the same in every model (`qldErr_le_errShape`).

### The shape of the soundness proof

**Remark (How the soundness proof of Theorem Pauli basis test / quantum low individual degree test is organized).** The soundness half of Theorem [Pauli basis test / quantum low individual degree test](../part-01/thm-qld.md) is developed in the five-file appendix of the pinned paper (AuditSync26). Its architecture is worth stating before its pieces, because the pieces are unreadable without it. There are five stages.

1. **Preliminaries**: three utility lemmas about closeness, one of which (Corollary [Orthonormalization from consistency](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/03_background_results.tex#L2095)) the paper uses on almost every page.
2. **Win implications**: unpack “succeeds with probability $1 - \varepsilon$” into one consistency or commutation statement per subtest (Lemma [Win implications](lem-qld-win.md)).
3. **Expansion**: adjoin EPR ancillas and replace the strategy's measurements by ones on the enlarged space that approximately commute across the two bases (Lemmas [Expanded point measurements](lem-qld-expanded-points.md) and [Expanded line measurements](lem-qld-expanded-lines.md)).
4. **Combining**: build a *single* measurement returning both the $X$- and the $Z$-polynomial, by padding the ambient space and applying seeded CL soundness to the padded strategy (Lemma [The simultaneous global measurement](../part-03/lem-qld-simultaneous.md)).
5. **Separation and the swap isometry**: extract exact Pauli operators from that single measurement and rotate them onto honest EPR pairs (Lemmas [Exact Pauli operators](../part-03/lem-qld-exact-paulis.md) and [The swap isometry](../part-03/lem-qld-swap.md)).

Stage 4 consumes Theorem [Quantum soundness of the seeded CL test](../part-01/thm-lidt-cl-soundness.md) at $(q,4m,d,1)$, after constructing a strategy for the seeded CL game and dilating it to a projective one. Theorem [Quantum soundness of the canonical-line low individual degree test](../part-01/thm-lidt-soundness.md) justifies this application through Theorem [Quantum soundness of the seeded CL test, one codeword](../part-01/thm-lidt-cl-soundness-one.md), the $\mathrm{ldc}=1$ case being exactly what stage 4 consumes; what stage 4 must still supply is the padding itself. Stage 5 recovers the Pauli group relations exactly, which permits the standard swap isometry; the expanded measurements in stage 3 satisfy only approximate commutation.

*In the formalization* every stage is stated for a strategy in a bipartite model (Definition [Bipartite model](../../downstream-results/part-02/def-bipartite-model.md)), and the tensor-product model is the instance in which each statement below is written. A strategy of the model is projective from the start (Definition [Projective strategy in a bipartite model](../../downstream-results/part-02/def-model-proj-strategy.md)), so the orthonormalization of stage 1 is used nowhere: where the paper replaces a POVM by a nearby projective measurement, the formalization's measurement is projective already. The registers the later stages adjoin are ancilla extensions of the model (Definition [Ancilla extension of a bipartite model](../../downstream-results/part-02/def-ancilla-extension.md)): the expanded state of stage 3 is its register model (Definition [The register model](../../downstream-results/part-03/def-register-model.md)), and the padded and physical states of stages 4 and 5 are the models of Definition [The models of stages 4 and 5](../part-03/def-qld-physical-model.md), between which registers move by local isometries. The seeded CL theorem enters as a hypothesis on the model, its soundness in every ancilla extension by a unit vector (Definition [The low-individual-degree test is sound in a model](../../downstream-results/part-03/def-lidt-sound-in.md)), and is applied once per player orientation, in the padded model, to the projective dilation of the padded strategy.

Comments. Two departures from the paper's statement and proof, both recorded in the formalization's own docstring. First, the paper states the bound as $(1 - q^{-m})(1 - q^{-1})(1 - md/q)/2$; its own correction note removes the case distinction on $u_X = 0$ that produced the first factor, observing that ${\mathrm{ind}}_m(u_X)$ is never the zero vector, and keeps the factor only because it remains a valid lower bound. Second, the low-degree encoding is *multilinear*, so Schwartz–Zippel costs $m/q$ and not $md/q$; the formalized bounds are $(1 - (m+1)/q)/2$ and $(1 - 1/q)/2$, and the paper's $(1 - 3md/q)/2$ is derived from them for $m, d \geq 1$. The ‘$d$’ in this fact is therefore inert: it is the individual-degree parameter of the surrounding test, and the bound does not depend on it.

The complementarity statement is not decoration. Each of the two bounds is a lower bound on a probability, so either alone would be implied by the wrong definition of its event; that they hold for complementary events, and add to one, is what pins them down.

### The point observables

The stage that follows works with the $\pm 1$-observables of the two-outcome probes rather than with the measurements themselves, and needs two statements about them: they are cross-party consistent, and they commute up to the sign $(-1)^{\gamma}$. The first is Lemma [Win implications](lem-qld-win.md) item 1 and costs one factor of two; the second is the synthesis of items 4 to 7 and is where the sign that Lemma [Expanded point measurements](lem-qld-expanded-points.md) cancels first appears.

- [Signed commutation, the commuting case](lem-qld-obs-commutation-comm.md)

Comments. Formalized in `MIPRE/Background/QLD/Commutation.lean`. One departure from the paper's route, and two from its bookkeeping, both forced by which side an object lives on.

**No orthonormalization.** The paper's strategy is a POVM strategy, and it makes the $\mathtt{Pair}$ measurement projective with Corollary [Orthonormalization from consistency](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/03_background_results.tex#L2095), at $18$ times the inconsistency of the two players' $\mathtt{Pair}$ measurements and a factor two per marginal. Here the first player's measurements are projective, as a strategy of a bipartite model is, and so is the joint $\mathtt{Pair}$ reading, a coarse-graining of hers (`isPVMIn_pairPOVM`). The inconsistency term is kept at the weight $72$ the orthonormalization charged, with its bound `sum_incQty_le`, so that the constant is the matrix formalization's, unchanged; the strict positivity the corollary asks of its error bound, and the limit that discharged it in the matrix formalization, are gone.

**The analysis is run on Bob's observables and carried back.** Lemma [Commutation analysis](../../foundations/part-02/lem-commutation-analysis.md) wants the two commuting families on one side and the joint projective measurement on the *other*, and the projective $\mathtt{Pair}$ measurement is *Alice's*. So the analysis is applied to Bob's point measurements against Alice's $\mathtt{Pair}$ measurement, and the conclusion is carried to Alice's observables by the order-reversal rule. Running it on Alice's side instead would ask Bob's measurements to be projective too.

**The chain is run in the mirrored orientation.** Every rule of `fig:decider_pauli` is stated in both orientations, so items 5 and 1 are available with the players exchanged and item 4 is used as it stands.

**Data processing is a consistency-level fact.** The chain's links are converted to the state-dependent distance only after the coarse-grainings are taken, because $\approx_\delta$ *has no data-processing inequality*: NW19's Fact 4.26 is stated for $\simeq_\delta$, and the remark following it gives a counterexample for $\approx_\delta$. The paper's `fact:data-processing` is the $\simeq$ form, correctly; a formalization that reached for the $\approx$ form would be proving something false.

- [Signed commutation, the anticommuting case](lem-qld-obs-commutation-acomm.md)

Comments. Formalized in `MIPRE/Background/QLD/Commutation.lean`.

**This half costs $\varepsilon$, not $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$.** Every input above is a squared-norm bound linear in $\varepsilon$, and the only inequalities used are the triangle inequality and $(a+b+c)^2 \leq 3(a^2+b^2+c^2)$, so nothing introduces a square root. *Correction to an earlier reading of this:* the square root in [(1)](lem-qld-obs-commutation.md) as the paper writes it does *not* belong to the commuting case either — that case is $O(\varepsilon)$ too (Lemma [Signed commutation, the commuting case](lem-qld-obs-commutation-comm.md)), since [(4)](../../foundations/part-02/lem-commutation-analysis.md) is linear in its hypothesis and the paper's orthonormalization, Corollary [Orthonormalization from consistency](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/03_background_results.tex#L2095), is linear in the inconsistency, while the formalization needs no orthonormalization at all. The paper's $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ is a uniform *weakening*, and its own text says so where the anticommuting case is proved: “direct averaging proves the anticommutation relation with squared error $O(\varepsilon)$. In particular it implies the following weaker bound, retained in the form used below”. Nothing downstream needs the sharper form — `lem:qld-comm-cons` asks only for $\mathrm{poly}(\varepsilon)$ — so this is a note about where a square root is *not* spent, not a repair.

**The sign is the double reversal.** Both order reversals run in the same direction — a product transferred across the tensor factors comes back reversed — and what sits between them is Bob's *anti*commutator, a sum. So the two reversals do not cancel: Alice's two observables anticommute because Bob's do, and that is the $(-1)^{\gamma}$ the expansion stage of Lemma [Expanded point measurements](lem-qld-expanded-points.md) cancels against the ancillas.

- [Expanded point observables commute](lem-qld-expanded-commutation.md)

Comments. Formalized in `MIPRE/Background/QLD/Expanded.lean`. This is what the expansion exists for, and it is worth saying precisely what is bought: *the sign is gone, not smaller*. Without the ancillas the two bases commute only up to an error carrying $(-1)^{\gamma(\omega)}$, which no amount of soundness improves, because $\gamma$ is a property of the question and not of the strategy. With them the two signs multiply to one identically, and the error that remains is the strategy's own.

The constant carries over unchanged. That is the content of items 3 and 4 of Definition [The expanded state, and an inert ancilla](../../foundations/part-02/def-expanded-state.md): the expansion is free, in the sense that no step of it loses anything, which is also why it is stated there rather than here.

$\delta_{\mathrm{Line}}$ is *linear* in $\varepsilon$, at the constant $172\varepsilon$ of item 1 of Lemma [Win implications](lem-qld-win.md), not $O(\sqrt\varepsilon)$ as this lemma previously claimed. The paper says $\mathrm{poly}(\varepsilon)$ and is consistent with either; the square root was this blueprint's own and had no source. Nothing in the proof takes a square root, because nothing in it converts an operator inequality into a norm.

Projectivity does not come from Corollary [Orthonormalization from consistency](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/03_background_results.tex#L2095), and that reference is gone from the `uses` list. It comes from projectivity of the strategy's own line measurement, which the paper takes for granted and which is without loss of generality; the formalized statement carries it as a hypothesis rather than hiding it.

The paper's two forms of item 2 — comparing a line outcome with a point outcome, and comparing the *evaluation* of the line polynomial at the point — are one statement here. The paper needs a direct calculation to pass between them, because it works with $\approx$ and Fact `fact:data-processing` is a statement about $\simeq$ (a defect it flags itself). At the Born level the passage is free: summing the line outcomes over a fibre of $\mathrm{eval}_u$ is a coarse-graining, which increases agreement, so the estimate proved is the stronger one and the weaker follows without a separate argument.

One thing is deliberately *not* claimed. For an axis-parallel line the honest restriction is affine, hence of degree at most $d$ rather than $md$; that refinement is what Lemma [Pairs of lines](lem-qld-pairs-of-lines.md) will need in order to keep the padded answers in the degree-$d$ format, and it needs the individual-degree bound along the line's own direction, not the total-degree bound proved here. The convolution above is stated at degree $md$ for both line types, with the axis-parallel answers padded, which is what the paper's printed definition does too.

### Combining the two bases

The point of this stage is a single measurement that returns the $X$-polynomial and the $Z$-polynomial at once. It is reached by padding the ambient space from $\mathbb{F}_q^m$ to $\mathbb{F}_q^{4m}$, so that a point of the padded space encodes a point in each basis together with a pair of coefficients, and then applying the seeded CL theorem to the padded strategy.

- [Exact linearity from approximate linearity](thm-linearity.md)

Source and correction. The paper imports this from Natarajan and Vidick (NV17) (Theorem 10 of the arXiv version) and proves nothing about it; the source is vendored in the *paper* repository, not in this one. An earlier version of this proof text said “the source is vendored and was audited line by line”, which is true of `paper/external/nv17/` and false of `MIPRE/Background/`, whose vendored trees are GowersHatami, LIDT, Orthonormalization and Repetition. A reader would have planned the wrong work. The theorem is therefore **proved here**, from Parseval and Lemma [Naimark dilation of a question-indexed family](../../foundations/part-02/lem-naimark-dilation.md); it is formalized in `MIPRE/Foundations/Linearity.lean`.

The ledger node this statement accounts for records the *paper*'s status — imported from NV17, with the import audited in its three children: the statement match, the source's state-dependent-distance toolkit, and the Fourier proof line by line. The mark here records the *Lean*'s status, which is a proof. The two are consistent claims about different artifacts, and the Lean proof is the argument the third child audits: the same transform, the same Parseval step, the same dilation, the same inversion.

Comments. Three things are worth separating from the proof.

Theorem [Gowers–Hatami](../part-01/thm-gowers-hatami.md) is *not* used, and has left this theorem's `uses` list. Gowers–Hatami is a statement about the Hilbert–Schmidt distance and approximate *representations*; the hypothesis here is in the state-dependent distance, and the exact-POVM step above replaces the stability machinery entirely. The conclusion is an **equality**, which is more than the paper's statement and more than the application needs. - [Combined $XZ$ point measurements](lem-qld-combined-points.md)

Source and correction. Formalized in `MIPRE/Background/QLD/Combined.lean`, with the generic mathematics in `MIPRE/Foundations/Sandwich.lean`, the Fourier dictionary in `MIPRE/Foundations/Parseval.lean` and the player exchange in `MIPRE/Foundations/Swap.lean` and `MIPRE/Background/QLD/Swap.lean`.

The **route differs from the paper's**, and the statement is the same. The paper builds $\widehat{Q}$ in three steps: the sandwich, then *orthonormalization* (Corollary [Orthonormalization from consistency](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/03_background_results.tex#L2095)) to make it projective, then the quantum linearity test (Theorem [Exact linearity from approximate linearity](thm-linearity.md)) to turn the resulting binary observables into an $\mathbb{F}_q^2$-valued measurement. Given that the strategy may be taken projective — which the paper also assumes — the last two steps are unnecessary: the sandwich is already a POVM, so its Naimark dilation is already the projective joint measurement wanted, and the compression identity carries the estimates. So `thm:linearity` has left this lemma's `uses` list, together with `cor:ortho-from-consistency`, which was never in it. Theorem [Exact linearity from approximate linearity](thm-linearity.md) is consequently used nowhere in this blueprint — Lemma [Exact Pauli operators](../part-03/lem-qld-exact-paulis.md) says explicitly that it is not used there either — and is kept because it is a statement of the paper's and is now proved rather than imported, not because anything downstream needs it.

The **extra register is explicit**. The paper writes “by assuming that $|\hat\psi\rangle$ has sufficiently many ancilla zero qubits, we do not need to extend the state further”; here the register the dilation needs, two copies of $\mathbb{F}_q \times \mathbb{F}_q$, is adjoined to each party by name (the ancilla extension `BipartiteModel.jointModel`), which is the same device with the bookkeeping visible. It is adjoined to *both* parties, unlike the one-sided extension of Theorem [Exact linearity from approximate linearity](thm-linearity.md), because item 1 compares the two players' copies of the same construction.

The **constants are explicit and linear in the inputs**. $\delta_Q$ has a square root because three of the chain's five links are Cauchy–Schwarz steps; the $57676416\varepsilon$ under the first two is Lemma [Signed commutation of the point observables](lem-qld-obs-commutation.md)'s constant, unchanged by the expansion, and the $86\varepsilon$ under the third and in the linear term is item 1 of Lemma [Win implications](lem-qld-win.md)'s, halved by projectivity. Nothing in the chain is a per-outcome estimate, so no constant depends on $q$.

- [Pairs of lines](lem-qld-pairs-of-lines.md)

Comments. Formalized in `MIPRE/Background/QLD/Lines.lean`, on top of Lemma [Pasting, the two-measurement case](../../foundations/part-02/lem-pasting-updated.md) in `MIPRE/Foundations/Pasting.lean`. The headline declaration `qld_pairs_of_lines` takes only game-level hypotheses — a strategy, its projectivity, and $1 - \mathrm{val} \leq \varepsilon$ — and bounds by $\delta_P$ the probability that the first player's sandwich and the second player's $T$, read at the sampled points, disagree on the register model, at the axis-parallel line of each side. That is [(3)](lem-qld-pairs-of-lines.md) in agreement form: $\widehat{Q}$ compresses to the sandwich, so its Born probabilities against $T$ on the extended state are the sandwich's on the register model, and [(1)](../../foundations/part-02/def-cross-distance.md) converts. `pairs_of_lines_gen` and `pairs_of_lines` state the distance form for the dilated $\widehat{Q}$ itself. `pairs_of_lines_of_items` is the agreement form for any pair of presentations, and `aPres_items`, `dPres_items` and `sum_content_collProb_dPres` are what the other three pairs of line types need.

**The question distribution is a parameter.** `pairs_of_lines_gen` proves the lemma over any finite nonempty sample space carrying a content per side — $k_X$ presenting the $X$ line, $k_Z$ the $Z$ line — together with a shift of the $X$ point that both contents see as such; the two contents need share nothing, because no hypothesis of Lemma [Pasting, the two-measurement case](../../foundations/part-02/lem-pasting-updated.md) involves both lines. `pairs_of_lines` is its instance at one content per sample, the same one on both sides, where the two lines are therefore dependent; `pairs_of_lines_prod` (Lemma [Padded line POVM](../part-03/lem-qld-padded-lines.md)) is its instance at a pair of line–point data, where they are independent. `pasteLine` and `pasteJ_eq_pasteLine` accordingly take the $X$ line and the $Z$ line from two contents rather than one.

**Per pair of line types, not for the mixture.** The paper states the lemma on average over the line–point distribution, which is an equal mixture of the axis-parallel and diagonal branches. Here the line type is a parameter and the statement is proved for each of the four pairs, which is *stronger*: the mixture is a convex combination of the four.

**A degenerate diagonal line makes the collision probability one, and the paper's $\varepsilon = md/q$ does not account for it.** The seeded test's diagonal direction is $\mathtt{zeroBelow}(\chi(s), v)$, which vanishes for a $1/q$ fraction of the contents at worst; on such a content the “line” is a single point, every polynomial is evaluated at the same parameter, and two distinct ones can agree with probability $1$. The bound above therefore carries $md/q + 1/q$ on the diagonal side, and $\delta_P$ is still $\mathrm{poly}(\varepsilon, md/q)$. See `reports/pasting-degenerate-diagonal-line.md`.

**The shift replaces the paper's appeal to `lem:alnf` and `lem:dlnf`.** Those say that conditioned on the line the point is uniform on it, which is what the collision bound uses. Rather than form the conditional distribution, the formalization shifts the point along the direction and uses that the shift is a bijection of contents for each fixed $t$ (`sum_content_shift_gen`); the same device, applied to the raw direction $v$ instead of the point, is what bounds the probability of a degenerate diagonal line.

**What the refined contract still asks for.** Three items of the contract recorded for this lemma are not part of what is proved above, and are wanted by the lemmas that consume it: that the outcome polynomials have degree at most $d$ on an axis-parallel component, which is Lemma [Axis degree for expanded line measurements](lem-qld-axis-degree.md) and an additional target there; and the restriction to specified line types and direction indices, which costs the reciprocal probability of those choices, at most $4m^2$. That the two lines be drawn *independently* is no longer one of them: `pairs_of_lines_gen` leaves the question distribution free, so the padded line–point distribution of Lemma [Padded line POVM](../part-03/lem-qld-padded-lines.md) is an instance rather than a gap. The version above fixes the line type on each side as a parameter and is proved for each of the four pairs, so the restriction factor is not incurred; the reciprocal cost appears only when a caller wants the statement for the mixture.
