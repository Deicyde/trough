# Linearity in the two combining coefficients

Let $(G_A, G_B)$ be the global measurements of Lemma [A global padded polynomial PVM](lem-qld-global-pvm.md), with error $\delta$, in the padded model, with state $\Phi$, and let $\Delta = 2\delta + 2 \cdot 57676416\,\varepsilon$ be the bound of Lemma [Ordered products with global outcome multipliers](lem-qld-global-products.md) for the order $\widehat M^X_a \widehat M^Z_b$. Call an outcome $g$ linear in $(\alpha, \beta)$ if every monomial of $g$ has exponents $(1, 0)$ or $(0, 1)$ on the two combining coordinates, that is $g = \alpha g_1(x, z, w) + \beta g_2(x, z, w)$, and let $L$ be the set of such outcomes. Then, with $\eta = (1 + 2d + 4md)/q$,

$$
(1 - 2\eta) \sum_{g \notin L} \langle G_{A,g} \otimes {I} \rangle_\Phi \leq 2\Delta ,
$$

and likewise for $G_B$. Under $16md \leq q$ this is $O(\Delta)$, linear in $\delta$ and in $\varepsilon$; the sum over outcomes introduces no factor $q^2$. The Lean proves it for a global measurement in any bipartite model in a unit state, and for a `GlobalPair` in any model; the statement above is its tensor-product instance.

## Proof

Fix $g \notin L$, write $S = G_{A,g}$, and let $B_u(c)$ be the combination of the ordered products $\widehat M^X_a \widehat M^Z_b$ along $\alpha a + \beta b = c$, for the padded point $u = (x, z, \alpha, \beta, w)$. Since $(S \otimes {I})\Phi = (S \otimes B_u(g(u)))\Phi + (S \otimes ({I} - B_u(g(u))))\Phi$,

$$
\langle S \otimes {I} \rangle
\leq 2\,\|(S \otimes B_u(g(u)))\Phi\|^2 + 2\,\|(S \otimes ({I} - B_u(g(u))))\Phi\|^2
$$

for every $u$. Averaging over $u$ and summing over $g \notin L$, the second terms total at most $2\Delta$, so it suffices to show $\mathbb E_u \|(S \otimes B_u(g(u)))\Phi\|^2 \leq \eta \langle S \otimes {I} \rangle$ for each $g \notin L$.

Read the uniform padded point as a uniform base point $u_0$ with a fresh uniform pair $(\alpha, \beta)$ substituted in the two combining coordinates. The blocks $x, z$ are those of $u_0$, and $g(u) = p_{u_0}(\alpha, \beta)$ for the bivariate polynomial $p_{u_0} = \sum_{i,j} g_{ij}(u_0)\,\alpha^i \beta^j$, whose coefficients $g_{ij}$ are polynomials of individual degree at most $d$ in the remaining coordinates. As $g \notin L$, some $g_{i_0 j_0}$ with $(i_0, j_0) \notin \{(1, 0), (0, 1)\}$ is a nonzero polynomial in $4m$ variables, so by Schwartz–Zippel it vanishes at a uniform $u_0$ with probability at most $4md/q$; at such a base point the average over $(\alpha, \beta)$ is bounded by $\langle S \otimes {I} \rangle$, each $B_u(c)$ being a contraction. At every other base point, $p_{u_0}$ differs from every linear form $\alpha a + \beta b$. The pairs with $\beta = 0$ contribute at most $\langle S \otimes {I} \rangle / q$. For $\beta \neq 0$ the fibre $\{\alpha a + \beta b = c\}$ has one $b$ per $a$, and the $\widehat M^X_a$ are orthogonal projectors, so $\|(S \otimes B_u(c))\Phi\|^2 = \sum_{\alpha a + \beta b = c} W(a, b)$ with $W(a, b) = \|(S \otimes \widehat M^X_a \widehat M^Z_b)\Phi\|^2$, weights that do not depend on $(\alpha, \beta)$ and sum to $\langle S \otimes {I} \rangle$. Exchanging the two sums, each $W(a, b)$ is counted with the probability that $p_{u_0}(\alpha, \beta) = \alpha a + \beta b$ at a uniform $(\alpha, \beta)$, which is at most $2d/q$ by Schwartz–Zippel in two variables. Altogether $\eta = 1/q + 2d/q + 4md/q$. Bob's version applies the same argument with the players exchanged.
