# Approximate representation to Pauli basis test / quantum low…

- [Approximate representation](def-approx-rep.md)
- [Gowers–Hatami](thm-gowers-hatami.md)

Source. Gowers and Hatami (GH17). A quantitative form suited to applications in nonlocal games, and a version adapted to the tracial setting, is given by de la Salle (dlS21) and in (Vid22).

Comments. This is the stability result underlying all rigidity (self-testing) arguments in the proof: an approximate representation of the Pauli group $\mathbb{F}_2^{2k}$ (with its cocycle) is close to an exact one, which forces the presence of EPR pairs. The statement above is fully formalized (statement and proof) in `MIPRE/Background/GowersHatami/Basic.lean`, following the regular-representation argument sketched in the proof; the intermediate statement, with the exact representation living on the concrete index set $G \times \{1, \dots, d\}$, is `MIPRE.gowers_hatami_prod`. Two remarks on the statement. (i) It asserts only $d' \geq d$, with witness $d' = |G| d$; it does not include the dimension bound $d' \leq (1 + C\varepsilon) d$ of (GH17), whose proof goes through the Fourier-analytic route via irreducible representations. Whether the sharper bound is needed downstream (in the Pauli-group application feeding Theorem [Pauli basis test / quantum low individual degree test](thm-qld.md)) is to be revisited; if so it will be added as a separate strengthening. (ii) In the Lean encoding the isometry is stored as the $d \times d'$ matrix of its adjoint, so the isometry condition reads $V V^\dagger = {I}$ and the compression is written $V \rho(x) V^\dagger$. The normalized Hilbert–Schmidt norm is the tracial form needed for the synchronous framework (dlS21, Vid22).

## The orthonormalization lemma

[$\bullet\bullet$ medium] [[$\uparrow$ QuantumLib](https://github.com/Timeroot/Lean-QuantumInfo)]

- [Orthonormalization](thm-orthonormalization.md)

**Remark (What is proved, and in what generality).** The statement above is the finite-dimensional case, which is the one the main theorem needs and the one that is formalized. The vendored development proves three unconditional cases, in increasing generality of the algebra and decreasing generality of the state: every von Neumann algebra on a finite-dimensional space, for every normal state (`MIPRE.Orthonormalization.povm_orthogonalization_finDim`, the statement above, bridging `povm_orthogonalization_finDim_vn`); $\mathcal{B}(H)$ for arbitrary $H$ (`povm_orthogonalization_fullAlgebra_general`); and II$_1$ factors whose trace and state are both of trace-class form (`povm_orthogonalization_II1Factor`). This repository adds a fourth, for the commuting-operator track: every von Neumann algebra with no nonzero abelian projection and a faithful tracial state given by finitely many vectors, factor or not, for every state of trace-class form (Theorem [Orthonormalization in algebras without abelian projections](thm-orthonormalization-no-abelian.md)), from a centre-valued trace constructed without any structure theory (Lemma [A centre-valued trace from a vector trace](lem-center-valued-trace.md)). A tracial commuting-operator strategy lives in a finite von Neumann algebra; on an infinite-dimensional space that algebra is now covered whenever it has no type I part, whatever its centre, and a type I part with diffuse centre still needs the general case below.

The general case — every von Neumann algebra, every normal state — is proved only as an implication. Its hypothesis, `MvNStructureTheory`, bundles eight facts: seven are classical Murray–von Neumann structure theory that Mathlib does not have (normal functionals are of trace-class form; the type decomposition; the finite decomposition; increasing nets of finite projections in the semifinite part; the center-valued trace; halving in type III; polar decomposition inside the algebra, which the development proves separately as `exists_polar`), and the eighth is the measurable-selection step of the source's own Lemma 3.1 for the finite type I part. No term of that type is constructed, so the unconditional general statement remains open upstream, and with it the three companion results (Theorems 1.1 and 1.4 and Corollary 1.5 of (dlS21)), which are formalized in finite dimension and conditionally in general. `MIPRE/Background/Orthonormalization/Axioms.lean` makes the split mechanical: it fails to build if an unconditional result acquires an axiom, or if one of the four open targets is closed upstream without this blueprint being updated.

Source. de la Salle (dlS21), which gives a dimension-free bound with explicit constants; earlier versions with worse parameters appear in (KPS18) and in (JNVWY20). The Lean proof is vendored from the `orthogonalization` package of the `commuting-repetition` repository (the same upstream as Theorem [Direct parallel repetition for commuting-operator strategies](../../parallel-repetition/thm-direct-repetition-co.md)) by `scripts/vendor-repetition.py`; see that directory's `README.md` for what the copy changes (import prefixes, nothing else).

Comments. Used constantly: soundness analyses produce POVMs that are only approximately projective, and this lemma replaces them by genuinely projective measurements on the same space, which is what makes the “projective strategies throughout” convention of Section [Departures from the paper](../../introduction/README.md) viable (avoiding Naimark dilation). The finite-dimensional case suffices for the main theorem and is a clean, self-contained formalization target in linear algebra.

The first of the two claims about the source made by the verification campaign (Audit26) — that this statement was over-hypothesized, the result needing only a *normal* state rather than a normal faithful tracial one, with constant $9$ and exponent $1$ — is now settled in the direction the campaign predicted, and settled by proof rather than by reading: the statement above carries neither faithfulness nor traciality and is checked. That matters beyond bookkeeping, because the tracial hypothesis excluded the bipartite vector-state applications that several of the projectivization sites of Section [Structure of the proof](../../structure-of-the-proof/README.md) need; those sites can now use this lemma, where before they needed Naimark dilation or the projective-by-definition convention of Section [Departures from the paper](../../introduction/README.md). What is settled is the mathematics, not the attribution: the campaign's second claim, that crediting the weaker predecessor to (KPS18) and (JNVWY20) is wrong and the source credits the low individual degree paper and Kempe–Vidick instead, is still unchecked here, (dlS21) not being vendored in this repository. The campaign also offers a consistency-to-projectivity step giving $O(\delta)$ in place of $O(\delta^{1/4})$, which would sharpen the proof of Theorem [Almost-synchronous strategies are near synchronous strategies](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/03_background_results.tex#L137).

The formalized hypothesis and conclusion are both strict. For a POVM, completeness identifies the defect with $1-\varphi(\sum_a M_a^2)$, but does not turn a non-strict bound into a strict one. A compactness argument in finite dimension would retain the constant $9$ at the non-strict bound — apply the theorem at every larger error, then pass to a convergent subsequence of projective measurements and use continuity — and would also cover zero defect. Corollary [Orthonormalization from consistency](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/03_background_results.tex#L2095), the one application in this chapter, does not need it: its Cauchy–Schwarz step produces slack $\gamma^2$ over the bound it has to clear, so the strict hypothesis holds as stated. The comments after that corollary record the repair. That corollary is no longer on the formalized route: the analysis of the Pauli basis test is stated for projective strategies in a bipartite model, whose measurements need no orthonormalization, so nothing outside the vendored tree applies this theorem at present.

For the commuting-operator track this repository adds the fourth case of Remark What is proved, and in what generality: algebras without abelian projections, factor or not.

## Magic Square rigidity

[$\bullet$ easy] [[$\uparrow$ QuantumLib](https://github.com/Timeroot/Lean-QuantumInfo)]

Source. Wu, Bancal, McKague and Scarani (WBMS16); the game is the Mermin–Peres magic square. The form above is (JNVWY20, Section 8).

[$\bullet\bullet$ medium]

- [Direct Magic Square anticommutation — what the pipeline actually needs](lem-ms-direct-anticomm.md)

Comments. This lemma is why Theorem [Magic Square rigidity](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/03_background_results.tex#L420) is no longer an assumption of this blueprint (Remark What is still admitted, after the discharges of September 2026). Full two-EPR self-testing is strictly stronger and is *not* proved by this route — but the pipeline never needed it: what the Pauli basis test consumes is one anticommutation bound on the original observables, and the argument above delivers it from the game alone, with an explicit constant and without any of the structural hypotheses rigidity would impose. For a formalization this is a much better target than Theorem [Magic Square rigidity](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/03_background_results.tex#L420): the ingredients are the projectivity of one player's measurements, a telescoping substitution bound, and one six-step word identity in a group generated by nine reflections with six relations.

**One player is projective, which is a choice, not a correction.** An earlier statement here, following the paper, allowed arbitrary POVM families on both sides, and its proof dilated the constraint measurements to projective ones on one ancilla shared by the six constraints (Lemma [Naimark dilation of a question-indexed family](../../foundations/part-02/lem-naimark-dilation.md)). The formalization is now stated in a bipartite model, where every strategy of the Pauli basis test is projective from the start (Definition [Projective strategy in a bipartite model](../../downstream-results/part-02/def-model-proj-strategy.md)), and Lemma [Win implications](../part-02/lem-qld-win.md), the one consumer, applies the bound with the constraint player projective; so the repaired measurement is used as it is, on the model's own state, and the dilation has no consumer. The POVM form would follow from the projective one by dilating the constraint player alone on a one-sided ancilla extension (Definition [The one-sided ancilla extension](../../downstream-results/part-03/def-one-sided-extension.md), Lemma [Projective dilation against a fixed state, in a star-ordered ring](../../downstream-results/part-03/lem-kraus-dilation.md)), which changes neither the other player's observables nor their norms on the state; that step is not formalized. The paper was not consulted for this revision.

Comments. The direct bound is the elementary anticommutation input that the Pauli test amplifies to many qubits. Its bipartite form already matches the soundness proof's use; a tracial specialization is optional and is not an additional pipeline prerequisite.

One clause moved out of the statement. An earlier revision also asserted the *transfer* to the point observables of the Pauli basis test: if $P, Q$ have cross-party consistency errors $s_X, s_Z$ against $B_1, B_5$ then $\left\| {\{P,Q\}|\psi\rangle} \right\| \leq 2 s_X + 2 s_Z + \left\| {\{B_1,B_5\}|\psi\rangle} \right\|$. That is not a statement about the Magic Square game at all — $P$ and $Q$ are the *Pauli* test's observables — so it belongs to Lemma [Win implications](../part-02/lem-qld-win.md), which is where it is used, and it is stated there. The argument is two substitutions across the parties: $P$ and $Q$ sit on one factor and $B_1, B_5$ on the other, so $PQ|\psi\rangle \approx_{s_Z} P B_5 |\psi\rangle = B_5 P |\psi\rangle \approx_{s_X} B_5 B_1 |\psi\rangle$, and symmetrically for $QP$.

The game this lemma is about is the one of Definition [LCS game](../../foundations/part-01/def-lcs-game.md) at the magic square's layout, and that definition is *symmetric in the players*: an equation, a variable of it, and a uniform orientation, so $36$ equiprobable oriented incidences. That is what makes the statement's Alice half expressible at all — a one-directional game would never send Alice a variable, so $A^{\textsc{Variable}_j}$ would not exist — and it is where the constant comes from: $\sum_{c,j} \ell_{c,j} \leq 36\varepsilon$ gives $\gamma^2 \leq 144\varepsilon$ and $36^2 \cdot 144 = 186624$, the paper's constant. An earlier revision of the formalization had the one-directional game, where the same argument gives $18\varepsilon$, $\gamma^2 \leq 72\varepsilon$ and $36^2 \cdot 72 = 93312\,\varepsilon$; either serves Lemma [Win implications](../part-02/lem-qld-win.md), which needs only $O(\varepsilon)$, but the game and the constant must match.

## The classical low individual degree test

[$\bullet\bullet\bullet$ hard]

- [Canonical-line low individual degree test](def-lidt.md)

Comments. This is Figure 1 of (JNVWY21qld). The direction $v$ of the diagonal lines test may be zero, in which case the “line” is the single point $u$; this degenerate case is part of the test as stated. In the formalization, line questions are the lines themselves, presented canonically (the direction is rescaled so that its first nonzero coordinate is $1$, and the base point is the point of the line whose corresponding coordinate vanishes), so that the same line asked by two sub-tests is the same question; a line answer is a polynomial in the parameter of that canonical parametrization.

- [Quantum soundness of the canonical-line low individual degree test](thm-lidt-soundness.md)

Source. Ji, Natarajan, Vidick, Wright and Yuen (JNVWY21qld), Theorem 1.3 (the formal version for general, not necessarily symmetric, strategies). The proof is a formalization by Sirui Lu and collaborators (<https://github.com/LionSR/MIPStarRE>), vendored into this repository with the authors' permission at `MIPRE/Background/LIDT/MIPStarRE/`; the statement above is this project's own, and is connected to theirs by an explicit bridge.

Comments. Two hypotheses correct the printed statement, as established by that formalization: the sampling parameter satisfies $k \geq 400md$ where the paper prints $k \geq md$, and $k$ must be positive, since the printed error vanishes at $k = 0$. The parameter $k$ remains free: the error carries both a factor $k^2$ and a term $e^{-k/(2560000m^2)}$. A chosen-$k$ corollary in the shape of $\delta_{qld}$ below is a separate quantitative derivation; it is not part of the displayed Lean theorem.

### The paper's seeded CL test and its tensor-code route

The following is the paper's route to a different game interface. Its tensor-code reduction and reviewed intermediate statements are not the proof of Theorem [Quantum soundness of the canonical-line low individual degree test](thm-lidt-soundness.md), and have no Lean proof marks here. The seeded game and its theorem are in the pinned paper snapshot (AuditSync26), `ldt.tex`, Definition of the game and Theorem `lem:ld-soundness`.

The single-codeword case of the seeded theorem *is* proved here, by a different route: an explicit adapter to the canonical-line test, Theorem [Quantum soundness of the seeded CL test, one codeword](thm-lidt-cl-soundness-one.md) below. It certifies none of the paper's intermediate statements, and they certify none of it.

- [Seeded CL low individual degree test](def-lidt-cl.md)

Comments. The formalization is `MIPRE.LIDT.CL.clGame` (`MIPRE/Background/LIDT/CLGame.lean`), beside the canonical-line game `MIPRE.LIDT.lidtGame` of Definition [Canonical-line low individual degree test](def-lidt.md) whose soundness is proved; keeping both in one vocabulary is what makes Remark The CL-to-canonical adapter and parameter corollary's adapter expressible at all. Three points.

The base point of a line question is the canonical representative $L^{\mathrm{Ln}}_w(u)$ of Definition [Orthogonal and canonical complements](../../structure-of-the-proof/part-01/def-cl-canonical.md) — the canonical linear map with kernel $\mathrm{span}\{w\}$ — which is why that vocabulary had to come first. The seed function $\chi$ is the paper's `eq:chi-func`, $0$-indexed: under the fixed bijection $\mathbb{F}_q \cong \{0, \ldots, q-1\}$, $\chi(s)$ is the block of $q/m$ elements that $s$ falls into, and $m \mid q$ is where it is used.

The seeding is not cosmetic, and `aline_question_ne` records it: two seeds in the same block give the same base point and the same direction, hence the same geometric line, and yet are *different questions*. A strategy may answer them differently, which is exactly the obligation Lemma [Setup, and the two tests are not the same test](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/03_background_results.tex#L956) discharges by choosing a seed in each fibre.

The format check is not decoration, and the formalization now separates it from the subtests (`Question.fmtOk` and `subtests`, with `accepts` their conjunction). Two clauses of `fig:ld-decider` look alike and are opposite: an answer whose format does not match its question's type is *rejected* (“If the answers returned by the players do not fit this format the decision procedure rejects”), whereas a well-formatted answer on one of the two cross type pairs is *accepted* (“in all cases where no action is indicated, accept”). An earlier version of `clGame` collapsed both into one catch-all that accepted, which made the test vacuous: the constant strategy answering a point-format answer to *every* question, the two line types included, passes every subtest, so the game has a value-$1$ strategy whose point measurements are an arbitrary function of the point, and Theorem [Quantum soundness of the seeded CL test](thm-lidt-cl-soundness.md) is false for it. That defect and its repair are recorded in `reports/clgame-format-check-missing.md`; the statement of this definition was correct throughout, which is why the repair changes only the Lean and this paragraph.

Where `fig:ld-decider` says “$t \in \mathbb{F}_q$ is such that $x = u_0 + tw$”, the point may fail to lie on the line and the condition is then vacuous. The formalization requires membership, as the canonical-line game already does: the two readings agree on the support of the question distribution, and following the convention of the test whose soundness is proved is what keeps the two comparable. The distribution itself is uniform over the nine ordered type pairs and the ambient $(u, s, v)$, so normalization is a single division rather than the per-subtest bookkeeping Definition [Canonical-line low individual degree test](def-lidt.md) needs.

Each $\chi$-fibre has exactly $q/m$ seeds (`card_chi_fiber`), so a uniform seed gives a uniform direction index and the induced axis-parallel line distribution is uniform over lines. This is the hypothesis $m \mid q$ earning its place, and it is the input Lemma [Transfer of the line and pair tests](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/03_background_results.tex#L973) consumes.

**Remark (The printed transfer corollary is false without the marginal condition).** The marginal condition $p_{{\mathcal{X}}} \leq R\nu$ in Corollary [Rigidity transfer to symmetric strategies, with the marginal condition](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/03_background_results.tex#L1096) is not book-keeping. Corollary 4.1 of (Vid22) is stated for an arbitrary auxiliary distribution $p$, and in that unrestricted form it is false; the source applies an estimate averaged over $\nu$ to $p_{{\mathcal{X}}}$ without justification. The counterexample is two questions wide. Let $\mathfrak{G}$ ask only question $0$ and check equality, let $p$ be supported on question $1$ alone, let $g(a) = \{a\}$, and let $\kappa = 1$. Every symmetric PME strategy meets the auxiliary hypothesis, using the transpose of its question-$1$ measurement: on a maximally entangled state, agreement is the normalized trace of $\sum_a (Q^1_a)^2={I}$. But the symmetric projective strategy $|00\rangle$ measuring $\sigma^Z$ at question $0$ and $\sigma^X$ at question $1$ has game value $1$ and synchronicity defect $0$, while every Bob POVM achieves auxiliary agreement exactly $\tfrac12$ — so the conclusion would force $\tfrac12 \geq \kappa(1) = 1$. Here $p_{{\mathcal{X}}}$ is supported where $\nu$ vanishes, so no finite $R$ exists and Corollary [Rigidity transfer to symmetric strategies, with the marginal condition](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/03_background_results.tex#L1096) does not apply.

## The quantum low individual degree test

[$\bullet\bullet\bullet$ hard] [[$\uparrow$ QuantumLib](https://github.com/Timeroot/Lean-QuantumInfo)]

**Remark (Scope of the formalized classical theorem).** Theorem [Quantum soundness of the canonical-line low individual degree test](thm-lidt-soundness.md) is proved from Mathlib through the vendored formalization of (JNVWY21qld). The bridge modules in `MIPRE/Background/LIDT/Bridge/` translate the canonical-line game, strategies and consistency relation to that development and back. Its axiom guard checks the Lean proof, with the corrected hypotheses $k\geq400md$ and $k>0$.

The paper's node $1.2.1$ is Theorem [Quantum soundness of the seeded CL test](thm-lidt-cl-soundness.md): a seeded CL game, simultaneous outputs and a collapsed error. Its reduction to the two-prover tensor-code theorem and the reviewed decomposition of that theorem are mathematical source material (AuditSync26), not Lean certificates. The existing canonical-line proof therefore does not discharge that node or its tensor-code subtree. Its reuse goes through the adapter and chosen-$k$ estimate of Remark The CL-to-canonical adapter and parameter corollary, both now supplied for $\mathrm{ldc}=1$: Theorem [Quantum soundness of the seeded CL test, one codeword](thm-lidt-cl-soundness-one.md) discharges the single-codeword content of node $1.2.1$, by a route that certifies nothing in the subtree, and Lemma [The parameter choice](lem-lidt-cl-adapter-params.md) is where the growth in $k^2$ and the decay in $e^{-k/(2560000m^2)}$ are accounted for.

One node of the subtree is worth reading before reusing anything from it. Node $1.2.1.7.2.4$ records that the interface between the product-basis transposes of (JNVWY20) and the Schmidt-diagonal symmetric strategies of (Vid22) is *false* as stated, with an explicit $2 \times 2$ counterexample. It is closed, not open: the repair is an approximate Takagi bridge, which puts every strategy of the first form within a controlled synchronicity defect of one of the second after a local basis change, with the closure constants re-derived and checked by a registered claim-test. It does not affect the Lean proof of Theorem [Quantum soundness of the canonical-line low individual degree test](thm-lidt-soundness.md), which does not go through the interface at all — but it is not idle on the paper's route, where the bridge is what supplies Corollary [Rigidity transfer to symmetric strategies, with the marginal condition](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/03_background_results.tex#L1096) with the Schmidt-basis input that Lemma [From the synchronous to the two-prover form](https://github.com/vidick/MIPRE-formalization/blob/5926bdff5db621f44682a101007ef2fe3ae8d970/blueprint/src/content/03_background_results.tex#L1164) needs. The lesson for a formalization is that the bridge is the thing to state, not the exact identity it replaces.

Source. Comments. The formalization is `MIPRE.QLD.qldGame` (`MIPRE/Background/QLD/Game.lean`). Five points, four of them the consequences of having a second twenty-six-type game in the same vocabulary as Definition [Seeded CL low individual degree test](def-lidt-cl.md) rather than a fresh one.

The line and point content is read off the sample by the *same* functions as the seeded CL test — $\chi$, $\pi$, $L^{\mathrm{Ln}}$, all from Definition [Seeded CL low individual degree test](def-lidt-cl.md) — and rule 2 calls that test's decider rather than restating it: `lowDeg_eq_cl` and `lowDeg_eq_cl_dline` are the two identities, and they hold by `rfl`. This is what stage 4 of Remark How the soundness proof of Theorem Pauli basis test / quantum low individual degree test is organized needs in order to feed Theorem [Quantum soundness of the seeded CL test](thm-lidt-cl-soundness.md) at all, and getting it by sharing definitions rather than by a translation lemma is the reason Definition [Seeded CL low individual degree test](def-lidt-cl.md) was written first.

The Magic Square indices are the layout's own, $0$-indexed (`MIPRE.LCS.MagicSquare.layout`), against the paper's $1$-indexed $\mathtt{Constraint}_1, \ldots, \mathtt{Constraint}_6$ and $\mathtt{Variable}_1, \ldots, \mathtt{Variable}_9$. The paper's distinguished pairs $(\mathtt{Point}, X)$–$\mathtt{Variable}_1$ and $(\mathtt{Point}, Z)$–$\mathtt{Variable}_5$ are therefore variables $0$ and $4$ in the Lean, which are also the two the anticommutator of Lemma [Direct Magic Square anticommutation — what the pipeline actually needs](lem-ms-direct-anticomm.md) is taken between — the same two cells, and they have to be, since rule 7 is what transfers that lemma's conclusion to the point observables.

The format check is separated from the rules for the reason recorded at Definition [Seeded CL low individual degree test](def-lidt-cl.md): leaving it out made that test vacuous (`reports/clgame-format-check-missing.md`), and the same error is available here with twenty-six types instead of three. Each of the eight rules accordingly carries an explicit pair of formatted answers it rejects (`rejects_*`). The four rules gated on $\gamma$ take the gate as a hypothesis, since each of them by design checks nothing on the other value of $\gamma$ — that division of labour between the commutation check and the Magic Square is what the gate is for.

The remaining vacuity risk is a rule that is simply *missing*, so that an edge of $G^\textsc{Pauli}$ falls through to the catch-all. `handled_of_adj` closes it by `decide`: every non-loop edge of the type graph has a rule. Together with the rejection witnesses — one per rule — that says no question pair the distribution asks is accepted regardless of the answers. The converse fails harmlessly: some rules also reject pairs the graph does not contain, such as $(\mathtt{Point}, X)$ against $\mathtt{Variable}_j$ for $j \neq 1$.

Finally, the answer alphabet for $\mathtt{Constraint}_i$ is $\mathbb{F}_2^3$ and not the full assignment $\mathbb{F}_2^9$ of Definition [LCS game](../../foundations/part-01/def-lcs-game.md). The two give the same value, the decider reading only the three support coordinates either way, and the full assignment would have been markedly easier to work with; the paper's format is kept because answer lengths are what the introspection of the compression chapter accounts for, and a game whose answers are six bits too long is a statement about a different verifier.
