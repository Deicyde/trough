## Set of 3-transpositions

A subset $D$ of a group $G$ is a set of 3-transpositions when it
is closed under conjugation ($g d g^{-1} \in D$ for all $g \in G$ and $d \in D$), every $d \in D$
is an involution ($d \ne 1$ and $d^2 = 1$), and for all $d, e \in D$ the product $de$ has order
at most $3$, that is, $de = 1$, $(de)^2 = 1$ or $(de)^3 = 1$.

## Locally finite group, via finite subsets

A group $G$ is locally finite when every finite
subset $S \subseteq G$ generates a finite subgroup; equivalently, every finitely generated subgroup
of $G$ is finite.

## Sozutov's question on 3-transposition groups (Kourovka 12.76)

The statement that for
every group $G$ and every set $D$ of 3-transpositions of $G$ that generates $G$, the group $G$ is
locally finite.

## Groups generated by 3-transpositions are locally finite

If a group $G$ is generated by a set
$D$ of 3-transpositions (a conjugation-invariant set of involutions whose pairwise products have
order at most $3$), then $G$ is locally finite: every finite subset of $G$ generates a finite
subgroup of $G$.

The Kourovka Archive answers 12.76 "Yes, it is" (H. Cuypers and J. I. Hall, 1992).
