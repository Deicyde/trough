A **maximal ideal** of a commutative [ring](trough:Ring) is a proper [ideal](trough:Ideal) that is not contained in any larger proper ideal. Maximal ideals are exactly the ideals whose [quotient ring](trough:Ideal.Quotient.ring) is a [field](trough:Field), which makes them the standard way to produce fields from rings: the integers modulo a prime, finite fields, and fields obtained by adjoining a root of a polynomial all arise this way. They correspond to the closed points of the prime spectrum, which makes them central in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry, and every nonzero commutative ring has at least one.

## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring. An ideal $M$ of $R$ is [maximal](lean:Ideal.IsMaximal) if $M \neq R$ and for every ideal $J$ with $M \subseteq J \subseteq R$, either $J = M$ or $J = R$. In the language of order theory, [the maximal ideals are the coatoms of the lattice of ideals](lean:Ideal.isMaximal_def), the elements just below the top element $R$.

Since an ideal equals $R$ exactly when it contains $1$, the definition can be restated elementwise: [$M$ is maximal if and only if $1 \notin M$ and every ideal that contains $M$ together with some element outside $M$ also contains $1$](lean:Ideal.isMaximal_iff).

The main characterization is through quotients: for a commutative ring $R$, [an ideal $M$ is maximal if and only if $R/M$ is a field](lean:Ideal.Quotient.maximal_ideal_iff_isField_quotient). The reason is that the ideals of $R/M$ correspond to the ideals of $R$ containing $M$, and that [a nonzero commutative ring is a field exactly when its zero ideal is maximal](lean:Ring.isField_iff_maximal_bot), that is, when it has no ideals other than $0$ and itself.

## Examples

- **Fields.** In a field $K$ [the zero ideal is maximal](lean:Ideal.bot_isMaximal), and it is the only maximal ideal, since $0$ and $K$ are the only ideals.
- **The integers.** For every prime $p$, [the ideal $p\mathbb{Z}$ is maximal](lean:Int.ideal_span_isMaximal_of_prime), and its quotient is the field $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ with $p$ elements. Every ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$ has the form $n\mathbb{Z}$ with $n \ge 0$; for composite $n$ the ideal is not maximal (for instance $6\mathbb{Z} \subsetneq 2\mathbb{Z}$), neither is the zero ideal, since $\mathbb{Z}/0 \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is not a field, and $1\mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}$ is not proper. So the maximal ideals of $\mathbb{Z}$ are exactly the ideals $p\mathbb{Z}$ with $p$ prime.
- **Principal ideal domains.** In any principal ideal ring, [the ideal generated by an irreducible element is maximal](lean:PrincipalIdealRing.isMaximal_of_irreducible). Conversely, [in a principal ideal domain that is not a field, the ideal $(p)$ is maximal exactly when $p$ is irreducible](lean:Ideal.irreducible_iff_isMaximal_span_singleton_of_not_isField) (see [irreducible element](trough:Irreducible)). Applied to the [polynomial ring](trough:Polynomial) $K[x]$ over a field, this shows that $(f)$ is maximal for every irreducible polynomial $f$; for example $(x^2 + 1)$ is maximal in $\mathbb{R}[x]$, with quotient isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}$.
- **Several variables.** In $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ the ideal $(x)$ is not maximal, since $\mathbb{Z}[x]/(x) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, but $(2, x)$ is, with quotient the field with two elements. Over an algebraically closed field $K$, one form of [Hilbert's Nullstellensatz](trough:MvPolynomial.vanishingIdeal_zeroLocus_eq_radical) says that [the maximal ideals of the polynomial ring in finitely many variables over $K$ are exactly the ideals of polynomials vanishing at a single point](lean:MvPolynomial.isMaximal_iff_eq_vanishingIdeal_singleton) $a = (a_1, \dots, a_n)$ of $K^n$; such an ideal is $(x_1 - a_1, \dots, x_n - a_n)$.

## Properties

- **Maximal ideals are prime.** In a commutative ring [every maximal ideal is a prime ideal](lean:Ideal.IsMaximal.isPrime) (see [prime ideal](trough:Ideal.IsPrime)): the quotient is a field, and a field has no zero divisors. The converse fails, as the zero ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$ shows, but it holds in two important cases: [in a principal ideal domain every nonzero prime ideal is maximal](lean:IsPrime.to_maximal_ideal), and [in an Artinian commutative ring the prime ideals and the maximal ideals coincide](lean:IsArtinianRing.isPrime_iff_isMaximal).
- **Existence.** Krull's theorem states that [every ideal other than $R$ is contained in a maximal ideal](lean:Ideal.exists_le_maximal); in particular [every ring with $0 \neq 1$ has a maximal ideal](lean:Ideal.exists_maximal). The argument applies Zorn's lemma to the proper ideals containing the given one: the union of a nonempty chain of proper ideals is an ideal, and it is proper because it does not contain $1$. It follows that [every non-unit of a commutative ring lies in some maximal ideal](lean:exists_max_ideal_of_mem_nonunits), so an element is a unit exactly when it lies in no maximal ideal.
- **Comaximality.** If $M$ and $N$ are distinct maximal ideals, then [$M + N = R$](lean:Ideal.IsMaximal.coprime_of_ne), because $M + N$ strictly contains $M$. Distinct maximal ideals are therefore pairwise coprime, which is the hypothesis of the [Chinese remainder theorem](trough:Ideal.quotientInfRingEquivPiQuotient).
- **Preimages.** [The preimage of a maximal ideal under a surjective ring homomorphism is maximal](lean:Ideal.comap_isMaximal_of_surjective). Surjectivity matters: the zero ideal of $\mathbb{Q}$ is maximal, but its preimage under the inclusion $\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q}$ is the zero ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$, which is not.

## Related concepts

A commutative ring with exactly one maximal ideal is a [local ring](trough:IsLocalRing): [a commutative ring with a unique maximal ideal is local](lean:IsLocalRing.of_unique_max_ideal), and in a local ring [every maximal ideal equals the maximal ideal of the ring](lean:IsLocalRing.eq_maximalIdeal), the ideal of non-units. Examples are fields, the ring of rational numbers whose denominators are prime to a fixed prime $p$, and rings of formal power series over a field; [localization](trough:Localization) at a prime ideal produces local rings in general. In the [prime spectrum](trough:PrimeSpectrum) of a commutative ring, the set of its prime ideals with the [Zariski topology](trough:PrimeSpectrum.zariskiTopology), the maximal ideals are exactly the closed points. The intersection of all maximal ideals is called the Jacobson radical of the ring.

## In Mathlib

Mathlib defines maximality for ideals of an arbitrary semiring, as the property of being a coatom in the complete lattice of ideals. An ideal there is a submodule of the ring over itself, so for a noncommutative ring the notion is that of a maximal left ideal; for commutative rings it is the usual one. Several basic facts, including Krull's theorem and the comaximality of distinct maximal ideals, are proved at this generality, while the field structure on the quotient and the primality of maximal ideals assume commutativity. The field structure on $R/M$ is supplied as a construction to be invoked explicitly rather than found automatically. The maximal ideal of a local ring is a separately named ideal, defined as the set of non-units, and is shown to be maximal.
