# Minimal primes

> Classifies minimal primes over nonzero principal ideals in commutative semirings with unique factorization by normalized irreducible factor values. Relates these factors to order-dual irreducible components of principal zero loci. For principal quotients in commutative rings with unique factorization, identifies associated primes with minimal primes over the principal ideal for every generator, and characterizes quotient-spectrum points by associated primes and exact annihilators. For arbitrary dependent families of semifields, classifies all prime ideals of the product by ultrafilters, with zero-set membership, evaluation and germ-kernel bridges, and finite-coordinate and infinite non-coordinate controls. The same equivalence is a homeomorphism for the Zariski and existing Stone topologies, with both basis pullback laws and dense coordinate primes. For arbitrary filters and independently universed dependent monoids with zero, the total-section filter product is reduced exactly when its reduced fibres form a filter member. For dependent semiring fibres, equips the existing total-section Filter.Product with semiring operations, fibrewise maps and principal-filter restriction equivalences. Ultrafilters give division semirings and semifields with pointwise inversion. The section-map kernels are the existing zero-set ideals, compatibly with constant-family germs. For arbitrary dependent type families, the total-section filter product is nontrivial exactly when a total section exists and nontrivial fibres occur frequently. Associated primes also transport along compatible algebra maps. In commutative rings with well-founded DvdNotUnit, prime generators give principal ideals of height at most one, with height one for non-zero-divisors and for prime elements in domains. Finite ambient initial height gives well-founded lower intervals in preorders and stabilization of antitone prime chains over commutative semirings, with an ideal interface over commutative rings using only initial FiniteHeight and primeness at every index. Both chain interfaces identify the all-index ideal infimum at the same tail index. For integral homomorphisms of arbitrary commutative rings in independent universes, bounds target Krull dimension by source dimension, with equality for injective maps. Without injectivity, the target has the dimension of the source modulo the kernel. A faithful integral algebra has the dimension of its base ring for its given algebra structure. These results use the full WithBot ℕ∞ dimension, including zero rings and infinite dimension. Minimal primes over arbitrary ideals of finite dependent products of commutative semirings correspond to labelled coordinate minimal primes, using the evaluation-image coordinates of Ideal.piOrderIso. Evaluation comap transports minimality over arbitrary ideals without finiteness of the index. The finite classification includes empty indices, subsingleton factors and whole base ideals, and retains the labels of repeated isomorphic factors. For arbitrary finite sets and WithBot ℕ∞ values, the bottom-aware successor of the nonempty-subset product sum equals the product of successors in ℕ∞, including empty indices and arbitrary bottom and top values. For arbitrary dependent families of given preorders in independent universes, the height and coheight of a supplied section in the original pointwise order are the suprema of finite coordinate-rank sums in ENat. For finite indices these are the coordinate sums, including empty indices and infinite ranks. The dimension of an arbitrary dependent pointwise product is the supremum of its finite coordinate-restriction image dimensions, with inherited subtype orders. An empty full product makes every image empty, including at the empty coordinate set. Over a finite linearly ordered index, their canonical lexicographic point ranks are sums of original coordinate ranks weighted by the whole later fibres' strict-chain vertex heights, with the same suffix priority for height and coheight. For any relation and finite family of disjoint cross-comparable sets, chain height is the sum of piece heights, with both hypotheses restricted to the chosen support.

## Scope

In arbitrary-universe commutative UFDs, associated primes of the principal quotient equal minimal primes over the principal ideal for every generator, including zero and subsingleton cases, without normalization, nonzero, domain or Noetherian premises. A quotient-spectrum point is associated over the quotient ring, or has an exact singleton quotient-ring annihilator, exactly when its forward comap image is minimal over that ideal; both criteria cover every generator. For compatible actions over commutative semirings, associated primes contract along any algebra map and reflect along a surjective one; colon contraction allows a semiring target and no surjectivity. The normalized-factor classification, factor-to-minimal-prime equivalence and component equivalence apply to commutative semirings with UniqueFactorizationMonoid. They require a nonzero generator and the caller's NormalizationMonoid; repeated factors count as one value. The factor equivalence sends each factor value p to the principal ideal generated by p. The geometric result is an Equiv into order-dual components of the zero-locus subspace, not a homeomorphism or a classification of arbitrary ideals. Mathlib's associated primes use radicals of annihilators; the separate exact-annihilator criterion is specific to these principal quotients, not a generic radical-equals-annihilator principle. In any preorder, finite ambient height of x gives a well-founded strict order on its lower interval. An antitone natural-number sequence of spectrum points over a commutative semiring stabilizes when its initial point has finite ambient order height. The commutative-ring ideal interface requires every term prime and only an initial Ideal.FiniteHeight instance; initial primeness excludes that class's unit-ideal exception. Both interfaces give one common index for tail constancy and for the all-index ideal infimum. They add no UFD, domain, global finite-dimension, ambient well-foundedness, Noetherian, Artinian or nontriviality premise. The relevant height is on the prime spectrum, not Order.height on the full ideal lattice. For a prime element p in a commutative ring with WfDvdMonoid, the ideal span {p} has height at most one. If p belongs to nonZeroDivisors R, that height is one; this includes every prime element in a commutative domain with WfDvdMonoid. The upper bound requires no domain, UFM, Noetherian or separate nontriviality premise and does not apply to arbitrary principal ideals. WfDvdMonoid is well-foundedness of DvdNotUnit, equivalent to ACCP for principal ideals in domains, not merely irreducible factorization. For any field K, the infinite-variable polynomial examples in MinimalPrimesTest/RingTheory/Ideal/PrincipalPrimeHeight.lean instantiate all three principal-prime height results at X₀, derive initial FiniteHeight from height one, and apply the ideal chain interface to a sequence staying at (X₀) through any index d and genuinely dropping to zero at d+1. The interface supplies one common index for tail constancy and the all-index infimum. The ambient ring has infinite Krull dimension. The separate infinite-variable argument in the finite-height ring client's module documentation remains a mathematical sketch. No negative-Noetherianity certificate is included in the principal-prime client; infinite Krull dimension alone does not establish non-Noetherianity. For an integral homomorphism f : R →+* S of commutative rings in independent universes, ringKrullDim S ≤ ringKrullDim R, with equality when f is injective. Without injectivity, ringKrullDim S = ringKrullDim (R ⧸ RingHom.ker f). A given Algebra R S that is integral and faithful gives ringKrullDim R = ringKrullDim S. These statements use the full WithBot ℕ∞ codomain, including bottom for the zero ring and top for infinite dimension. The finite-product successor identity uses WithBot ℕ∞ products and sums over nonempty subsets, with equality in ℕ∞ and no index equality, nonempty-index, nonbottom or finite-value hypothesis. For arbitrary given preorders α : Type u and β : Type v in independent universes and points a : α, b : β, the canonical binary Prod.Lex preorder satisfies height (toLex (a,b)) = height a * Hβ + height b and coheight (toLex (a,b)) = coheight a * Hβ + coheight b in ℕ∞, with the same Hβ = (Set.univ : Set β).chainHeight (· < ·), the whole second factor's strict-chain vertex cardinality. This coefficient is not its Krull dimension, the rank of b, or its carrier cardinality. Weak comparison is first-coordinate strict comparison or first-coordinate equality followed by second-coordinate weak comparison; strict comparison uses second-coordinate strict comparison instead. Distinct weakly equivalent first coordinates give incomparable fibres, while fixed-first fibres retain distinct weakly equivalent second points and their mutual weak comparisons. This is not the dependent-function equality-or-strict partial order or the pointwise product order. The point-rank bridges identify height a and coheight a with the strict-chain vertex heights of the original Iio a and Ioi a for the given strict relation, without a closed-segment decomposition that fails for general preorders. There are no linearity, antisymmetry, global nonemptiness, decidable-equality, bottom/top, finiteness, finite-height or attainment premises. The given points inhabit the factors; empty strict segments have height zero, 0 * ⊤ = 0, and unbounded finite chains need not attain infinite height. The middle point of Fin 3 ×ₗ Fin 3 has both height and coheight four, rather than the pointwise values two; its second-factor weight is three, not dimension two. For a supplied section of an arbitrary dependent family of given preorders, height and coheight in the original pointwise product equal the suprema of finite coordinate-rank sums in ENat. The finite-index formulas remain coordinate sums. Empty indices and infinite or unattained ranks are included, without antisymmetry, fibre inhabitation, finite-height or finite-support hypotheses. The dimension of an arbitrary dependent pointwise product is the supremum in WithBot ENat of the dimensions of the ranges of its finite coordinate restrictions. Each range retains the inherited pointwise subtype order and has the original full product as the restriction domain. An empty full product makes all images empty, including the empty-coordinate image, and gives bottom; an empty ambient index gives zero. No nonemptiness, finiteness, antisymmetry, finite-rank, attainment or finite-support hypothesis is required. An infinite supremum need not be attained by a finite image. No topological product formula is asserted. For an original index ι : Type u with Fintype and LinearOrder, independently universed arbitrary given fibre preorders β : ι → Type v and a given section f, height (toLex f) is the sum of height (f i) times the product over original j > i of (Set.univ : Set (β j)).chainHeight (· < ·); coheight uses coordinate coheights and exactly the same suffix. All arithmetic is ENat. The target is the existing Pi.Lex.partialOrderOfLT, equality or strict lexicographic comparison with equality prefixes, not the pointwise order or the binary Prod.Lex final weak fallback. Every coordinate rank, strict comparison and whole-fibre height uses the original preorder. The weights are neither dimensions, carrier cardinalities nor ranks of f j. No fibre partial order, linearity or finiteness, global fibre inhabitation, nonempty index, finite height, attainment, decidable equality, bounds, coherence or separate irreflexivity premise is required. An empty index gives ranks zero; an empty strict cut absorbs an infinite suffix by 0 * ∞ = 0. A singleton index preserves ranks, without asserting a weak-order isomorphism. Unbounded finite chain heights need not be attained. For the all-one section of Fin 3 → Fin 3, both canonical lexicographic ranks are 9 + 3 + 1 = 13, versus 1 + 1 + 1 = 3 for the original pointwise order. For Fin 2 followed by Fin 3 at (1,1), coheight is 1, not the reversed-priority value 2. The formulas compose the whole existing finite-PiLex chain-height, finite disjoint cross-comparable union and original strict-cone/point-rank APIs on first-change cones. For arbitrary q : X → X → Prop, P : κ → Set X and J : Finset κ, chain height of the union over i ∈ J equals the sum of the piece heights when disjointness and cross comparability hold only for distinct indices in J. There are no relation laws, ambient finiteness, decidable-equality, nonemptiness or attainment hypotheses; empty pieces and unbounded unattained heights are included.
