## Locally nilpotent subgroup

A subgroup $N$ of a group $G$ is locally nilpotent when every
subgroup generated by finitely many elements of $N$ is nilpotent.

## Hirsch–Plotkin radical

The Hirsch–Plotkin radical of a group $G$ is the subgroup
generated by all locally nilpotent normal subgroups of $G$. By the Hirsch–Plotkin theorem it is
the largest locally nilpotent normal subgroup of $G$.

## Locally nilpotent group (Kourovka 13.16)

A group $G$ is locally nilpotent if for every
finite subset $S \subseteq G$ the subgroup generated by $S$ is nilpotent, that is, every finitely
generated subgroup of $G$ is nilpotent.

## Minimal condition on centralizers (Kourovka 13.16)

For a subset $X$ of a group $G$, let
$C_G(X)$ be its centralizer, the subgroup of elements of $G$ that commute with every element of
$X$. The group $G$ satisfies the minimal condition on centralizers if every nonempty set of such
centralizers has a minimal element with respect to inclusion; equivalently, there is no infinite
strictly descending chain $C_G(X_0) > C_G(X_1) > \cdots$ of centralizers.

## Hypercentral group (Kourovka 13.16)

A group $G$ is hypercentral if for every proper normal
subgroup $N$ of $G$ there is some $g \in G$ with $g \notin N$ and $g h g^{-1} h^{-1} \in N$ for all
$h \in G$; that is, every nontrivial quotient $G/N$ has nontrivial centre (equivalently, the
transfinite upper central series of $G$ reaches $G$).

## Locally nilpotent groups with the minimal condition on centralizers are hypercentral

Every
locally nilpotent group satisfying the minimal condition on centralizers is hypercentral. This
answers Kourovka Problem 13.16 (F. O. Wagner) in the affirmative (V. V. Bludov, 1998).
