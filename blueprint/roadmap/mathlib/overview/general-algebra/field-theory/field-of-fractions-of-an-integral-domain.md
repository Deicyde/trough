# Field of fractions of an integral domain

The **field of fractions** of an integral domain $A$ is the smallest
[field](field.md) in which $A$ sits as a subring: its elements are quotients
$a/b$ of elements of $A$ with $b \neq 0$, added and multiplied by the rules of
school arithmetic. The passage from the [integers](../numbers/integer.md) to the
[rational numbers](../numbers/rational-number.md) is the model case, and the passage from polynomials
to [rational functions](../../../undergraduate/ring-theory/field-theory/rational-fraction-fields-with-one-indeterminate.md) is another. The construction lets questions
about a domain be asked inside a field, where linear algebra and the theory of field
extensions are available, and it is the special case of
[localization](../rings/localization.md) in which every nonzero element is inverted.

## Definition

Let $A$ be an integral domain, that is, a nonzero commutative [ring](../rings/ring.md)
without zero divisors. On pairs $(a, b)$ with $a, b \in A$ and $b \neq 0$, call
$(a, b)$ and $(c, d)$ equivalent when $ad = bc$. Write $a/b$ for the class of
$(a, b)$ and define

$$\frac{a}{b} + \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ad + bc}{bd}, \qquad \frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ac}{bd}.$$

These operations are well defined, and the classes form the
[field of fractions](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/FractionRing.html#FractionRing) of $A$, written $\operatorname{Frac}(A)$.
The same classes arise from the general construction of a localization, in which
$(a, b)$ and $(c, d)$ are related when $u(ad) = u(bc)$ for some allowed denominator
$u$. There [two fractions are equal exactly when their pairs are so related](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/MonoidLocalization/Basic.html#Localization.mk_eq_mk_iff), and, because no allowed denominator is a
zero divisor, [the pairs $(a, b)$ and $(c, d)$ are related exactly when $ad = bc$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/Basic.html#Localization.r_iff_of_le_nonZeroDivisors). The ring obtained
[is a field](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/FractionRing.html#FractionRing.field): a nonzero fraction $a/b$ has $a \neq 0$, and
its inverse is $b/a$. The map $a \mapsto a/1$ is an injective
[ring homomorphism](../rings/ring-morphism.md) through which $A$ is regarded as a subring.

Since many fields contain a copy of a given domain, it is useful to say when a
given field $K$, together with a ring homomorphism $f$ from $A$ to $K$,
[is a field of fractions of $A$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/FractionRing.html#IsFractionRing). For a field $K$ this happens
exactly when $f$ is injective and every element of $K$ is a quotient $f(a)/f(b)$:
[these two conditions suffice](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/FractionRing.html#IsFractionRing.of_field), and conversely, if $K$
is a field of fractions, then [$f$ is injective](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/FractionRing.html#IsFractionRing.injective) and
[every element of $K$ has the form $f(a)/f(b)$ with $b$ a non-zero-divisor](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/FractionRing.html#IsFractionRing.div_surjective), which in a domain means $b \neq 0$.

## Universal property

The field of fractions is the most economical field containing $A$. Let $K$ be a
field of fractions of $A$, let $L$ be any field and $g$ an injective ring
homomorphism from $A$ to $L$. Then
[$g$ extends to a ring homomorphism $h$ from $K$ to $L$, so that $h(f(a)) = g(a)$ for all $a \in A$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/FractionRing.html#IsFractionRing.lift_algebraMap), namely
$h(f(a)/f(b)) = g(a)/g(b)$, and
[any ring homomorphism from $K$ to $L$ with this property equals $h$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/FractionRing.html#IsFractionRing.lift_unique). Moreover
[the image of $h$ is the subfield of $L$ generated by $g(A)$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/FractionRing.html#IsFractionRing.lift_fieldRange). So whenever a domain is a subring of a
field, the [subfield](subfield.md) it generates is a copy of its field of
fractions; for instance the subfield of $\mathbb C$ generated by the Gaussian
integers $\mathbb Z[i]$ is $\mathbb Q(i)$.

It follows that the field of fractions is unique up to unique isomorphism: every
field of fractions $K$ of $A$
[is isomorphic, as an $A$-algebra, to the construction $\operatorname{Frac}(A)$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/FractionRing.html#FractionRing.algEquiv).

## Examples

- **Integers.** [$\mathbb Q$ is the field of fractions of $\mathbb Z$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/FractionRing.html#Rat.isFractionRing).
- **Polynomials.** For a field $k$, the field of rational functions $k(X)$, whose
  elements are quotients of [polynomials](../polynomials-and-power-series/polynomial-in-one-indeterminate.md),
  [is isomorphic, as a $k$-algebra, to the field of fractions of $k[X]$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/FieldTheory/RatFunc/Basic.html#RatFunc.toFractionRingAlgEquiv).
- **Fields.** An integral domain
  [is all of its field of fractions, in the sense that $a \mapsto a/1$ is onto, exactly when it is already a field](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/FractionRing.html#IsFractionRing.surjective_iff_isField).
- **Rings of integers.** If $A$ is an integral domain with field of fractions $K$
  and $L$ is a finite extension of $K$, then
  [the integral closure of $A$ in $L$ has field of fractions $L$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/Integral.html#integralClosure.isFractionRing_of_finite_extension). With
  $A = \mathbb Z$ and $K = \mathbb Q$ this says that a number field (a finite
  extension of $\mathbb Q$) is the field of fractions of its ring of integers (the
  integral closure of $\mathbb Z$ in it); for example $\mathbb Q(i)$ is that of $\mathbb Z[i]$.

## Properties

- **Generation.** [The subfield of $K$ generated by the image of $A$ is all of $K$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/FractionRing.html#IsFractionRing.closure_range_algebraMap), so no smaller field contains
  $A$.
- **Domains and fields.** The same definition makes sense for any commutative ring
  $R$ if one inverts only the elements that are not zero divisors; the result is
  called the total ring of fractions. It is a field only in the case of this
  article: [$R$ is an integral domain if and only if its ring of fractions is a field](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/FractionRing.html#IsFractionRing.isDomain_iff_isField). At the other extreme,
  [$R$ is its own ring of fractions exactly when every element that is not a zero divisor is a unit](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/FractionRing.html#IsFractionRing.self_iff_nonZeroDivisors_le_isUnit), as in
  $\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$, whose non-zero-divisors $1$ and $5$ are units.
- **Integral closure.** A domain is
  [integrally closed](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/IntegralClosure/IntegrallyClosed.html#IsIntegrallyClosed) when every element of its field of
  fractions that is a root of a monic polynomial with coefficients in the domain
  already lies in the domain. In a [unique factorisation domain](../divisibility-in-integral-domains/unique-factorisation-domain.md)
  [every such element lies in the domain](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Polynomial/RationalRoot.html#UniqueFactorizationMonoid.integer_of_integral),
  as the monic case of the [integral root theorem](../../../thousand-plus/algebra/12-field-theory-and-polynomials/integral-root-theorem.md)
shows, itself a consequence of the [rational root theorem](../../../thousand-plus/algebra/12-field-theory-and-polynomials/rational-root-theorem.md).

## Related concepts

The field of fractions is the [localization](../rings/localization.md) of $A$ at the
set of its nonzero elements; localizing at smaller multiplicative sets gives
rings between $A$ and $\operatorname{Frac}(A)$, such as the ring of rationals with
odd denominator. [Fractional ideals](../ideals-and-quotients/fractional-ideal.md) of $A$ are
$A$-submodules of $\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ that behave like ideals with a common
denominator, and they are the setting for the ideal theory of Dedekind domains.

## In Mathlib

Mathlib separates the property from the construction. The property says that a
given algebra $K$ over a commutative ring $R$ is a ring of fractions of $R$, and it
is defined as being the localization of $R$ at its non-zero-divisors; the
construction is one particular such algebra, and any other is isomorphic to it, so
results are stated for an arbitrary $K$ with the property. Because the property is
stated for any commutative ring, not only domains, it describes the total ring of
fractions in general, and the field structure is proved, not assumed, when $R$ is a
domain. It is even stated for commutative semirings, which covers the nonnegative
rationals as the semiring of fractions of the natural numbers. The criterion for a
field to be a field of fractions phrases injectivity as faithfulness of the scalar
action of $R$ on $K$, which for an algebra amounts to the same thing. Integral
closedness is defined with respect to the standard construction, and Mathlib proves
that any other field of fractions may be used instead.
