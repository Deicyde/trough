# Powerful p-groups

## Kourovka 21.137, main question

The proposition that there is a prime $p$ such that for
every finite $p$-group $G$ and every subgroup $P$ of $G$ whose underlying set is exactly the set
$\{x^p : x \in G\}$ of $p$-th powers in $G$, the subgroup $P$ is powerful: its commutator subgroup
$[P, P]$ is contained in the subgroup $P^m$ generated by the $m$-th powers of the elements of $P$,
where $m = p$ for odd $p$ and $m = 4$ for $p = 2$.

Kourovka Problem 21.137 (L. Wilson) asks this for each prime; it is stated here for some prime, so
that its refutation covers all of them.

## Kourovka 21.137, odd-prime form

The proposition that there is an odd prime $p$ such that
for every finite $p$-group $G$ of exponent $p^2$ and every subgroup $P$ of $G$ whose underlying
set is exactly the set $\{x^p : x \in G\}$ of $p$-th powers in $G$, the subgroup $P$ is abelian.

Kourovka Problem 21.137 (L. Wilson) asks this for each odd prime; it is stated here for some odd
prime, so that its refutation covers all of them.

## Kourovka 21.137 fails for every prime

For every prime $p$ there is a finite $p$-group $G$
with a subgroup $P$ whose underlying set is exactly the set $\{x^p : x \in G\}$ of $p$-th powers in
$G$, such that $P$ is not powerful: the commutator subgroup $[P, P]$ is not contained in the
subgroup $P^m$ generated by the $m$-th powers of the elements of $P$, where $m = p$ for odd $p$
and $m = 4$ for $p = 2$.

For odd $p$ a counterexample is Muliarchyk's group of order $p^{3p+1}$ and exponent $p^2$, whose
$p$-th powers form a non-abelian subgroup of exponent $p$; for $p = 2$ it is the Sylow
$2$-subgroup of $S_8$, whose squares form $C_2 \times D_8$ (letter of A. Chang). The 21st issue of
the Kourovka Notebook marks Problem 21.137 solved ("No, it need not be").

## Kourovka 21.137 fails for every odd prime

For every odd prime $p$ there is a finite
$p$-group $G$ of exponent $p^2$ with a subgroup $P$ whose underlying set is exactly the set
$\{x^p : x \in G\}$ of $p$-th powers in $G$, such that $P$ is not abelian.

Muliarchyk's counterexample has order $p^{3p+1}$, and its subgroup of $p$-th powers is isomorphic
to $\mathrm{UT}_3(\mathbb{F}_p) \times C_p^{p-1}$. The 21st issue of the Kourovka Notebook marks
Problem 21.137 solved ("No, it need not be"), citing this preprint for all odd primes.
