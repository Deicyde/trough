## Partial sums of indicators

For sets $A_0, A_1, \dots$ in $\Omega$ and $n \in \mathbb{N}$,
$S_n = \sum_{i < n} 1_{A_i}$ is the sum of the indicators of the first $n$ sets, viewed as a
real-valued function on $\Omega$.

## The partial sum as a sum of indicator functions

For sets $A_0, A_1, \dots$ in $\Omega$ and
every $n$, the partial sum $S_n$, defined pointwise by $S_n(\omega) = \sum_{i < n} 1_{A_i}(\omega)$,
equals, as a function on $\Omega$, the sum $\sum_{i < n} 1_{A_i}$ of the real-valued indicator
functions.

## Variance of an indicator is at most its probability

Let $\mu$ be a probability measure
on $\Omega$ and let $s \subseteq \Omega$ be measurable. Then the indicator function $1_s$
satisfies $\operatorname{Var}_\mu(1_s) \le \mu(s)$.

In fact $\operatorname{Var}_\mu(1_s) = \mu(s)(1 - \mu(s))$.

## Variance of a sum of pairwise independent indicators is at most its mean

Let $\mu$ be
a probability measure on $\Omega$ and let $A_0, A_1, \dots$ be pairwise independent measurable
subsets of $\Omega$. Then for every $n$, the partial sum $S_n = \sum_{i < n} 1_{A_i}$ satisfies
$\operatorname{Var}(S_n) \le \mathbb{E}[S_n]$, where variance and expectation are taken
with respect to $\mu$.

This follows from $\operatorname{Var}(1_{A_i}) \le \mu(A_i)$ and the fact that pairwise
independence makes covariances vanish.

## Divergent total probability makes the expected partial sums diverge

## Quadratic subsequence for the pairwise-independent strong law

Let $\mu$ be a
probability measure on $\Omega$, let $A_0, A_1, \dots$ be subsets of $\Omega$, and write
$S_n = \sum_{i < n} 1_{A_i}$. Assuming $\mathbb{E}[S_n] = \int S_n \, d\mu \to \infty$ as
$n \to \infty$, this is the sequence $\varphi : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ for which
$\varphi(k)$ is the least $n$ with $\mathbb{E}[S_n] \ge (k+1)^2$.

Because the thresholds grow quadratically, the Chebyshev tail bounds for
$S_{\varphi(k)} / \mathbb{E}[S_{\varphi(k)}]$ are summable.

## Almost sure convergence from shrinking tail bounds

Let $\mu$ be a probability measure on
$\Omega$, let $f_k : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ for $k \in \mathbb{N}$, and let $a \in \mathbb{R}$.
Suppose that for every $m \in \mathbb{N}$, for $\mu$-almost every $\omega$, we have
$|f_k(\omega) - a| < 1/(m+1)$ for all sufficiently large $k$. Then for $\mu$-almost every
$\omega$, $f_k(\omega) \to a$ as $k \to \infty$.

## Chebyshev bound along the subsequence

Let $\mu$ be a probability measure on $\Omega$, let
$A_0, A_1, \dots \subseteq \Omega$ be pairwise independent measurable sets, let
$S_n = \sum_{i\lt n} 1_{A_i}$, and suppose that $\mathbb{E}[S_n] \to \infty$. For $k \in \mathbb{N}$
let $\varphi(k)$ be the least $n$ with $\mathbb{E}[S_n] \ge (k+1)^2$. Then for every
$\varepsilon > 0$ and every $k \in \mathbb{N}$, the probability of the set of $\omega$ with
$|S_{\varphi(k)}(\omega)/\mathbb{E}[S_{\varphi(k)}] - 1| \ge \varepsilon$ is at most
$1/(\varepsilon^2 (k+1)^2)$.

These bounds are summable in $k$, which gives almost sure convergence along the subsequence.

## Summable deviation probabilities along the quadratic subsequence

Let $\mu$ be a
probability measure on $\Omega$, let $A_0, A_1, \dots$ be pairwise independent measurable
subsets of $\Omega$, write $S_n = \sum_{i < n} 1_{A_i}$, and suppose that
$\mathbb{E}[S_n] = \int S_n \, d\mu \to \infty$ as $n \to \infty$. Let $\varphi(k)$ be the least
$n$ with $\mathbb{E}[S_n] \ge (k+1)^2$. Then for every real $\varepsilon > 0$, the sum over
$k \in \mathbb{N}$ of the probabilities
$\mu\{\omega : |S_{\varphi(k)}(\omega) / \mathbb{E}[S_{\varphi(k)}] - 1| \ge \varepsilon\}$
is finite.

By Chebyshev's inequality the $k$-th term is at most $1/(\varepsilon^2 (k+1)^2)$; this is the
Borel–Cantelli input along the subsequence.

## Upper bound for the expected partial sums along the subsequence

Let $\mu$ be a probability
measure on $\Omega$, let $A_0, A_1, \dots \subseteq \Omega$ be measurable sets, let

## Ratios of consecutive subsequence expectations tend to one

Let $\mu$ be a probability
measure on $\Omega$, let $A_0, A_1, \dots \subseteq \Omega$ be measurable sets, let
$S_n = \sum_{i\lt n} 1_{A_i}$, and suppose that $\mathbb{E}[S_n] \to \infty$. For $k \in \mathbb{N}$
let $\varphi(k)$ be the least $n$ with $\mathbb{E}[S_n] \ge (k+1)^2$. Then
$\mathbb{E}[S_{\varphi(k+1)}] / \mathbb{E}[S_{\varphi(k)}] \to 1$ as $k \to \infty$.

This slow growth lets the proof of the pairwise-independent strong law pass from the subsequence
to the full sequence.

## Pairwise-independent strong law for indicator sums

Let $A_1, A_2, \dots$ be pairwise
independent events in a probability space with $\sum_n P(A_n) = \infty$, and write
$S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n 1_{A_i}$. Then almost surely $S_n / E[S_n] \to 1$ as $n \to \infty$, where
$E[S_n] = \sum_{i=1}^n P(A_i)$.
