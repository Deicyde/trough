## Minimal number of generators of a subgroup

For a subgroup $H$ of a group $G$, the
minimal number of generators of $H$ is the least natural number $n$ such that $H$ is generated by
some finite set of $n$ elements of $G$; it is $0$ when $H$ is not finitely generated.

## Rank of a group

The rank of a group $G$ is the supremum, in the natural numbers, of the
minimal numbers of generators of the subgroups of $G$, where an unbounded set of natural numbers
has supremum $0$. For a finite group $G$ it is the largest minimal number of generators of a
subgroup of $G$.

## Cyclic lower central factors from the second on

Let $\gamma_1(P) = P$ and
$\gamma_{i+1}(P) = [\gamma_i(P), P]$ be the lower central series of a group $P$. The group $P$
has cyclic lower central factors from the second on if for every $i \ge 2$ there is an element
$g \in \gamma_i(P)$ such that every $x \in \gamma_i(P)$ satisfies $x g^{-k} \in \gamma_{i+1}(P)$
for some integer $k$, that is, the factor $\gamma_i(P)/\gamma_{i+1}(P)$ is cyclic.

## Rank analogue of the Shepherd-Leedham-Green-McKay theorem (Kourovka 16.103)

The
statement that for some prime $p$ there is a natural number $r$ such that every finite $p$-group
$P$ generated by two elements whose lower central factors $\gamma_i(P)/\gamma_{i+1}(P)$ are
cyclic for all $i \ge 2$ has a normal subgroup $N$ of nilpotency class at most $2$ with $P/N$ of
rank at most $r$. Here $\gamma_1(P) = P$ and $\gamma_{i+1}(P) = [\gamma_i(P), P]$, and the rank
of a finite group is the largest minimal number of generators of its subgroups.

Kourovka 16.103 (E. I. Khukhro) asks for a bound $r(p)$ for each prime $p$; this form asks for
one prime, so its negation says that no prime has such a bound.

## No rank analogue of the Shepherd-Leedham-Green-McKay theorem (Kourovka 16.103)

For every
prime $p$ and every natural number $r$ there is a finite $p$-group $P$ generated by two elements,
whose lower central factors $\gamma_i(P)/\gamma_{i+1}(P)$ are cyclic for all $i \ge 2$, such that
for every normal subgroup $N$ of $P$ of nilpotency class at most $2$ the quotient $P/N$ has rank
greater than $r$. Here $\gamma_1(P) = P$ and $\gamma_{i+1}(P) = [\gamma_i(P), P]$, and the rank of
a finite group is the largest minimal number of generators of its subgroups. That is, the
statement of Kourovka Problem 16.103 fails for every prime.

Khukhro's groups even have no normal subgroup of bounded derived length with quotient of bounded
rank; the Kourovka Archive answers 16.103 "No".
