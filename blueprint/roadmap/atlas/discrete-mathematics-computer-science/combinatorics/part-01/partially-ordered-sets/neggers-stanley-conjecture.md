## Number of descents of a permutation of $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$

For a permutation $w$ of
$\{0, 1, \dots, n-1\}$, read as the word $w(0)\,w(1) \cdots w(n-1)$, the number of descents
$d(w)$ is the number of indices $i$ with $i + 1 < n$ and $w(i) > w(i+1)$.

## The $W$-polynomial of a labeled poset

Let $R$ be a relation on the labels
$\{0, 1, \dots, n-1\}$, with $R(i, j)$ read as $i \lt _P j$ in the poset $P$ it generates. A linear
extension of $P$ is a permutation $w$ of the labels such that $a < b$ whenever $R(w(a), w(b))$,
that is, a word $w(0)\,w(1) \cdots w(n-1)$ listing every label once with $w(a)$ before $w(b)$
whenever $R(w(a), w(b))$. The $W$-polynomial is the integer polynomial
$W(P, x) = \sum_w x^{d(w)}$, summed over the linear extensions $w$ of $P$, where $d(w)$ is the
number of descents of $w$.

Linear extensions of $R$ and of its transitive closure coincide, so $R$ need not be transitive.

## Neggers' conjecture

For every $n$ and every relation $R$ on the labels
$\{0, 1, \dots, n-1\}$ that is naturally labeled, meaning that $R(i, j)$ implies $i < j$, every
complex zero of the $W$-polynomial $W(P, x)$ of the poset $P$ generated by $R$ is real.

This is Stanley, Positivity Problems, Problem 20, for naturally labeled posets.

## Neggers' conjecture is false

There exist $n$ and a naturally labeled relation $R$ on the
labels $\{0, 1, \dots, n-1\}$, one in which $R(i, j)$ implies $i < j$, such that the
$W$-polynomial $W(P, x)$ of the poset $P$ generated by $R$ has a complex zero that is not real.

Stembridge found naturally labeled posets on $17$ elements whose $W$-polynomials have non-real
zeros, disproving Neggers' conjecture and hence the Neggers–Stanley conjecture (Brändén had
earlier given counterexamples with non-natural labelings).
