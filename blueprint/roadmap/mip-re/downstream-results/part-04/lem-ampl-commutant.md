# The commutant of a matrix amplification

Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a Hilbert space and $\iota$, $\alpha$, $\beta$ finite sets.

1. An operator $T$ on $\mathcal{H} \otimes \mathbb{C}^\iota$ is the operator of the matrix of its compressions $T_{ij}$, from the $j$-th copy of $\mathcal{H}$ to the $i$-th; the matrix of the operator of a matrix is that matrix; and $T$ commutes with the operator of a matrix of operators exactly when the matrix of $T$ commutes with that matrix.
2. A matrix over a ring commutes with the constant diagonal matrix of an element $f$ exactly when its entries commute with $f$. An $(\alpha \times \beta)$-matrix commuting with every ${I}_\alpha \otimes E_{bb'}$ is $X \otimes {I}_\beta$, and symmetrically. The matrix units of a register are generated by its diagonal projections and the projection $|u\rangle\langleu|$ onto its uniform superposition: $E_{bb'} = |\beta|\, E_{bb} |u\rangle\langleu| E_{b'b'}$.
3. Consequently, if $\beta$ is nonempty and an operator $T$ on $\mathcal{H} \otimes \mathbb{C}^{\alpha \times \beta}$ commutes with $f \otimes {I}$ for every $f$ in a set $s$ of operators and with every ${I}_\alpha \otimes E_{bb'}$, then $T = X \otimes {I}_\beta$ with every entry of $X$ commuting with $s$; and symmetrically for the other register. The matrices of one register act injectively when the other is nonempty, and both registers of a unit vector are nonempty.

An element commuting with a $*$-closed set lies in its commutant, and $\{P, {I} - P\}$ is a projective measurement for every orthogonal projection $P$.

## Proof

A vector of $\mathcal{H} \otimes \mathbb{C}^\iota$ is the sum of its embedded coordinates, so an operator is determined by its compressions, and composition is the matrix product of the compressions. Commuting with ${I}_\alpha \otimes E_{bb'}$ moves the blocks of a matrix: the block at $(b, b')$ is the block at $(b_0, b_0)$ when $b = b'$ and vanishes otherwise. The identity for the matrix units is a computation with $|u\rangle\langleu|$, all of whose entries are $1/|\beta|$. Item 3 combines the two previous items through the operators of matrices.
