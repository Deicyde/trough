The **abelianization** of a [group](trough:Group) $G$ is the largest abelian quotient of
$G$: the quotient obtained by imposing the relations $gh = hg$ for all
elements $g, h$ and no others. It is written $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$. Every homomorphism
from $G$ to an abelian group passes through it, so it carries exactly the
information about $G$ that is detected by homomorphisms to abelian groups. It appears throughout algebra and topology, from the sign of a
permutation to the first homology group of a space.

## Definition

For elements $g, h$ of $G$ the commutator is $[g, h] = g h g^{-1} h^{-1}$; it
equals the identity exactly when $g$ and $h$ commute. The commutator subgroup
$[G, G]$ is the [subgroup generated by](trough:Subgroup.closure) all commutators
(the commutator subgroup is the closure of the set of commutators).
It is a [normal subgroup](trough:Subgroup.Normal), indeed a characteristic one,
since every automorphism of $G$ sends commutators to commutators. The
abelianization is the [quotient group](trough:QuotientGroup.Quotient.group)

$$G^{\mathrm{ab}} = G / [G, G],$$

which is the abelianization of $G$, together with the
quotient map $G \to G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, a surjective
[homomorphism](trough:MonoidHom) whose kernel is the commutator subgroup.

The quotient is abelian because the image of $ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$ is the identity,
so the images of $g$ and $h$ commute. More is true: for a normal subgroup $N$ of
$G$, the quotient $G/N$ is abelian if and only if $N$ contains $[G, G]$.
So $[G, G]$ is the smallest normal subgroup with abelian quotient, and
$G^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is the largest abelian quotient of $G$.

## Universal property

Let $A$ be an abelian group and $f \colon G \to A$ a homomorphism. Since $A$ is
abelian, $f$ sends every commutator to the identity, so
the commutator subgroup lies in the kernel of $f$.
Hence $f$ factors through the quotient map: there is a homomorphism
$\bar f \colon G^{\mathrm{ab}} \to A$ with $\bar f(g[G, G]) = f(g)$, and it is
the only homomorphism with this property.
In other words, homomorphisms $G \to A$ correspond bijectively to homomorphisms $G^{\mathrm{ab}} \to A$,
the correspondence being composition with the quotient map. This property
characterizes $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$ up to unique isomorphism, and it is the usual way
to compute with it.

## Examples

- **Abelian groups.** $G$ is abelian exactly when
  its commutator subgroup is trivial, and then
  $G$ is isomorphic to its abelianization.
- **Symmetric groups.** For $n \ge 2$ the commutator subgroup of the symmetric
  group $S_n$ is the [alternating group](trough:alternatingGroup) $A_n$, so the
  [sign](trough:Equiv.Perm.sign) induces an isomorphism $S_n^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \{\pm 1\}$.
  For every $n$, the commutator subgroup of $S_n$ is contained in $A_n$,
  since the sign of a commutator is $1$.
  For at least five letters the two are equal;
  for $n = 2, 3, 4$ equality can be checked directly.
- **Perfect groups.** A group equal to its own commutator subgroup has trivial
  abelianization. Every non-abelian [simple group](trough:IsSimpleGroup) is of
  this kind, and so is $A_n$ for $n \ge 5$:
  the commutator subgroup of $A_n$ is all of $A_n$.
- **Dihedral and quaternion groups.** In the dihedral group of order $2n$, with
  rotation $r$ and reflection $s$, one has $[r, s] = r^2$, and the commutator
  subgroup is generated by $r^2$. The
  abelianization is cyclic of order $2$ when $n$ is odd and is the Klein four-group
  when $n$ is even. The quaternion group of order $8$ also has the Klein
  four-group as abelianization.
- **Free groups.** The abelianization of the [free group](trough:FreeGroup) on a
  set $S$ is the free abelian group on $S$, the group of finitely supported
  functions $S \to \mathbb{Z}$.

## Properties

- **Functoriality.** A homomorphism $f \colon G \to H$
  induces a homomorphism $G^{\mathrm{ab}} \to H^{\mathrm{ab}}$,
  and this is compatible with composition. In the
  language of category theory, abelianization is a [functor](trough:CategoryTheory.Functor)
  from groups to abelian groups, and the universal property says that it is
  left adjoint to the inclusion of abelian groups into groups
  (see [adjunction](trough:CategoryTheory.Adjunction)).
- **Size.** As a quotient of $G$, the abelianization is no larger than $G$, and
  it is finite, or finitely generated, whenever $G$ is. A finitely generated
  abelianization is then described by the
  [structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups](trough:AddCommGroup.equiv_free_prod_directSum_zmod).
- **Derived series.** Taking commutator subgroups repeatedly gives the derived series
  $G \ge [G, G] \ge [[G, G], [G, G]] \ge \cdots$, whose
  first step is the commutator subgroup. A group
  is solvable when this series reaches the trivial group after finitely many steps.

## Related concepts

In topology, the first homology group of a path-connected space is the
abelianization of its fundamental group; this is the first case of the
Hurewicz theorem. In number theory, a topological
variant matters: the quotient of the absolute Galois group of a field by the
closure of its commutator subgroup is the Galois group of the maximal abelian
extension of the field inside a fixed separable closure, the object described by class field theory. Abelianization
is also the first step of the [nilpotent](trough:Group.IsNilpotent) and solvable
hierarchies, which measure how far a group is from being abelian.

## In Mathlib

Mathlib defines the abelianization of a group as its quotient by the commutator
subgroup, where the commutator subgroup is defined as the commutator of the
whole group with itself and then shown to equal the subgroup generated by the
commutator elements, with the convention $[g, h] = g h g^{-1} h^{-1}$ (some texts
use $g^{-1} h^{-1} g h$, which generates the same subgroup). The universal
property is packaged as a bijection between homomorphisms out of $G$ and
homomorphisms out of $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, for any target group whose
multiplication is commutative. Mathlib's statement that the commutator subgroup
of $S_n$ is $A_n$ assumes at least five letters, though it proves the inclusion
of the commutator subgroup in $A_n$ for every $n$. The
free abelian group on a type is defined in Mathlib as
the abelianization of the free group, written additively.
