# Tailored compression from its stages; II:5643, value form

A question reduction at input level $7$, an answer reduction at input level $5$ and a parallel repetition at level $7$ (Definition [The three stage contracts](../part-03/def-tailored-stages.md)) give a tailored gap compression at level $7$.

## Proof

As Theorem [Gap-preserving compression from ambient stage contracts](../../structure-of-the-proof/part-09/thm-compression.md): question reduction, then answer reduction at $(\lambda, \mu, \sigma(\lambda))$, then repetition at $(\lambda, \tau)$, each soundness clause read contrapositively inside the margin the previous stage left (Lemmas [The margin claim](../../structure-of-the-proof/part-09/lem-compress-margin.md) and [A universal repetition exponent](../../structure-of-the-proof/part-09/lem-compress-tau.md)). Three things change. The size parameter $\sigma(\lambda)$ dominates the three introspective programs, the linear-constraints processor's by the size clause of question reduction and the other two because they are computed from $\lambda$ in polynomial time. The compressed answer-length calculator is repetition's on the answer-reduced sampler and calculator, which depend on $\lambda$ only. And repetition reads its input's lengths from the calculator, so $\tau$ is chosen against a bound $(\lambda n + 1)^\beta$ on the answer-reduced lengths in place of a parse length. Completeness is the three completeness clauses chained, with no answer bound to adjust.
