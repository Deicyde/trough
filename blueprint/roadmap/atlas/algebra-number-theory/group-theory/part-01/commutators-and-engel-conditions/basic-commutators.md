## Formal commutators

The formal commutators (bracket arrangements) in an alphabet $\alpha$
are the formal expressions generated by two rules: every letter $a \in \alpha$ is a formal
commutator, and for formal commutators $u$ and $v$, the bracket $[u, v]$ is a formal commutator.

## Weight of a formal commutator

The weight of a formal commutator is the number of
occurrences of letters in it: a letter has weight $1$, and $[u, v]$ has weight the weight of $u$
plus the weight of $v$.

## Commutator, Kourovka–Hall convention

For elements $x$ and $y$ of a group, their
commutator is $[x, y] = x^{-1} y^{-1} x y$ (the convention of the Kourovka Notebook and of Hall).

## Value of a formal commutator

Each formal commutator in the letters $\alpha$ has a value in
the free group $F(\alpha)$ on $\alpha$: a letter $a$ has the value of the generator $a$, and
$[u, v]$ has the value $\bar u^{-1} \bar v^{-1} \bar u \bar v$, where $\bar u$ and $\bar v$ are the
values of $u$ and $v$.

## Basic commutators

Let $<$ be a relation on the formal commutators in the letters $\alpha$
(in applications, a linear order). Every letter is basic for $<$, and a formal commutator $[u, v]$
is basic for $<$ when $u$ and $v$ are basic for $<$, $v < u$, and, in case $u = [y, z]$ is itself a
bracket, $v < z$ fails (for a linear order: $z \le v$). This is M. Hall's definition.

## Gaglione–Spellman claim on equal commutators of basic commutators

The proposition
(Kourovka Problem 11.20, A. Gaglione and D. Spellman) that the following holds. Write
$[x, y] = x^{-1} y^{-1} x y$. For every linearly ordered type $\alpha$ and every linear order $<$
on the formal commutators in the letters $\alpha$ that orders the letters as in $\alpha$ and puts
formal commutators of smaller weight first (within one weight it is arbitrary), if $a$ and $b$ are
formal commutators such that the formal commutator $[a, b]$ is basic for $<$ (so $a$ and $b$ are
basic too), $c$ and $d$ are elements of the free group $F(\alpha)$, each of the form $[x, y]$ with
$x, y \in F(\alpha)$, and $[\bar a, \bar b] = [c, d]$ in $F(\alpha)$, where $\bar a$ and $\bar b$
are the values of $a$ and $b$ in $F(\alpha)$, then $\bar a = c$ and $\bar b = d$.

## Equal commutators of basic commutators do not determine their entries

The Gaglione–Spellman
claim on equal commutators of basic commutators (Kourovka Problem 11.20) is false: writing
$[x, y] = x^{-1} y^{-1} x y$, there are a linearly ordered type $\alpha$, a linear order $<$ on the
formal commutators in the letters $\alpha$ that orders the letters as in $\alpha$ and puts formal
commutators of smaller weight first, formal commutators $a$ and $b$ such that the formal commutator
$[a, b]$ is basic for $<$, and elements $c$ and $d$ of the free group $F(\alpha)$, each of the form
$[x, y]$ with $x, y \in F(\alpha)$, such that $[\bar a, \bar b] = [c, d]$ in $F(\alpha)$, where
$\bar a$ and $\bar b$ are the values of $a$ and $b$ in $F(\alpha)$, but $\bar a \ne c$ or
$\bar b \ne d$.
