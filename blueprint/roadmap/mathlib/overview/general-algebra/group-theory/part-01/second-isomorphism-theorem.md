# Second isomorphism theorem

The **second isomorphism theorem** describes what happens when a
[subgroup](subgroup.md) $H$ of a [group](group.md) $G$ meets a
[normal subgroup](normal-subgroup.md) $N$: the product $HN$ is a subgroup,
and the [quotient](quotient-group.md) $HN/N$ is isomorphic to
$H/(H \cap N)$. It lets one compute a quotient of a large subgroup by passing to
a quotient of a smaller one, and it is a basic tool in the study of composition
series. Because $H \cap N$ lies in both $H$ and $N$, which both lie in $HN$, the
four subgroups form a diamond in the lattice of subgroups, and the result is
also called the diamond isomorphism theorem.

## Statement

Let $G$ be a group, $H$ a subgroup of $G$ and $N$ a normal subgroup of $G$. Then:

1. The set $HN = \{hn : h \in H,\ n \in N\}$ is a subgroup of $G$; in fact
   [it is the subgroup generated by $H$ and $N$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Group/Subgroup/Pointwise.html#Subgroup.mul_normal).
2. $N$ is normal in $HN$, and
   [$H \cap N$ is normal in $H$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Group/Subgroup/Basic.html#Subgroup.Normal.subgroupOf).
3. The map $h(H \cap N) \mapsto hN$ is a well-defined
   [isomorphism of groups](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/QuotientGroup/Basic.html#QuotientGroup.quotientInfEquivProdNormalQuotient)
   $$H/(H \cap N) \cong HN/N.$$

In terms of indices, [the index of $N$ in $HN$ equals the index of $H \cap N$ in $H$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#Subgroup.relIndex_sup_right):
$$[HN : N] = [H : H \cap N].$$

## Examples

- **Integers.** In the additive group $\mathbb{Z}$, take $H = a\mathbb{Z}$ and
  $N = b\mathbb{Z}$ for positive integers $a$ and $b$. Every subgroup of
  $\mathbb{Z}$ is normal, $H \cap N = \operatorname{lcm}(a,b)\mathbb{Z}$ and
  $H + N = \gcd(a,b)\mathbb{Z}$. The
  [additive form of the theorem](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/QuotientGroup/Basic.html#QuotientAddGroup.quotientInfEquivSumNormalQuotient)
  gives $a\mathbb{Z}/\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)\mathbb{Z} \cong \gcd(a,b)\mathbb{Z}/b\mathbb{Z}$.
  Comparing orders, $\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)/a = b/\gcd(a,b)$, which is the
  identity $\gcd(a,b)\operatorname{lcm}(a,b) = ab$.
- **Symmetric group.** In $S_4$ let $N$ be the Klein four-group of double
  transpositions together with the identity, which is normal, and let $H \cong S_3$
  be the stabilizer of the point $4$. No double transposition fixes a point, so
  $H \cap N$ is trivial. Then the products $hn$ are pairwise distinct, since
  $hn = h'n'$ gives $h'^{-1}h = n'n^{-1} \in H \cap N$, so
  $|HN| = 6 \cdot 4 = 24$ forces $HN = S_4$. The
  theorem yields $S_4/N \cong S_3$.
- **Trivial cases.** If $H \le N$ both sides are trivial groups. If $H \cap N$ is
  trivial, $H$ embeds isomorphically in $G/N$ as the image $HN/N$.

## Consequences

- **Product formula.** For a finite group, write $[HN : N] = |HN|/|N|$ and
  $[H : H \cap N] = |H|/|H \cap N|$, using
  [Lagrange's theorem](../part-02/lagranges-theorem.md) in its counting
  form. The index identity then becomes
  $$|HN|\,|H \cap N| = |H|\,|N|.$$
- **Divisibility of indices.** When $[G : N]$ is finite, $HN/N$ is a subgroup of
  the finite group $G/N$, so by Lagrange's theorem the index of $H \cap N$ in $H$
  divides the index of $N$ in $G$. Mathlib records that
  [the index of $N \cap H$ in $H$ divides the index of $N$ in $G$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.html#Subgroup.relIndex_dvd_index_of_normal)
  for every normal $N$, with its convention that an infinite index is $0$, which
  every integer divides.
- **Composition series.** If $N_1$ and $N_2$ are distinct maximal normal subgroups
  of $G$, then $N_1 N_2 = G$ and the theorem gives
  $G/N_1 \cong N_2/(N_1 \cap N_2)$. This step drives the usual inductive proof of
  the [Jordan–Hölder theorem](../../../../thousand-plus/algebra/20-group-theory-and-generalizations/part-02/jordan-holder-theorem.md).

## Proof

Since $N$ is normal, $hN = Nh$ for every $h \in G$, so $HN = NH$; hence
$HN$ is closed under products and inverses and is a subgroup, and $N$, being
normal in $G$, is normal in $HN$. Define $\varphi \colon H \to HN/N$ by $\varphi(h) = hN$; it is the inclusion of
$H$ in $HN$ followed by the quotient map $HN \to HN/N$, so it is a homomorphism. It is surjective: an element of $HN/N$ has the form $hnN$ with
$h \in H$ and $n \in N$, and $hnN = hN = \varphi(h)$. Its kernel consists of the
$h \in H$ with $hN = N$, that is, $h \in N$; so the kernel is $H \cap N$, which is
therefore normal in $H$. The [first isomorphism theorem](first-isomorphism-theorem.md), in the form
[a surjective homomorphism induces an isomorphism from the quotient by its kernel onto its target](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/QuotientGroup/Basic.html#QuotientGroup.quotientKerEquivOfSurjective), now gives
$H/(H \cap N) \cong HN/N$. Taking cardinalities of the two quotients gives the
index identity.

## Generalizations

The argument uses only that each element of $H$ conjugates $N$ into itself, so
normality of $N$ in all of $G$ is more than needed. It suffices that $H$ lies in
the normalizer of $N$, and Mathlib states
[the theorem under exactly that hypothesis](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/QuotientGroup/Basic.html#QuotientGroup.quotientInfEquivProdNormalizerQuotient);
the normal case follows because
[every subgroup lies in the normalizer of a normal subgroup](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Group/Subgroup/Basic.html#Subgroup.le_normalizer_of_normal).

Analogous statements hold for rings and ideals, for modules, and more generally
for groups with operators. For
submodules $P$ and $Q$ of a [module](../../../linear-algebra/fundamentals/module.md) over a ring,
[$P/(P \cap Q)$ is isomorphic to $(P + Q)/Q$ as modules](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Isomorphisms.html#LinearMap.quotientInfEquivSupQuotient);
here every submodule is a valid kernel, so no normality condition appears. The
other isomorphism theorems are the
[first](first-isomorphism-theorem.md) and the
[third isomorphism theorem](third-isomorphism-theorem.md).

## In Mathlib

Mathlib works inside the lattice of subgroups of $G$. The product $HN$ appears as
the join of $H$ and $N$, the smallest subgroup containing both, and $H \cap N$
appears as $N$ pulled back to a subgroup of $H$; likewise $N$ is viewed as a
subgroup of the join. The theorem is a bundled isomorphism of groups, built by
choice from the first isomorphism theorem rather than by an explicit formula for
its inverse. The additive version, for additive groups such as $\mathbb{Z}$, is
generated automatically from the multiplicative one; the automatic translation
also turns the word Prod in the name into Sum, so the additive name differs from
the multiplicative one by more than its namespace. The index identity uses
Mathlib's relative index, which is $0$ when the index is infinite, so it holds for
every group, finite or not.
