> ### Dirac spinors
>
> A Dirac fermion is a pair consisting of a left-handed Weyl fermion and a dual right-handed Weyl fermion \{1}. [\[1\]](basic.md) Dirac fermions form a complex vector space, with addition and scalar multiplication acting on the two Weyl components. [\[2\]](basic.md) Decomposing a Dirac fermion into its Weyl components is a complex linear equivalence. [\[3\]](basic.md) The space has a four-element chiral basis, whose elements are the basis elements of the two Weyl components. [\[4\]](basic.md) SL(2, ℂ), the double cover of the Lorentz group \{2}, acts on Dirac fermions componentwise on the two Weyl factors. [\[5\]](basic.md) This representation is equivalent to the product of the representations on the Weyl components. [\[6\]](basic.md) The two-component treatment of Dirac fermions follows Dreiner, Haber and Martin \{3}.
>
> ### Gamma endomorphisms and chirality
>
> Clifford multiplication is a real linear map from Lorentz vectors to complex linear endomorphisms of the Dirac fermions, written in the chiral basis with the library's Pauli-matrix conventions; on the Lorentz basis it gives the upper-index gamma components γ^μ. [\[12\]](gammamatrices.md) For any two Lorentz vectors v and w, the anticommutator of their Clifford products is 2⟨v, w⟩ times the identity, where ⟨v, w⟩ is the Minkowski product; the component relations \{γ^μ, γ^ν} = 2η^\{μν}, the squares of the γ^μ and the anticommutation of distinct γ^μ follow, in the signature (+,−,−,−). [\[13\]](gammamatrices.md)
>
> The chirality endomorphism γ⁵ has chiral matrix diag(−1,−1,1,1), squares to the identity, and anticommutes with every gamma endomorphism. [\[14\]](gammamatrices.md) The left and right chiral projectors (1 − γ⁵)/2 and (1 + γ⁵)/2 have the corresponding diagonal matrices, are idempotent and mutually orthogonal, and sum to the identity. [\[15\]](gammamatrices.md) …

> ### The Dirac representation
>
> An algebra equivalence converts endomorphisms of the Dirac fermions into 4 × 4 matrices in the Dirac representation, with an explicit change-of-coordinates formula from the chiral basis. [\[16\]](basic.md) Under it the gamma endomorphisms become the gamma matrices γ⁰, γ¹, γ², γ³ of the Dirac representation, alongside the chirality matrix γ⁵ and the set the gamma matrices form. [\[7\]](../../cliffordalgebra.md) These matrices satisfy the Clifford anticommutator against the Minkowski metric in the signature (+,−,−,−). [\[8\]](../../cliffordalgebra.md)
>
> The Dirac slash k̸ of a Lorentz vector k is its Clifford multiplication in the Dirac representation, contracting the lowered coordinates of k with the gamma matrices. [\[9\]](slash.md) It is real linear in k, and extends to ordered products of slash factors. [\[9\]](slash.md)
>
> The Dirac algebra is the real subalgebra generated by the Dirac representations of the gamma endomorphisms, and it contains each generator. [\[10\]](../../cliffordalgebra.md) The Minkowski quadratic form induces an algebra map from its Clifford algebra onto the Dirac algebra, and this map is surjective. [\[11\]](../../cliffordalgebra.md) The sixteen products of the gamma matrices with increasing indices lie in the Dirac algebra and are linearly independent over ℝ \{3}. [\[17\]](../../cliffordalgebra.md) The Clifford algebra of the Minkowski quadratic form has dimension 16 over ℝ. [\[18\]](../../cliffordalgebra.md) The Dirac algebra has dimension 16 as well. [\[19\]](../../cliffordalgebra.md) The map from the Clifford algebra is therefore injective, hence an isomorphism of algebras. [\[20\]](../../cliffordalgebra.md)
