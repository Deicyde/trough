# The mass-weight grading of the fermion sector

The module `PhyslibAlpha.Particles.StandardModel.IsFermionSector.MassWeight.Basic`, [PhyslibAlpha/Particles/StandardModel/IsFermionSector/MassWeight/Basic.lean](https://github.com/leanprover-community/physlib/blob/2a1dfe43134b49b643642f13d33031754021bb5d/PhyslibAlpha/Particles/StandardModel/IsFermionSector/MassWeight/Basic.lean): 20 declarations, 16 with a docstring.

## i. Overview

> The elements of the fermion algebra of a given mass weight form a submodule. Mass weight is
> twice the mass dimension, so that it is a natural number: a fermion tower `∇ⁿψ` with `n`
> covariant derivatives has mass dimension `3/2 + n` and mass weight `3 + 2 * n`, and a term
> of mass dimension four, as in the Lagrangian, has mass weight eight.
>
> The fermion towers generate the fermion algebra, and `massWeightPoly` is a monomial on
> each of them. The results of `PhyslibAlpha.Mathematics.HomogeneousGenerators` then describe
> every mass-weight submodule. The submodules of weight at most eight are found by removing
> the leftmost tower of each product, and weight eight collapses, by the commutation of the
> derivative submodules, to the kinetic sector `derivSubmodule 0 * derivSubmodule 1`.

## ii. Key results

> - `massWeightSubmodule_eq_iSup_mul` : removing the leftmost fermion tower.
> - `massWeightSubmodule_eq` : the binary weight recursion.
> - `massWeightSubmodule_eq_bot` : the weights `1`, `2` and `4` are empty.
> - `massWeightSubmodule_three_eq` to `massWeightSubmodule_eight_eq` : the mass weights up to eight.

## C. The weight decompositions

> Every result here is a spanning statement: a submodule is a join of products of fermion
> towers, in the order written. It says nothing about the products being nonzero or
> independent.

**`StandardModel.IsFermionSector.massWeightSubmodule_eq`**, a lemma, [line 167](https://github.com/leanprover-community/physlib/blob/2a1dfe43134b49b643642f13d33031754021bb5d/PhyslibAlpha/Particles/StandardModel/IsFermionSector/MassWeight/Basic.lean#L167).

> The weight recursion: an element of positive mass weight `i` is a sum of single
> fermion towers of weight `i` and of products of two elements of lower positive weights
> summing to `i`.

```lean
lemma massWeightSubmodule_eq (i : ℕ) (hi : 0 < i) :
    h.massWeightSubmodule i
      = (⨆ k ∈ Finset.univ.filter (fun k : Fin i => 3 + 2 * (k : ℕ) = i),
          h.derivSubmodule (k : ℕ))
        ⊔ (⨆ p ∈ Finset.univ.filter (fun p : Fin i × Fin i => (p.1 : ℕ) + (p.2 : ℕ) = i),
            h.massWeightSubmodule (p.1 : ℕ) * h.massWeightSubmodule (p.2 : ℕ))
```

**`StandardModel.IsFermionSector.massWeightSubmodule_eq_iSup_mul`**, a lemma, [line 180](https://github.com/leanprover-community/physlib/blob/2a1dfe43134b49b643642f13d33031754021bb5d/PhyslibAlpha/Particles/StandardModel/IsFermionSector/MassWeight/Basic.lean#L180).

> Removing the leftmost fermion tower: an element of positive weight `w` is a sum of
> products of a tower `∇ⁿψ` of weight `3 + 2 * n ≤ w` with an element of the remaining
> weight.

```lean
lemma massWeightSubmodule_eq_iSup_mul (w : ℕ) (hw : 0 < w) :
    h.massWeightSubmodule w
      = ⨆ n ∈ (Finset.range (w + 1)).filter (fun n => 3 + 2 * n ≤ w),
          h.derivSubmodule n * h.massWeightSubmodule (w - (3 + 2 * n))
```

**`StandardModel.IsFermionSector.massWeightSubmodule_eq_bot`**, a lemma, [line 190](https://github.com/leanprover-community/physlib/blob/2a1dfe43134b49b643642f13d33031754021bb5d/PhyslibAlpha/Particles/StandardModel/IsFermionSector/MassWeight/Basic.lean#L190).

> The weights `1`, `2` and `4` are empty: the tower weights `3, 5, 7, …` and their sums
