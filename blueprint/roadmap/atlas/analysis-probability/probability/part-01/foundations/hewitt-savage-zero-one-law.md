## Hewitt–Savage zero–one law

Let $\mu$ be a probability measure on a measurable space $S$,
and let $E \subseteq S^{\mathbb{N}}$ be a set that is measurable for the product σ-algebra and
invariant under every finitely supported permutation $\sigma$ of $\mathbb{N}$, that is,
$\{\omega : \omega \circ \sigma \in E\} = E$ whenever $\sigma$ moves only finitely many points.
Then $E$ has measure $0$ or $1$ under the product measure $\mu^{\otimes \mathbb{N}}$.

No topological assumption on $S$ is needed.

## Hewitt–Savage zero–one law on a Polish space

Let $S$ be a Polish space with its Borel
σ-algebra, let $\mu$ be a probability measure on $S$, and let $E \subseteq S^{\mathbb{N}}$ be a set
that is measurable for the product σ-algebra and invariant under every finitely supported
permutation $\sigma$ of $\mathbb{N}$, that is, $\{\omega : \omega \circ \sigma \in E\} = E$
whenever $\sigma$ moves only finitely many points. Then $E$ has measure $0$ or $1$ under the
product measure $\mu^{\otimes \mathbb{N}}$.

The Polish and Borel assumptions are not used in the proof: the same conclusion holds for a
probability measure on any measurable space.

## Exchangeable event

Let $S$ be a measurable space. A set $A \subseteq S^{\mathbb{N}}$ is an
exchangeable (or permutable) event if it is measurable for the product σ-algebra and
$\{x : x \circ \sigma \in A\} = A$ for every permutation $\sigma$ of $\mathbb{N}$ that moves only
finitely many indices.

## Event exchangeable with respect to a sequence

Let $\Omega$ and $S$ be measurable spaces
and let $X_0, X_1, \ldots : \Omega \to S$ be a sequence of maps. An event $E \subseteq \Omega$ is
exchangeable with respect to $(X_n)$ if there is an exchangeable event $A \subseteq S^{\mathbb{N}}$
such that $E = \{\omega \in \Omega : (X_n(\omega))_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \in A\}$.

Equivalently, $E$ belongs to the σ-field of exchangeable events generated by $(X_n)$.

## Exchangeable events of a measurable sequence are measurable

Let $\Omega$ and $S$ be
measurable spaces and let $X_0, X_1, \ldots : \Omega \to S$ be measurable maps. If
$E \subseteq \Omega$ is exchangeable with respect to $(X_n)$, then $E$ is measurable.

## Hewitt–Savage zero–one law for i.i.d. sequences

Let $\mu$ be a probability measure on
a measurable space $\Omega$, let $S$ be a measurable space, and let
$X_0, X_1, X_2, \ldots : \Omega \to S$ be measurable maps that are independent under $\mu$ and
each have the same distribution under $\mu$ as $X_0$. If an event $E \subseteq \Omega$ is
exchangeable with respect to $(X_n)$, then $\mu(E) = 0$ or $\mu(E) = 1$.
