# Subfield

A **subfield** of a [field](field.md) $K$ is a subset of $K$ that contains $0$ and $1$ and is closed under addition, negation, multiplication and taking inverses of nonzero elements, so that it is itself a field under the operations of $K$. Subfields are central to field theory: a field extension $L/K$ can be viewed as a field $L$ together with a subfield isomorphic to $K$, and for a finite Galois extension the [Galois correspondence](galois-correspondence.md) describes the fields lying in between by means of subgroups of the Galois group.

## Definition

Let $K$ be a field. A subset $F \subseteq K$ is a [subfield](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Field/Subfield/Defs.html#Subfield) if

- $F$ is a [subring](../rings/subring.md) of $K$: it contains $0$ and $1$ and is closed under $+$, $-$ and $\times$;
- $F$ is closed under inverses: if $x \in F$ and $x \neq 0$, then $x^{-1} \in F$.

Thus [a subring that contains the inverse of each of its nonzero elements determines a subfield with the same elements](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Field/Subfield/Defs.html#Subring.toSubfield). With the addition and multiplication of $K$ restricted to it, [a subfield is a field in its own right](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Field/Subfield/Defs.html#Subfield.toField), and the inclusion $F \hookrightarrow K$ is a [ring homomorphism](../rings/ring-morphism.md). If $F$ is a subfield of $K$, one also says that $K$ is an extension field of $F$.

The same definition makes sense in a division ring (a ring with $1 \neq 0$ in which every nonzero element is invertible, but multiplication need not commute), and there [a subfield is again a division ring](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Field/Subfield/Defs.html#Subfield.toDivisionRing). Such a subset is usually called a sub-division ring; Mathlib still calls it a subfield.

## Examples

- The [rational numbers](../numbers/rational-number.md) form a subfield of the [real numbers](../numbers/real-number.md), which form a subfield of the [complex numbers](../numbers/complex-number.md).
- The set $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 2) = \{a + b\sqrt 2 : a, b \in \mathbb{Q}\}$ is a subfield of $\mathbb{R}$. It is a subring, since $(a + b\sqrt 2)(c + d\sqrt 2) = (ac + 2bd) + (ad + bc)\sqrt 2$; if $a + b\sqrt 2 \neq 0$ then $a^2 - 2b^2 \neq 0$, because $\sqrt 2$ is irrational, and

$$(a + b\sqrt 2)^{-1} = \frac{a}{a^2 - 2b^2} - \frac{b}{a^2 - 2b^2}\sqrt 2.$$

- The integers form a subring of $\mathbb{Q}$ but not a subfield, since $2^{-1} \notin \mathbb{Z}$.
- If $f, g \colon K \to L$ are ring homomorphisms from a field $K$, then [the set of $x \in K$ with $f(x) = g(x)$ is a subfield of $K$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Field/Subfield/Basic.html#RingHom.eqLocusField). Applied to complex conjugation and the identity of $\mathbb{C}$, this shows that $\mathbb{R}$, the set of complex numbers fixed by conjugation, is a subfield of $\mathbb{C}$.
- For a ring homomorphism $f \colon K \to L$ between fields, [the image of $f$ is a subfield of $L$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Field/Subfield/Basic.html#RingHom.fieldRange); more generally, [the image under $f$ of any subfield of $K$ is a subfield of $L$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Field/Subfield/Basic.html#Subfield.map), and [the preimage under $f$ of any subfield of $L$ is a subfield of $K$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Field/Subfield/Basic.html#Subfield.comap).

## Properties

**Intersections.** The intersection of any family of subfields of $K$ is a subfield: [the infimum of a set of subfields has as underlying set the intersection of their underlying sets](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Field/Subfield/Basic.html#Subfield.coe_sInf). A union of two subfields is usually not a subfield, but [the supremum of a nonempty directed family of subfields is exactly their union](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Field/Subfield/Basic.html#Subfield.mem_iSup_of_directed).

**Generated subfields.** For a subset $S \subseteq K$, the [subfield generated by $S$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Field/Subfield/Basic.html#Subfield.closure) is the intersection of all subfields containing $S$. It is the smallest subfield containing $S$: [it is contained in a subfield $F$ exactly when $S \subseteq F$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Field/Subfield/Basic.html#Subfield.closure_le). Concretely, [it consists of the quotients $a b^{-1}$ of elements $a, b$ of the subring generated by $S$, with $b \neq 0$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Field/Subfield/Basic.html#Subfield.mem_closure_iff). One consequence is that [two ring homomorphisms out of $K$ that agree on a set $S$ generating $K$ as a field are equal](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Field/Subfield/Basic.html#RingHom.eq_of_eqOn_of_field_closure_eq_top).

**The prime subfield.** The intersection of all subfields of $K$ is the smallest subfield of $K$, called its prime subfield. Since every subfield contains $1$, it contains every integer multiple of $1$ and every quotient of two such multiples with nonzero denominator; in a field of characteristic zero this says that [every subfield contains the image of every rational number](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Field/Subfield/Defs.html#SubfieldClass.ratCast_mem). The prime subfield is determined by the [characteristic](characteristic-of-a-ring.md) of $K$:

- if $K$ has [characteristic zero](characteristic-zero.md), [the prime subfield is the image of the canonical map $\mathbb{Q} \to K$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/FieldTheory/PrimeField.html#Subfield.bot_eq_of_charZero), a copy of $\mathbb{Q}$;
- if $K$ has [characteristic $p$](characteristic-p.md) for a prime $p$, [the prime subfield has exactly $p$ elements](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/FieldTheory/Finite/Basic.html#Subfield.card_bot), and it is a copy of the field $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$.

In particular $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ have no subfields other than themselves.

## Related concepts

A subfield of $K$ is the field analogue of a [subgroup](../group-theory/part-01/subgroup.md) of a group or a [subring](../rings/subring.md) of a ring. When a field $L$ is an extension of a field $K$, the subfields of $L$ that contain the image of $K$ are called [intermediate fields](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/FieldTheory/IntermediateField/Basic.html#IntermediateField); they are the objects of the Galois correspondence, and they appear throughout the theory of [algebraic extensions](algebraic-extension.md) and [splitting fields](splitting-field.md). The [finite fields](../../../undergraduate/ring-theory/field-theory/finite-fields.md) give a clean picture: a field with $p^n$ elements has exactly one subfield with $p^m$ elements for each divisor $m$ of $n$, and no others.

## In Mathlib

Mathlib defines subfields for any division ring, not only for fields, so its notion covers sub-division rings of noncommutative division rings such as the quaternions. A subfield is a bundled structure: a subring together with the condition that it is closed under inversion. Because Mathlib sets the inverse of $0$ to be $0$, this condition is stated for every element, with no exception for zero, and quotients by zero are likewise allowed in the description of generated subfields. The subfields of a division ring are ordered by inclusion and form a complete lattice, whose least element is the prime subfield and whose greatest element is the whole ring. Intermediate fields of an extension are a separate structure, each of which can be viewed as a subfield of the larger field.
