# Sphere topology

> Reusable Lean topology and singular homology: fixed points of complex-valued continuous maps on closed balls of nonnegative radius whose sphere-boundary images lie in the ball, with no interior image bound; degree-zero boundary restrictions and non-retraction for the complex closed unit disk; arbitrary-index circle bouquets, inclusions, positive-generator homomorphisms, retractions and generator nonidentity, and a positive-generator-preserving free-group identification with the universal property for group targets in independent universes; jointly injective factor retractions and factorwise T0/T1/T2 classification for arbitrary chosen-point wide pushouts, including empty families; normalization of nowhere-zero continuous fields, identity-to-antipodal homotopies from orthogonal fields, integral subdivision, cover-small chains and two-open Mayer-Vietoris, nonnegative reduced integral homology and degree-zero criteria for path connectedness. Finite-fiber coverings give transfers on all-simplex singular chains and ordinary homology with categorical coefficient objects. Signed-period circle fundamental-group coordinates, generic based lifting, monodromy, maps of covers and deck-group comparisons are supplied by Fundamental Groupoids, their canonical home, and re-exported through deprecated Sphere circle and covering module paths. H-space loop multiplication, circle degree, free homotopy and disk results remain in Sphere; private coordinate and circle H-space checks are in SphereTopologyTest.CircleHSpace. Explicit circle and two-sphere calculations give the antipodal action on H2 of the standard two-sphere and its continuous orthogonal-field hairy-ball theorem. The metric two-sphere lifted to any universe has a non-nullhomotopic identity, distinct identity and north-constant based homotopy classes, and a nontrivial second homotopy group.

## Scope

A complex-valued continuous map on a closed ball of nonnegative radius has a fixed point if its sphere-boundary images lie in that ball. Centers are arbitrary, no interior image bound or extension to the plane is assumed, and radius zero is included. Continuous self-maps specialize this result, giving the complex-disk case of Brouwer's theorem without contraction, smoothness or boundary-preservation assumptions. Every continuous map from the complex closed unit disk to Circle restricts along the coordinate-preserving boundary inclusion to a map of degree zero, hence not the identity, with no basedness hypothesis. No arbitrary-dimensional disk theorem or converse extension criterion is asserted. Arbitrary-index circle bouquets with inclusions, descent, positive-generator homomorphisms, circle retractions and generator nonidentity. The generator map is bijective and gives a multiplicative equivalence with the free group on the circle labels, including an empty family. Arbitrary generator values extend uniquely to homomorphisms into groups in independent universes; distinct positive generators do not commute. No geometric word-extraction algorithm or general wedge computation from ordinary contractibility is asserted. Retractions onto chosen-point factors jointly distinguish points without separation or finiteness assumptions, including an empty index family. The same wide pushout is T0, T1 or T2 exactly when each factor is, with canonical inheritance instances and reflection through factor embeddings. Continuous-field topology, integral subdivision and cover-small chains, ordinary two-open Mayer-Vietoris, nonnegative reduced integral homology, explicit circle and two-sphere calculations, and the unit two-sphere continuous orthogonal-field zero theorem. For the unit two-sphere in EuclideanSpace real (Fin 3), lifted to any universe as Metric.Sphere.basedSpace 1, the identity is not homotopic to any constant map. Its identity and last-axis north-constant classes differ in the based-homotopy category, and its second homotopy group at that north pole is nontrivial. No computation of the second homotopy group is asserted. The reduced degree-zero Mayer-Vietoris sequence is exact at the biproduct for every two-open cover; its final map is epic when the intersection is nonempty. Total degree-zero augmentation is invertible exactly when the space is path connected, for a nonzero coefficient object in a preadditive category with the requisite small coproducts and homology; nonemptiness of the space is not assumed. For nonempty spaces, integral reduced H0 vanishes exactly for path connectedness. The covering transfer takes a coefficient object in a preadditive category with coproducts indexed in the space universe; ordinary homology adds only CategoryWithHomology. Its lift-sum and fixed-sheet formulas concern transfer followed by pushforward; finite point fibers and their uniform cardinality are separate hypotheses. Nonempty, surjective and connected coverings are not required. Circle fundamental-group coordinates and generic covering foundations are supplied by Fundamental Groupoids rather than implemented twice; the based equal-image comparison is not a general covering-space classification. For every n >= 0, finite generalized-loop spaces are homeomorphic to the subtype of continuous unit n-sphere maps taking the specified last-axis north pole to the chosen target point. Mapping spaces use the ordinary compact-open topology and the subtypes use the induced topology, not May's compactly generated mapping-space convention. Inverse representatives precompose with the cube collapse. The natural set equivalence with the existing path-component set preserves constants and based postcomposition in independent target universes; equality of components is exactly homotopy relative to the singleton north pole. For every n >= 0 and arbitrary X : BasedTopCat in any universe u, HomotopyGroup.equivSphereHom is an ordinary set equivalence from HomotopyGroup (Fin n) X.right (BasedTopCat.point X) to morphisms in the based-homotopy category from the north-based ULift of the unit n-sphere in EuclideanSpace real (Fin (n + 1)) to X. The lift places the sphere in the target universe; X can be any under-category object, not only an object presented by BasedTopCat.of. Forward representatives precompose GenLoop.sphereHomeomorph n with ULift.down, and inverse representatives pull back a based map along ULift.up after Cube.collapseToSphere n. Here n = 0 means S0 and n = 1 means S1. This set comparison alone specifies no multiplication on the hom-set or group in dimension zero; the separate positive-dimensional group comparison below uses a multiplicative opposite. No compactly generated mapping-space comparison is asserted. For positive d = n + 1, the sphere pinch to the existing two-factor point-wide-pushout has the prescribed left-then-right cube pullback, compatible closed-half formulas and distinct non-head quarter-point values. Folding based maps F and G corresponds to J(G) * J(F) in the existing homotopy group, where J is the inverse sphere-class comparison, including in dimension one. Both factor retractions composed with the pinch are homotopic to the identity relative to the north pole. In sphere dimensions d = n + 2 >= 2, interchanging two arbitrary based factor maps after the pinch gives a homotopy relative to that north pole. In particular, the original categorical factor swap after the pinch is homotopic to the pinch relative to the north pole. The lifted original sphere of every positive dimension n + 1 is also a stock group object in the ordinary opposite based-homotopy category. Multiplication, unit and inversion are opposite to the quotient classes of, respectively, pinch with ordered wedge comparisons, collapse followed by the empty-wedge head, and first-coordinate reflection. The named group-object data gives the stock group on outgoing based homotopy classes into an arbitrary based target. The cogroup construction alone makes no identification with an existing homotopy group; the separate comparison below uses a multiplicative opposite. No dimension-one cocommutativity, zero-sphere pinch or cogroup, or choice of sphere orientations is asserted. For every n >= 0 and arbitrary X : BasedTopCat in any universe u, HomotopyGroup.mulOppositeEquivSphereHom is a multiplicative equivalence from the multiplicative opposite of HomotopyGroup (Fin (n + 1)) X.right (BasedTopCat.point X) to the canonical group-valued Yoneda evaluation of Metric.Sphere.cogroup n at op (op (Q.obj X)), where Q is BasedTopCat.homotopyQuotient. Its elements are incoming morphisms to the cogroup carrier in the ordinary opposite based-homotopy category; their unops are outgoing classes from the lifted north-based (n + 1)-sphere to X. These categorical opposites differ from the source multiplicative opposite. Writing E for HomotopyGroup.equivSphereHom (n + 1) X, the forward characteristic sends MulOpposite.op a to (E a).op, and the inverse sends f to MulOpposite.op (E.symm f.unop), without inversion or a change of representative. Target naturality identifies based postcomposition by h : X to Y with the Yoneda action on (Q.map h).op.op. The fold order J(G) * J(F) makes the source opposite essential even for n = 0, which is S1. No direct group comparison by the same class map without that opposite, S1 commutativity or group on S0 is asserted. For every n >= 0 in any universe, Metric.Sphere.cogroup_isCommMonObj_iff characterizes the stock IsCommMonObj property of cogroupCarrier n by 0 < n, exactly sphere dimension n + 1 >= 2. Its specified monoid-object data and original ordered-pinch multiplication are unchanged. Metric.Sphere.cogroup_isCommMonObj gives the positive case in sphere dimension n + 2 >= 2 using the sphere-map group comparison and Mathlib's Eckmann-Hilton theorem for all based targets. Metric.Sphere.cogroup_not_isCommMonObj_zero excludes the original circle S1 using Sphere's noncommuting positive generators of the two-circle bouquet and sphere-map group comparison, Mathlib's first-homotopy-group equivalence and its stock surjective-map and multiplicative-opposite commutativity lemmas. These are named properties, not global commutativity instances; no group on S0 is asserted. Targets need only a topology and a chosen point; the finite source sphere is locally compact Hausdorff. In dimension zero, the separate compactification point at infinity maps to north, the cube point collapses to south, and the south-pole value of a based sphere map is arbitrary.
