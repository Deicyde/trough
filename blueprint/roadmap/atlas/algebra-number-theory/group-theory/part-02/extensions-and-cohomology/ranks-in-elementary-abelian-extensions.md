## Elementary abelian subgroups

A subgroup $H$ of a group $G$ is elementary abelian if any
two of its elements commute and there is a prime $p$ with $x^p = 1$ for all $x \in H$.

## Zenkov's rank bound in elementary abelian extensions (Kourovka 21.150)

Call a subgroup
elementary abelian if it is abelian and there is a prime $p$ with $x^p = 1$ for all its elements
$x$. This is the proposition that for every finite group $G$, all elementary abelian subgroups $A$
and $B$ of $G$ (possibly for different primes) with $A$ normal, $A \cap B = 1$ and
$\langle A, B \rangle = G$ (so $B$ is a complement to $A$ and $B \cong G/A$), and every $a \in A$
whose centralizer $C_B(a)$ in $B$ is trivial, the following holds for $H = \langle a, B \rangle$,
with centre $Z(H)$ and derived subgroup $H' = [H, H]$: whenever $B$ is generated by a finite set of
$k$ elements, $Z(H) \cap H'$ (with $Z(H)$ viewed as a subgroup of $G$) is generated by a finite set
of at most $k$ elements. That is, the rank of $Z(H) \cap H'$ is at most the rank of $B$.

The question is due to V. I. Zenkov.

## Zenkov's rank bound fails (Kourovka 21.150)

Call a subgroup elementary abelian if it is
abelian and there is a prime $p$ with $x^p = 1$ for all its elements $x$. There are a finite group
$G$, elementary abelian subgroups $A$ and $B$ of $G$ with $A$ normal, $A \cap B = 1$ and
$\langle A, B \rangle = G$, and an element $a \in A$ whose centralizer $C_B(a)$ in $B$ is trivial,
such that, for $H = \langle a, B \rangle$, with centre $Z(H)$ and derived subgroup $H' = [H, H]$,
some finite generating set of $B$ has fewer elements than every finite generating set of
$Z(H) \cap H'$ (here $Z(H)$ is viewed as a subgroup of $G$).

Counterexample (Monticone; van Doorn, Judin, Monticone and Morrison, Theorem 9.1):
$A = \mathbb{F}_3[x, y]/(x, y)^3$, $B = \langle 1 + x, 1 + y \rangle$ acting by multiplication,
$G = A \rtimes B$ and $a = 1$; then $C_B(a) = 1$, while
$Z(H) \cap H' = \langle x^2, xy, y^2 \rangle$ has rank $3 > 2 = \operatorname{rank} B$. The 21st
issue of the Kourovka Notebook marks 21.150 solved ("No, not always").
