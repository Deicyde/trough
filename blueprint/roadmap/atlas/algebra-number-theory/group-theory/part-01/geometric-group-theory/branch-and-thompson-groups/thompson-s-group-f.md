## Relators of Thompson's group $F$

In the free group on two generators $x_0$ and $x_1$,
this is the set of the two elements $[x_0 x_1^{-1}, x_0^{-1} x_1 x_0]$ and
$[x_0 x_1^{-1}, x_0^{-2} x_1 x_0^2]$, where $[a, b] = a b a^{-1} b^{-1}$. They are the relators
of the standard presentation of Thompson's group $F$.

## Thompson's group $F$ by its standard presentation

Thompson's group $F$ is the group with
generators $x_0, x_1$ and relations $[x_0 x_1^{-1}, x_0^{-1} x_1 x_0] = 1$ and
$[x_0 x_1^{-1}, x_0^{-2} x_1 x_0^2] = 1$, where $[a, b] = a b a^{-1} b^{-1}$; that is, the
quotient of the free group on $x_0, x_1$ by the normal subgroup generated by these two
relators.

## Howson property for Thompson's group $F$

The statement that Thompson's group $F$ has the
Howson property: for all finitely generated subgroups $H$ and $K$ of $F$, the intersection
$H \cap K$ is finitely generated.

This is Kourovka Notebook Problem 19.40 (I. Kapovich).

## Thompson's group $F$ does not have the Howson property

It is not the case that the
intersection of any two finitely generated subgroups of Thompson's group $F$ is finitely
generated: there are finitely generated subgroups $H$ and $K$ of $F$ such that $H \cap K$ is not
finitely generated.

Resolved false: the Kourovka Archive answers 19.40 "No": $\mathbb{Z} \wr \mathbb{Z}$ fails the
Howson property (Kirkinskiĭ) and embeds in $F$ (Cleary), and the property is inherited by
subgroups.
