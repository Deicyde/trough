# The Eisenbud-Green-Harris conjecture

> Let $S = k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ with $x_1 > x_2 > \dots > x_n$ and let $I \subseteq S$ be a
> homogeneous ideal. The *Hilbert function* of $I$ is $d \mapsto \dim_k I_d$.
>
> A *monomial ideal* is an ideal generated by monomials. A monomial ideal $L$ is *lex* if for any
> two monomials $u, v$ of the same degree with $u >_{\mathrm{lex}} v$ we have $u \in L$ whenever
> $v \in L$. Macaulay's theorem says that every homogeneous ideal has the same Hilbert function as
> a unique lex ideal.
>
> **Eisenbud-Green-Harris conjecture.** If $I$ contains a regular sequence of forms of degrees
> $d_1 \le \dots \le d_c$, then there is a lex ideal $L$ such that $I$ has the same Hilbert
> function as $L + (x_1^{d_1}, \dots, x_c^{d_c})$.
>
> Eisenbud, Green and Harris proposed the conjecture in [EGH93] and [EGH96] to generalise a
> theorem of Castelnuovo and Cayley-Bacharach type theorems for zero-dimensional subschemes of
> $\mathbb P^m$. It refines Macaulay's theorem, which is the case $c = 0$.
>
> The conjecture is open in general. It holds when the regular sequence is monomial, by the
> Clements-Lindström theorem [CL69], and when the degrees grow quickly, namely when
> $d_i \ge \sum_{j < i} (d_j - 1)$ for all $i \ge 3$: this is due to Caviglia and De Stefani
> [CDS20], improving on Caviglia and Maclagan [CM08], who assumed the strict inequality. Other
> known cases are $c \le 2$, and $d_1 = \dots = d_c = 2$ with $c \le 5$ by Güntürkün and
> Hochster; [CDS20] lists these with references.
>
> The Lex-plus-powers conjecture, attributed to Charalambous and Evans, predicts that the ideal
> $L + (x_1^{d_1}, \dots, x_c^{d_c})$ above also has the largest graded Betti numbers among all
> ideals with that Hilbert function containing a regular sequence in those degrees. The
> Eisenbud-Green-Harris conjecture is its $i = 0$ case [CS18]. It is not formalised here.
>
> The Hilbert function `MvPolynomial.hilbertFunction`, lex ideals `MvPolynomial.IsLexIdeal` and the
> ideal of pure powers `MvPolynomial.purePowersIdeal` are defined in
> `FormalConjecturesForMathlib.RingTheory.MvPolynomial.LexPlusPowers`.
>

>   Algébrique d'Orsay, Astérisque 218 (1993), 187-202.
> - [EGH96] D. Eisenbud, M. Green, J. Harris, *Cayley-Bacharach theorems and conjectures*, Bull.
>   Amer. Math. Soc. 33 (1996), 295-324.

>   [arXiv:2007.15467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.15467), Definition 2.1, Conjecture 2.3 and

## `EisenbudGreenHarris.eisenbud_green_harris`

Open: stated without proof.

> **The Eisenbud-Green-Harris conjecture.** Let $I \subseteq k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ be a homogeneous
> ideal containing a regular sequence of forms of degrees $d_1 \le \dots \le d_c$. Then there is a
> lex ideal $L$ such that $I$ has the same Hilbert function as $L + (x_1^{d_1}, \dots, x_c^{d_c})$.

```lean

    (I : Ideal (MvPolynomial (Fin n) k))
```

## `EisenbudGreenHarris.eisenbud_green_harris.variants.macaulay`

Solved: stated here without proof.

> **Macaulay's theorem**: every homogeneous ideal has the same Hilbert function as a lex ideal.
> This is the case $c = 0$ of `eisenbud_green_harris`, where the ideal of pure powers is zero
> (`MvPolynomial.purePowersIdeal_zero`).

```lean
theorem eisenbud_green_harris.variants.macaulay (I : Ideal (MvPolynomial (Fin n) k))
    (hI : I.IsHomogeneous (homogeneousSubmodule (Fin n) k)) :
    ∃ L : Ideal (MvPolynomial (Fin n) k), IsLexIdeal L ∧
      ∀ e, hilbertFunction I e = hilbertFunction L e
```

## `EisenbudGreenHarris.eisenbud_green_harris.variants.caviglia_deStefani`

Solved: stated here without proof.

> The conjecture holds when the degrees grow quickly enough, namely when
> $d_i \ge \sum_{j < i} (d_j - 1)$ for all $i \ge 3$. This is [CDS20, Theorem A]; Caviglia and
> Maclagan [CM08] had proved it under the strict inequality. Indices here are `0`-based, so
> $i \ge 3$ becomes `2 ≤ i`. The subtraction is on `ℕ`, which is harmless because a regular
> sequence of degrees `d` forces `1 ≤ d j`.

```lean

    (hd : Monotone d) (hgrow : ∀ i : Fin c, 2 ≤ (i : ℕ) → ∑ j ∈ Finset.Iio i, (d j - 1) ≤ d i)
```
