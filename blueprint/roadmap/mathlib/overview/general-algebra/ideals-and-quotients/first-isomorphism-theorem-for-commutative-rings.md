# First isomorphism theorem for commutative rings

The **first isomorphism theorem for commutative rings** says that a surjective
[ring morphism](../rings/ring-morphism.md) $f \colon R \to S$ identifies the
[quotient ring](quotient-ring.md) of $R$ by the kernel of $f$ with $S$.
It turns the question of which rings are images of $R$ into the question of
which [ideals](ideal-of-a-commutative-ring.md) $R$ has, and it is the usual way to recognize a
quotient ring: to show that $R/I$ is isomorphic to a known ring $S$, one finds a
surjective morphism from $R$ onto $S$ whose kernel is $I$. It is the ring-theoretic
counterpart of the [first isomorphism theorem for groups](../group-theory/part-01/first-isomorphism-theorem.md).

## Statement

Let $f \colon R \to S$ be a morphism of commutative [rings](../rings/ring.md). Its kernel
$\ker f = \{ a \in R : f(a) = 0 \}$ [is an ideal of $R$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Maps.html#RingHom.ker), so the
quotient ring $R/\ker f$ is defined. If $f$ is surjective, then
[$R/\ker f$ is isomorphic to $S$ as a ring](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Operations.html#RingHom.quotientKerEquivOfSurjective),
by the isomorphism that
[sends the coset $a + \ker f$ to $f(a)$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Operations.html#RingHom.quotientKerEquivOfSurjective_apply_mk):

$$R/\ker f \;\cong\; S, \qquad a + \ker f \mapsto f(a).$$

Without surjectivity, the image $f(R)$ is a subring of $S$, and
[$R/\ker f$ is isomorphic to $f(R)$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Operations.html#RingHom.quotientKerEquivRange).

## Proof

Write $K = \ker f$ and define $\bar f(a + K) = f(a)$. Two cosets $a + K$ and
$b + K$ are equal exactly when $a - b \in K$, that is, when $f(a) - f(b) = 0$.
Read in one direction, this says $\bar f$ is well defined; read in the other, it
says $\bar f$ is injective. Since sums and products of cosets are computed on
representatives and $f$ preserves sums, products and the identity, $\bar f$ is a
ring morphism. Well-definedness and the morphism property are both instances of the
universal property of the quotient:
a ring morphism that vanishes on an ideal $I$
[induces a ring morphism on $R/I$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Defs.html#Ideal.Quotient.lift). The morphism induced
on $R/\ker f$ [is injective](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Operations.html#RingHom.kerLift_injective), and it is surjective
because $f$ is. A bijective ring morphism has a ring morphism as its inverse, so
$\bar f$ is an isomorphism.

## Examples

- **Integers modulo $n$.** Reduction modulo $n$ is a surjective morphism from the
  [integers](../numbers/integer.md) onto the ring of residues modulo $n$, and
  [its kernel is the ideal $n\mathbb{Z}$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/ZMod.html#ZMod.ker_intCastRingHom). Hence
  [$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is isomorphic to the ring of residues modulo $n$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Data/ZMod/QuotientRing.html#Int.quotientSpanNatEquivZMod).
- **Evaluation of polynomials.** For a commutative ring $R$ and $c \in R$,
  evaluation at $c$ maps the [polynomial ring](../polynomials-and-power-series/polynomial-in-one-indeterminate.md) $R[x]$ onto $R$
  (constants are sent to themselves), and
  [its kernel is the ideal generated by $x - c$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Polynomial/Ideal.html#Polynomial.ker_evalRingHom).
  So [$R[x]/(x - c)$ is isomorphic to $R$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Polynomial/Quotient.html#Polynomial.quotientSpanXSubCAlgEquiv).
- **The complex numbers.** The morphism $\mathbb{R}[x] \to \mathbb{C}$ sending a
  real polynomial $p$ to $p(i)$ is surjective, since $a + bx$ maps to $a + bi$, and
  its kernel is the ideal generated by $x^2 + 1$, the minimal polynomial of $i$
  over the reals. Hence $\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2 + 1) \cong \mathbb{C}$.
- **Products.** The projection $R \times S \to R$ is surjective with kernel
  $\{0\} \times S$, so $(R \times S)/(\{0\} \times S) \cong R$.

## Consequences

- **Ideals are kernels.** Conversely, every ideal $I$ is a kernel:
  [the kernel of the quotient map $R \to R/I$ is $I$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Operations.html#Ideal.mk_ker). Up to
  isomorphism, the surjective images of $R$ are therefore exactly its quotients $R/I$.
- **Prime and maximal kernels.** If $S$ is an integral domain, then $R/\ker f$ is
  one, and indeed for any morphism into a nonzero ring without zero divisors
  [the kernel is a prime ideal](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Maps.html#RingHom.ker_isPrime). If $f$ is surjective and
  $S$ is a field, or more generally a division ring,
  [the kernel is a maximal ideal](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Maps.html#RingHom.ker_isMaximal_of_surjective)
  (see [prime ideal](prime-ideal.md) and [maximal ideal](maximal-ideal.md)).
- **Ideals of the image.** For surjective $f$,
  [the ideals of $S$ correspond, by taking preimages, to the ideals of $R$ containing $\ker f$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Maps.html#Ideal.relIsoOfSurjective),
  and the correspondence preserves inclusion.
- **Third isomorphism theorem.** For ideals $I \subseteq J$ of a commutative ring
  $R$, applying the theorem to the surjection $R/I \to R/J$, whose kernel is the
  image $J/I$ of $J$, gives
  [$(R/I)/(J/I) \cong R/J$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Operations.html#DoubleQuot.quotQuotEquivQuotOfLE).
- **[Chinese remainder theorem](chinese-remainder-theorem.md).**
  For pairwise coprime ideals $I_1, \dots, I_k$ (that is, $I_i + I_j = R$ whenever
  $i \neq j$), the morphism
  $R \to R/I_1 \times \dots \times R/I_k$ is surjective with kernel
  $I_1 \cap \dots \cap I_k$, which yields the isomorphism of the theorem.

## Generalizations

The same argument gives analogous theorems for groups, modules and algebras, whose
quotients are likewise taken by kernels.
For a surjective group morphism $\varphi \colon G \to H$,
[$G/\ker\varphi \cong H$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/GroupTheory/QuotientGroup/Basic.html#QuotientGroup.quotientKerEquivOfSurjective). For a
surjective linear map $f \colon M \to N$ of [modules](../../linear-algebra/fundamentals/module.md), the quotient
of $M$ by the [kernel](../../../undergraduate/linear-algebra/fundamentals/kernel-of-a-linear-map.md)
[is linearly isomorphic to $N$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Isomorphisms.html#LinearMap.quotKerEquivOfSurjective). For a
surjective morphism of [algebras](../algebras-over-a-ring/associative-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring.md) over a commutative ring, the
quotient by the kernel
[is isomorphic to the codomain as an algebra](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Operations.html#Ideal.quotientKerAlgEquivOfSurjective).

## In Mathlib

Mathlib states the theorem in more generality than for commutative rings: the
domain may be any ring, commutative or not, and the codomain need only be a
semiring, a structure in which additive inverses need not exist. When the source
ring is noncommutative, the kernel is a two-sided ideal, which is what makes the quotient a ring. The theorem
is a construction rather than an existence statement: it builds a specific ring
isomorphism from the quotient by the kernel to the codomain. Its inverse is
obtained by choosing a preimage of each element, so the definition is
noncomputable; a computable variant
[exists for a morphism equipped with a right inverse](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Operations.html#RingHom.quotientKerEquivOfRightInverse),
and Mathlib uses it for the integers modulo $n$ and for evaluation of polynomials.
The version onto the image is derived from the surjective one by restricting the
codomain of the morphism to its image, and a further version allows a
[semiring as codomain with the image as a subsemiring](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Operations.html#RingHom.quotientKerEquivRangeS).
