# Subring

A **subring** of a [ring](ring.md) $R$ is a subset of $R$ that contains the identity
$1$ and is closed under addition, negation and multiplication, so that it is a ring in
its own right under the operations of $R$. Subrings are the substructures of ring theory,
as [subgroups](../group-theory/part-01/subgroup.md) are for groups: the [integers](../numbers/integer.md) inside the
[rational numbers](../numbers/rational-number.md), the Gaussian integers inside the
[complex numbers](../numbers/complex-number.md), and the image of any
[ring homomorphism](ring-morphism.md) are all subrings, and many constructions in algebra
and number theory consist of finding the right subring of a larger, better understood ring.

## Definition

Let $R$ be a ring with identity. A subset $S \subseteq R$ is a
[subring](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Defs.html#Subring) if

- $0 \in S$ and $1 \in S$;
- $a + b \in S$ and $-a \in S$ whenever $a, b \in S$;
- $ab \in S$ whenever $a, b \in S$.

(The condition $0 \in S$ follows from the others, since $0 = 1 + (-1)$.)

Equivalently, $S$ is an additive subgroup of $R$ that is closed under multiplication and
contains the identity of $R$. With the operations restricted from $R$,
[a subring is itself a ring](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Defs.html#Subring.toRing), and the inclusion $S \to R$ is a ring
homomorphism.

A convenient test: a subset $S$ is a subring exactly when $1 \in S$ and $S$ is closed
under subtraction and multiplication. Indeed
[a subring is closed under subtraction](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Defs.html#Subring.sub_mem); conversely, from $1 \in S$
and closure under subtraction one gets $0 = 1 - 1$, then $-a = 0 - a$ and
$a + b = a - (-b)$.

The requirement that $S$ contain the identity of $R$, not merely some identity of its own,
is part of the definition. Without it one speaks of a non-unital subring.

## Examples

- In the chain $\mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Q} \subset \mathbb{R} \subset \mathbb{C}$ of
  integers, rationals, [real numbers](../numbers/real-number.md) and complex numbers, each ring is a
  subring of the next.
- The Gaussian integers $\mathbb{Z}[i] = \{a + bi : a, b \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ form a subring
  of $\mathbb{C}$, since $i^2 = -1$ keeps products of this form.
- In the [polynomial](../polynomials-and-power-series/polynomial-in-one-indeterminate.md) ring $R[x]$ the constant polynomials form a
  subring, a copy of $R$.
- In the ring of $n \times n$ [matrices](../../linear-algebra/matrices/ring-valued-matrix.md) over a field, the upper triangular
  matrices form a subring, and so do the scalar matrices.
- Every ring has a smallest subring:
  [it consists of the integer multiples $n \cdot 1$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Basic.html#Subring.mem_bot),
  the image of the unique ring homomorphism $\mathbb{Z} \to R$.
- **Non-examples.** The even integers $2\mathbb{Z}$ are closed under addition, negation and
  multiplication but do not contain $1$, so they are not a subring of $\mathbb{Z}$. In
  $\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ the set $\mathbb{Z} \times \{0\}$ is a ring with identity
  $(1, 0)$, but it does not contain the identity $(1, 1)$ of the larger ring, so it is not a
  subring either.

## Properties

- **Intersections.** [The intersection of any family of subrings of $R$ is a subring](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Basic.html#Subring.coe_sInf).
  Unions usually are not: the union of $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ and $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt 2]$ in
  $\mathbb{C}$ contains $i$ and $\sqrt 2$ but not $i + \sqrt 2$. The union of a nonempty
  family that is directed under inclusion, such as an increasing chain,
  [is a subring](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Basic.html#Subring.coe_iSup_of_directed).
- **Generated subrings.** For a subset $X \subseteq R$, the
  [subring generated by $X$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Basic.html#Subring.closure) is the intersection of all subrings
  containing $X$. It [contains $X$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Basic.html#Subring.subset_closure), and
  [it lies inside a subring $T$ exactly when $X \subseteq T$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Basic.html#Subring.closure_le), so
  it is the smallest subring containing $X$. Concretely,
  [it is the additive subgroup generated by the set of finite products of elements of $X$, including the empty product $1$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Basic.html#Subring.mem_closure_iff).
  For instance $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ is the subring of $\mathbb{C}$ generated by $i$.
- **Homomorphisms.** For a ring homomorphism $f : R \to R'$,
  [the image of a subring of $R$ is a subring of $R'$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Basic.html#Subring.map) and
  [the preimage of a subring of $R'$ is a subring of $R$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Basic.html#Subring.comap). In particular
  [the range of $f$ is a subring of $R'$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Basic.html#RingHom.range). Given two homomorphisms
  $f, g : R \to R'$, [the set where they agree is a subring of $R$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Basic.html#RingHom.eqLocus).
- **The center.** [The center of $R$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Basic.html#Subring.center), the set of elements commuting
  with every element, is a subring; [an element $z$ lies in it exactly when $gz = zg$ for all $g \in R$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Basic.html#Subring.mem_center_iff).
  [The center of a division ring is a field](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Basic.html#Subring.instField).
- **Subrings and ideals.** An [ideal](../ideals-and-quotients/ideal-of-a-commutative-ring.md) is closed under addition, negation and
  multiplication, but [an ideal is the whole ring exactly when it contains $1$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Lattice.html#Ideal.eq_top_iff_one),
  so the only ideal that is a subring is $R$ itself.
- **Integral domains.** A subring of an integral domain has no zero divisors, so it is again
  an integral domain; every subring of a [field](../field-theory/field.md) is an integral domain.

## Related concepts

A [subfield](../field-theory/subfield.md) of a field is a subring that is also closed under taking
inverses of nonzero elements. A subring of an
[associative algebra](../algebras-over-a-ring/associative-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring.md) over a commutative ring $k$ that contains the image
of $k$ is a subalgebra. Weakening the requirement on $1$ gives non-unital subrings, and
dropping negation gives subsemirings, the analogous notion for semirings.

## In Mathlib

Mathlib treats a subring as a bundled structure: a subset of the ring together with proofs
that it is a subsemiring (it contains $0$ and $1$ and is closed under addition and
multiplication) and an additive subgroup. The definition is stated for rings that need
not be associative. A subring
[inherits a non-associative ring structure in general](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Defs.html#SubringClass.toNonAssocRing),
and a ring structure when the ambient ring is associative. Subrings of a fixed ring are ordered by inclusion and form a complete lattice:
the meet is the intersection,
[the least subring is the image of $\mathbb{Z}$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Basic.html#Subring.coe_bot), and the join of two
subrings is the subring generated by their union; more generally,
[the subring generated by $X \cup Y$ is the join of the subrings generated by $X$ and by $Y$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Basic.html#Subring.closure_union). A subring always shares the
identity of the ambient ring; the version without that requirement is the separate
[non-unital subring](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/NonUnitalSubring/Defs.html#NonUnitalSubring) structure, and
[a subsemiring that contains $-1$ can be turned into a subring](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Ring/Subring/Defs.html#Subsemiring.toSubring).
