# The Maxwell conjecture

> *References:*
> - [arXiv:2607.27197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27197) **The Maxwell Conjecture is False** by
>   *Philip Arathoon, Gavin Ball and Matthew D. Kvalheim* (2026)
> - [Mystery of point charges](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/pdm012) by *Andrei Gabrielov, Dmitry
>   Novikov and Boris Shapiro*, Proc. London Math. Soc. **95** (2007), 443-472
> - [Maxwell's conjecture on three point charges with equal magnitudes](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physd.2015.07.007)
>   by *Ya-Lun Tsai*, Phys. D **309** (2015), 86-98
>
> In his 1873 *Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism* (§113) J. C. Maxwell discusses the number of
> equilibria of the electric field generated by $n$ point charges. Based on their reading of that
> passage, Gabrielov, Novikov and Shapiro formulated the *Maxwell conjecture*: if the critical
> points of the electrostatic potential
> $$V(x) = \sum_{j=1}^n \frac{q_j}{\|x - a_j\|}$$
> generated by $n$ point charges are all non-degenerate, then their number is at most $(n-1)^2$.
>
> Arathoon, Ball and Kvalheim disproved the conjecture by exhibiting a configuration of five
> positive point charges — three unit charges at the vertices of an equilateral triangle together
> with two small charges placed symmetrically on the axis of the triangle — whose potential has at
> least $24$ non-degenerate critical points, whereas $(5-1)^2 = 16$.

## `Arxiv.«2607.27197».maxwell_conjecture.counterexample_morse`

Solved: stated here without proof.

> The second half of **Theorem 1** of Arathoon–Ball–Kvalheim: after a small perturbation of the
> charge strengths, the counterexample potential is a Morse function, i.e. it has finitely many
> critical points, all of which are non-degenerate, and there are still at least $24$ of them.
>
> *Reference:* [arXiv:2607.27197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27197)

```lean
theorem maxwell_conjecture.counterexample_morse :
    ∃ (q : Fin 5 → ℝ) (a : Fin 5 → EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3)), Function.Injective a ∧
      (∀ j, 0 < q j) ∧
      (∀ x ∈ equilibria q a, HasNondegenerateHessianAt (potential q a) x) ∧
      (equilibria q a).Finite ∧ 24 ≤ (equilibria q a).encard
```

## `Arxiv.«2607.27197».maxwell_conjecture.counterexample`

Solved: stated here without proof.

> **Theorem 1** of Arathoon–Ball–Kvalheim: there is an $\varepsilon_0 > 0$ such that for all
> $0 < \varepsilon < \varepsilon_0$ the potential generated by the five charges located at
> $a_1, \dots, a_5$ with respective strengths $1, 1, 1, q_\varepsilon, q_\varepsilon$, where
> $q_\varepsilon = \frac{3}{4}\varepsilon^3 - \frac{5}{32}\varepsilon^5$, admits at least $24$
> non-degenerate critical points.
>
> *Reference:* [arXiv:2607.27197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27197)

```lean
theorem maxwell_conjecture.counterexample :
    ∃ ε₀ > 0, ∀ ε ∈ Set.Ioo 0 ε₀,
      24 ≤ (nondegenerateEquilibria (counterexampleCharges ε)
        (counterexamplePositions ε)).encard
```

## `Arxiv.«2607.27197».maxwell_conjecture.variants.polynomial_bound`

Open: stated without proof.

> Is the number of equilibria of $n$ point charges bounded by a polynomial in $n$?
>
> With the quadratic bound of Maxwell's conjecture gone, the gap between the known bounds is
> wide: the constructions of Arathoon–Ball–Kvalheim give configurations with at least $10n$
> equilibria asymptotically, while the best known upper bounds — $2^{2n^2}(3n)^{2n}$ by
> Gabrielov–Novikov–Shapiro, $5 \cdot 9^{3+n}$ by Zolotov and $2^n (3n-2)^3$ by
> Edelsbrunner–Fillmore–Oliveira — are all exponential in $n$.
>
> *References:* [arXiv:2607.27197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27197),
> [Counting equilibria of the electrostatic potential](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05315)

```lean
theorem maxwell_conjecture.variants.polynomial_bound :
    ∃ C d : ℕ, ∀ (n : ℕ), 0 < n → ∀ (q : Fin n → ℝ) (a : Fin n → EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3)),
      Function.Injective a → (∀ j, q j ≠ 0) →
      (∀ x ∈ equilibria q a, HasNondegenerateHessianAt (potential q a) x) →
      (equilibria q a).encard ≤ (C * n ^ d : ℕ)
```
