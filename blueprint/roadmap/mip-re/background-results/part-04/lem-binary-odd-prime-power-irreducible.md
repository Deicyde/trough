# Effective irreducibles of every odd-prime-power degree

A fixed polynomial-time program takes unary $q$ and unary $r$. If $q$ is an odd prime and $r=q^{e+1}$ for $e\geq 0$, it prints exactly $r+1$ coefficient bits of a monic irreducible binary polynomial of degree $r$. On arbitrary inputs its output has at most $r+1$ bits, where $r$ denotes the second input length.

## Proof

Construct the nonresidue polynomial $f$ of degree $m=\mathrm{ord}_q(2)$. The odd-prime Kummer criterion makes $f(X^r)$ irreducible. In its explicitly represented quotient, with root $\beta$, compute $\gamma=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\beta^{2^{ri}}$ by repeated modular squaring and addition. Its Frobenius period divides $r$. To show its degree is exactly $r$, use the subfield generated by $\beta^q$: membership of $\gamma$ in that subfield would produce a nonzero polynomial of degree less than $q$ vanishing at $\beta$. The residues of $2^{ri}$ modulo $q$ are distinct because $\mathrm{gcd}(r,m)=1$, which gives a nonzero coefficient in that polynomial. The relative degree of $\beta$ is $q$, a contradiction. The closed orbit program therefore prints the minimal polynomial of $\gamma$. The flat extension has degree $mr$, and all arithmetic, trace, and orbit loops use the supplied unary parameters and fixed quotient widths, giving a global polynomial bound.
