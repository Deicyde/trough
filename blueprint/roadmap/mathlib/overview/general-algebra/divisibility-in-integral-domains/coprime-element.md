# Coprime element

Two elements $x$ and $y$ of a commutative [ring](../rings/ring.md) are **coprime** when $1$ can be
written as a combination $ax + by$ with coefficients $a, b$ in the ring. For the integers
this is the familiar condition that the greatest common divisor is $1$, by
[Bézout's identity](../../../hundred-theorems/part-03/bezouts-theorem.md). In a general ring it is a stronger condition
than having no common non-unit factor, and it is the form of coprimality that appears in results
such as the [Chinese remainder theorem](../ideals-and-quotients/chinese-remainder-theorem.md): it says that the [ideals](../ideals-and-quotients/ideal-of-a-commutative-ring.md) generated by $x$ and $y$ together generate the whole ring,
which is the hypothesis of that theorem.

## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $x, y \in R$. Then $x$ and $y$ are
[coprime if there exist $a, b \in R$ with $ax + by = 1$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Coprime/Basic.html#IsCoprime).
The definition is symmetric:
[$x$ and $y$ are coprime exactly when $y$ and $x$ are](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Coprime/Basic.html#isCoprime_comm).

In terms of ideals, $x$ and $y$ are coprime if and only if
[the principal ideals $(x)$ and $(y)$ satisfy $(x) + (y) = R$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Operations.html#Ideal.sup_eq_top_iff_isCoprime),
since $(x) + (y)$ consists of all combinations $ax + by$ and an ideal is all of $R$ exactly when
it contains $1$.

## Examples

- **Integers.** Two integers are
  [coprime if and only if their greatest common divisor is $1$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Coprime/Lemmas.html#Int.isCoprime_iff_gcd_eq_one).
  For instance $4$ and $9$ are coprime, as $(-2)\cdot 4 + 1 \cdot 9 = 1$, while $4$ and $6$ are
  not, since every combination $4a + 6b$ is even.
- **Units and zero.** The element $1$ is [coprime to every element](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Coprime/Basic.html#isCoprime_one_left).
  An element is [coprime to $0$ if and only if it is a unit](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Coprime/Basic.html#isCoprime_zero_right), and
  [coprime to itself if and only if it is a unit](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Coprime/Basic.html#isCoprime_self).
- **Fields.** In a [field](../field-theory/field.md), and more generally in a semifield (a commutative
  semiring with $0 \neq 1$ in which every nonzero element has an inverse), two elements are
  [coprime if and only if they are not both zero](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Coprime/Basic.html#Semifield.isCoprime_iff).
- **Polynomials in two variables.** In the [polynomial ring](../polynomials-and-power-series/polynomial-in-several-indeterminates.md) $k[X, Y]$ over
  a field $k$, the only common divisors of $X$ and $Y$ are the nonzero constants, which are
  units. Yet $X$ and $Y$ are not coprime: every combination $aX + bY$ has constant term $0$.
  Likewise the only common divisors of $2$ and $X$ in the [polynomial ring](../polynomials-and-power-series/polynomial-in-one-indeterminate.md)
  $\mathbb{Z}[X]$ are $\pm 1$, yet they are not coprime, because the constant term of $2a + Xb$
  is even.

## Properties

Throughout, $x$, $y$, $z$ are elements of a commutative ring.

- **Common divisors.** If $x$ and $y$ are coprime, then
  [every common divisor of $x$ and $y$ is a unit](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Coprime/Basic.html#IsCoprime.isUnit_of_dvd'): if $d$ divides
  both, it divides $ax + by = 1$.
- **Euclid's lemma.** If $x$ and $y$ are coprime and
  [$x$ divides $yz$, then $x$ divides $z$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Coprime/Basic.html#IsCoprime.dvd_of_dvd_mul_left). Indeed
  $z = axz + byz$, and $x$ divides both terms.
- **Products of divisors.** If $x$ and $y$ are coprime and
  [both divide $z$, then $xy$ divides $z$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Coprime/Basic.html#IsCoprime.mul_dvd).
- **Products and powers.** The product $xy$ is
  [coprime to $z$ if and only if both $x$ and $y$ are coprime to $z$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Coprime/Basic.html#IsCoprime.mul_left_iff).
  Consequently, if $x$ and $y$ are coprime, then
  [$x^m$ and $y^n$ are coprime for all natural numbers $m$ and $n$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Coprime/Lemmas.html#IsCoprime.pow).
- **Euclidean steps.** For every $z$,
  [$x + yz$ is coprime to $y$ if and only if $x$ is](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Coprime/Basic.html#IsCoprime.add_mul_left_left_iff), so in a
  [Euclidean domain](euclidean-domain.md) the steps of the
  [Euclidean algorithm](../../../hundred-theorems/part-03/greatest-common-divisor-algorithm.md) do not change whether two elements are coprime.
- **Ring morphisms.** If $x$ and $y$ are coprime and $f$ is a
  [ring morphism](../rings/ring-morphism.md), then [$f(x)$ and $f(y)$ are coprime](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Coprime/Basic.html#IsCoprime.map),
  since $f(a)f(x) + f(b)f(y) = f(1) = 1$.

## Relation to greatest common divisors

Call $x$ and $y$ relatively prime when every common divisor is a unit; in a ring with
[greatest common divisors](greatest-common-divisor.md) this says that $\gcd(x, y)$ is a unit. The first
property above shows that coprime elements are relatively prime, and the polynomial examples show
that the converse fails in general. The converse does hold when the ideal $(x, y)$ is principal,
generated by some $d$: then $d$ divides $x$ and $y$, so a relatively prime pair forces $d$ to be a
unit and $(x, y) = R$. Thus,
[when the ideal generated by $x$ and $y$ is principal, $x$ and $y$ are relatively prime if and only if they are coprime](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/PrincipalIdealDomain.html#isRelPrime_iff_isCoprime).
In particular the two notions agree in every
[principal ideal domain](principal-ideal-domain.md), such as $\mathbb{Z}$ or the polynomial
ring $k[X]$ over a field.

## Coprime ideals

The ideal form of the definition extends to arbitrary ideals: two ideals $I$ and $J$ of $R$ are
coprime, or comaximal, when $I + J = R$ (Mathlib states this as the same combination condition
in the semiring of ideals; the two agree, as noted below). Two elements are coprime exactly when
[the principal ideals they generate are coprime](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Operations.html#Ideal.isCoprime_span_singleton_iff).
For example, [two distinct maximal ideals are coprime](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Operations.html#Ideal.isCoprime_of_isMaximal)
(see [maximal ideal](../ideals-and-quotients/maximal-ideal.md)). The Chinese remainder theorem says that for
pairwise coprime ideals $I_1, \dots, I_n$, the quotient of $R$ by their intersection is isomorphic
to the product of the quotients $R/I_k$.

## In Mathlib

Mathlib defines coprimality for commutative semirings, not only rings, by the same condition that
some combination $ax + by$ equals $1$. Since the ideals of a commutative semiring themselves form a
commutative semiring, the same definition applies to ideals, and Mathlib proves that
[ideals $I$ and $J$ are coprime in this sense if and only if $I + J = R$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Operations.html#Ideal.isCoprime_iff_sup_eq).
In a semiring without negatives the definition can be too strict: for natural numbers it
[holds only when one of the two numbers is $1$](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Coprime/Basic.html#Nat.isCoprime_iff). Mathlib therefore keeps a
separate notion for natural numbers, greatest common divisor equal to $1$, and proves that
[natural numbers satisfy it exactly when their images in the integers are coprime](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Coprime/Lemmas.html#Nat.isCoprime_iff_coprime).
