# Padded line POVM to Succinctly described 3SAT and…

Comments. Formalized in `MIPRE/Background/QLD/Padded.lean`, on the block identities of `MIPRE/Foundations/Blocks.lean`.

**The construction.** `LPData` is the triple $(u, s, v)$ a seeded line–point pair is generated from, in either dimension; `subX` and `subZ` are the sampling procedure, reading the two $m$-dimensional pairs off a padded one together with a fresh seed and raw direction per side. `padCase` is the four-way case distinction, and `seedIn` is the retargeting of a seed to a prescribed block keeping its offset — the paper's “choose $s_X$ uniformly at random subject to $\chi(s_X) = i$”, made a function so that the construction is a function of its randomness rather than a conditional distribution.

**The containment** is `xBlk_dir_sub` and `zBlk_dir_sub`, which say that in each case the relevant block of the padded direction either *is* the subline's direction or vanishes, and `xBlk_on_subX` / `zBlk_on_subZ`, which turn that into $u_X \in \ell_X$ and $u_Z \in \ell_Z$.

**The law is stated as four identities, one per case**, and they are identities of averages rather than of sums: `avgSub_free`, `avgSub_zc`, `avgSub_xc` and `avgSub_xc_dline` each say that the average over the sampling space *equals* the average over a product of two line–point laws, restricted on a side exactly when that side is forced. No constant survives; the multiplicities — the assignments to the coordinates and the fresh randomness the construction does not read — are what the normalization divides out, and they differ from case to case, which is why the four are separate statements. `sumAll_eq_sum_sumRestr` is the mixture: the unrestricted law is the sum of the $m$ restricted ones, so a side that is not forced contributes a uniform mixture.

**Property 1 is definitional and Property 3 is definitional**, for the same reason: the padded line–point pair is an input the construction does not modify, and the line type is a parameter handed to both sides. `subX_question_aline` and `subZ_question_aline` record the second of these.

**Why $4m$.** $\alpha$ and $\beta$ sit at positions $2m$ and $2m+1$, so the dummy block is the $2m-2$ positions above them and every index bound needs only $m \geq 1$. The asymmetry between the two sides in the diagonal case is what the paper's round-12 note on this lemma repairs: the missing primes on $w_X, w_Z$, and the unspecified law of the coordinate registers $s_X, s_Z$.

- [Padded line POVM](lem-qld-padded-lines.md)

Comments. The statement and its proof are formalized; the consistency bound $\delta_{\mathrm{combine}}$ was the last piece to close. Everything the statement asserts *about the measurement* is formalized — the combining map, its two degree bounds, the POVM $\widehat Q^\ell$ itself and the coarse-graining that turns it into an $\mathbb{F}_q$-valued outcome — in `MIPRE/Background/QLD/Combine.lean`, on top of the transfer in `MIPRE/Background/QLD/Product.lean`. The transfer is worth separating from the rest because it is where an apparent obstruction turns out not to be one.

**The obstruction, and why it is not one.** Lemma [Pairs of lines](../part-02/lem-qld-pairs-of-lines.md) is proved on the Pauli basis test's own question distribution: one content, carrying both sides' points but a *single* seed and a *single* raw direction, so the $X$ line and the $Z$ line it presents are not independent. This lemma needs the same conclusion on a *product* of two independent line–point laws, which is what Lemma [The sublines distribution](../part-02/lem-qld-sublines.md) delivers. The two distributions are genuinely different — but **no hypothesis of the pasting lemma involves both lines**. Each marginal consistency involves one line and the two points; the fine self-consistency and the collision term involve one line only. So the only laws that have to agree are one side's line–point data on its own, one side's data together with the *other side's point*, and the two points; and each of those has the same law under a content as under a product, because a content's two points and its seed and raw direction are independent and uniform. `avg_content_eq_pair_X`, `avg_content_eq_pair_Z` and `avg_content_eq_pair_pts` are those three identities, each one line on top of `avg_comp_equiv_fst`: exhibit the splitting of each sample space that isolates the part the quantity depends on, and the marginals match.

**The change of variables generalizes for free.** The device that makes the point uniform on its line without forming a conditional distribution needs a finite nonempty sample space, a shift action, and a direction the shift does not move — nothing about contents. `bijective_shift` and `sum_shift_gen` are that statement, and `sum_pair_shift` is its instance at a pair with the shift acting on the $X$ side only.

**The combining map, and where its whole geometry sits.** `combine` is built through Mathlib's `Polynomial` and truncated back to a coefficient vector: it is $\alpha(t) f_X(x(t)) + \beta(t) f_Z(z(t))$ with all four substitutions *affine* in the padded parameter $t$, so the degree bound $md+1$ is a product of degrees and nothing more. `eval_padCombine` is the paper's parametrization-free defining property: at the padded parameter $t$ the combined polynomial takes the value $\alpha \cdot f_X(x) + \beta \cdot f_Z(z)$ at the corresponding point, each side's polynomial read at the parameter of its *own* subline.

The only geometric input is `xBlk_dir_sub` and its $Z$ twin (Lemma [The sublines distribution](../part-02/lem-qld-sublines.md)): the $X$ block of the padded direction is *either* the $X$ subline's own direction *or* zero. In the first case the subline parameter moves by exactly the padded shift, in the second it does not move at all — so in both it is $p_0 + c\,t$ with $c$ one or zero, and `subAff` is that pair. The paper's case analysis over the branches of the subline construction is not needed: the disjunction is proved once and used once.

**The axis bound is the same case distinction `padCase` already makes.** A padded direction in the $X$ or $Z$ block leaves $\alpha$ and $\beta$ constant along the line, so the combined polynomial inherits the degree of $f_X$ and $f_Z$ — degree $d$ exactly when Lemma [Axis degree for expanded line measurements](../part-02/lem-qld-axis-degree.md) supplies it, which is why `degLE_padCombine_aline` takes that as a hypothesis. A direction at $\alpha$, $\beta$ or a dummy coordinate leaves *both* blocks constant, so the combined polynomial is affine, and there $d \geq 1$ is all that is used.

**The measurement.** `padLineMats` is $\widehat Q^\ell$: the uniform average, over the fresh randomness the subline construction draws, of the pasted line measurement $T^{\ell_X,\ell_Z}$ coarse-grained by `padCombine`. Conditioning on the padded line is exactly averaging over that randomness and nothing else, so the paper's conditional expectation is a plain uniform average. `sum_padLineMats` and `posSemidef_padLineMats` are that it is a POVM, and `sum_filter_padLineMats` is the data-processing step the paper's proof ends with, in the form `pairs_of_lines_prod` applies to.

**Lemma [Pairs of lines](../part-02/lem-qld-pairs-of-lines.md), on the product.** `pairs_of_lines_prod` is that lemma at $k_X = \mathtt{pairCX}$, $k_Z = \mathtt{pairCZ}$ and `pairShift` as the shift: each side's content is its own point, seed and raw direction, with the *other* side's point filled in as the content's other point, which no quantity of that side's line depends on. The two $\mathtt{rfl}$s `pairCX_pairShift` and `pairCZ_pairShift` are the whole of the instantiation — the generalization it instantiates is recorded under Lemma [Pairs of lines](../part-02/lem-qld-pairs-of-lines.md), since that is the lemma it proves.

**And from the game.** `pairs_of_lines_prod_of_items` takes only game-level hypotheses and delivers the product form, which is what the consistency chain consumes. Two things had to be arranged for it. First, the combined point measurement is re-indexed by the *pair of points* (`combined_points_pts`) rather than by the content: all four inputs of the joint-measurement construction see the content only through its two points, so the same construction runs at the pair, and the resulting measurement is usable at any sample space carrying two points — whereas the content-indexed one would have to be fed contents with zeroed probes, whose average is not the content average. Second, `FactorsX` and `FactorsZ` say that a line presentation reads the content only through that side's line-point data; both presentations satisfy them by $\mathtt{rfl}$, and that is what lets `avg_content_eq_pair_X` carry each content-level bound to the product (`avg_pair_eq_content_X` and its three companions).

**The restricted laws, and where the factors of $m$ come from.** `avgRestr` was already the paper's $D_{\mathrm{ty},i}$. `sum_avgRestr` says the $m$ restricted laws average to the unrestricted one, so each is at most $m$ times it (`avgRestr_le_mul_avgAll`) and a product of two is at most $m^2$ times the product (`avgRestr_prod_le_mul_avgAll`). The paper's factor is $2m$ because it mixes over the two line types; here the type is a parameter, so it is $m$. This is the whole source of the leading $m$ in $\delta_{\mathrm{combine}}$.

**The paper's three Cauchy–Schwarz claims are avoidable here, and this is a finding.** The paper proves the consistency by chaining Claims 17-1, 17-2 and 17-3, and says in a comment that the chain is needed because “under the distribution $D$ the pair is correlated (for diagonal lines both coordinates share the line parameter), so that decomposition is not available”. The correlation is real *given the padded line* — a uniform point of it moves both blocks with one parameter — but the quantity to be bounded conditions on the two *sublines*, which know each block's coset in its own space and not the relative offset of the two blocks. Marginalizing that out restores independence, and `avgSub_free` and its three companions (Lemma [The sublines distribution](../part-02/lem-qld-sublines.md)) already prove the joint law to be *exactly* a product of the two marginals at a fixed padded seed. `avgSub_le_mul_avgAll` combines that with the restricted-law transfer: the padded line–point law is at most $m^2$ times the product of the two unrestricted subline laws, uniformly in the padded seed and the line type. `avgSubAB_le_of_forall` does the same for $\alpha$ and $\beta$ at the sampled point: translating the padded point in those two coordinates alone is a bijection that leaves both sublines fixed, so they are uniform and independent of everything else and a bound holding for each fixed $(\alpha,\beta)$ holds for the joint law. No refinement of the block decomposition is needed. The report is `reports/padded-lines-product-law.md`.

**The consistency bound is proved**, in `MIPRE/Background/QLD/PaddedLines.lean`. `padded_lines_consistency` is it: on the padded line–point law, at every padded seed and line type, the combined point measurement read along $(\alpha,\beta)$ at the sampled point agrees with the pasted line measurement coarse-grained by the combining map, up to $m^2 \cdot \delta_P$. The proof is the coarse-graining step (`sum_bornProb_le_fibre`: agreement only increases under a common coarse-graining, for bare matrix families rather than `POVM` structures, which is what the pasted measurement is) followed by the two domination lemmas.

**What of the statement is conditional.** Two of the three gaps this paragraph used to list are closed. The error form was corrected to $\mathrm{poly}(m)\cdot\mathrm{poly}(\varepsilon,md/q)$ in the statement above, which is what is proved; and the second register version is `padded_lines_consistency_swap`, by the game's player symmetry. What remains is the degree bound $d$ on an axis-parallel line, which is conditional on Lemma [Axis degree for expanded line measurements](../part-02/lem-qld-axis-degree.md) — an additional target there, and the reason the degree clause is written conditionally above. So the proof mark covers the consistency bound and the measurement's being a POVM, and the degree clause only given that lemma. `reports/padded-lines-product-law.md` records the history, together with the size of $\delta_P$ itself: $\delta_P \approx 7\cdot10^3\,\varepsilon^{1/4} + 1.5\sqrt{md/q}$, so the bound is vacuous unless $\varepsilon \leq 4\cdot 10^{-16}$ — a property of the whole chain's constants, not of this step.

- [The simultaneous global measurement](lem-qld-simultaneous.md)

Comments. The conclusion is formalized as the structure `SimulPair` (`MIPRE/Background/QLD/Simul.lean`): for a projective strategy of a bipartite model and an error $\delta$, a model $K$ into which the register model embeds (Definition [Embeddings of bipartite models](../../downstream-results/part-04/def-model-embedding.md)) — a local isometry carrying the state to the state and keeping both units, along which every expectation of the expanded measurements transfers exactly — projective pair measurements $S_A$, $S_B$ in $K$'s algebras, with outcomes in pairs of individual-degree-$d$ polynomials, and the two evaluated-marginal consistencies against the opposite party's expanded point measurements carried to $K$. The lemma is the existence of a `SimulPair` at error $\delta_S$ in the padded model, along the inert embedding (`exists_simulPair`). A `SimulPair` moves along any further embedding (`SimulPair.transport`), and stage 5 uses it on the first cut of the physical model, along the embedding $\iota_1$ of Definition [The models of stages 4 and 5](def-qld-physical-model.md) (`CutSimul`); the transport along $j_1$ of the padded model's `SimulPair` is one by definition. Two departures from the paper's statement. The sandwiched joint estimate it also announces is not carried in the structure: stage 5 consumes only the two evaluated marginals, and the sandwich is what Lemma [Sandwich estimates and the two marginals](lem-qld-global-sandwich.md) passes through on the way to them. And the hypothesis is $48md \leq q$ rather than the standing $16md \leq q$, which is what makes the factor $1 - 2\eta$ of Lemma [Separating the two point variables](lem-qld-global-separate.md) at least $1/2$; as with $16md > q$, the regime $48md > q$ is absorbed in the assembly by enlarging the universal constant (Lemma [The error of the Pauli basis test](lem-qld-error.md)).

- [Admissible padded setup](lem-qld-global-setup.md)

Comments. The ledger node dilates the line POVMs to projective measurements at this point, “using fresh local product ancillas”. The formalization keeps the strategy as a family of POVMs and dilates once, at the application of the soundness theorem (Lemma [A global padded polynomial PVM](lem-qld-global-pvm.md)), on the padding register of Definition [The models of stages 4 and 5](def-qld-physical-model.md): on the padded model the Born rule sees only the reference entries of the dilated measurements, which are the padded strategy's, so nothing has to be proved about the dilation beyond its compression (Lemma [Projective dilation against a fixed state, in a star-ordered ring](../../downstream-results/part-03/lem-kraus-dilation.md)). The node's “$16md > q$” clause belongs to the assembly of Theorem [Pauli basis test / quantum low individual degree test](../part-01/thm-qld.md), where the trivial bound is used instead of the padded theorem (Lemma [The error of the Pauli basis test](lem-qld-error.md)). The node also lists $4m \mid q$ beside the regime, as though it were a further hypothesis; here it is derived, and the derivation is worth getting exactly right, because the obvious reading is false: $m \mid q$ with $q$ a power of two gives only that $m$ is a power of two, and at $m = q$ the conclusion fails. What gives $4m \mid q$ is $m \mid q$ together with $4m \leq q$ (`four_mul_dvd_card`), which the regime $48md \leq q$ supplies (`four_mul_dvd_card_of_regime`, `MIPRE/Background/QLD/Regime.lean`); stages 4 and 5 derive it there, and outside the regime the trivial bound is used and the divisibility is never needed. Nor is admissibility what the step uses — the oddness of the degree is for the self-dual basis — since a finite field of characteristic two has power-of-two size already (`exists_card_eq_two_pow`).

- [Exact Pauli operators](lem-qld-exact-paulis.md)

Comments. Both halves are formalized, for a simultaneous pair measurement (Lemma [The simultaneous global measurement](lem-qld-simultaneous.md)) of a strategy of failure $\varepsilon$, and both are about the *same* objects: $\tilde{M}^{W,{\mathrm{ind}}_m(u)}$ and $\tilde{W}^e(\tilde{u})$ built from that measurement, on that party's registers. The closeness constant is explicit,

$$
\delta \;=\; \delta_S + 2\Bigl( \delta_S + \sqrt{688\,\varepsilon} + \frac{md}{q} \Bigr) ,
$$

which is the $O(\delta_S + \sqrt{\varepsilon} + md/q)$ the paper absorbs into $\delta_S$. The rest of this comment records how it was reached, because two of the obstacles it used to list turned out not to exist.

Of the approximate half's ingredients, three were in place first: the agreement form of Schwartz–Zippel against every multilinear polynomial at once, the aggregation that turns it into a bound on the mass at non-multilinear outcomes, and the substitution error of two families agreeing off that mass (`MIPRE/Background/QLD/NonMultilinear.lean`). A fourth is now too: the strategy's $(\mathtt{Point}, W)$ measurement and the low-degree reading of its $(\mathtt{Pauli}, W)$ answer are close *on one party*, at $688\,\varepsilon$ (`sum_normSq_point_sub_pauli_le`), which is what lets the point be sampled independently of the operators. The two inputs are cross-party items of Lemma [Win implications](../part-02/lem-qld-win.md), and they share Alice's point measurement, so one triangle inequality removes it. A fifth carries such a statement from the strategy's state to the expanded one: every hatted measurement is a convolution $\widehat X_c = \sum_{a + b = c} X_a \otimes T_b$ with the ancilla's Weyl measurement, and for a *projective* $T$ the cross terms of $\widehat X^\dagger \widehat X$ vanish, so the common ancilla factor drops out exactly: $\sum_c \|({I} \otimes \widehat X_c) \widehat\psi\|^2 = \sum_a \|({I} \otimes X_a) \psi\|^2$ (`sum_normSq_stateVecB_conv_eq`). The two together put the closeness on the expanded state with the same constant (`sum_normSq_hat_point_sub_pauli_le`). The aggregation has now been applied to the simultaneous measurement's outcomes, in `MIPRE/Background/QLD/Multilinear.lean`, and the bound the appendix's repair turns on is formalized: with $\widehat S^W$ a simultaneous pair measurement of error $\delta_S$ for a strategy of failure $\varepsilon$,

$$
\sum_{g \text{ not multilinear}} \bigl\langle \widehat S^W_g \otimes {I} \bigr\rangle_\Phi
\;\leq\; \delta_S + \sqrt{688\,\varepsilon} + \frac{md}{q}
$$

(`SimulPair.sum_bornProb_not_isML_le`). Its three inputs are the point-independent family indexed by cube data (`hatPauli`, the strategy's $(\mathtt{Pauli}, W)$ measurement convolved with the ancilla's Weyl measurement, projective and independent of the sampled point, whose reading at a point is the hatted Pauli measurement there — `hatPauli_map`); the Cauchy–Schwarz that substitutes it for the point measurement at cost $\sqrt{688\,\varepsilon}$ (`abs_sum_weighted_bornProb_le`, `SimulPair.sum_bornProb_hatPauli_ge`); and Lemma [Schwartz–Zippel](../part-05/lem-schwartz-zippel.md) per non-multilinear outcome, uniformly in the cube datum (`sum_uniform_agree_ldEnc_le`). The marginal is taken *indexed by polynomials* rather than by their value at the point (`polyMarg`), which is what makes the outcome operator independent of the point that Schwartz–Zippel then samples.

*And the agreement itself is formalized*: expanding $\tilde{M}^{W,{\mathrm{ind}}_m(u)}_a$ and contracting the generalized Pauli against the strategy's point measurement turns the first item's left-hand side into the outcome sum with the label ${\mathrm{Coded}}(g) \cdot {\mathrm{ind}}_m(u)$, and

$$
\mathbb E_u \sum_g \bigl\langle \widehat S^W_g \otimes
\widehat M^{(\mathtt{Point}, W), u}_{{\mathrm{Coded}}(g) \cdot {\mathrm{ind}}_m(u)} \bigr\rangle_\Phi
\;\geq\; 1 - \delta_S - 2\Bigl( \delta_S + \sqrt{688\,\varepsilon} + \frac{md}{q} \Bigr)
$$

(`SimulPair.sum_bornProb_cubeData_ge`), from the helper's agreement read by polynomial (`SimulPair.sum_bornProb_polyMarg_ge`) and the mass bound, through `sum_sub_le_of_eq_on`.

The arithmetic that turns the two near-invariances into the closeness is `norm_sub_sq_le_of_re_inner_ge`, `re_inner_ge_of_two_close` and `norm_sub_normalize_sq_le`, and it is the *repaired* chain: the pinned paper bounded $\left\| {(H_W - {I}) v} \right\|$ by $\delta_S$ where its own preceding display gives $\delta_S$ for the square, and compared a squared triangle inequality with $2\sqrt{\delta_S}$ where the bound on the square is $4 \delta_S$. The last step is `eq:qld-unitary-5` at the *mirror* instance (`sum_bornProb_physVec_ge`): Bob's exact Pauli measurement against Alice's $(\textsc{Pauli}, W)$ reading is the second cut's $\tilde{M}$ against the swapped strategy's Pauli reading. `sum_snorm_sq_alice_le` applies the chain at $\Delta = |\textsc{aux}\rangle \otimes |\textsc{EPR}_q\rangle^{\otimes M}$ read on the physical model (`physAux`, the register model's state carried there by the associativity of item 1's cut, which `unassoc` inverts: `unassoc_W_physAux`), for any $|\textsc{aux}\rangle$ close to the swapped state. `sum_snorm_sq_bob_le` is Alice's at the mirror, relative to the *same* $|\textsc{aux}\rangle$: the mirror's product state and its swapped physical state are this one's with the parties exchanged (`mirror_physSwap_mulVec`), so no second auxiliary state is needed. One point of encoding: the Schwartz–Zippel packaging reads its index through coefficient tables of bounded individual degree, and `ancPoly` is the table of ${\mathrm{Coded}}(h)$, faithful because the encoding is multilinear and $d \geq 1$ (`toMv_coeffTable`).

*And `eq:qld-unitary-5`.* It needed no new estimate: its engine is the paper's `fact:triangle-for-simeq`, already in Foundations as `agreeSum_triangle` (with $11\delta$ for the paper's $9\delta$, as Lemma [All padded subtests succeed](lem-qld-global-success.md) records). Two adapters put it to work. `inconsistency_triangle` is the estimate in the vocabulary this interface states everything in — an inconsistency rather than an agreement, the two being the same number. The three legs are not read in the same model: $\tilde{M}^{W,u}$ needs the ancilla half with the first party, which is the physical model, while the strategy's own measurements live in its own model. They are read on the physical model with the registers inert (`physInert`), an embedding of models, along which every inconsistency is the same number. `SimulPair.inconsistency_mTilde_pauli_le` is then the display, with the two middle legs — the game's point–point and point–Pauli consistencies, the latter read through `pauliAtPOVM` — as hypotheses, in the same way `exists_auxVec_close` takes item 1's near-invariances. They are asked for in the form the game supplies them: an inconsistency of the strategy's own measurements carried along that embedding is their inconsistency in the strategy's own model, so `inconsistency_mTilde_pauli_le'` takes them on $|\psi\rangle$, where `agree_subtest_le` leaves them.

*And those two legs are discharged.* They are items 1 and 3 of Lemma [Win implications](../part-02/lem-qld-win.md) at the point and Pauli types — `item_consistency` and `item_pauli_consistency`, both already in — and three steps carry each into the form the triangle wants. The point question a content asks is the point the content carries, so the average over contents of a function of that point is the uniform average over points (`sum_content_pt`); and for *projective* families a cross-party deviation is exactly twice the inconsistency (`inconsistency_eq_half_xPovmDist` — only $\leq$ holds for general POVMs, and what makes it an equality is that both families' masses are exactly one). That gives `inconsistency_pt_pt_le` and `inconsistency_pt_pauli_le` at $86\varepsilon$, which is `agree_subtest_le`'s $172\varepsilon$ halved, and `inconsistency_mTilde_pauli_le_of_win` is then `eq:qld-unitary-5` from the game's soundness and item 1 of Lemma [Exact Pauli operators](lem-qld-exact-paulis.md) alone.
